## What changed for a reader

- A **reference section** that explains the vocabulary, the law and the documents, so the record pages can
  stay short.
- A **claim ledger**: every factual claim on one page, downloadable, each with how strong it is and what
  limits go with it.
- A **named index**: for every person and body named, why they appear and what the record does not say.
- A **record page for every source**, showing where it is cited and which claims rest on it.
- **Search**, a **start-here** page with four reading paths, an FAQ, and privacy and accessibility statements.

## What changed for a maintainer

- `npm run check` now refuses to pass if a citation points nowhere, a link or anchor is broken, a page
  lacks the non-affiliation notice, a claim is graded above its best source, an absence has no caveat, an
  allegation is marked verified, or a reference article names a person or university from the subject matter.
- `npm run audit` lists which sources to check first, ranked by how much rests on each.
- Each source has a printable worksheet listing every claim and sentence that depends on it.

## What did not change

**No source has been verified.** All 58 sources and all 70 claims are still marked pending. Today's volume
went into structure, tooling and general reference material on purpose, because adding more claims about
named people on top of unverified sources would have made the site worse. The new tooling exists to make
that verification faster; it does not replace it.

The record pages (Program for Evolutionary Dynamics, medical school, donor pathways, DNA record, campus
safety, comparison) were not expanded.

## Quality notes on the new content

The reference content was written from general knowledge, without access to the statutes or agency pages.
It is institution-neutral and names no one from the site's subject matter (enforced by test), but it has not
been fact-checked against primary texts. The writers flagged these for an editor:

- **Time-sensitive law.** The state of the Title IX regulations after the 2025 court ruling; a recent
  amendment adding hazing to campus safety reporting; federal indirect-cost rate policy; the annual
  spending-bill provision on embryo editing; recent revisions to research misconduct and dual-use rules.
- **Specific numbers stated.** The $250,000 foreign-gift reporting threshold; the $75 and $250 charitable
  disclosure and acknowledgment thresholds; the five percent private foundation payout; the 15-employee
  threshold; crime-log timing; the 1 October security report deadline; the 20-working-day FOIA period.
- **Entries that deserve a lawyer's read:** defamation, fair report privilege, nominative use of a name.
- **Massachusetts law.** The public records explainer (private universities, campus police logs) and the
  employment discrimination explainer state specific thresholds and deadlines and should be read by a
  Massachusetts lawyer.
- **External links** (about 60 across the reference content) were written from memory and not opened. Run
  `npm run check:links` from a machine with open network access.
- **Near-duplicate terms** kept and cross-linked: ombuds and ombuds office; private foundation and family
  foundation; the two "quid pro quo" entries; document production and Bates number.
- Guides refer to each other in words, not links, because the writers did not know the final URLs.
