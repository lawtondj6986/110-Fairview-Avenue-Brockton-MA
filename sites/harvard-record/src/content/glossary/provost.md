The provost is the senior academic officer. At most U.S. universities the provost reports to the
president and ranks immediately below, with the deans of the schools reporting to the provost or
jointly to the provost and president.

The office oversees academic policy for the whole institution. Typical responsibilities include
reviewing faculty appointments and tenure cases before they go to the president or board,
coordinating academic budgets, supervising university-wide research policy, and running central
offices for matters such as research integrity, conflict of interest and faculty affairs. Many
cross-school centers and institutes report to the provost. The provost's office is also commonly
where university-wide policies on professional conduct and outside activities are written and where
serious faculty discipline is reviewed.

The strength of the role varies. In a centralized university the provost controls academic budgets
and can direct deans. In a highly decentralized one, schools hold their own money and the provost
works more through coordination and persuasion. Some institutions created the office comparatively
late or give it a narrow portfolio. The title also has other meanings outside the United States,
where a provost may be the head of a college. A reader should check what the provost's office
actually controlled at the institution and time in question.
