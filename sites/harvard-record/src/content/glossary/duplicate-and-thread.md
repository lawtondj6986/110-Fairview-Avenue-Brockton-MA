Collections of released records contain much repetition. A duplicate is an identical copy of a
record that appears more than once, for example because the same email was collected from the
sender's and the recipient's accounts, or was printed and scanned as well as kept electronically. A
near-duplicate differs slightly, as with drafts of one letter or the same message with a different
footer. A thread is a chain of messages in which each reply or forward quotes the earlier ones, so
that the first message may be reproduced in every later one.

Document review tools handle this with deduplication, which removes exact copies using a digital
fingerprint of each file, and with threading, which groups related messages and identifies the most
complete version. Public releases are often not deduplicated, or are only partly so.

Ignoring duplication inflates counts and can mislead. Ten copies of one message are one
communication. A long thread in which a name appears in a quoted first line shows one mention
carried forward, not repeated contact. Threads also need careful reading for who wrote which part
and who received it. A person added to a late reply, or copied on a message, did not write the
earlier messages and may never have read them. Each message should be attributed to its actual
author and date.
