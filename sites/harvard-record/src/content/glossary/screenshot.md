term: "Screenshot (as a source)"

A screenshot records what a screen looked like at a moment. Researchers use screenshots to preserve
content that may be edited or deleted, such as a social media post, a website or a message thread.
They are quick to make and easy to share, which is why they circulate widely as evidence.

Their weakness is that an image is easy to fabricate or alter. Text can be edited in a browser
before capture, and image tools can change any detail. A screenshot also strips away much of the
context: the full thread, the account's identity, the exact time and the underlying page code.
Cropping can remove information that changes the meaning.

Better practice combines a screenshot with other evidence: a capture by a public web archive, which
records the page independently; a saved copy of the page with its address and retrieval time; or
confirmation from the account holder or platform. When a screenshot is the only source, it should
be described as such, with what is known about who made it and when. A screenshot from an unknown
source is a lead to be checked, not a verified record.
