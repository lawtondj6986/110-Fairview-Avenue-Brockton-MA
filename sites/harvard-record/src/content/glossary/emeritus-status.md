Emeritus (feminine emerita) is a title attached to a retired faculty member's former rank, as in
professor emeritus. It signals honorable retirement after long service.

Practice differs on how the title is obtained. At some institutions it is conferred automatically on
retiring tenured professors who meet a service requirement. At others it requires a recommendation
and a vote of the department, dean or governing board. Privileges vary too. Common ones are
continued use of the title, a university email address, library access, an identification card, and
listing on the department website. Some emeriti keep an office or laboratory, continue as principal
investigators on grants, or advise students, subject to approval. They do not ordinarily vote in
faculty meetings or draw a salary, though they may be paid for specific teaching.

Emeritus status is not employment in the usual sense and is not the same as tenure, which ends at
retirement. It is also not necessarily permanent. Many institutions have adopted or clarified rules
allowing the title and its privileges to be revoked for serious misconduct, including misconduct
discovered after retirement. The procedures for revocation are often less developed than those for
disciplining active faculty. A person described as emeritus still carries the institution's name,
which is why the status receives attention when conduct is in question.
