---
term: "Update log"
short: "A dated, public list of the changes made to a publication, including additions, revisions and corrections."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["changelog", "revision history", "change log"]
related: ["correction", "verification-date", "retraction", "footnote", "link-rot"]

An update log is a running record of what changed in a publication and when. Each entry normally
gives the date, the page or section affected, a short description of the change, and the reason,
such as a newly released document, a correction or a response received from a person discussed.

For a reference work that is revised over time, the log does several things. It lets returning
readers see what is new without rereading everything. It makes corrections visible, which is the
difference between an acknowledged fix and a silent edit. It provides accountability, since anyone
can see whether the site changed a statement and why. And it helps people who cited an earlier
version understand what they were relying on.

On this site, when a new public document appears it is added to the source register with a grade,
the affected pages are revised, each page's "last reviewed" date is changed, and the change is
recorded in the update log. Corrections follow the same path.

An update log has limits. It depends on the diligence of whoever maintains it and usually omits
trivial edits such as typographical fixes. It complements, and does not replace, independent records
of a page's history such as web archive snapshots.
