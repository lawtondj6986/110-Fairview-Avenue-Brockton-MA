A corporate foundation is a private foundation established by a business. It is legally separate
from the company, although its funding comes from the company and its board is usually made up of
company executives.

Corporate support reaches universities by several routes, and the foundation is only one. A
corporate foundation makes charitable grants and files a public Form 990-PF listing them. The
company itself may give directly from its budget, which is not publicly itemized. It may run an
employee matching gift program. And it may pay for research through a sponsored research agreement,
which is a contract, not a gift, and typically gives the company negotiated rights such as early
access to results or licensing options. Universities often have a corporate and foundation relations
team to coordinate these contacts.

The distinction between a company's philanthropy and its business dealings matters. Private
foundation rules bar a foundation's assets from being used to benefit the company or its insiders,
so a grant from a corporate foundation should not purchase services or rights for the company. Where
a company wants something in return, the proper channel is a contract. A reader looking at a
company's relationship with a university should expect to find it spread across gift records,
research agreements and sometimes licensing or recruiting arrangements.
