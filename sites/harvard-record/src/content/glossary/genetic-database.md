A genetic database is a structured store of genetic information. The category is broad. It includes
research databases holding the genomes of study participants, reference databases that record how
common each variant is in different populations, clinical databases that collect interpretations of
variants, the customer databases of consumer testing companies, and forensic databases kept by law
enforcement.

Access rules differ by type. Some databases publish only summary statistics and are open to
everyone. Many research databases use controlled access: individual-level data are released only to
approved researchers who agree to conditions, such as not attempting to identify participants. A few
projects publish individual genomes openly with the participants' explicit agreement. Consumer
databases are governed mainly by company policy and contract.

A database of data is different from a biobank of physical samples, although one organization may
keep both. It is also worth separating genotype data from the linked phenotype data, since the
combination is what gives a database research value and what makes it sensitive. Genetic data are
unusual among personal data because they reveal information about relatives who never agreed to take
part.
