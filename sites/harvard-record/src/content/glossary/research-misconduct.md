Research misconduct has a specific meaning in U.S. federal policy. It is fabrication, falsification
or plagiarism in proposing, performing or reviewing research, or in reporting research results.
Fabrication is making up data or results. Falsification is manipulating materials, equipment or
processes, or changing or omitting data, so that the research is not accurately represented in the
record. Plagiarism is appropriating another person's ideas, processes, results or words without
giving appropriate credit. The definition excludes honest error and differences of opinion.

A finding of misconduct requires more than a flawed paper. Under the federal rules there must be a
significant departure from accepted practices of the research community, the conduct must have been
intentional, knowing or reckless, and the allegation must be proven by a preponderance of the
evidence. Institutions that receive federal research funds must have procedures to assess
allegations, usually through an inquiry followed, if warranted, by an investigation. The detailed
federal regulations have been revised, so the current text should be consulted.

Many serious problems fall outside this definition, including mistreatment of research participants,
undisclosed conflicts of interest, misuse of funds and authorship disputes. They are handled under
other rules. Institutions may also adopt broader definitions of their own.
