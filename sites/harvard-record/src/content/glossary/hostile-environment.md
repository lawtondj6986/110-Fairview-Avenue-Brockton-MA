term: "Hostile environment"
short: "A form of unlawful harassment in which unwelcome conduct is serious enough to deny or limit a person's ability to work or to take part in an education program."
category: "Law and compliance"

Hostile environment is one of the two classic categories of harassment under civil rights law. It
exists when unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic, such as sex or race, is serious
enough to interfere with a person's work or education. No tangible penalty, such as a failing grade
or a firing, is required.

The exact threshold depends on the law and the period. Under Title VII, courts ask whether conduct
was severe or pervasive enough to alter the conditions of employment. For Title IX damages suits,
courts require conduct so severe, pervasive and objectively offensive that it effectively denies
equal access to education. The 2020 Title IX regulations adopted that stricter wording for school
grievance processes, while earlier federal guidance used a broader formulation. In every version,
the conduct is judged both from the affected person's view and from that of a reasonable person in
the same position.

The term is a legal test, not a description of an unpleasant workplace or department. Rudeness, a
single offhand remark, or general unfairness usually does not meet it. Conduct that falls short of
the legal threshold can still violate an institution's own conduct rules, which are often written
more broadly.
