---
term: "Interim measures"
short: "Steps a college takes while a misconduct complaint is pending to protect the people involved and their access to education, before any finding is made."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["interim protective measures", "accommodations", "emergency removal"]
related: ["supportive-measures", "no-contact-order", "formal-complaint"]
---

Interim measures are temporary arrangements put in place between a report and its resolution. The
term was standard in federal Title IX guidance before 2020 and remains common in campus policies and
older documents. Examples include changes to class schedules, housing or work assignments, academic
extensions, escorts, no-contact directives, and in some cases restrictions on a respondent's access
to parts of campus or a temporary suspension.

The 2020 Title IX regulations replaced the term with supportive measures and drew a firmer line.
Supportive measures must be non-disciplinary and non-punitive and are available to both parties.
Removing a respondent from the education program before a finding is permitted only as an emergency
removal, after an individualized assessment concludes that the person poses an immediate threat to
someone's physical health or safety, and with notice and an opportunity to challenge the decision.
Employees can be placed on administrative leave during a pending process.

Clery Act regulations use similar language of their own, requiring institutions to tell people who
report certain offenses about available accommodations and protective measures.

Interim measures say nothing about the merits. They are risk management decisions made on incomplete
information. A person placed on leave or moved to different housing during an investigation has not
been found responsible, and the lifting of a measure is not an exoneration.
