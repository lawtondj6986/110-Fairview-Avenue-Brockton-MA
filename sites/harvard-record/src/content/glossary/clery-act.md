The Clery Act is a consumer information law first passed in 1990 and now part of the Higher
Education Act. It applies to every college and university, public or private, that participates in
federal student aid programs. Its premise is that students, employees and families are entitled to
know about crime on and near campus.

The act requires an institution to publish an annual security report by October 1 with three years
of crime statistics and a set of policy statements, to keep a public daily crime log if it has a
police or security department, to issue timely warnings and emergency notifications, and to submit
its statistics to the U.S. Department of Education, which posts them online. Amendments in 2013
added dating violence, domestic violence and stalking, along with procedural and prevention
requirements. The Department can fine institutions for violations.

Clery statistics have a narrow design. They count reports of listed crimes, in defined locations,
that reached designated campus officials or local police. They do not measure how much crime
occurred, and they exclude incidents at private off-campus addresses. A report is counted whether or
not anyone was charged or found responsible. The act is about disclosure. It does not itself decide
how an individual case should be resolved.
