---
term: "Affinity rating"
short: "A fundraiser's estimate of how close a prospect feels to the institution and how likely they are to give, as distinct from how much they could afford."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Inclination rating", "Likelihood rating", "Engagement score", "Affinity score"]
related: ["capacity-rating", "prospect", "cultivation", "prospect-management"]
---

An affinity or inclination rating estimates a prospect's willingness to give. It complements the
capacity rating, which estimates ability. Fundraisers usually consider the two together when
deciding where to spend time.

Affinity is judged from evidence of connection. Common indicators include degrees held, past gifts
and their frequency, volunteer roles, attendance at events, family ties to the institution, replies
to outreach and what the prospect has said to staff about their interests. Some offices use a
statistical score calculated from database fields. Others rely on a gift officer's judgment after a
first meeting. A prospect with high capacity and high affinity is a top priority. One with high
capacity and low affinity becomes a target for cultivation, meaning efforts to build interest
through meetings, visits and introductions to faculty.

Terminology is not standardized. Some offices separate affinity (emotional connection) from
inclination (readiness to give now), and others use one word for both. Whatever the label, the
rating reflects the institution's view, not the prospect's. It can also create a feedback loop: when
a wealthy person shows interest in a particular researcher or field, staff have a reason to deepen
that contact, because it is the clearest path to raising the rating and eventually to a gift.
