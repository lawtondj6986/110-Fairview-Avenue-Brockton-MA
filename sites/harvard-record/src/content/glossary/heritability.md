---
term: "Heritability"
short: "The proportion of the variation in a trait within a particular population that is statistically attributable to genetic differences among its members."
category: "Genetics and biomedical research"

---

Heritability is a statistic. It estimates how much of the variation in a trait, within a specific
population living in a specific range of environments, is associated with genetic differences
between the individuals in that population. It runs from 0 to 1, or 0 to 100 percent. Estimates come
from comparing relatives, classically identical and fraternal twins, and more recently from genomic
data on unrelated people.

The number describes a population, not a person. A heritability of 80 percent for height does not
mean that 80 percent of any individual's height comes from genes. It means that, in the group
studied, most of the differences in height between people track genetic differences.

Several misreadings are common. Heritable does not mean fixed: a highly heritable trait can change
greatly when the environment changes, as average height has with better nutrition. Heritability is
not constant, and it can differ between populations and eras. It says nothing about the causes of
average differences between groups. And it does not measure how well a trait can be predicted from
DNA, which with current methods is usually much lower than the heritability estimate would suggest.
