A research integrity officer (RIO) is the official an institution designates to administer its
research misconduct procedures. U.S. institutions that receive federal health research funding must
have such procedures and must file an assurance with the government that they follow them.

The officer receives allegations and assesses whether they fall within the definition of research
misconduct and are specific enough to pursue. If so, the officer secures the relevant records and
data, notifies the person accused, and organizes an inquiry, which is a preliminary review. If the
inquiry finds sufficient basis, a formal investigation follows, usually conducted by a committee of
faculty. The officer protects the confidentiality of those involved, guards against retaliation
toward people who raise concerns in good faith, and reports to the relevant federal oversight office
when federally funded work is involved. A separate senior official normally makes the final
institutional decision.

The role is procedural. The officer does not personally decide guilt, and proceedings are largely
confidential, so the public often learns of a case only if a federal finding is published or a paper
is corrected or retracted. The officer's remit is misconduct in the technical sense. Human subjects
complaints, financial conflicts and harassment are generally handled by other offices.
