short: "A conclusion reached by reasoning from evidence, as opposed to something the evidence states directly."

An inference is a step from what the evidence says to what one concludes it means. If a calendar
shows a meeting between two people and a gift arrives a week later, the calendar and the gift are
facts in the record. That the meeting led to the gift is an inference. Inference is unavoidable and
legitimate. Courts, scientists and historians all draw conclusions from indirect evidence. The
discipline lies in marking where the record ends and the reasoning begins.

A sound inference has identifiable features. The underlying facts are established. The conclusion is
the most reasonable explanation and not merely one possible explanation. Alternative explanations
have been considered. And the strength of the conclusion matches the strength of the support. Chains
of inference weaken quickly, because each link's uncertainty compounds.

In writing about real people, the cost of a wrong inference falls on the person described. For that
reason this site states what the documents show and avoids supplying conclusions of its own about
motive, knowledge or coordination. Where a cited source draws an inference, it is attributed to that
source. Readers can apply a simple test to anything they read: could this sentence be checked
directly against a document, or does it depend on words such as "suggests", "appears", "raises
questions" or "it is hard to believe"?
