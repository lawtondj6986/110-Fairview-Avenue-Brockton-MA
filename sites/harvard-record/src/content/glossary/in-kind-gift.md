term: "In-kind gift"
short: "A gift of property, goods or services instead of money, such as equipment, artwork, books, software, real estate or the use of facilities."

An in-kind gift is a contribution of something other than cash or publicly traded securities.
Universities receive scientific equipment, art, rare books and papers, land and buildings, software
licenses, and sometimes services or the use of property such as aircraft or event space.

In-kind gifts take more work to accept than cash. Gift acceptance policies usually require review of
whether the institution can use, maintain or sell the item, and whether it carries costs or
liabilities, as real estate often does. The institution records the gift at an estimated fair value.
For the donor's tax purposes, valuation is the donor's responsibility, and U.S. tax rules require a
qualified independent appraisal for non-cash contributions above certain amounts. The institution
acknowledges receipt and describes the item but generally does not state a value on the receipt.

In-kind support is easy to overlook when tracing a relationship. Travel, hospitality, use of a
private residence or facility, and payment of an expense directly to a vendor may never be booked as
a gift at all, either because no one reported them or because they were provided to an individual
and not to the institution. Such benefits may fall instead under conflict of interest or outside
activity disclosure rules.
