---
term: "Resolution agreement"
short: "A written agreement between a school and a federal civil rights office in which the school commits to specific steps to resolve an investigation."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["voluntary resolution agreement"]
related: ["office-for-civil-rights", "letter-of-findings", "consent-decree", "compliance-review"]
---

A resolution agreement is a signed document in which an institution promises a federal agency, most
often the Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights, that it will take listed actions to
settle a civil rights investigation. It is the usual way such investigations end when the agency has
concerns.

Typical terms include revising policies, training staff, re-examining past complaints, conducting
climate surveys, and sending the agency periodic reports. The agency then monitors the school until
it decides the terms have been met. If a school fails to comply, the agency can reopen the matter or
move toward formal enforcement.

Two points are easy to miss. First, an agreement can be reached before the agency completes its
investigation, in which case there may be no findings at all. Even after findings, the agreement
often says that the school does not admit a violation. Second, a resolution agreement is an
administrative settlement, not a court order. It differs from a consent decree, which a judge
approves and can enforce directly. Readers should look at the accompanying letter to learn what, if
anything, the agency actually concluded.
