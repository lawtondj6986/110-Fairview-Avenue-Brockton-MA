---
term: "Supportive measures"
short: "Non-disciplinary, individualized services a school offers to someone affected by alleged sex-based harassment, whether or not a formal complaint is filed."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["interim-measures", "no-contact-order", "formal-complaint", "title-ix-coordinator"]
---

Supportive measures are practical adjustments a school provides to restore or preserve a person's
access to education after a report of sexual harassment. The 2020 Title IX regulations define them
as non-disciplinary, non-punitive, individualized services, offered as appropriate and without
charge, to a complainant or a respondent. They are available before or after a formal complaint is
filed, and when no formal complaint is filed at all.

Common examples are counseling, deadline extensions, changes to class or work schedules, changes to
housing, campus escort services, leaves of absence, and mutual restrictions on contact. The Title IX
coordinator is responsible for arranging them and must consider the wishes of the person who
reported. The measures are meant not to burden the other party unreasonably.

Supportive measures are not sanctions, and receiving or being subject to them implies no finding. A
person who has had a class schedule changed or been asked to avoid contact has not been found
responsible for anything. Removing a student from campus entirely is treated differently. The
regulations permit emergency removal only after an individualized safety and risk analysis, with a
chance to challenge the decision. The older term interim measures covered similar ground and is
still found in many documents.
