term: "Gift agreement amendment"

A gift agreement amendment is a document that changes the terms of an existing gift agreement. It
may extend a pledge payment schedule, widen or narrow the purpose a fund may be spent on, change the
name attached to a gift, or add conditions that were not in the original. It is usually signed by
the same parties, or their successors, as the original agreement.

In practice, amendments are common and often routine. A donor may add money to an existing fund, or
a program the gift supported may close, leaving the institution to agree a new use with the donor.
When the donor has died or cannot be reached and the original restriction can no longer be
followed, a change usually requires a different route, such as a court proceeding or a statutory
release with notice to the state attorney general.

A reader who sees only the original agreement may not be seeing the current terms. The reverse is
also true: an amendment changes only what it says it changes, and the rest of the original agreement
normally stays in force. An amendment is not evidence of wrongdoing on its own; the question is what
it changed and why.
