---
term: "Prompt and equitable"
short: "The Title IX regulatory requirement that a school's grievance procedures resolve complaints of sex discrimination without undue delay and fairly to both parties."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["title-ix", "formal-complaint", "office-for-civil-rights"]
---

Title IX regulations have long required schools to adopt and publish grievance procedures that
provide for the prompt and equitable resolution of complaints of sex discrimination. The phrase is
the basic test federal reviewers apply to a school's complaint process.

Prompt means the school acts within reasonable time frames that it has set out in advance, and
explains delays. Earlier federal guidance suggested a typical investigation should take about sixty
days. The 2020 regulations dropped any fixed number and required schools to set their own reasonably
prompt time frames. Equitable means both the person complaining and the person accused get
comparable rights: notice of the allegations, a chance to present evidence and witnesses, an
unbiased decision-maker, written notice of the outcome, and equal access to any appeal.

Many federal findings against schools concern this requirement instead of the underlying incident.
Typical problems include long unexplained delays, failure to tell a complainant the outcome, or
procedures that gave one side opportunities the other did not have.

The phrase describes the process, not the result. A process can be prompt and equitable and still
reach a conclusion that one party believes is wrong.
