term: "Advocate"
short: "A trained support person who helps someone affected by sexual or relationship violence understand options, obtain services and get through institutional or legal processes."

An advocate is a person whose job is to support an individual who has experienced sexual assault,
relationship violence or stalking. Advocates explain reporting options, accompany people to medical
examinations, police interviews or campus meetings, help arrange academic and housing adjustments,
and connect people with counseling and legal services. Some work for a campus office, and others for
independent community organizations such as rape crisis centers.

The role is defined by whom it serves. An advocate works for the individual and follows that
person's choices, including a choice not to report. This is unlike a Title IX coordinator or
investigator, who must remain neutral between the parties. Many institutions designate campus
advocates as confidential. In a number of states, communications with a certified advocate are also
protected by a statutory privilege, although the scope differs by state and may not cover every
campus employee who uses the title.

An advocate is usually not a lawyer and does not give legal advice or represent a person in a
hearing. Federal rules give each party in certain campus proceedings the right to an adviser of
their choice, who may be an attorney, and that adviser role is distinct. Some institutions also
offer comparable support staff for respondents, typically under a different name.
