Colleges handle security in different ways. Some employ sworn police officers who have completed
state-recognized training, carry the powers of arrest, and investigate crimes. Others employ
non-sworn security staff who patrol, respond and observe but must call outside police to make
arrests. Many have both. Municipal, county and state police are public agencies that serve a
geographic area that may include the campus.

State law determines whether a college can have its own police force and what powers it holds.
Jurisdiction is often limited to campus property and adjacent streets, sometimes extended by
agreement with the local department. The Clery Act requires each institution to state in its annual
security report what enforcement authority its officers have, whether they can make arrests, and how
they work with state and local agencies.

Two points are easily overlooked. First, campus officers report to the institution's administration,
which makes the department's independence a recurring question in sensitive cases. Second, access to
records can differ. Records of a public university's police are generally covered by state public
records law. Whether the police department of a private university is covered depends on the state,
and practice has changed in several states. The Clery daily crime log must be open to the public in
either case.
