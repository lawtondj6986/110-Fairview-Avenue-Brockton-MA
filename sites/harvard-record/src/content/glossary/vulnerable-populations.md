In research ethics, a vulnerable population is a group whose members may be at heightened risk of
being coerced, unduly influenced or harmed in research. U.S. federal regulations contain specific
additional protections for pregnant women, fetuses and newborns, for prisoners, and for children.
They also direct ethics boards to consider safeguards for others who are vulnerable to coercion or
undue influence, such as people with impaired decision-making capacity and economically or
educationally disadvantaged persons.

Added safeguards take several forms. Children generally take part only with a parent's permission
and, where they are capable of it, their own assent. Research with prisoners is limited to certain
categories and requires a prisoner representative in the review. For adults who cannot consent, a
legally authorized representative may decide. Boards may require independent consent monitors or
limits on the level of risk allowed.

Current thinking treats vulnerability as depending on the situation as well as on group membership.
Students, employees and trainees can be vulnerable in relation to a study run by someone with power
over them, although no regulation lists them by name. Labeling a group vulnerable is also not meant
to exclude its members from research, since exclusion can deny them the benefits of the knowledge
produced.
