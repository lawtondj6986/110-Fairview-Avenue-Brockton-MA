A visitor policy governs short-term guests: people who come to campus for a meeting, a seminar, a
tour or a brief collaboration and who hold no appointment. Universities often have several such
policies, for residence halls, laboratories, libraries and events, each written by a different
office.

Typical provisions make a host responsible for a guest's conduct, require the guest to be escorted
in restricted areas, require sign-in at certain buildings, and limit how long a person can remain a
visitor before a formal affiliation is needed. Laboratory visitor rules add safety training and may
restrict work with hazardous materials. Separate requirements apply to minors on campus and, in some
research settings, to foreign nationals under export control rules. Visits by prominent or
high-profile people are usually arranged directly by the host and are not subject to any special
approval unless they involve a public event.

Visitor rules are among the least uniformly applied on a campus. In many academic buildings a guest
of a professor needs no registration, so no record is created. Repeated visits can amount to a
regular presence without the person ever acquiring a formal status. That gap, between a casual guest
and an appointed affiliate, is where institutional knowledge is weakest. A policy's existence should
not be taken as evidence that a sign-in log was kept.
