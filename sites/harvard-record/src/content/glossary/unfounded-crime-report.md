term: "Unfounded crime report"
short: "In campus crime statistics, a reported crime that law enforcement has formally determined to be false or baseless, which is removed from the count but reported as a separate total."

A Clery crime report can be withdrawn from an institution's statistics only if it is unfounded. In
Department of Education guidance, that determination must be made by sworn or commissioned law
enforcement personnel, after a full investigation, and must find that the report was false or
baseless. Other staff, such as a dean or a Title IX coordinator, cannot unfound a crime for Clery
purposes, and a victim's decision not to pursue a case does not make a report unfounded.

When a report is unfounded, it is taken out of the crime statistics, but the institution must
report the number of unfounded crimes separately in its annual submission to the Department. That
gives readers a way to see whether unfounding is frequent.

Unfounded does not mean unproven. A report that never led to an arrest, a charge or a finding of
responsibility remains in the statistics. Clery counts are reports, not convictions, so a reader
should not treat either the statistics or the unfounded total as a measure of how many crimes were
proved. The rules on unfounding are set in regulation and guidance that have been revised; check the
current version.
