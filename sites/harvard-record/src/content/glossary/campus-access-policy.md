---
term: "Campus access policy"
short: "The rules that determine who may enter an institution's buildings and grounds, at what times, with what credentials and on whose authority."
category: "University governance"
aliases: ["Building access policy", "Facilities access policy", "Trespass policy", "No-trespass order"]

A campus access policy sets out who is allowed where. Universities are a mix of open spaces, such as
quadrangles, museums and public lecture halls, and controlled spaces, such as laboratories,
residence halls, offices and libraries.

Access is usually tied to status. Students, faculty and staff receive identification cards that open
the buildings their role requires. Affiliates and visiting scholars receive access when a sponsoring
unit requests it. Members of the public may enter open areas and attend public events. The policy
states who can authorize access to a building (often a building manager, department administrator or
laboratory head), how after-hours entry works, and how access ends when a person's status ends.
Private universities own their property and may exclude any person. They typically do so by a
written no-trespass notice issued through campus police or security.

In practice, access decisions are local. A department or a principal investigator can often sponsor
a card or admit a guest without review by any central office. No single list may exist of who could
enter a given building. A policy also describes what should happen, not what did. Establishing a
person's actual presence requires other evidence, such as card records, visitor logs, correspondence
or witness accounts. See `/campus-safety/` for related material on this site.
