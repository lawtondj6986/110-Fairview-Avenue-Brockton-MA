---
term: "No-finding outcome"
short: "The result of a disciplinary or misconduct process that ends without a finding that a policy was violated, whether because evidence was insufficient, a complaint was withdrawn or a case was dismissed."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["Not responsible", "Insufficient evidence", "Not substantiated", "Unsubstantiated", "Case closed without findings"]
related: ["finding-vs-allegation", "preponderance-of-the-evidence", "outcome-letter", "decision-maker", "absence-of-evidence", "informal-resolution"]
---

A no-finding outcome is any ending to a process in which the body did not conclude that the
respondent violated a policy. Labels differ: "not responsible," "not substantiated," "insufficient
evidence," "no violation found" and "closed" are all common. The process may have run its full
course and found the evidence did not meet the required standard. It may instead have stopped early,
because the complainant withdrew, the respondent left the institution, the conduct fell outside the
policy, or the parties chose informal resolution.

These outcomes are easy to misreport. A finding that the evidence did not meet the standard is not a
finding that the allegation was false, and it is not an exoneration in the ordinary sense. Equally,
it is not a hidden finding of guilt. Under a preponderance standard, the decision-maker concluded
only that a violation was not shown to be more likely than not.

When a record describes a no-finding outcome, note exactly which kind it was, which standard applied
and who decided. A case dismissed because the respondent left differs from one decided after a full
hearing, and both differ from a finding that the report was unfounded.
