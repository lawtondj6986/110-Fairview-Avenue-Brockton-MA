---
term: "Genome"
short: "The complete set of DNA in an organism or cell, including all of its genes and the much larger stretches of DNA between them."
category: "Genetics and biomedical research"
related: ["dna", "gene", "exome", "whole-genome-sequencing"]
---

A genome is the complete set of DNA of an organism. The human genome contains roughly three billion
base pairs (the paired chemical letters of DNA) in each of the two copies a person inherits, one
from each parent. It includes about 20,000 genes that code for proteins, which together take up only
a small fraction of the total. The rest includes sequences that regulate when genes are switched on,
repeated sequences, and regions whose function is not yet well understood.

Any two people have genomes that are overwhelmingly alike. The differences, called variants, number
in the millions per person and account for inherited variation in traits and disease risk.
Researchers compare an individual's genome with a reference genome, a standard sequence assembled
from several people, to find where that individual differs.

The phrase "having your genome sequenced" can describe quite different things. A consumer test
usually reads well under one percent of the genome. Exome sequencing reads the protein-coding
portion. Only whole-genome sequencing attempts to read nearly all of it, and even then some regions
are hard to read accurately. When a document mentions a genome, it is worth checking which of these
was actually done.
