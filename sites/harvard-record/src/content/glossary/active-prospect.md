term: "Active prospect"
short: "A prospect who has been assigned to a specific fundraiser and is being deliberately moved toward a gift request."

An active prospect is one the institution is currently pursuing. The prospect has been assigned to a
named gift officer, sits in that officer's portfolio, and has a recorded strategy and a stage in the
fundraising cycle.

Assignment matters inside a development office because it sets who may contact the prospect. Under
most prospect management rules, other staff must clear any approach with the assigned manager, so
the prospect is not asked for competing gifts by different parts of the university. The record for
an active prospect usually shows the stage (for example identification, cultivation, solicitation or
stewardship), a target amount, a planned request date, and contact reports written after each
meeting or call. Managers review portfolios regularly and remove prospects who show no progress.

Active status describes the institution's internal posture. It does not mean that the prospect has
agreed to anything or even knows a strategy exists. The opposite status also deserves care. A
prospect may be marked inactive, disqualified or "do not solicit" for many reasons, including lack
of interest, a request from the person, or a decision that the institution should not seek their
money. The reason is recorded in the database, if at all, and is rarely visible from outside.
