---
term: "Unrestricted gift"
short: "A gift the institution may spend on any of its purposes, because the donor has set no conditions on its use."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Unrestricted funds", "Flexible gift", "Discretionary gift"]
related: ["restricted-gift", "current-use-gift", "donor-intent", "decentralized-budgeting"]
---

An unrestricted gift carries no donor-imposed limit on use. The institution, through its normal
budget process, decides where the money goes.

Annual fund contributions from alumni are the most familiar example. Institutions value unrestricted
money because it can cover costs that few donors choose to fund, such as building maintenance,
administration and financial aid shortfalls. In financial statements it appears as net assets
without donor restrictions. A governing board may set aside unrestricted money for a purpose or
treat it like endowment (often called a board-designated or quasi-endowment fund), but the board can
reverse that decision, which a donor restriction does not allow.

The word unrestricted describes the absence of legal conditions from the donor. It does not always
mean the money is unassigned in practice. A gift may be unrestricted in law but given to one school
or one dean's discretionary fund, so only that unit decides its use. Some gifts are also described
loosely as unrestricted support "for the work of" a named researcher. In that case the gift is
restricted to that person's program, even though spending within the program is flexible. Reading
the gift agreement or fund terms is the only reliable way to know which meaning applies.
