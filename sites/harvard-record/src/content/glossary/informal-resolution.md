---
term: "Informal resolution"
short: "A voluntary way of resolving a campus misconduct complaint, such as mediation or an agreed outcome, without a full investigation and formal decision."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["alternative resolution", "adaptable resolution"]
related: ["formal-complaint", "supportive-measures", "settlement"]
---

Informal resolution is any process that settles a complaint without a completed investigation and
adjudication. Forms include mediation, facilitated conversation, restorative practices, and an
agreement in which the respondent accepts certain terms, such as training, restricted contact or
leaving a role.

Under the 2020 Title IX regulations, a school may offer informal resolution only after a formal
complaint has been filed. Both parties must give voluntary, informed, written consent, and either
may withdraw and return to the formal process before an agreement is reached. A school may not
require anyone to take part. The regulations do not allow informal resolution of an allegation that
an employee sexually harassed a student. Earlier federal guidance was more restrictive and
discouraged mediation in sexual assault cases altogether.

For someone reading the record, the main consequence is that informal resolution usually produces no
finding. The matter is closed by agreement, the terms are often confidential, and the respondent is
neither found responsible nor cleared. Statistics that count only findings of responsibility
therefore leave these cases out. An informally resolved complaint should not be described as either
substantiated or unsubstantiated.
