A capacity rating estimates how much a prospect could give if they chose to. It is usually expressed
as a dollar range or a coded band and refers to a total given over a multi-year period, not a single
check.

Researchers build the rating from visible assets and indicators of wealth: real estate,
shareholdings disclosed in public filings, business ownership, compensation, family foundations and
known gifts elsewhere. A formula or rule of thumb converts estimated wealth into an estimated giving
capacity. The rating is stored in the donor database and used to decide which gift officer handles
the prospect, what size of request is realistic and how much senior attention the prospect receives.
Prospects rated at the top levels are often managed by a dedicated principal gifts team and may be
introduced to the president.

Capacity is not intention. A high rating says a person could give a large amount, not that they will
or that they have any connection to the institution. It is also an estimate from partial information
and can overstate or understate real wealth. The rating matters for understanding institutional
behavior, because it helps explain why some people received a level of attention and access that
others did not.
