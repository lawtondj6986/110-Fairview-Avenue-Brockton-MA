The Office of Research Integrity, or ORI, oversees research misconduct proceedings involving funding
from the Public Health Service, which includes the National Institutes of Health. Its rules are in
42 CFR Part 93. They define research misconduct as fabrication, falsification or plagiarism and set
out what institutions must do when they receive an allegation.

Institutions carry out the fact-finding themselves, through an assessment, an inquiry and, where
warranted, an investigation. They then report to ORI, which reviews the institution's work and may
make its own finding. When ORI makes a finding, it can impose administrative actions such as a period
of supervision on future federal research or exclusion from federal funding, and it publishes a
notice of the case summary in the Federal Register and on its website. The rules were revised in
recent years, so the current text should be checked.

ORI does not cover research funded by other agencies, such as the National Science Foundation, which
have their own offices. It does not handle human subjects protection, which is the role of the
Office for Human Research Protections, or financial conflicts of interest. A case that never reaches
ORI may still have been handled inside an institution.
