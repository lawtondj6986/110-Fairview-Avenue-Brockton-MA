---
term: "Corroboration"
short: "Independent support for a claim from a second source or piece of evidence that does not simply derive from the first."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["independent confirmation", "cross-checking"]
related: ["single-source-reporting", "reliability-grade", "second-hand-account", "attribution", "primary-source"]
---

Corroboration is confirmation of a claim by evidence that is independent of the original source. If
a person says a meeting took place, a calendar entry, a travel record or another attendee's account
would corroborate it. The strength of corroboration depends on the independence and quality of the
supporting evidence, not on the number of times a claim has been repeated.

Independence is the test that matters most. Two sources are independent only if each had its own
access to the facts. Ten articles that all rely on the same original report amount to one source.
Two people who both heard a story from a third person corroborate that the story was told, not that
it is true. Documents created at the time of an event, before anyone had a reason to shape the
account, are usually the strongest corroboration.

Corroboration is often partial. Evidence may confirm one element of an account, for example that two
people corresponded, without confirming another, such as what they agreed. Careful writing says
which parts are supported. Corroboration also differs from verification of a document's
authenticity, and from consistency. An account that merely fits with known facts has not been
confirmed by them. Uncorroborated does not mean false. It means the claim rests on a single support
and is labeled accordingly.
