A faculty handbook is the main written statement of the rules that apply to faculty. It typically
covers the types of appointment, procedures for hiring and promotion, the meaning and protections of
tenure, teaching and research obligations, outside activities, standards of conduct, and the steps
for discipline, dismissal and grievances. Large universities may have one handbook for the whole
institution and separate ones for each school.

Handbooks are usually adopted or approved by the governing board, often after review by faculty
bodies, and amended over time. Courts in many states have treated some handbook provisions as
binding contract terms, especially where an appointment letter refers to them, though the law varies
by state and depends on the handbook's own wording. For anyone reading a disciplinary record, the
handbook in force at the time of the events is the rule book that the institution was supposed to
follow.

A handbook describes procedures, not outcomes. It does not show how often a procedure is used, and
institutions sometimes act through informal routes such as negotiated departures that never reach
the formal process. Older versions may be hard to find, so check the date of any copy.
