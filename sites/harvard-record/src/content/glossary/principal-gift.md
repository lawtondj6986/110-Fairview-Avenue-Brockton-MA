term: "Principal gift"
short: "A gift in the highest size tier an institution recognizes, typically handled by a small senior team with direct involvement from the president."

A principal gift is one at the top of an institution's scale, above the ordinary major gift level.
Each institution defines the threshold for itself, and many smaller institutions do not use the
category at all.

Universities that use the term usually have a principal gifts office: a few senior fundraisers who
manage the wealthiest prospects on behalf of the whole institution. These prospects are relatively
few. Their relationships are long, often involve several family members and advisers, and are
conducted at the highest level, with the president, provost, deans and trustees taking part
personally. Proposals at this level frequently involve naming a school, building or institute, or
creating a new program, and may be negotiated over years with lawyers on both sides.

The size of principal gifts gives them unusual weight in institutional planning. A single commitment
can decide whether a project goes ahead. That is also why this tier attracts the most questions
about influence. Policies that protect academic independence apply to principal gifts as to any
others, but the people negotiating them are the same senior officials who would otherwise oversee
the fundraisers. The term describes a size and a method of handling. It does not indicate anything
about the donor's motives or the propriety of the gift.
