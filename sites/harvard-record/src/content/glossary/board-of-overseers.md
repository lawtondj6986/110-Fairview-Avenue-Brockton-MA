term: "Board of overseers (second governing board)"
short: "A second, usually larger board that some universities have alongside the main fiduciary board, with advisory, review or consent powers set by the charter."
category: "University governance"

Most U.S. universities have a single governing board. A small number, mostly older private
institutions, have two. In such a bicameral arrangement, one board is small and holds fiduciary
control of finances and management. The second, often called a board of overseers, is larger and has
a role defined by the institution's charter and bylaws.

The powers of a second board differ from one institution to another. Common functions include giving
advice on academic direction, consenting to certain actions of the primary board such as some senior
appointments, and supervising visiting committees that periodically review schools and departments.
Members are frequently elected by alumni for fixed terms, which makes the second board a channel for
alumni opinion. It generally does not control the budget or the endowment.

The name is not a reliable guide. At some institutions a body called a board of overseers or board
of visitors is purely advisory to a single school. At certain public universities "board of
visitors" is the name of the actual governing board. To understand a given body, a reader has to
consult the institution's founding documents. When responsibility for a decision is at issue, the
question is which board had legal authority over that kind of decision, and whether the matter was
ever put before it.
