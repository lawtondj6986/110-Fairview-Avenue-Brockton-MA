term: "Dean"
short: "The head of a school or faculty within a university, responsible for its budget, faculty appointments, academic programs and often its own fundraising."

A dean leads one of the major academic units of a university, such as a school of medicine, law or
engineering, or a faculty of arts and sciences. The dean is appointed by the president or provost,
usually after a search, and serves at their pleasure, often while keeping a tenured professorship.

Within the school, the dean is the chief executive. The dean proposes and manages the budget,
approves or recommends faculty hiring and promotion, allocates space, appoints department chairs and
center directors, and is the first senior official to handle most questions of faculty conduct.
Deans of schools with their own development staff are also the principal fundraisers for those
schools and meet regularly with donors.

The title is used for other roles too. A dean of students, a dean of admissions or an associate dean
for research is an administrator with a defined portfolio and not the head of a school. In records,
it is important to identify which is meant. In a decentralized university the dean of a school, not
the president, is often the highest official with direct knowledge of what happens in its
laboratories and centers, and the dean's office may hold the relevant files. Central policies may
still require a dean to refer certain matters, such as unusual gifts or serious misconduct, upward.
