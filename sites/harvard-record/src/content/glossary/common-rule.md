The Common Rule is the Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects. It is called common
because many federal departments and agencies adopted the same text in 1991. The Department of
Health and Human Services version is at 45 C.F.R. Part 46, Subpart A. A substantially revised
version took general effect in 2019.

The rule applies to research involving human subjects that a signatory agency conducts or funds. Its
two core requirements are review by an institutional review board, a committee that weighs risks and
benefits before a study starts, and informed consent from participants. Some low-risk categories of
research are exempt, and boards can waive or alter consent under stated conditions. Additional
subparts of the HHS regulations add protections for pregnant women, prisoners and children. The
Office for Human Research Protections oversees compliance for HHS-funded work.

The Common Rule does not cover all research. Privately funded studies fall outside it unless the
institution has chosen to apply it to everything, as many universities do, or unless Food and Drug
Administration rules apply because a drug or device is being tested. It governs research, not
clinical care, and it protects living individuals. It also regulates how a study is conducted, not
who may fund it.
