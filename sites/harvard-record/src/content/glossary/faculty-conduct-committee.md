A faculty conduct committee is the peer body in a university's disciplinary process for professors.
Its name, composition and powers are set by the faculty handbook and differ among institutions and
among schools of the same institution.

Some committees are standing bodies elected by the faculty. Others are formed case by case. Their
work commonly comes in two stages. A preliminary or screening panel decides whether an allegation
warrants formal proceedings. A hearing panel then receives evidence, hears from the accused
professor and witnesses, and issues written findings with a recommended outcome. The committee
usually advises, and the final decision belongs to a dean, the provost, the president or the
governing board. Separate bodies with their own procedures typically handle allegations of research
misconduct and of sexual harassment or discrimination, because federal rules shape those processes.

The role of faculty peers reflects the principles of tenure and shared governance: professors should
not lose their positions without the judgment of colleagues. Proceedings are confidential, and
results are seldom published. Not every concern reaches such a committee. Many matters are resolved
informally by a dean, ended by a resignation, or handled under a different policy. The existence of
a committee process therefore does not show that a given matter went through it.
