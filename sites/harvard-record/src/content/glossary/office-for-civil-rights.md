The Office for Civil Rights, usually shortened to OCR, is the part of the U.S. Department of
Education that enforces several civil rights laws in education. These include Title VI (race, color
and national origin), Title IX (sex), Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act (disability) and the Age
Discrimination Act. Its authority comes from the federal funding a school accepts.

OCR works mainly in two ways. It investigates complaints filed by individuals or groups, and it
opens compliance reviews on its own initiative. An investigation can end with a dismissal, a finding
of insufficient evidence, or a finding of noncompliance. Most findings of noncompliance are settled
through a resolution agreement in which the school promises specific changes. OCR can, in principle,
begin proceedings to end federal funding or refer a matter to the Department of Justice, but that is
rare.

OCR does not award money damages to individuals and does not prosecute crimes. An open OCR
investigation means only that a complaint met the threshold for investigation. It is not a finding
that the school broke the law. Several other federal departments have their own office with the same
name, most notably the Department of Health and Human Services, which enforces HIPAA.
