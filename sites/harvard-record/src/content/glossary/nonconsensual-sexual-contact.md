Nonconsensual sexual contact is a measurement category used by researchers, not a legal offense.
Campus climate surveys use it to report how many respondents experienced sexual contact they did not
agree to. The category typically combines two kinds of behavior, penetration and sexual touching
such as kissing or groping, and several tactics: physical force or threats of force, inability to
consent because the person was passed out, asleep or incapacitated by alcohol or drugs, and in some
surveys coercion by non-physical threats or contact without active, ongoing agreement.

Surveys ask about specific behaviors in plain language and avoid legal labels such as rape, because
many people who have had these experiences do not apply those labels to them. Results are usually
reported separately by behavior and tactic, by gender and student status, and for a defined period
such as the current academic year or since enrollment.

Any figure needs its definition attached. A headline rate that includes sexual touching by any
tactic will be much higher than a rate for penetration by force, and neither corresponds to the
Clery Act's offense categories or to a state criminal statute. Survey results also count experiences
reported anonymously by respondents. They are not counts of incidents reported to the institution,
and they are not findings about any person.
