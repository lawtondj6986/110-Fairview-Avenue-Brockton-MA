An ancestry test compares a person's DNA with DNA from reference groups in order to estimate where
their ancestors may have lived. Most such tests also compare the customer with other customers and
report likely relatives, based on how much DNA two people share.

The two functions rest on different footing. Relative matching is a direct measurement. Close
relationships, such as parent and child or full siblings, are identified with very high reliability,
and confidence falls as relationships become more distant. Ancestry percentages are statistical
estimates. They depend on which reference populations a company has, how it defines regions, and its
algorithm. The same person can receive different percentages from different companies, and a company
may revise them as its reference data change.

An ancestry estimate does not establish nationality, ethnicity, culture or legal membership in any
group. It also does not describe the whole family tree, because a person inherits only part of each
distant ancestor's DNA and none from some of them. Relative matching can reveal previously unknown
family relationships, including for relatives who never took a test themselves. That is a privacy
consequence of the method, not a malfunction.
