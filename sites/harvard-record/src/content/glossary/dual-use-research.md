---
term: "Dual-use research"
short: "Legitimate scientific research whose methods or results could also be misused to cause harm, for example to public health or security."
category: "Research ethics"
aliases: ["dual use research of concern", "DURC"]
related: ["publication-ethics", "institutional-review-board", "gene-editing", "research-protocol"]
---

Dual-use research is research carried out for beneficial purposes that could also be misapplied to
do harm. Much of science is dual-use in a loose sense. Policy focuses on a narrow subset, often
called dual use research of concern: life sciences work that could reasonably be expected to yield
knowledge, products or technologies that might be directly misused to pose a significant threat to
public health, agriculture, the environment or national security. The standard examples involve
experiments that could make a dangerous pathogen more transmissible or more lethal.

Oversight of this subset works through funders and institutions. In the United States, federal
policy requires institutions receiving federal life sciences funding to review certain categories of
experiments with specified agents, assess risks and benefits, and adopt plans to reduce risk. Those
policies have been revised and expanded, so the current version should be consulted. Journals may
also review whether publishing particular methods creates a security risk.

Two uses of the phrase should be kept apart. In export control law, dual-use describes goods and
technologies with both civilian and military applications. In research ethics the phrase is about
potential misuse of knowledge. Dual-use review is also separate from human subjects review, which
protects study participants.
