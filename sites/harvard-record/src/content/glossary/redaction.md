---
term: "Redaction"
short: "The removal or obscuring of portions of a document before it is released, usually to protect private, privileged or otherwise protected information."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["redacted", "blacked out", "withheld in part"]
related: ["document-release", "court-record", "public-record", "de-identification", "absence-of-evidence"]
---

Redaction is the editing of a document to hide specific content before release. The familiar form is
a black bar over a name or passage. Redactions are applied to protect, among other things, the
identities of victims and minors, personal identifiers such as account numbers, privileged legal
communications, confidential sources, and ongoing investigations. Court rules require filers to
redact certain personal identifiers. Public records laws permit agencies to withhold exempt portions
while releasing the remainder, typically noting the exemption relied upon.

Redaction is done by people or software under time pressure, and results are imperfect. A name
hidden on one page may appear on another. Material can be over-redacted or under-redacted. Technical
failures also occur, in which a visual bar is laid over text that remains in the file.

Responsible readers follow two rules. First, do not guess. The length or position of a black bar is
not a sound basis for inferring a name, and publishing such a guess can attach a serious implication
to the wrong person. Second, do not treat redaction as a sign of guilt or concealment. Most
redactions protect bystanders and victims. A redaction shows that something was withheld. It gives
no information about what that content says.
