Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code exempts from federal income tax an organization that
is organized and operated exclusively for charitable, educational, religious, scientific or similar
purposes. Private nonprofit colleges and universities hold this status. Donors to such organizations
can generally deduct their gifts, which is the feature that makes the status central to fundraising.

The exemption comes with conditions. None of the organization's net earnings may inure to the
benefit of private individuals with influence over it. It may not take part in political campaigns
for or against candidates, and lobbying may not be a substantial part of its activities. Most exempt
organizations must file an annual information return, Form 990, which is open to public inspection.
The Internal Revenue Service can impose penalty taxes on insiders who receive excessive benefits,
and in serious cases can revoke the exemption.

Tax-exempt does not mean free of all tax. Exempt organizations pay tax on income from unrelated
businesses, and some private universities with large endowments pay a federal excise tax on
investment income. Public universities are usually exempt on a different footing, as arms of state
government, though many also have affiliated 501(c)(3) foundations. Exempt status is also not a
government endorsement of an organization's conduct.
