---
term: "Estate (as a party)"
short: "The property and legal obligations left by a person who has died. An estate can sue or be sued through its personal representative."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["decedent's estate", "probate estate", "claim against the estate"]
related: ["executor", "plaintiff-and-defendant", "settlement", "statute-of-limitations"]
---

When a person dies, their assets and debts are gathered into an estate, which is administered under
the supervision of a probate court until debts and taxes are paid and what remains is distributed.
The estate is treated as a party for litigation purposes, although it acts only through a personal
representative, such as an executor. A case caption may read "Estate of" followed by the name, or
name the representative in that capacity.

Whether a legal claim survives a death depends on the law of the jurisdiction. Most claims for money
damages against a person can continue against that person's estate, and many claims the person held
pass to the estate. People with claims usually must present them within a short period set by
probate law, which can be much shorter than the ordinary statute of limitations. Estates sometimes
set up a claims program to resolve many similar claims outside court. Criminal prosecution, by
contrast, ends with the defendant's death.

A suit against an estate seeks recovery from the assets the person left, not from the heirs or the
representative personally. A payment by an estate is a settlement like any other and usually
includes no admission. Since the deceased person cannot answer, allegations first made after death
should be reported with particular care about their status.
