In fundraising, a prospect is anyone the institution thinks might give. The term covers individuals,
family foundations, corporations and other organizations.

Prospects are identified in several ways. Alumni and past donors are prospects by default. Others
are suggested by trustees, faculty, existing donors or staff, or found by researchers scanning news
and public filings. Once identified, a prospect gets a record in the donor database, may be rated
for wealth and interest, and may be assigned to a gift officer who is responsible for the
relationship. A prospect who is being actively worked toward a gift is sometimes called an active or
managed prospect. One who has been identified but not yet assessed is often called a suspect or
lead.

Being a prospect implies nothing about the person's wishes or conduct. A person can be listed as a
prospect for years without knowing it, without being contacted and without ever giving. The label
also does not show that the institution has vetted the person. Research into capacity often comes
first, and a closer look at reputation may happen only when a gift is in view. In records, the word
signals that someone at the institution saw fundraising potential, and little more.
