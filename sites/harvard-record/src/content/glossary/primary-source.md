---
term: "Primary source"
short: "An original document or first-hand record created by a participant or by the body responsible for the event, as distinct from later accounts of it."
category: "Records and sourcing"

A primary source is original material that comes directly from the event, person or institution
being studied. Examples include a court filing, a statute, an institutional report, meeting minutes,
a contract, an email, a laboratory record, a published dataset, or a statement made on the record by
someone with direct knowledge. The defining feature is closeness: nothing stands between the reader
and the source's own words.

Primary sources are valued because they let a reader check a claim without relying on someone else's
summary. On this site, the highest reliability grade is reserved for primary official records, and
each citation is meant to let the reader go to the original. The approach is described at
`/methodology/`.

Primary does not mean true. A primary source is the best evidence of what it says, and of the fact
that someone said it. A complaint filed in court is a primary record of an allegation, not proof of
the conduct alleged. A press release is a primary record of an institution's position, written to
serve its interests. An email shows what its author wrote, which may be mistaken, boastful or
incomplete. Whether a source is primary also depends on the question being asked. A news article is
secondary for the events it covers and primary for what that outlet reported.
