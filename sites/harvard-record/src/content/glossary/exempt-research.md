Exempt research is research with human subjects that fits one of the categories that U.S. federal
regulations excuse from most of their requirements. The categories cover activities considered low
risk. They include research on normal educational practices, many surveys, interviews and
observations of public behavior, benign behavioral interventions with adults, and certain secondary
uses of existing data or biospecimens, for instance where the information is publicly available or
is recorded so that individuals cannot readily be identified.

Each category has conditions, and some are unavailable for particular groups such as children or
prisoners. An exempt study does not go through full board review and may not require a signed
consent form. Federal guidance recommends, and most institutions require, that someone other than
the investigator decide whether a study qualifies. Researchers therefore usually submit an
application to the review office and receive a written determination.

Exempt does not mean exempt from ethics, from institutional policy or from other laws, such as
privacy rules. It also differs from "not human subjects research", a separate determination that the
activity falls outside the regulatory definition altogether. A researcher's own belief that a
project is exempt has no formal standing where the institution requires a determination.
