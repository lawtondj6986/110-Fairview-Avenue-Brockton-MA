---
term: "Separation agreement"
short: "A contract that sets the terms on which an employee leaves an organization, usually exchanging payment or other benefits for a release of legal claims."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["Severance agreement", "Release agreement", "Negotiated departure", "Exit agreement"]
related: ["nondisclosure-agreement", "nondisparagement-clause", "settlement", "termination-for-cause", "without-admission-of-liability"]
---

A separation agreement records how an employment relationship ends. In a typical agreement the
employer agrees to pay severance, continue benefits for a period, provide a neutral reference or
let the employee describe the departure as a resignation. In return the employee releases claims
against the employer, often agrees to cooperate with any later proceedings, and accepts
confidentiality or nondisparagement terms.

Agreements are common when a dispute is brewing, but they are also routine in layoffs and
reorganizations. Federal law sets specific requirements for a valid release of age discrimination
claims by workers aged 40 and over, including time to consider and revoke the agreement, and some
other claims, such as certain rights to report to government agencies, cannot be waived. State law
adds further rules that change from time to time.

A separation agreement is not an admission of wrongdoing by either side, and most contain language
saying so. It also does not show why the person left. When an agreement's existence is known but its
terms are not, a reader can say only that a negotiated departure occurred, not what was alleged or
paid.
