---
term: "Deliberate indifference"
short: "The legal standard under which a school is liable for harassment only if it knew of it and responded in a way that was clearly unreasonable in light of the known circumstances."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["title-ix", "hostile-environment", "burden-of-proof"]
---

Deliberate indifference is the test courts use to decide whether a school must pay money damages
under Title IX for harassment committed by an employee or another student. The U.S. Supreme Court
set the standard in two decisions in the late 1990s. A school is liable only if an official with
authority to take corrective action had actual knowledge of the harassment and the school's response
was clearly unreasonable in light of the known circumstances.

The standard is demanding for plaintiffs. A slow, clumsy or mistaken response usually does not meet
it. Courts look for something closer to a decision not to act, or a response so inadequate that it
amounts to one. The 2020 Title IX regulations also adopted the phrase as the baseline for
administrative enforcement, requiring schools to respond to known sexual harassment in a manner that
is not deliberately indifferent.

The term describes the institution's response, not the conduct of the person accused of harassment.
A court that finds no deliberate indifference has not found that the harassment did not happen. It
has found only that the school's response did not fall below this particular legal floor. Other
laws, and an institution's own policies, can require more.
