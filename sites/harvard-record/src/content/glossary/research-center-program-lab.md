Universities use the words lab, center, institute, program and initiative without fixed definitions.

A lab, or research group, is the team of one principal investigator: the professor plus students,
postdoctoral fellows and staff. It exists because the professor has an appointment and funding, and
it has no separate legal or administrative identity. A center or institute brings several faculty
members together around a theme. It has a director and typically a budget, staff, space and a
reporting line to a department chair, a dean or the provost. Many institutions require formal
approval to create a center and review it periodically, though the rigor of this varies. A program
or initiative may be a degree offering, a center under another name, or simply a label attached to
one professor's activities, sometimes created to match a donor's gift.

None of these units is a separate legal entity. Gifts "to" a center are gifts to the university,
restricted for that center's use. The name on the door also does not reveal how much oversight
exists. A unit with an impressive title may consist of one professor and a single funding source,
and it may answer in practice to no one beyond its director. Establishing who approved a unit, who
funded it and to whom it reported is basic to understanding it.
