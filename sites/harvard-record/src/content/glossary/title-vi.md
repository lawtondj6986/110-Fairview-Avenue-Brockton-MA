Title VI says that no person in the United States may be excluded from, denied the benefits of, or
subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving federal financial assistance on
the ground of race, color or national origin. It was the model for Title IX, which borrowed its
wording and its enforcement structure eight years later.

For colleges and universities, Title VI reaches admissions, financial aid, housing, discipline and
the handling of racial or national-origin harassment. Each federal agency that gives out funds
enforces it for its own recipients. For most schools that means the Department of Education's Office
for Civil Rights. The ultimate sanction is termination of federal funding after a formal process,
though nearly all cases end in a negotiated agreement.

The statute does not list religion or sex. Federal agencies have interpreted it to cover
discrimination based on shared ancestry or ethnic characteristics, which can include harassment of
students who belong to a religious group when it is based on ancestry and not on belief alone. Title
VI also covers employment only in limited circumstances. Workplace discrimination is mainly the
subject of Title VII.
