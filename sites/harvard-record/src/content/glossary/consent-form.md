---
term: "Consent form"
short: "The written document that sets out what a research study involves and records a participant's agreement to take part."

A consent form is the document given to a prospective research participant that describes the study
and, once signed, records the person's agreement. It normally covers who is conducting the study and
why, what the participant will be asked to do, how long it will take, the risks and possible
benefits, how data and samples will be stored and shared, whether there is payment or cost, whom to
contact with questions, and the right to withdraw. Studies that collect genetic material usually add
sections on future use, sharing with other researchers, commercial use and whether individual
results will be returned.

The form is reviewed and approved by an ethics board, and the approved version is the one that must
be used. Revised U.S. federal rules require the form to begin with a concise summary of the key
information a reasonable person would want. A copy is given to the participant, and the research
team keeps the signed original.

A consent form is not the whole of informed consent, which is a process of explanation and
discussion. It is also not a contract that waives legal rights. Federal rules prohibit language that
appears to waive a participant's rights or release the investigators from liability for negligence.
As a record, a form shows what was disclosed and agreed.
