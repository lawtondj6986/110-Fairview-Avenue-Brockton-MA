---
term: "Provenance"
short: "The documented origin and history of a document or object: who created it, who has held it, and how it reached the person now presenting it."
category: "Records and sourcing"
related: ["authenticity-vs-accuracy", "metadata", "sample-custody", "document-release", "mirror-site"]
---

Provenance is the record of where something came from and how it got here. For a document,
provenance covers who created it and when, in what system or file it was kept, who has had custody
since, and by what route, such as a court filing, an official release or a leak, it became
available.

Provenance supports judgments about authenticity and meaning. A document obtained from the official
website of the body that released it has clear provenance. The same text copied on an anonymous site
does not, even if the copy happens to be accurate, because there is no way to rule out alteration,
omission or fabrication without going back to the origin. Provenance also supplies context. Knowing
that an email came from one person's account, or was produced by a particular party in litigation,
shapes how it should be read and what may be missing around it.

Provenance is distinct from content. Clear provenance shows that a document is what it claims to be.
It does not show that what the document says is true. Unknown provenance, in turn, does not show
that a document is fake. It means that its authenticity cannot be established from the copy alone,
which is a reason to cite it with caution or not at all.
