---
term: "Tertiary source"
short: "A reference work that compiles and condenses primary and secondary sources, such as an encyclopedia, textbook, index or glossary."
category: "Records and sourcing"
related: ["primary-source", "secondary-source", "citation", "mirror-site"]
---

A tertiary source collects and summarizes material from primary and secondary sources without adding
original research. Encyclopedias, textbooks, almanacs, bibliographies, databases of abstracts,
timelines and glossaries fall into this category. So do collaboratively edited online encyclopedias
and wikis.

Tertiary sources are a starting point. They offer orientation: the basic facts, the vocabulary, the
key names and dates, and above all references that lead to stronger sources. A well-edited reference
work is usually dependable for settled general knowledge, such as what a statute provides or how a
laboratory technique works.

They are weak support for specific or contested factual claims. Each step of summarizing loses
detail and can introduce error, and the reader often cannot tell which underlying source a given
sentence rests on. Openly editable resources can change without notice and may reflect the views of
whoever edited them last. The sound practice is to follow a tertiary source's references to the
original and to cite that. This site does not rest factual claims about people on tertiary sources.
Its own glossary, law explainers and guides are tertiary material by this definition. They explain
terms and context, and they are not evidence about any person or event.
