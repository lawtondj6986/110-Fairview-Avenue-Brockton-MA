A gift agreement is the written record of what a donor and an institution have agreed. It is the
primary source for the terms of any significant gift.

A typical agreement states the amount, the form of payment and schedule, the purpose and any
restrictions, whether the gift is endowment or current-use, what recognition the donor will receive,
what reports the donor will get, and what happens if the stated purpose becomes impossible or
impractical. Many include a clause affirming that the institution keeps full control over academic
decisions. Agreements are drafted by the development office, often from a template, and reviewed by
the general counsel's office when the terms are unusual. Small gifts usually have no agreement and
are governed by the appeal they responded to or a short gift form.

Gift agreements are usually confidential. Private universities are generally not required to release
them, and public universities often hold them through a separate fundraising foundation, where state
open records laws apply unevenly. A press release about a gift is not the agreement and may omit
conditions. When a record refers to a gift's "terms," the agreement is the document that would
settle the question, and its absence from the public record is a limit worth noting.
