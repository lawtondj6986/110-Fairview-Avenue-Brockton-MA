A press office statement is the form in which an institution speaks officially to the news media and
the public. It is prepared by the office of communications or public affairs and attributed either
to the university itself or to a named spokesperson.

Statements on sensitive matters are drafted with care. Communications staff write them, and senior
officials and the general counsel's office commonly review the wording before release. They are
designed to be accurate as far as they go while limiting legal exposure and protecting confidential
information, including personnel and student matters that privacy rules may bar the institution from
discussing.

A statement is evidence of what the institution chose to say on a given date. It is a primary source
for the institution's position and a secondary source at best for the underlying facts. Close
reading matters. Qualifiers such as "to our knowledge," "based on our review" or "the university has
no record of" limit a claim to what a search found. Tense matters too: "does not accept" differs
from "did not accept." Statements may be revised as new information emerges, so the date and exact
wording of each version should be preserved, and a statement should be cited directly and not
through a paraphrase.
