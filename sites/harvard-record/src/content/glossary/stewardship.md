term: "Stewardship"
short: "What an institution does after a gift: using the money as agreed, reporting to the donor, giving thanks and recognition, and keeping the relationship going."

Stewardship has two meanings in fundraising, and both apply. The first is the duty to manage a gift
faithfully: to spend it for the agreed purpose, invest endowment prudently and account for it. The
second is the continuing care of the donor relationship.

In practice a stewardship or donor relations team sends acknowledgments and tax receipts, prepares
annual reports on endowed funds, arranges for donors to meet the students or professors their gifts
support, invites donors to events and oversees recognition such as plaques and donor lists. For
large donors, stewardship is personal. A senior official or a faculty member may stay in regular
contact, and the gift officer continues to record those contacts. Stewardship is also understood as
preparation for the next gift, since past donors are the most likely future donors.

Stewardship is not a period in which the donor acquires authority over the gift. Reports inform.
They do not seek approval. Still, the wish to keep a donor satisfied is real, and it can shape how
readily staff and faculty accommodate a donor's requests for visits, introductions or involvement.
When reading records, continued contact after a gift is normal and expected. What matters is what
the contact consisted of.
