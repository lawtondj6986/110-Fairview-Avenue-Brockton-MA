An appeal in campus discipline asks a reviewer to change the result of a case. Unlike a new trial,
an appeal is usually confined to stated grounds. Under the 2020 Title IX regulations, institutions
must offer both parties an appeal from a determination or a dismissal on at least three grounds: a
procedural irregularity that affected the outcome, new evidence that was not reasonably available
before and could affect the outcome, and a conflict of interest or bias on the part of the Title IX
coordinator, investigator or decision-maker. Institutions may add others, such as a sanction that is
disproportionate.

The appeal is normally decided by someone not involved earlier, sometimes a dean, a provost or a
panel. Possible results include upholding the decision, changing the sanction, or sending the case
back for further proceedings. Faculty discipline often has its own appeal route, set in a faculty
handbook, which may end with the president or the governing board.

An appeal is not a lawsuit, and a decision on appeal is still an internal decision. A party who loses
may in some cases go to court or to a federal agency, but courts generally give institutions wide
latitude in internal discipline.
