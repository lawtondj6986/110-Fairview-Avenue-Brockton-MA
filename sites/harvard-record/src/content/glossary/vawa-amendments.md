The Violence Against Women Reauthorization Act of 2013 amended the Clery Act. The campus provisions
are often called the Campus SaVE Act. The Department of Education's implementing regulations took
effect in 2015.

The amendments did three main things. They added dating violence, domestic violence and stalking to
the offenses an institution must count and report, alongside sexual assault. They required the
annual security report to describe the institution's disciplinary procedures for these offenses,
including the standard of evidence used, the right of both the accuser and the accused to have an
adviser of their choice present, and simultaneous written notice of the outcome to both. They also
required prevention and awareness programs for incoming students and new employees, with ongoing
campaigns, covering topics such as the definition of consent in the local jurisdiction and safe
options for bystander intervention. Institutions must give anyone who reports one of these offenses
a written explanation of their rights and options.

These requirements sit beside Title IX and are separate from it. They come from a different statute,
are enforced by a different office of the Department of Education, and apply to these four offenses
wherever Title IX procedures may or may not reach. An institution must satisfy both sets of rules at
once.
