term: "Dating violence"

Dating violence became a reportable Clery Act category through the 2013 amendments. The regulations
define it as violence committed by a person who is or has been in a social relationship of a
romantic or intimate nature with the victim. It includes sexual or physical abuse and the threat of
such abuse.

Whether such a relationship existed is determined from the reporting party's statement, taking into
account the length of the relationship, its type, and how often the people involved interacted. The
institution counts a reported incident in its annual statistics when it occurred within Clery
geography. An institution's policy must also address dating violence in its prevention programs, in
the information given to people who report, and in its disciplinary procedures.

The Clery definition excludes acts that meet the definition of domestic violence, so an incident is
counted in one category or the other, not both. The dividing line depends partly on state law, since
domestic violence is tied to the relationships a jurisdiction's own statutes protect. Dating
violence under Clery is also not limited to conduct that state criminal law labels with that name.
Many states have no crime called dating violence, and institutions must still count incidents that
fit the federal definition.
