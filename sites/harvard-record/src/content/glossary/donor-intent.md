---
term: "Donor intent"
short: "The purpose a donor meant a gift to serve, as expressed in the gift's terms, which the institution is legally and ethically bound to respect."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Donor's intent", "Donor wishes", "Charitable intent"]
related: ["restricted-gift", "gift-agreement", "endowment", "stewardship", "gift-return"]
---

Donor intent is the principle that a gift must be used for the purpose the donor specified. It is
both a legal obligation and a professional norm in fundraising.

Intent is established by the written terms of the gift: a gift agreement, a will, a letter or the
wording of the appeal the donor answered. The institution must spend restricted funds within those
terms. If a purpose later becomes impossible, impractical or wasteful, the institution can ask the
donor to agree to a change. If the donor has died or cannot be found, state law provides ways to
modify a restriction, through a court or, for some funds, with notice to the state attorney general.
The traditional legal doctrine is called cy pres, under which a court redirects a charitable gift to
a purpose as near as possible to the original. In most states the attorney general, not the donor's
family, is the official with standing to enforce charitable restrictions, though rules on donor
standing differ.

Donor intent is a limit on the institution's use of money. It is not a source of continuing donor
control. Honoring intent means using a fund for its stated purpose, not following a donor's later
preferences on matters the gift terms do not cover.
