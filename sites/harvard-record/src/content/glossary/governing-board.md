The governing board is the legal owner of a university's authority. A private university is usually
a nonprofit corporation, and the board is the body that the charter empowers to act for it. Public
universities are governed by boards created under state law, whose members are often appointed by
the governor or elected.

The board's core functions are to appoint, evaluate and if necessary remove the president, approve
the budget and major financial commitments, oversee the endowment, approve the granting of degrees
and senior appointments, and safeguard the institution's mission. Members of a private university
board are fiduciaries, meaning they owe legal duties of care and loyalty to the institution. They
are typically unpaid volunteers, many of them alumni and major donors. Work is done through
committees such as finance, investment, audit and governance. Board size ranges from under ten to
several dozen members.

Boards govern and do not manage. They meet a few times a year, rely on information that the
administration provides, and by custom leave academic matters to the faculty. A board is therefore
unlikely to know of events inside a department unless management reports them. Boards do have the
power to order an independent investigation, and when the conduct of senior leadership is in
question, the board is the only body with authority over it.
