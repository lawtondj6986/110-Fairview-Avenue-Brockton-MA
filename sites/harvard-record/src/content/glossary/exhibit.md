---
term: "Exhibit"
short: "A document or object attached to a court filing or introduced at a hearing or trial as evidence."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["exhibits", "attachment", "Exhibit A"]
related: ["affidavit", "deposition", "docket", "sealed-record", "document-production"]
---

An exhibit is an item of evidence presented to a court. In written filings, exhibits are the
attachments: emails, contracts, photographs, financial records, transcript excerpts and similar
material, each labeled with a letter or number. A lawyer's or witness's declaration usually
introduces them and states what each one is. At trial, exhibits are offered through witnesses and,
if the judge admits them, become part of the evidence the jury may consider.

Exhibits are valuable to researchers because they are primary documents. A filing may describe an
email in argumentative terms, while the exhibit shows the actual words, sender, recipients and date.
Exhibits attached to public filings can be obtained from the docket unless they are sealed. Some are
filed with redactions or as placeholders noting that the item was filed under seal.

Attachment to a filing does not make a document authentic, complete or admitted into evidence. The
other side may dispute what it is or what it means, and the court may never rule on it. Exhibits are
also selected by the party who files them, often as excerpts, so surrounding context may be missing.
A document should be cited as an exhibit to a specific filing, with its docket number, and described
for what it shows on its face.
