---
term: "Letter of findings"
short: "The letter a civil rights agency sends at the end of an investigation, setting out the facts it found and its conclusions on whether the law was violated."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["findings letter", "resolution letter"]
related: ["office-for-civil-rights", "resolution-agreement", "finding-vs-allegation"]
---

A letter of findings is the document in which an enforcement agency, such as the Office for Civil
Rights, reports the outcome of an investigation to the institution and to the person who complained.
It normally describes the allegations, the legal standard, the evidence reviewed, and the agency's
conclusion on each issue.

The letter may conclude that the evidence was insufficient to show a violation, or that the
institution failed to comply with the law in specific ways. When an investigation is settled early,
the agency may instead issue a shorter resolution letter that summarizes its concerns without formal
findings. These letters are often released publicly, usually with names and identifying details
redacted.

A letter of findings is an agency's administrative determination. It is not a court judgment, and it
does not decide whether any individual committed misconduct. Its subject is the institution's
response: whether policies, procedures and actions met legal requirements. A letter also covers only
the period and issues the agency examined, so it should not be read as a general verdict on an
institution.
