The Higher Education Act of 1965 is the basic federal statute on postsecondary education. Its
best-known part, Title IV, authorizes federal grants, loans and work-study for students. Congress
revises the act through periodic reauthorizations and through other legislation, so its text has
changed considerably over time.

The act matters for public accountability because of how it is enforced. To receive Title IV funds,
an institution signs a program participation agreement with the Department of Education and promises
to follow a long list of requirements. Several disclosure laws discussed on this site are sections
of the act and attach to institutions in this way. The Clery Act, which requires campus crime
reporting, is one. Section 117, on foreign gifts and contracts, is another. The Department can fine
an institution, or limit or end its participation in aid programs, for violations.

Title IV of the Higher Education Act should not be confused with the similarly numbered titles of
other laws. Title IX belongs to the Education Amendments of 1972. Title VI and Title VII belong to
the Civil Rights Act of 1964. They are separate statutes with separate enforcement offices, although
the same federal funding is the reason each applies to a college.
