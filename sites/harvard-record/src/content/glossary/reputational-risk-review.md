A reputational risk review asks a question beyond legality: even if a gift is lawful, should the
institution take it? It considers how the association would look to students, faculty, alumni, the
public and other donors, and whether it fits the institution's stated mission.

The review usually starts from due diligence findings. Factors commonly weighed include the nature
and seriousness of any misconduct linked to the donor, whether it was proven or alleged, how recent
it was, whether the gift money is connected to it, how visible the gift would be (a named building
carries more exposure than an unnamed fund), and whether the donor seems to want the association in
order to repair their own image. The decision rests with senior officials or a gift acceptance
committee. Outcomes include acceptance, acceptance without public recognition, deferral or refusal.

Two cautions apply. First, reputational risk is a judgment about the institution's interests, not a
finding about the donor. Second, accepting a gift quietly to limit exposure is itself a recognized
response to reputational concern, and it can later be read as evidence that officials saw a problem.
Policies in this area are often brief and leave wide discretion, so practice has varied between
institutions and, within one institution, between schools and over time.
