Audited financial statements usually include a statement of financial position, a statement of
activities showing revenue and expenses, a statement of cash flows and detailed notes. An independent
auditor examines the records and issues an opinion. An unmodified opinion, sometimes called a clean
opinion, says the statements are fairly presented in all material respects. Qualified, adverse and
disclaimed opinions signal problems of increasing seriousness.

Many universities post their audited statements online each year, after the fiscal year closes. The
notes are often the most useful part for a reader. They break down endowment funds, pledges
receivable, debt, related-party transactions and the split between restricted and unrestricted net
assets. Institutions that spend federal awards above a threshold also undergo a separate Single
Audit that tests compliance with federal grant rules, and its report is filed publicly.

An audit is not an investigation of every transaction. Auditors test samples and focus on amounts
large enough to matter to the overall picture, which for a large university can be big sums. A clean
opinion does not mean that no individual gift or expense was improper. Audited statements are also
distinct from the Form 990, which follows tax rules and can present the same year differently.
