---
term: "Single-source reporting"
short: "A report whose key claim rests on one source alone, without independent confirmation from a second source or a document."

Single-source reporting is reporting in which a central claim depends on one person or one document.
Many newsrooms have a working rule that a contested factual claim, particularly one from an unnamed
source, should be confirmed by at least two independent sources before publication. A single-source
claim is one that has not met that kind of test.

One source is not always inadequate. A single authoritative document, such as a court order or an
institution's published report, is sufficient for what it states. A named person describing their
own actions on the record is a strong single source for those actions. The concern is greatest when
the single source is unnamed, is second-hand, has an interest in the story, or is describing other
people's conduct or state of mind.

Two errors are worth avoiding. The first is mistaking repetition for confirmation. A claim that
appears in many places may still trace to one original account. The second is treating
single-sourced as a synonym for false. It is a statement about the support for a claim, not about
the claim's truth. On this site, an account that could not be checked against a second source or a
primary document receives the lowest reliability grade and is flagged as such wherever it is used.
