---
term: "Complaint (civil)"
short: "The document a plaintiff files to start a civil lawsuit, setting out the facts alleged, the legal claims and the relief sought."
category: "Legal procedure"

A complaint is the first filing in a civil case. It names the parties, explains why the court has
authority to hear the dispute, sets out in numbered paragraphs the facts the plaintiff says
occurred, lists the legal claims (often called counts or causes of action), and states what the
plaintiff wants, such as money damages or a court order. Some state courts call the same document a
petition.

Filing the complaint and serving it on the defendant starts the case. The defendant must then
respond, usually with an answer or a motion to dismiss. Complaints are normally public records,
available from the court's docket, and are often the most detailed public account of a dispute at
its early stage.

A complaint is one side's version of events, written by that side's lawyers to support its claims.
Nothing in it has been tested or proven. A court does not review a complaint for truth before it is
filed. Statements quoted from a complaint should therefore be described as allegations, and readers
should look for what happened next: whether the claims were dismissed, settled, or decided.

The word has other meanings. A criminal complaint is a charging document. A formal complaint under
Title IX is a request to a school, not a court filing.
