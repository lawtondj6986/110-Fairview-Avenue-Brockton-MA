---
term: "Cultivation"
short: "The stage of fundraising in which an institution builds a prospect's interest and involvement before asking for a gift."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Donor cultivation", "Engagement", "Relationship building"]
related: ["solicitation", "stewardship", "prospect-management", "affinity-rating", "influence-versus-access"]
---

Cultivation is the period of relationship building between identifying a prospect and asking for
money. Its goal is to increase the prospect's knowledge of, and attachment to, the institution so
that a later request is welcome.

Typical cultivation steps include meetings with a gift officer, invitations to lectures and dinners,
tours of laboratories, introductions to faculty whose work matches the prospect's interests,
briefings from deans or the president, and seats on advisory boards or visiting committees. Each
step is planned and recorded as part of the prospect's strategy. For the largest prospects,
cultivation can last years and involve many people across the institution.

Cultivation is the stage at which a prospect receives access before giving anything. A wealthy
person may meet senior scientists, attend private events and form personal ties with faculty purely
on the expectation of a future gift. This is ordinary practice and not improper in itself. It does
mean that the absence of a recorded gift does not show the absence of a relationship, and that
benefits of association (introductions, invitations, the appearance of connection to a respected
institution) can flow to a prospect who never gives. Cultivation should not be confused with
stewardship, which is the care of a donor after a gift.
