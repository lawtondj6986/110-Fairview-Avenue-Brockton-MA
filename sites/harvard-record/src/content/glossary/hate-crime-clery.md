Under the Clery Act, colleges must report hate crimes in their annual security report alongside
other crime statistics. A hate crime is a crime reported to local police or a campus security
authority that shows evidence the victim was chosen because of the perpetrator's bias. The
regulations list the categories of bias: race, gender, gender identity, religion, sexual
orientation, ethnicity, national origin and disability.

The count includes any of the main Clery crimes, such as assault or burglary, when bias is present,
and also four offenses counted only when they are hate crimes: larceny-theft, simple assault,
intimidation, and destruction, damage or vandalism of property. Each hate crime is reported by
category of bias, crime type, year and location under Clery geography.

The Clery definition is not identical to state hate crime statutes, so a campus statistic and a
criminal charge may not match. A bias incident that is not a crime, such as offensive speech without
a threat, is not counted, although many campuses track such incidents separately. Small numbers are
common and change sharply from year to year, so a single year's figure says little about a trend.
