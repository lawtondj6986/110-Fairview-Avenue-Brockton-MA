A contemporaneous written acknowledgment is the document a donor needs to claim a federal income tax
deduction for a single contribution at or above a dollar threshold set in the tax code. It must come
from the charity and must be obtained by the donor before filing the return for the year of the
gift, which is what "contemporaneous" means here.

The acknowledgment states the amount of cash given, or describes property given without valuing it,
and says whether the charity provided goods or services in return. If it did, the acknowledgment
must give a good-faith estimate of their value, because only the amount above that value is
deductible. Benefits that the rules treat as insubstantial, and purely religious benefits, are
handled differently.

In a record, a gift receipt confirms that money or property was received and how the charity
characterized the exchange. It does not show the full terms of a gift, which are in the gift
agreement, and it is not the same as a donor recognition letter or a stewardship report. The rules
are in the tax code and IRS guidance; check current versions before relying on a detail.
