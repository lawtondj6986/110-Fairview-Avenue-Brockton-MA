A CLIA laboratory is one that holds a certificate under the Clinical Laboratory Improvement
Amendments of 1988, the federal law that sets quality standards for laboratory testing on human
specimens in the United States. A laboratory needs the appropriate certificate if it tests samples
from people in order to provide information for the diagnosis, prevention or treatment of disease or
for the assessment of health. The program is administered by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid
Services, with roles for other federal health agencies. Some states add their own licensing
requirements.

Certification is tied to the complexity of the tests performed. Genetic sequencing is treated as
high complexity, which brings requirements for personnel qualifications, quality control,
proficiency testing and periodic inspection.

CLIA regulates how well a laboratory performs a test: accuracy, reliability and record keeping. It
does not judge whether a test is medically useful, and certification is not approval of a particular
test by the Food and Drug Administration. Laboratories that test human samples purely for research,
and do not report results for individual patients, are generally outside the requirement. This is
one reason research findings are usually confirmed in a CLIA-certified laboratory before being given
to a participant.
