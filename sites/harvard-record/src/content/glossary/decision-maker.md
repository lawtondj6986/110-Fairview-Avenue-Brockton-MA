---
term: "Decision-maker"
short: "The person or panel in a campus disciplinary process who decides whether a policy was violated and, often, what sanction follows, as distinct from the investigator who gathers evidence."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["Hearing officer", "Adjudicator", "Hearing panel", "Single-investigator model"]

---

In campus sexual misconduct and other disciplinary proceedings, the decision-maker weighs the
evidence and decides whether the respondent is responsible under the institution's policy, using
the stated standard of evidence. The role may be filled by a trained staff member, an outside
professional or a panel of faculty, staff and sometimes students.

The 2020 Title IX regulations required that the decision-maker be a different person from the Title
IX coordinator and the investigator, ending what was called the single-investigator model for those
cases. The 2024 regulations would have allowed one person to investigate and decide again, but they
were vacated in early 2025. Institutions also use separate decision-makers on appeal. Rules change,
so readers should check current regulations and the institution's own policy.

A decision-maker's written determination is usually the document that states findings, reasoning
and any sanction. It is distinct from the investigation report, which gathers and summarizes evidence
but, in many processes, does not decide. A finding of responsibility reflects the decision-maker's
assessment under a campus standard, not a legal judgment by a court.
