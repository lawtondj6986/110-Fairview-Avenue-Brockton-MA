term: "Search hit versus document versus message"
short: "Three different units for counting records: a hit is one match to a search, a document is one file, and a message is one email or text within a thread."

When a large set of records is searched, the results can be counted in several ways, and the numbers
are not interchangeable. A search hit is one match to a query. Depending on the tool, that may mean
one occurrence of the word, one page containing it, or one file containing it. A document is a
single file or record as it is stored, which may be one page or hundreds. A message is one email,
text or letter. A single document can hold a chain of many messages, and one message can be
reproduced in many documents.

The differences produce large discrepancies. A name that returns two thousand hits may appear in a
few hundred documents, which reduce to a few dozen distinct messages once duplicates, forwards and
quoted replies are removed. Many of those may be news articles, mailing lists or calendar notices
and not correspondence with the person. Common names and short search terms also produce false
matches.

A count should therefore state its unit and method. Hit counts are a starting point for reading.
They are not a measure of the closeness of a relationship or of anyone's conduct. Frequency of
mention reflects many things, including who kept the records and how long a thread ran. What matters
is what the distinct messages actually say.
