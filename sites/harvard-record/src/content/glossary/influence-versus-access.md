---
term: "Influence versus access"
short: "A distinction between a donor's ability to meet people and be present at an institution (access) and an ability to affect its decisions (influence)."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Donor influence", "Donor access", "Access and influence"]
related: ["cultivation", "quid-pro-quo-fundraising", "donor-conflict-of-interest", "advisory-board", "campus-access-policy", "academic-freedom"]
---

Access and influence are two different things a donor may gain from a relationship with a
university, and evidence of one is not evidence of the other.

Access is contact and presence: meetings with the president or leading faculty, invitations to
private events, visits to laboratories, a seat on an advisory board, a title, a campus office or a
building pass. Fundraising practice offers access deliberately, as part of cultivation and
stewardship, and institutions do not regard it as improper. Influence is an effect on decisions:
what is researched, who is hired, admitted or honored, how a complaint is handled, what the
institution says publicly. Policies on academic freedom and gift acceptance are designed to prevent
donors from having this kind of effect.

The distinction is useful, but it has limits. Access has value in itself. Being seen with respected
scholars and being able to cite a university connection can confer credibility, regardless of
whether any decision was affected. Access also creates the conditions for informal influence,
because people tend to accommodate those they know and depend on. A careful account therefore states
what the record shows at each level: that contact occurred, that a privilege was granted, or that a
specific decision was changed. See `/donor-pathways/` and `/methodology/` for how this site applies
the distinction.
