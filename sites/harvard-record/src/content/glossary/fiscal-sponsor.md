---
term: "Fiscal sponsor"
short: "A tax-exempt organization that accepts and manages charitable money on behalf of a project or group that does not have tax-exempt status of its own."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Fiscal sponsorship", "Fiscal agent", "Sponsored project"]
related: ["tax-exempt-status", "intermediary", "donor-advised-fund", "form-990", "gift"]
---

A fiscal sponsor is a charity that lends its tax-exempt status to a project. Donors give to the
sponsor, which can issue a tax receipt, and the sponsor then funds the project's work. The project
might be a new initiative, a research collaboration, a community group or an effort that has not yet
formed its own organization.

Arrangements vary. In some, the project is legally part of the sponsor and its staff may be the
sponsor's employees. In others, the sponsor makes grants to a separate group it has approved. In
either case the sponsor is expected to keep control over how charitable money is used, so that it
goes to charitable purposes. Sponsors usually charge a fee, and their own public filings report the
money as theirs.

For a reader, the main consequence is that the name on a filing or a receipt may not be the name of
the project that spent the money. A gift that appears in one organization's records may have been
directed to a project it hosts. A fiscal sponsor is not the same as a donor-advised fund sponsor,
which holds accounts that individual donors advise on.
