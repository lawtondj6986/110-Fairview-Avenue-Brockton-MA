---
term: "Allegation"
short: "A statement of fact that a party asserts to be true in a legal or administrative proceeding but that has not been proven."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["alleged", "allege"]
related: ["finding-vs-allegation", "complaint-civil", "answer", "burden-of-proof"]
---

An allegation is a claim that something happened, made by a party who undertakes to prove it.
Complaints, indictments, agency charges and campus disciplinary notices are all made up of
allegations. The word signals status, not doubt. It tells the reader that no court or other
decision-maker has yet determined whether the statement is true.

Allegations vary in weight. Some are made on personal knowledge, some on information and belief,
which is the pleading phrase for a statement the party believes but has not verified firsthand. Some
are supported by attached documents, and others stand alone. Lawyers who sign court filings certify
that the factual claims have, or are likely to have, evidentiary support, but that is a low
threshold and not a judicial test. In most jurisdictions, statements made in court filings are also
protected from defamation claims, which allows parties to plead freely.

An allegation remains an allegation until it is admitted by the other side, or established by a
verdict, judgment or formal finding. If a case settles or is dismissed on procedural grounds, the
allegations are never resolved either way. Careful writing attributes each allegation to its source
and document, avoids presenting it as fact, and records the response of the person or institution it
concerns.
