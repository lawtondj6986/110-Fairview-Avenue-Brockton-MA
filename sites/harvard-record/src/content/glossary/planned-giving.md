Planned giving covers gifts that are structured through a donor's financial or estate plans. The
simplest and most common is the bequest, a gift left to an institution in a will or living trust.

Other planned gifts include naming the institution as beneficiary of a retirement account or life
insurance policy, and life income arrangements such as charitable gift annuities and charitable
remainder trusts, in which the donor or another person receives payments for life or a term and the
institution receives what remains. Development offices employ planned giving specialists, often with
legal or financial training, who work with donors and their advisers. Donors who disclose a bequest
intention are commonly enrolled in a legacy society.

A bequest intention is usually revocable. A person can change a will at any time, so institutions
treat stated intentions differently from signed pledges and count them, if at all, under separate
rules. The amount finally received depends on the estate's value at death and may be very different
from the estimate. Bequests also raise a distinct donor intent issue: the terms are fixed in a
document written years earlier, and the donor is no longer available to clarify or agree to changes.
An institution can decline a bequest, but it has to decide without the possibility of discussion
with the giver.
