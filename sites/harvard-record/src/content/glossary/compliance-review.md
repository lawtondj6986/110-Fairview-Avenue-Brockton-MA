---
term: "Compliance review"
short: "An investigation that a federal agency opens on its own initiative, without an individual complaint, to check whether an institution is following the law."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["program review", "directed investigation"]
related: ["office-for-civil-rights", "resolution-agreement", "letter-of-findings", "clery-act"]
---

A compliance review is an agency-initiated examination of an institution. The Office for Civil
Rights uses the term for investigations it starts itself under Title VI, Title IX and related laws,
as opposed to investigations opened in response to a complaint. The agency chooses targets using
sources such as statistical data, news reports and information from earlier cases.

A review tends to be broader than a complaint investigation. It may look at an institution's
policies and its handling of many cases over several years, through document requests, data analysis
and interviews. The possible endings are the same: a finding of no violation, or a resolution
agreement that commits the institution to corrective steps. Reviews often take years.

The Department of Education also enforces the Clery Act through a separate office that oversees
federal student aid. Its examinations are usually called program reviews and can end in fines. Other
agencies, including research funders, conduct their own reviews under their own names.

The opening of a compliance review is not a finding. It shows that an agency decided to look.
Equally, the lack of any review says little, since agencies examine only a small fraction of
institutions in any period.
