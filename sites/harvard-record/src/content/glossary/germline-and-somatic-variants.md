Genetic variants are differences in DNA sequence. Where they come from matters. A germline variant
was present in the egg or sperm that formed a person, so it is found in essentially every cell of
the body and can be passed to the next generation. Testing for inherited conditions, ancestry
analysis and most consumer genetic tests look at germline DNA, usually from blood or saliva.

A somatic variant arises after conception, when a cell makes a copying error or is damaged, for
example by ultraviolet light. Only that cell and its descendants carry it. Somatic variants
accumulate with age and are central to cancer, where tumor sequencing is often compared with a
sample of normal tissue to separate the tumor's acquired changes from the person's inherited ones.

The distinction affects how a result should be read. A somatic variant found in a tumor says little
about relatives' risk; a germline variant may. Laboratory reports usually state which kind of
sample was tested. This entry concerns naturally occurring variants. Deliberate changes made by gene
editing are discussed under somatic versus germline editing, which uses the same cell categories.
