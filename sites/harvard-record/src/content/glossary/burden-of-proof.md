---
term: "Burden of proof"
short: "The obligation of a party to prove its claims, together with the level of certainty the evidence must reach."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["standard of proof", "beyond a reasonable doubt", "burden of persuasion"]
related: ["preponderance-of-the-evidence", "clear-and-convincing-evidence", "civil-vs-criminal", "finding-vs-allegation"]
---

Burden of proof answers two questions: who must prove a disputed point, and how convincingly. The
party with the burden loses if the evidence falls short. In a criminal case the burden is on the
prosecution. In a civil case it is on the plaintiff for the claims, and on the defendant for
affirmative defenses.

The second question is the standard of proof. Three standards are in general use. Preponderance of
the evidence means more likely than not and applies in most civil cases. Clear and convincing
evidence means highly probable and applies to certain civil matters. Beyond a reasonable doubt, the
criminal standard, is the highest. It requires proof strong enough that no reasonable doubt about
guilt remains.

In campus disciplinary proceedings, federal rules place the burden of gathering evidence and proving
responsibility on the institution, not on either party, and the respondent is presumed not
responsible until a determination is made.

The outcome of a proceeding has to be read with its burden in mind. A not guilty verdict means the
highest standard was not met. It does not mean that a lower one could not have been. A failure to
meet the burden is also different from proof of the opposite.
