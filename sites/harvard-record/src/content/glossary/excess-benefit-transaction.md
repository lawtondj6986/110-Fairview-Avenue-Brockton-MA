An excess benefit transaction occurs when a public charity, or certain other exempt organizations,
provides an economic benefit to a disqualified person that is worth more than what the organization
gets back. A disqualified person is someone in a position to exercise substantial influence over the
organization, such as a trustee, a senior officer or a major donor with that kind of influence, along
with their family members and controlled businesses. Overpaying an executive or selling property to
an insider below its value are typical examples.

Section 4958 of the tax code imposes an excise tax on the disqualified person, who must also correct
the transaction, and a smaller tax on managers who knowingly approved it. These penalties are called
intermediate sanctions because they fall short of revoking the organization's exemption. A
transaction can benefit from a presumption that it was reasonable if an independent body approved it
in advance, relied on comparable data and documented its decision.

An excess benefit is not the same as any benefit to an insider. Fair-value transactions with insiders
are lawful. Private foundations are governed by the separate and stricter self-dealing rules.
