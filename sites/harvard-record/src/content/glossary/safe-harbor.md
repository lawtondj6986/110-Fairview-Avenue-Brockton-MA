---
term: "Safe harbor"
short: "A provision stating that a person who follows specified steps will be treated as complying with a law, or will be protected from liability under it."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["preemption", "hipaa", "compliance-review", "de-identification"]
---

A safe harbor is a clearly marked zone of compliance inside a rule that is otherwise general or
uncertain. The law sets out particular conduct, and anyone who stays within it is deemed to have met
the requirement or is shielded from a penalty. Legislatures and agencies use safe harbors to give
predictability where a broad standard, such as acting reasonably, would leave people guessing.

A familiar example is in the HIPAA Privacy Rule. Health information is treated as de-identified, and
so outside the rule, if a listed set of identifiers such as names, detailed geography and most dates
has been removed and the holder has no actual knowledge that what remains could identify a person.
This is called the safe harbor method. The alternative is an expert's statistical determination.
Tax, securities and copyright law contain many other safe harbors.

Two cautions apply. A safe harbor is usually one way to comply, not the only way. Conduct outside it
is not automatically unlawful. It simply loses the automatic protection and is judged under the
general standard. Also, a safe harbor protects only against the specific liability it names. Data
that meet the HIPAA de-identification safe harbor, for instance, may still be covered by research
rules, contracts or state law.
