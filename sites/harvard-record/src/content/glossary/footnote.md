---
term: "Footnote"
short: "A numbered note, placed at the foot of a page or the end of a text, that gives a source or adds a brief comment without interrupting the main text."
category: "Records and sourcing"

A footnote is a note linked to a point in the text by a number or symbol. Notes printed at the
bottom of the page are footnotes. Notes gathered at the end of a chapter or document are endnotes.
On web pages the two merge, and a note marker usually links to an entry in a list of sources.

Footnotes have two uses. A reference note gives the citation for the sentence it follows. A
substantive note adds something that would clutter the main text: a qualification, a definition, a
conflicting figure from another source, or an explanation of how a number was calculated. In
official reports, footnotes often carry important limits on a finding, such as who declined to be
interviewed or which records were unavailable. In legal and scholarly writing, they may contain a
concession or the opposing view.

For that reason footnotes should be read and not skipped. The qualification that changes the meaning
of a sentence is frequently in the note. Two cautions apply. A footnote number shows that a source
is offered. It does not show that the source supports the sentence, which can be learned only by
checking. And a note placed at the end of a paragraph may cover only the last sentence, leaving
earlier sentences without stated support.
