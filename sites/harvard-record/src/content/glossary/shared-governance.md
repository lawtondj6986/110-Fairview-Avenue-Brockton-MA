Shared governance is the principle that authority in a university is divided among its constituent
parts. Its best-known statement is a 1966 document on the government of colleges and universities,
formulated jointly by the American Association of University Professors and two national
associations representing institutions and governing boards.

Under the usual division, the governing board holds final legal authority and oversees finances and
the presidency. The president and administration manage operations. The faculty has primary
responsibility for curriculum, methods of instruction, research, standards for degrees, and the
appointment, promotion and tenure of faculty. On those academic matters, the board and president are
expected to defer to faculty judgment except in rare cases and for stated reasons. Faculty take part
through senates, councils and committees.

Shared governance is a custom and a set of institutional rules, not generally a legal requirement,
and legal authority remains with the board. Its scope is also narrower than the name suggests.
Fundraising, gift acceptance, investment, legal affairs and campus security have traditionally been
administrative functions with little or no faculty role. Faculty may therefore have no say in which
donors an institution courts, even when a gift affects an academic program. Some institutions have
added faculty members to gift review committees in response to that gap.
