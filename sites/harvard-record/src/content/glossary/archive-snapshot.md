term: "Archive snapshot"
short: "A saved copy of a web page as it appeared at a particular date and time, made and stored by a web archiving service."

An archive snapshot is a capture of a web page at a specific moment, stored by a web archive.
Archiving services run automated crawlers that visit pages and save copies, and many also let a user
request a capture of a given address. Each snapshot is stored with the original address and the date
and time of capture, so several snapshots of one page show how it changed.

Snapshots serve two purposes in sourcing. They keep a citation usable after the original page moves
or disappears. And they provide evidence of what a page said at an earlier time, which matters when
content has been edited or removed. Careful citations give both the original address and the
archived one, with the capture date.

The limits are practical. A snapshot shows what the crawler received at that moment. Pages that load
content dynamically, sit behind a login or paywall, or block crawlers may be captured partly or not
at all. The capture date is not the publication date. A page absent from an archive may never have
been crawled, so the absence does not show that it never existed. Site owners can ask for exclusion.
A snapshot is good evidence that a page displayed certain content on the capture date. It is no
evidence that the content was true.
