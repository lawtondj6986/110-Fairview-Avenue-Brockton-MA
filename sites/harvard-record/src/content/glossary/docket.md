A docket is the index to a court case. It lists, in order and with a date and number, each document
filed by the parties and each order or notice issued by the court. The header identifies the court,
the case number, the judge, the parties and their lawyers.

Federal dockets are available through the judiciary's electronic system, known as PACER, which
charges modest fees, and through free archives that republish what users have obtained. State courts
run their own systems, which range from fully searchable online records to paper files at a clerk's
office. A citation to a specific docket entry number, together with the case number and court, lets
a reader locate the exact document.

The docket is the best guide to the status of a case. It shows whether claims were dismissed,
whether a judgment was entered, or whether the case simply stopped after a notice of settlement. It
also shows gaps, such as sealed entries.

A docket entry is a label, usually written by the filing party or the clerk, and is not a ruling.
The title of a motion describes what a party asked for, not what the judge decided. To learn the
outcome, find the order that resolved it.
