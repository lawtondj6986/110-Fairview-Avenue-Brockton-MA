Clery geography is the map that decides whether an incident is counted in an institution's Clery
statistics. It has three categories. On-campus means buildings and property the institution owns or
controls within the same reasonably contiguous area and uses for its educational purposes, including
residence halls, which are also reported as a separate subset. Noncampus means property owned or
controlled by an officially recognized student organization, or property the institution owns or
controls and uses for its educational purposes that is not within the main campus area. Public
property means streets, sidewalks, parking facilities and similar public land within the campus or
immediately adjacent to and accessible from it.

Institutions must determine and keep track of these boundaries themselves. For the daily crime log,
the area also includes the patrol jurisdiction of the campus police or security department.

The boundaries exclude a great deal of student and faculty life. A crime in a private apartment, a
private home, a hotel used for a single event, or a restaurant a few blocks away usually falls
outside Clery geography, even if everyone involved is affiliated with the institution. Such
incidents can still be covered by Title IX or by institutional policy, but they will not appear in
the Clery tables.
