Faculty sanctions are the penalties available when a professor is found to have violated
institutional policy or professional standards. Each institution lists them in its faculty handbook
or equivalent rules.

A typical range, from least to most severe, includes a written warning or reprimand, required
training, loss of a salary increase, removal from administrative posts or committees, removal of an
endowed chair or title, restrictions on advising or supervising students, limits on applying for
grants, closure of a center or loss of laboratory space, suspension without pay, and dismissal.
Lesser sanctions are usually imposed by a dean. Severe ones, especially suspension or dismissal of a
tenured professor, require formal proceedings that normally include written charges, a hearing
before a faculty body and a final decision by the president or governing board.

Sanctions are often confidential. Institutions treat them as personnel matters, so the public may
learn only that "appropriate action" was taken, or learn the outcome only if the institution or the
faculty member discloses it. A sanction should also be distinguished from an interim measure such as
paid leave, which precedes a finding, and from voluntary steps such as resignation or retirement,
which can end a process before any sanction is imposed. Retired faculty are harder to sanction,
though titles and privileges can be withdrawn.
