---
term: "Margin of error"
short: "The range above and below a survey estimate within which the true population value is likely to fall, reflecting only the uncertainty that comes from surveying a sample rather than everyone."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["Sampling error", "Plus or minus", "MOE"]
related: ["confidence-interval", "response-rate", "campus-climate-survey", "statistical-significance", "base-rate", "per-capita-rate"]
---

A survey asks a sample of people and uses their answers to estimate something about a larger
population. Because a different sample would give a slightly different answer, the estimate has
uncertainty. The margin of error expresses it. A result reported as 20 percent with a margin of
error of 3 points means that, under the survey's assumptions, the true figure is likely to lie
between 17 and 23 percent. "Likely" usually means at a 95 percent confidence level.

The margin shrinks as the sample grows, and it is larger for subgroups than for the whole sample,
because each subgroup has fewer respondents. A campus climate survey with a reasonable overall
margin may have a wide margin for a single school or a small demographic group, and some reports
suppress estimates that are too uncertain.

The margin of error covers sampling error only. It does not cover bias from who chose to respond,
from how questions were worded, or from people misremembering or declining to disclose. In surveys
with low response rates, those other errors can be much larger than the stated margin. Differences
between two estimates smaller than their combined uncertainty should not be reported as real
differences.
