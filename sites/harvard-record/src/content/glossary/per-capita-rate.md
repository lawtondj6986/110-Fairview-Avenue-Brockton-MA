term: "Per-capita rate"
short: "A count divided by the size of the population it comes from, often expressed per 1,000 or per 100,000 people, so that places or groups of different sizes can be compared."
category: "Records and sourcing"

A raw count, such as the number of reported burglaries on a campus, is hard to compare across
institutions of different sizes. A per-capita rate divides the count by a population figure and
multiplies by a convenient base. A campus with 30 reports and 10,000 students has a rate of 3 per
1,000 students; one with 30 reports and 30,000 students has a rate of 1 per 1,000.

The hard part is choosing the denominator. Students, students plus employees, residential students,
or daily campus population can all be defended, and each gives a different rate. Clery Act
statistics are reported as counts and do not require a rate, so any rate built from them depends on
choices the writer has made and should state. Clery geography also includes some places where
non-students are present, which complicates any per-student figure.

Rates of reported incidents are not rates of actual incidents, because reporting varies between
institutions. With small counts, a single report can move the rate a lot from year to year. A higher
rate can reflect better reporting systems rather than more harm, so rates should be compared with
caution and with their underlying counts.
