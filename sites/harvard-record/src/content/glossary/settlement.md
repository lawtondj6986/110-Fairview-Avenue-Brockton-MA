---
term: "Settlement"
short: "An agreement between parties that ends a legal dispute without a court deciding who was right, usually in exchange for payment or other commitments."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["settlement agreement", "release", "confidential settlement"]
related: ["without-admission-of-liability", "voluntary-dismissal", "dismissal-with-prejudice", "consent-judgment", "class-action"]
---

A settlement is a contract that resolves a dispute. One side typically agrees to pay money or take
certain actions, and the other agrees to give up its claims by signing a release. Settlements can be
reached before any lawsuit is filed, at any point during a case, or even after a verdict while an
appeal is pending. Most civil cases end this way.

When a filed case settles, the parties usually submit a short notice or stipulation of dismissal.
The agreement itself is normally not filed, and its terms, including the amount, are often
confidential. There are exceptions. Class action settlements and settlements on behalf of minors
require court approval and are therefore largely public. Settlements by government bodies are often
obtainable under public records laws. Some states limit confidentiality clauses in cases involving
sexual harassment or abuse.

A settlement is not a judgment on the merits. Parties settle for many reasons, among them the cost
of litigation, uncertainty about the outcome, and the wish to avoid publicity. Payment does not
establish that the claims were true, and agreeing to settle does not show they were weak. The
accurate description is that the claims were resolved without a finding. The underlying allegations
stay unproven and undisproven unless established elsewhere.
