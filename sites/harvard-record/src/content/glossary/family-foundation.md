A family foundation is a private foundation whose money comes from, and whose board is controlled
by, an individual or family. It is a separate legal entity, usually a nonprofit corporation or
trust, recognized as tax-exempt under federal law.

The founder contributes assets to the foundation and may take a tax deduction at that point. The
foundation then makes grants over time. U.S. private foundations are subject to rules that do not
apply to public charities, including a required minimum annual distribution, restrictions on
self-dealing between the foundation and its insiders, and an annual information return, Form 990-PF.
That return is public and lists the foundation's officers, assets and each grant paid with the
recipient's name and amount.

When a gift to a university comes from a family foundation, the foundation is the legal donor and
the name on the receipt. The person behind it may be obvious from the foundation's name or not at
all. Form 990-PF filings are therefore one of the best public sources for tracing who gave what to
whom. A family foundation should not be confused with a donor-advised fund, an account held at a
sponsoring charity. Sponsors report their grants in aggregate and generally do not publicly identify
which account holder recommended each one.
