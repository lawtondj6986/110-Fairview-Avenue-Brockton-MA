Name removal means withdrawing a name that an institution attached to a space, fund, position or
program, usually in recognition of a gift. Common triggers include a donor's failure to pay a
pledge, the demolition or repurposing of a named building, the merger of named funds, or a change in
the institution's own view of what a historical name represents.

Many institutions have a written naming policy that says who may approve and remove names, often the
governing board or a committee it delegates to. Some gift agreements say how long a name lasts, for
example for the life of a building, or list circumstances in which it may be removed. Others are
silent, which can leave the question to negotiation or, rarely, to a
court. Some institutions publish principles and a report when they consider removing a name.

Removing a name does not by itself mean the gift is returned. The money may already be spent or
held in an endowment, and the agreement may say nothing about refunds. A name can also stay in place
after a controversy because of contract terms or a decision on the merits. Readers should look for
the stated reason, the body that decided, and whether the gift itself was affected.
