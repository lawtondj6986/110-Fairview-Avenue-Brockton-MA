---
term: "Cy pres"
short: "A legal doctrine that lets a court redirect a charitable gift to a purpose as close as possible to the donor's original one when that purpose can no longer be carried out."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Cy pres doctrine", "Modification of charitable purpose", "Release of restriction"]

Cy pres, from an old French phrase meaning "as near," is a doctrine courts use to modify charitable
gifts. When the purpose a donor set becomes impossible, impracticable or unlawful, a court may
direct the money to a related purpose that comes as near as it can to what the donor wanted, instead
of letting the gift fail. The state attorney general, who represents the public interest in
charitable assets, is normally given notice and may take part.

In the university setting, cy pres can arise when a donor endowed a program that has closed or a
scholarship whose eligibility rules have become unlawful. The Uniform Prudent Management of
Institutional Funds Act, adopted in most states, adds the ground that a restriction has become
wasteful and provides a simpler route for some small, old funds. Details vary by state, so the
current state law should be checked.

Cy pres is not a way for an institution to change a gift it would simply prefer to use differently.
It is also distinct from a donor agreeing to amend a gift, which needs no court. In class action
law, the same phrase refers to sending unclaimed settlement money to a related charity, a different
use of the term.
