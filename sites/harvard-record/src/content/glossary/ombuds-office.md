An ombuds (also ombudsman or ombudsperson) is a designated neutral who helps people in an
organization deal with problems. Many universities maintain an ombuds office for students, faculty,
staff or all three.

The role is defined by standards of practice published by the profession's international
association, which rest on four principles: independence from ordinary management, impartiality,
confidentiality and informality. A visitor to the office can describe a concern, learn what policies
and channels exist, think through options and, if they wish, ask the ombuds to make informal
inquiries or to facilitate a conversation. The ombuds may also report general trends to senior
leadership without identifying individuals. The office keeps minimal records.

An ombuds office does not investigate, decide who is right, impose discipline or act as anyone's
advocate. Under the profession's standards, telling the ombuds about a problem generally does not
count as formally notifying the institution, so a person who wants the institution to act must use a
formal channel as well. Confidentiality has exceptions, commonly an imminent risk of serious harm,
and its treatment under federal reporting rules and in litigation is not uniform. The office's
design means that it leaves few traces. An issue may have been raised there repeatedly without any
record reaching decision makers.
