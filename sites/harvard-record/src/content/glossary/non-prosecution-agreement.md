---
term: "Non-prosecution agreement"
short: "A contract in which prosecutors agree not to bring charges, or further charges, against a person or company in return for meeting stated conditions."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["NPA", "deferred prosecution agreement"]
related: ["plea-agreement", "conviction", "indictment", "finding-vs-allegation"]
---

A non-prosecution agreement is an agreement between a prosecuting office and a person or
organization under investigation. The government promises not to prosecute specified conduct. In
return the other side accepts conditions, which can include cooperating with investigators, paying
money, admitting to a statement of facts, or pleading guilty to charges in another court.

Because no charges are filed under the agreement itself, it is usually not submitted to a judge for
approval and may not appear on any court docket. Its terms can be confidential. That sets it apart
from a deferred prosecution agreement, in which charges are filed in court and then held in
suspension while the defendant meets conditions, and from a plea agreement, which results in a
conviction entered by a court. Such agreements are common in corporate cases and less usual for
individuals.

A non-prosecution agreement is not an acquittal and not a finding of innocence. It reflects a
prosecutor's decision, made for reasons that may include the strength of the evidence, resources, or
cooperation received. It normally binds only the office that signed it, and the question of whether
other offices are bound has been litigated. It also does not prevent civil lawsuits by private
parties over the same conduct.
