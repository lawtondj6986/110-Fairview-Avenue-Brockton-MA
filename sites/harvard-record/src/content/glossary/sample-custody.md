---
term: "Sample custody and chain of custody"
short: "The documented control of a biological sample from collection onward, recording who held it, where, when, and under what conditions."
category: "Research ethics"
aliases: ["chain of custody", "sample tracking", "specimen custody"]

Sample custody is the question of who has control of a biological sample at each moment. Chain of
custody is the documentation that answers it: an unbroken record showing when the sample was
collected and by whom, how it was labeled, where it was stored, each person or laboratory it passed
to, and what was done with it.

Laboratories maintain the chain with unique identifiers on every tube, barcodes, logs or tracking
software, signatures at handover, and controlled storage. In research, this protects scientific
validity, since a mislabeled or swapped sample attaches results to the wrong person. It also
supports the ethical commitments made to donors, because an institution cannot honor limits on use
or a request to withdraw if it does not know where samples are. In forensic and some clinical
testing the standard is stricter, with witnessed collection and tamper-evident seals, because the
result must be defensible as coming from a particular person.

The term has a narrower meaning in evidence law, where it concerns whether an item can be admitted
in court. In a research context it is a quality and accountability practice. A gap in the chain does
not prove a sample was misused. It means that its identity or handling cannot be fully verified.
