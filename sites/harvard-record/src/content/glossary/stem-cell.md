---
term: "Stem cell"
short: "A cell that can both copy itself and give rise to more specialized cell types."

A stem cell is a cell with two abilities: it can divide to make more stem cells, and it can give
rise to more specialized cells. Stem cells differ in how many cell types they can produce.
Pluripotent stem cells can form essentially any tissue of the body. Embryonic stem cells, derived
from very early embryos, are the original example. Adult (or tissue) stem cells are more restricted.
Blood-forming stem cells in the bone marrow, for instance, make the various blood cells but not
other tissues.

In research, stem cells are used to study development and disease and to produce cell types that are
otherwise hard to obtain, such as human nerve or heart cells. In medicine, the long-established use
is the transplantation of blood-forming stem cells to treat certain blood cancers and blood
disorders. Some other cell-based treatments have been approved for specific conditions after
clinical trials.

The term is widely misused in marketing. Many clinics have offered "stem cell" treatments for aging,
joint pain and other conditions without good evidence that they work, and regulators have warned
about harm from such products. That a treatment involves stem cells says nothing about whether it
has been tested or approved.
