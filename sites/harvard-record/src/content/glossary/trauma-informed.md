---
term: "Trauma-informed"
short: "An approach to services, interviews and investigations that takes account of how traumatic experiences can affect a person's behavior, memory and willingness to engage."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["trauma-informed care", "trauma-informed investigation"]

Trauma-informed describes a way of working with people who may have experienced violence, abuse or
other overwhelming events. The idea originated in health and social services. Its usual principles
are physical and emotional safety, transparency about what will happen, giving the person choices
where possible, and avoiding practices that needlessly cause further distress.

In campus settings the phrase is applied to counseling, advocacy, policing and Title IX work. A
trauma-informed interviewer might explain the process in advance, let the person tell the account in
their own order before asking detailed questions, allow breaks and a support person, and avoid
questions framed as blame. Federal rules require that officials who conduct certain campus
proceedings receive training on relevant issues, and many institutions describe that training as
trauma-informed.

The approach is a matter of method. It does not mean that an account is presumed to be true, that
inconsistencies are ignored, or that the accused person's rights are reduced. Critics have argued
that some training materials overstated the science on trauma and memory and encouraged
investigators to treat gaps or contradictions as confirmation. Current regulations require that
training materials not rely on sex stereotypes and that they promote impartial investigations. An
institution's statement that its process is trauma-informed describes an intention and says little
about how a particular case was handled.
