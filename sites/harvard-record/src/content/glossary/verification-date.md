---
term: "Verification date"
short: "The date on which a source was last opened and checked against the claims that cite it; it tells readers how current the checking is."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["last verified", "last checked", "date accessed", "pending check"]
related: ["update-log", "archive-snapshot", "link-rot", "correction", "reliability-grade"]
---

A verification date records when someone last confirmed that a cited source says what it is cited
for. It differs from the publication date of the source and from the date a page was written or last
edited. The "accessed on" date in a conventional citation is a simple form of the same idea.

The date matters because sources change. Web pages are edited or removed, court dockets gain new
entries, articles receive corrections, and reports are superseded. A claim that was accurately
sourced when written can drift out of date. A verification date tells the reader the point up to
which the check holds, and tells maintainers which entries are due for review.

On this site, each entry in the source register carries the date on which a maintainer last opened
the original document and checked the cited facts against it. Entries without a date are labelled
"pending check" and should be treated as provisional, because they were compiled from published
summaries of the source. Details are at `/methodology/` and `/sources/`.

A verification date is a limited assurance. It means the citation was checked against the source on
that day. It does not mean the source is correct, and it does not cover anything that has happened
since. A recent date is also no substitute for following the citation oneself.
