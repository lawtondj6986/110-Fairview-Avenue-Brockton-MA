---
term: "Amnesty policy"
short: "A campus rule under which a person who reports or witnesses serious misconduct, or seeks medical help, will not be disciplined for minor violations such as underage drinking connected to the incident."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["medical amnesty", "Good Samaritan policy", "immunity policy"]
related: ["bystander-intervention", "disclosure-vs-formal-complaint", "retaliation"]
---

An amnesty policy removes one deterrent to reporting. Students who were drinking under age or using
drugs when an incident occurred may fear that coming forward will expose them to discipline. Under
an amnesty policy, the institution commits not to bring conduct charges for those minor violations
against a person who reports sexual misconduct in good faith, who is a witness, or who calls for
medical help for themselves or someone else.

Policies differ in scope. Some cover only alcohol and drug possession or use. Some extend to
respondents and witnesses in a sexual misconduct process. Some allow non-disciplinary responses such
as an educational conversation. Several states require colleges to adopt amnesty provisions for
people who report sexual violence, and many states have medical amnesty or Good Samaritan statutes
that limit criminal liability for someone who seeks emergency help during an overdose.

Amnesty is limited to the collateral violation. It does not excuse the serious misconduct being
reported, and it usually does not cover conduct that put others at risk, such as supplying drugs. A
campus policy also binds only the institution. It cannot prevent police or prosecutors from acting,
unless a state statute separately provides protection. Readers should check the wording of the
specific policy and the applicable state law.
