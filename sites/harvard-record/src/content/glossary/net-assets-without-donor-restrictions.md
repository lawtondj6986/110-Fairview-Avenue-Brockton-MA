term: "Net assets without donor restrictions"
short: "In a nonprofit's financial statements, resources that no donor has limited, including money the board has set aside for its own purposes."

Net assets without donor restrictions are the part of a nonprofit's net assets that carries no
donor-imposed limit. They include unrestricted gifts, earned revenue such as tuition and fees after
expenses, the value of buildings and equipment net of debt, and restricted gifts once their
restrictions have been met. Older statements call this class unrestricted net assets.

The class also includes amounts the governing board has chosen to set aside, such as a
quasi-endowment or a reserve for a future building. These are often labeled board-designated. Because
the board imposed the limit, the board can usually lift it, which is why accounting treats the money
as unrestricted even though it is earmarked.

A common misreading is to treat this figure as cash on hand. Much of it may be tied up in property
or long-term investments, and some of it may be committed by board policy. A large balance also says
nothing about whether a particular unit, such as one school in a decentralized university, has
money available. The notes to the financial statements usually break the total down and are the
place to look for detail.
