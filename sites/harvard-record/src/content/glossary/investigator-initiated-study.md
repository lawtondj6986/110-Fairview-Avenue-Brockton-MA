---
term: "Investigator-initiated study"
short: "A study conceived, designed and run by a researcher or academic team, as opposed to one designed and controlled by a commercial sponsor."
category: "Research ethics"
aliases: ["investigator-initiated research", "investigator-sponsored study", "sponsor-investigator"]

An investigator-initiated study is one in which the idea, the design and the conduct originate with
the researcher, not with a company seeking to test its product. The investigator writes the
protocol, obtains ethics approval, oversees data collection and analysis, and decides on
publication.

Funding can come from many places: a government grant, a foundation, institutional funds,
philanthropy, or a company that supplies money or product without controlling the study. When an
investigator both initiates and conducts a study of a drug or device regulated by the Food and Drug
Administration, the regulations treat that person as a sponsor-investigator, who carries the legal
duties of both roles, including safety reporting and record keeping.

Investigator-initiated does not mean unfunded, informal or free of oversight. The same requirements
for ethics review and consent apply. The model does shift responsibility. Without a commercial
sponsor's monitors and regulatory staff, much depends on the investigator and the institution, and
smaller studies may have less independent checking. The term also says nothing by itself about the
funder's influence. A study can be investigator-initiated and still supported by a party with an
interest in the result, which is a matter for conflict of interest rules.
