A professional conduct policy states what an institution expects of its members beyond competent
performance of their jobs. It may be a single code or a collection of separate policies in a faculty
handbook and staff manual.

Subjects commonly covered include honesty in research and teaching, fair treatment of students and
colleagues, harassment and discrimination, relationships between people of unequal power, abuse of
authority, conflicts of interest and commitment, proper use of institutional funds, facilities and
name, and the duty to cooperate with investigations. The policy normally explains how to report a
concern, who reviews it, what procedure follows and what sanctions are possible. Some categories of
conduct, notably research misconduct and sex-based harassment, have their own procedures because
federal requirements apply.

Conduct policies are revised over time, and what matters for any event is the version in force when
it happened. Older policies were often shorter and left more to custom. They may have said little
about matters now addressed directly, such as bringing outside persons into contact with students or
accepting personal benefits from donors. A policy also applies only to those it names. Faculty,
staff, students, visiting scholars and unpaid affiliates may be covered by different rules or, in
the case of loosely affiliated visitors, by none that clearly fit.
