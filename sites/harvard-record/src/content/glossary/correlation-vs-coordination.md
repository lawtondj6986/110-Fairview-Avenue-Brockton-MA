---
term: "Correlation versus coordination"
short: "Two events occurring together or in sequence (correlation) do not show that the people involved planned or acted together (coordination)."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["coincidence versus coordination", "correlation is not causation", "guilt by association"]
related: ["inference", "base-rate", "confirmation-bias", "search-hit-vs-document", "absence-of-evidence"]
---

Correlation is a pattern in which two things occur together, at similar times, or in a sequence.
Coordination is a claim about people: that they agreed, planned or acted in concert. The first can
be read off a timeline. The second needs evidence of communication, agreement or direction.

The gap between them is wide. Events line up for many ordinary reasons. People in the same field
attend the same conferences, know the same intermediaries and react to the same news. Institutions
follow annual cycles, so gifts, appointments and announcements cluster at predictable times. In a
large enough set of records, striking coincidences are certain to appear. This is the familiar rule
that correlation does not establish causation, applied to human conduct. Appearing in the same
document, photograph or guest list as someone is an association of the same kind and shows no shared
purpose.

Evidence that does bear on coordination includes messages in which the parties discuss the plan, an
agreement or instruction, payments tied to a stated purpose, or testimony from a participant. This
site's rule is that two facts placed side by side are not presented as connected unless a source
connects them. Readers should watch for writing that sets facts next to each other and lets the
arrangement imply what no source states.
