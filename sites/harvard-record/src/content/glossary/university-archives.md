A university's archives are the official home of its permanent records. They are usually part of the
library system and are run by professional archivists.

Holdings typically include the records of the governing boards and the president's office, files
from deans and departments, official publications, catalogs and directories, photographs, student
newspapers and yearbooks, and the personal and professional papers of faculty members who chose to
donate them. Offices transfer material under the records retention schedule or when they clear out
files. Archivists appraise what is worth keeping, arrange it and write finding aids, which are
descriptive guides that tell researchers what a collection contains.

Access is the main constraint. Archives commonly close administrative records for a fixed period,
often several decades from their creation, and close personnel and student records for longer.
Recent records of governing boards and senior officers are therefore usually unavailable. Faculty
papers are personal property given under a deed of gift, and the donor may impose restrictions of
their own. Material from recent decades may also not yet have been transferred, or may exist only as
unmanaged electronic files. Archives are thus rich sources for older history and thin for recent
events. Published directories, catalogs and newspapers, which carry no restriction, are often the
most accessible evidence of who held what position and when.
