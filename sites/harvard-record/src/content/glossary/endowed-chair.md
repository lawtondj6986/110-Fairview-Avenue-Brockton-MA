---
term: "Endowed chair"
short: "A faculty position paid for by income from a dedicated endowment fund and usually named for the donor or a person the donor chooses."
category: "Fundraising and development"

An endowed chair, or endowed professorship, is a faculty position supported by an endowment fund
created for that purpose. The annual distribution from the fund helps pay the holder's salary and
sometimes research costs. The position carries a name, typically the donor's or that of someone the
donor wishes to honor.

A donor funds the chair through a gift agreement that states its field and name. Each institution
sets its own minimum gift for a chair, and the amounts differ greatly. The institution, not the
donor, selects the holder through its normal academic appointment process. Being named to a chair is
an honor for a professor and frees budget money for the department. Some chairs are tied to a field,
others can move between fields at the dean's discretion.

Funding a chair does not give the donor the right to choose the professor, review the holder's work
or remove them. Institutions that have allowed donors a formal role in selection have been
criticized on academic freedom grounds, and most policies rule it out. A chair is also distinct from
tenure. A tenured professor may hold no chair, and a chair holder keeps tenure if the chair is
reassigned. The name on a chair records where the money came from, not a continuing relationship.
