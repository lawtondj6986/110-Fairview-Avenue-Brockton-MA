A live hearing is a meeting at which a decision-maker hears from the parties and witnesses in a
campus sexual harassment case before deciding whether a policy was violated. Federal Title IX
regulations issued in 2020 required postsecondary institutions to hold one in formal complaint cases,
with each party's advisor permitted to ask the other party and witnesses relevant questions. The
hearing can take place with the parties in separate rooms connected by video.

Regulations issued in 2024 made a live hearing optional, allowing other ways of assessing
credibility. A federal court vacated the 2024 regulations in early 2025, and the Department of
Education announced it would enforce the 2020 rules. Because these rules have changed repeatedly and
are the subject of litigation, the current text and any institution's own policy should be checked.
State law may add requirements.

A campus hearing is not a trial. Rules of evidence are looser, there is no jury, and the outcome is
a finding under a school policy, not a criminal verdict. Institutions often apply hearing procedures
to cases outside Title IX as well, so a hearing does not by itself show that Title IX applied.
