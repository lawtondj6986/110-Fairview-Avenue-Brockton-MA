Absence of evidence means that a search turned up nothing bearing on a question. The familiar maxim
that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence is a useful warning, though not strictly correct
as logic. How much an absence tells us depends on how strongly we would expect to find evidence if
the thing were true, and on how thoroughly anyone looked.

If an event would certainly have produced a record in a place that was fully searched, finding
nothing is meaningful. If the event would probably have left no trace, or the relevant records are
private, destroyed, redacted or were never examined, finding nothing shows very little. Most private
activity never enters the public record.

This site uses a specific label, "nothing on record", for claims on which it found no public record,
and states that this is not proof of absence. The principle applies in both directions. A missing
record does not show that something did not happen. It equally does not suggest that something did
happen and was hidden. Treating missing evidence as a sign of concealment is a pattern of reasoning
that cannot be proven wrong, because every absence can be explained as further concealment. The
honest statement is the limited one: this is what was searched, and nothing was found.
