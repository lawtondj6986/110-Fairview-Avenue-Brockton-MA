---
term: "No-contact order"
short: "A directive from a college that tells one or more members of its community not to communicate with or approach each other. It is an administrative measure, not a court order."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["no-contact directive", "mutual no-contact order", "NCO"]
related: ["supportive-measures", "interim-measures", "protective-order", "injunction", "stalking"]
---

A no-contact order, often called a no-contact directive, is an instruction issued by a college
office such as the dean of students or the Title IX office. It tells the people named not to contact
each other in person, by phone, electronically or through third parties. It may add practical terms
about shared classes, dining halls or residence buildings.

Institutions issue these directives as supportive measures after a report, during an investigation,
or as part of an outcome. They are frequently mutual, restricting both people, because a restriction
imposed on one person alone before any finding can be treated as disciplinary. A directive can be
issued without any determination about what happened. Violating it is handled through the
institution's conduct process and can lead to discipline.

The authority behind a directive is the institution's power over its own students and employees. It
has no force against someone outside the institution and cannot be enforced by police through
arrest. That is the difference from a civil protection order or restraining order, which a court
issues under state law after a legal showing and which carries criminal penalties for violation.
Institutions are generally expected to tell people how to seek a court order and to honor one that
exists. Being subject to a no-contact directive is not a finding of misconduct.
