A grievance is a complaint that someone has been treated in a way the institution's rules do not
permit. A faculty member might grieve a denial of promotion on the ground that the review did not
follow the handbook, or a change in teaching load. A staff member might grieve a disciplinary action.
Students often have parallel procedures for academic decisions.

Grievance procedures are usually set out in a faculty or staff handbook, or, for unionized employees,
in a collective bargaining agreement. They typically name a deadline for filing, an initial informal
step, a review by a committee or administrator, and a final decision. Many grievance reviews ask
whether the process was followed rather than whether the underlying academic judgment was correct.
In a unionized workplace the final step may be arbitration.

A grievance is distinct from a complaint of discrimination or harassment, which normally goes through
a separate office with its own rules, and from a disciplinary case brought against the grievant. A
grievance decision is an internal outcome, and a pending grievance says nothing about its merit.
