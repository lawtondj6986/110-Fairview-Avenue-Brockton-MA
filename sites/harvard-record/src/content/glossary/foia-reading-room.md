The Freedom of Information Act requires agencies not only to answer requests but also to publish
some categories of records on their own initiative. These include final opinions and orders in
adjudicated cases, statements of policy, staff manuals that affect the public, and records that have
been released and are likely to be requested again or have been requested often. Agencies post them
in an online reading room, sometimes called a FOIA library.

For researchers, a reading room is often the first place to check before filing a request. Resolution
agreements, findings letters, inspection reports and logs of past requests are often posted there.
Some agencies also publish their FOIA logs, which list what others have asked for and can show that a
record has already been released.

A reading room is not a complete archive. What appears there depends on the agency's practice, its
staffing and its own judgment about what is frequently requested, and items can move when a site is
redesigned. The absence of a document from a reading room does not mean the document does not exist
or has never been released.
