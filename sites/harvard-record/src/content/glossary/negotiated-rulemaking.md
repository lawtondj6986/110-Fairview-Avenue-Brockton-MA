---
term: "Negotiated rulemaking"
short: "A process in which a federal agency drafts a proposed regulation together with a committee of affected interests before publishing it for public comment."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["neg reg", "regulatory negotiation"]
related: ["notice-and-comment-rulemaking", "higher-education-act", "vacatur"]
---

Negotiated rulemaking brings the people a regulation will affect into the drafting. An agency
convenes a committee of representatives, such as institutions, students, lenders, state officials
and advocacy groups, with an agency negotiator and a neutral facilitator. The committee meets in
public sessions and tries to reach consensus on proposed regulatory text.

For most agencies the process is optional. For student aid regulations under Title IV of the Higher
Education Act, the Department of Education is generally required to use it. If the committee reaches
consensus, meaning no member dissents, the Department ordinarily publishes that agreed language as
its proposed rule. If consensus fails, the Department is free to write its own proposal. In either
case the proposal must still go through notice-and-comment rulemaking before it becomes final.

Negotiated rulemaking supplements the ordinary process and does not replace it. Agreement in
committee does not make a rule law, and the final rule can differ from what the committee discussed.
Not every education regulation goes through it. The Title IX regulations, for example, rest on a
different statute and were issued through notice and comment alone. Session materials and proposed
text are usually posted publicly, which makes them a useful record of how a rule developed.
