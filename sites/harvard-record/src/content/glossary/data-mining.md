term: "Data mining"

Data mining means searching large datasets for patterns, associations or groupings using statistical
and computational methods. In biomedicine the data may be health records, insurance claims, genetic
databases, published papers or laboratory measurements. Typical aims include finding links between
variants and diseases, spotting unexpected drug side effects, or identifying subgroups of patients.

Data mining is usually exploratory. It generates hypotheses from data that already exist, as opposed
to collecting new data to test a hypothesis stated in advance. That makes it efficient, and it also
creates a well-known statistical hazard. When very many possible relationships are examined, some
will appear significant by chance alone. Responsible practice corrects for the number of comparisons
made and confirms findings in an independent dataset.

The term is neutral and describes a method. It is sometimes used pejoratively for "data dredging",
meaning an undisciplined search for any publishable result. It should not be confused with scraping,
the automated collection of data from websites. When the data concern people, mining them for
research can require ethics review and permission from the data holder even if no new information is
collected from anyone.
