term: "Official record"
short: "A document created or issued by a government body, court or institution in the course of its formal functions."

An official record is a document that an authority produces as part of its formal work. Statutes and
regulations, court orders and filings, agency reports and letters, legislative hearing transcripts,
corporate filings required by law, and reports formally issued by an institution about its own
affairs are examples.

Official records carry weight for specific reasons. They are created under defined procedures, often
with legal consequences for false statements. They are attributable to an identifiable body. And
they are stable, so a reader can find the same text later. On this site an official document
speaking for itself receives the highest reliability grade, as explained at `/methodology/`.

Three cautions apply. First, official does not mean neutral. An institution's report on itself
reflects what it chose to examine, whom it interviewed and what it chose to publish. Second, an
official record establishes that something was formally stated, filed or decided. Whether the
contents are accurate is a further question, and a filing by one party to a dispute is advocacy.
Third, official is not the same as public. Many official records are confidential, sealed or
released only in part, while much public material, such as a news article, is not official at all.
