short: "The narrative attachment to a Form 990 where an organization explains its answers to other parts of the form, including how it reviews the return and handles conflicts of interest."

Schedule O is titled Supplemental Information to Form 990. It has no fixed questions of its own.
Instead, other parts of the form direct the filer to use Schedule O to explain an answer, and the
filer can also add any explanation it chooses. For large organizations it can run to many pages.

Readers often go to Schedule O for the governance section of the form. There the organization
describes the process its board uses to review the return before filing, how it monitors and
enforces its conflict of interest policy, how it sets pay for top officials, and whether and how it
makes its governing documents and financial statements public. Explanations of changes to governing
documents and of significant changes in activities also appear here.

Schedule O is written by the organization about itself. It describes stated processes, not whether
they were followed in a given case. A statement that a policy exists and is monitored is evidence of
the policy's existence, not of how any particular conflict was handled. Because the text is free-form,
it is also the part of the filing most likely to change wording from year to year.
