---
term: "Quid pro quo contribution"
short: "In tax law, a payment to a charity made partly as a gift and partly in exchange for goods or services, such as a gala ticket. Only the gift portion is deductible."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["tax-exempt-status", "form-990", "quid-pro-quo-harassment", "quid-pro-quo-fundraising"]

A quid pro quo contribution is a payment to a charitable organization that is part donation and part
purchase. A standard example is a ticket to a fundraising dinner: some of the price pays for the
meal, and the rest is a gift. The donor may deduct only the amount by which the payment exceeds the
fair market value of what was received.

Federal tax law places a disclosure duty on the charity. For a quid pro quo contribution of more
than $75, the charity must give the donor a written statement that says only the excess is
deductible and provides a good-faith estimate of the value of the goods or services. Token items of
small value and certain intangible religious benefits are disregarded. A separate rule requires a
donor to hold a written acknowledgment from the charity for any single gift of $250 or more, stating
whether anything was given in return.

The term is a technical tax label and carries no suggestion of wrongdoing. It is unrelated to quid
pro quo harassment. It also does not describe the informal benefits a major donor may receive, such
as access, recognition or influence. Those have no assigned market value under these rules, although
other rules on private benefit can apply.
