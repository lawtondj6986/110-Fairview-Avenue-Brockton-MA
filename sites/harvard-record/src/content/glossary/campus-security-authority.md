term: "Campus security authority"
short: "A person whose role makes them a designated channel for crime reports under the Clery Act, and who must pass those reports on for the institution's statistics."

A campus security authority, or CSA, is someone to whom a crime may be reported for Clery Act
purposes. The regulations define four groups: the campus police or security department; other people
with responsibility for campus security, such as those who monitor building entrances; any person or
office the institution's policy names as a place to report crimes; and officials with significant
responsibility for student and campus activities, such as deans of students, residence life staff,
athletic coaches and advisers to student groups.

When a CSA receives a report of a Clery crime that they believe was made in good faith, they must
forward it to the office that compiles the statistics. The report typically covers what happened,
when and where. It does not need to identify the people involved, and the CSA is not expected to
investigate or to decide whether a crime really occurred. The information also feeds decisions about
timely warnings.

Pastoral counselors and professional counselors are exempt when acting in those roles. Most
classroom faculty and clerical staff are not CSAs unless they hold an additional role, such as
advising a student organization.

CSA status is distinct from being a responsible employee under Title IX, which concerns reporting to
the Title IX office. The two duties have different purposes and can attach to the same person.
