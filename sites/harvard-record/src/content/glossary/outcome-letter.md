short: "The written notice a campus disciplinary office sends to the parties stating the result of a proceeding, any sanction, and how and when the result may be appealed."

An outcome letter tells a party in a disciplinary case what was decided. In cases of dating
violence, domestic violence, sexual assault and stalking, the Clery Act regulations require
institutions to inform the accuser and the accused in writing, at the same time, of the result of
any institutional proceeding, the procedures for appeal, any change to the result before it becomes
final, and when it becomes final. Title IX regulations add their own notice requirements in sexual
harassment cases.

The letter typically states whether the respondent was found responsible for each alleged policy
violation, the reasoning or a summary of it, any sanctions and any remedies for the complainant.
Privacy law, chiefly FERPA for students, limits what may be shared with others, but it allows
disclosure of certain outcomes to a victim and, in some cases, more widely.

Outcome letters are private documents and are seldom public. When one surfaces in reporting, the
reader is usually seeing one party's copy, and it may be incomplete or later changed on appeal. A
letter states the institution's finding under its own policy and standard, not a legal judgment.
