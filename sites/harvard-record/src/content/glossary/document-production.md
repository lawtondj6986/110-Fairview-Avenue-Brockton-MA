A document production is the set of records one party hands over to another under a legal demand or
a negotiated request. It happens in civil discovery, in response to grand jury and agency subpoenas,
and in congressional and regulatory inquiries. Large productions are delivered in stages, called
rolling productions.

The producing side first identifies likely sources, such as particular employees' accounts and
shared drives, then searches them using agreed date ranges and search terms, and reviews the results
for relevance and privilege. Each page is stamped with a unique identifier, traditionally called a
Bates number, made up of a short prefix and a running number. The prefix usually indicates who
produced the document. Privileged documents are withheld or redacted and listed on a privilege log.

Bates numbers help readers. When a published document bears one, its source and its place in the
production can be identified, and consecutive numbers can show that pages belong together.

A production is limited by the request and the search. It contains what was asked for, found and not
withheld, from the custodians and period that were searched. It is not a complete archive, and a
missing document may never have been within scope. Production also implies nothing about wrongdoing,
since uninvolved third parties produce documents routinely.
