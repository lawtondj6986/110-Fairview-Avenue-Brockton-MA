The audit committee is the part of a governing board charged with checking that the institution's
financial statements are reliable and that its systems for preventing error, fraud and legal
violations work.

The committee selects and meets with the external auditors, an independent accounting firm that
examines the annual financial statements. It also oversees internal audit, a staff department that
reviews operations and controls across the university and reports its findings to the committee as
well as to management. At many institutions the committee's charter extends to legal compliance,
conflict of interest policies, enterprise risk and the hotline through which employees report
suspected wrongdoing. Members are expected to be independent of management, and at least some should
have financial expertise.

An audit has a specific and limited purpose. External auditors give an opinion on whether the
financial statements are fairly presented in material respects. They test samples of transactions
and do not review every gift or judge whether a donor was suitable. A clean audit opinion says
nothing about the wisdom or propriety of a relationship. Internal audit can look more closely at a
particular unit or process, but only when it is directed to do so or its risk assessment selects
that area. The committee, like the full board, depends on what is brought to it.
