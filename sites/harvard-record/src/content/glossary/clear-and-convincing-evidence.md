---
term: "Clear and convincing evidence"
short: "A standard of proof that requires a claim to be highly probable, higher than preponderance of the evidence and lower than beyond a reasonable doubt."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["preponderance-of-the-evidence", "burden-of-proof"]
---

Clear and convincing evidence is the middle standard of proof. To meet it, the evidence must leave
the decision-maker with a firm belief that the claim is highly probable. It demands more than a
showing that something is more likely than not, but less than the near certainty required for a
criminal conviction.

Courts use the standard where more is at stake than money alone, for example in some fraud claims,
in proceedings to end parental rights, and in civil commitment. In higher education it appears
mostly in disciplinary policies. Some institutions historically used it for misconduct cases against
students or faculty. The 2020 Title IX regulations allow a school to adopt it for sexual harassment
complaints, as long as the same standard applies whether the accused person is a student or an
employee.

The choice of standard matters most in close cases, where the evidence consists largely of two
conflicting accounts. It does not change who has the burden of proof, and it does not change what
evidence may be considered. When comparing outcomes across institutions or across years, check which
standard each process used, because the same evidence can lead to different results under different
standards.
