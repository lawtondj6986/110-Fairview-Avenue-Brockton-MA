---
term: "Sealed record"
short: "A court filing or case file that a judge has ordered closed to the public, in whole or in part."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["filed under seal", "sealing order", "unsealing"]
related: ["redaction", "protective-order", "docket", "discovery", "court-record"]
---

A sealed record is a document filed with a court that the public cannot inspect. Sealing can cover a
single exhibit, an entire filing, or occasionally a whole case. The docket often still shows that a
sealed item exists, with an entry such as "sealed document," even though its content is unavailable.

Court records in the United States are presumed open. A party who wants something sealed must
normally ask the judge and show a reason that outweighs the public interest in access. Recognized
reasons include protecting minors and victims of sexual offenses, trade secrets, medical
information, and the integrity of ongoing investigations. Some categories are sealed by statute or
rule, such as grand jury materials and juvenile proceedings. Courts generally prefer narrow
measures, such as redaction, to sealing a whole document.

Sealing is not necessarily permanent. A party, a news organization or any member of the public can
move to unseal, and judges sometimes release records years later, often with redactions. Records
unsealed in this way are a significant source for researchers.

A seal says nothing about the truth or importance of what is inside. Sealed material may be mundane.
Unsealed material is still whatever it was before: an allegation, a piece of testimony, or an
exhibit, with the same evidentiary status.
