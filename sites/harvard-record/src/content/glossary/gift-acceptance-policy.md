---
term: "Gift acceptance policy"
short: "An institution's written rules on which gifts it will take, from whom, in what form, on what terms, and who has authority to approve them."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Gift policy", "Gift acceptance guidelines"]

A gift acceptance policy is the document that tells staff what kinds of gifts the institution
accepts and how decisions are made. It is approved by senior leadership or the governing board.

Most policies cover the same ground. They list acceptable forms of gift (cash, securities, real
estate, artwork, cryptocurrency and so on) and the checks each requires. They say which restrictions
are acceptable and which are not, such as conditions that would give a donor control over hiring or
research. They set approval levels, so that larger or more unusual gifts go to more senior officials
or to a committee. Many now also address the source of funds and the donor's background, and
describe when a gift should be referred for further review or declined.

A policy on paper does not show how it was applied. Policies tend to be written in general terms,
leave wide discretion to officials, and depend on staff referring a gift for review in the first
place. Whether a policy bound all parts of a decentralized university, or only the central office,
is a separate question. Policies also change over time, often after a controversy, so the version in
force when a gift was made may differ from the one published today.
