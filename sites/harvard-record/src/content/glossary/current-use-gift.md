---
term: "Current-use gift"
short: "A gift meant to be spent in full within the near term, as opposed to an endowment gift whose principal is invested and preserved."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Expendable gift", "Current-use fund", "Spendable gift"]
related: ["endowment", "restricted-gift", "unrestricted-gift", "gift"]
---

A current-use gift is available to be spent right away or over a short period. The whole amount, not
just investment income, can be used. It is the counterpart of an endowment gift, where the principal
is invested and only a portion of the return is spent each year.

Current-use gifts may be restricted or unrestricted. A donor might give a current-use gift to pay
for a conference, a few years of a research program, a fellowship or equipment. The institution
places the money in a spendable account, the receiving unit draws it down, and the fund closes when
the balance reaches zero. Because the money is spent rather than invested, a current-use gift has a
more immediate effect on a program than an endowment gift of the same size, but it creates no
lasting source of income.

Current-use does not mean informal. These gifts go through the same acceptance, recording and
acknowledgment steps as any other gift. The term also says nothing about size: a current-use gift
can be small or very large. For a reader trying to trace money, current-use gifts matter because a
research group that depends on them needs repeated gifts to continue, which can make an ongoing
donor relationship more important to that group.
