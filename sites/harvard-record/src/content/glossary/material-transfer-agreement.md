A material transfer agreement (MTA) is a contract used when one organization sends physical research
material to another. The material may be cell lines, DNA, tissue, laboratory animals, chemical
compounds or other items. The agreement defines what the recipient may do with the material.

Typical terms cover the permitted research use, a ban on passing the material to third parties
without permission, a ban on use in humans, ownership of the material and of anything derived from
it, rights to resulting inventions, publication and acknowledgment, confidentiality, liability, and
return or destruction at the end. When the material comes from people, the agreement commonly
requires that it was collected with appropriate consent and ethics approval, limits the recipient to
uses consistent with that consent, and forbids attempts to identify the donors. Agreements are
normally signed by an authorized official of each institution, not by the scientists alone. Standard
templates exist to speed transfers between academic institutions.

An MTA covers physical material. Data are usually transferred under a separate data use agreement.
An MTA also does not replace ethics review at the receiving institution. From a records standpoint,
an MTA is useful evidence that a transfer was authorized and on what terms. The absence of one may
indicate an informal transfer, or simply that the agreement is not public.
