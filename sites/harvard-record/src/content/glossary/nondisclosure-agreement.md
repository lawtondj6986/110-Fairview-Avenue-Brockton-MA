A nondisclosure agreement, or NDA, binds a person to keep certain information confidential. In
universities, NDAs are routine in research collaborations with companies, in the review of
unpublished technology and in hiring for sensitive roles. They also appear in settlement and
separation agreements, where they may cover the amount paid or the facts of a dispute.

The enforceability of an NDA depends on its wording and on the law that applies. Courts generally
will not enforce a confidentiality promise that hides a crime or prevents a person from testifying
under subpoena. Federal law limits the use of such agreements to block reports to government
agencies. A federal law enacted in 2022, the Speak Out Act, makes many confidentiality clauses signed
before a sexual assault or harassment dispute arises unenforceable as to that dispute, and a number
of states have their own restrictions. Check the current law of the relevant jurisdiction.

An NDA is not a court order, and breaking one is a contract matter rather than contempt of court. Its
existence also does not show that anything improper was concealed. Many protect ordinary commercial
or personal information.
