Indirect costs, formally called facilities and administrative (F&A) costs and informally called
overhead, are the real expenses of doing research that are not tied to a single project. Examples
include laboratory space, heating and power, research compliance offices, libraries and central
accounting.

For federal awards, a university negotiates an indirect cost rate with a designated federal agency
under government-wide rules found in 2 CFR Part 200. The rate is applied to a defined base of a
project's direct costs, and the resulting amount is paid to the institution in addition to the
direct costs. Private foundations and companies often pay a lower rate set by their own policy or by
negotiation. How the recovered money is shared among the central administration, schools,
departments and investigators is an internal budget decision that differs by institution.

Gifts normally carry no federally negotiated indirect charge, although many institutions levy a
smaller internal fee or assessment on gifts to help cover fundraising and administrative costs. This
difference gives researchers a reason to prefer gift funding, since more of each dollar reaches the
laboratory. Federal policy on indirect cost rates has been under active dispute in recent years, so
readers should check the current rules. Overhead is not profit. It is reimbursement for costs, and
institutions commonly state that it does not cover them fully.
