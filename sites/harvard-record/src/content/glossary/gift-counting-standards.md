Gift counting standards are the rules an institution uses to decide what counts toward its
fundraising totals and whom to credit. In the United States the most widely used reference is the
reporting standards published by the Council for Advancement and Support of Education (CASE), which
also runs a long-standing annual survey of giving to education.

Counting is different from accounting. Financial statements follow accounting principles and record
revenue in specific ways. Campaign reports follow counting rules and may include items the financial
statements treat differently, such as the full face value of multi-year pledges or certain bequest
intentions. Counting rules address what is eligible, how to value non-cash and deferred gifts, and
how to avoid counting the same money twice.

A key distinction is between legal (or hard) credit and recognition (or soft) credit. Hard credit
goes to the legal donor, the person or entity that actually made the payment and receives the tax
receipt. Soft credit acknowledges someone else associated with the gift: a spouse, the individual
behind a foundation or donor-advised fund, or an employee whose gift was matched. Soft credit
explains how a person can appear in records as associated with gifts they did not legally make.
Institutions adopt and adapt these standards voluntarily, so a reader should ask which rules a given
total follows.
