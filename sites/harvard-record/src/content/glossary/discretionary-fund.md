---
term: "Discretionary fund"
short: "A pool of money that a named officer, such as a president, dean or department chair, may spend at their own judgment within broad institutional rules."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Discretionary account", "President's fund", "Dean's fund", "Chair's discretionary fund"]
related: ["unrestricted-gift", "current-use-gift", "dean", "university-president", "decentralized-budgeting"]
---

A discretionary fund is money placed under the control of a particular officeholder rather than
assigned to a specific project. It is often built from unrestricted gifts, from the payout of an
endowment given for the officer's use, or from internal budget allocations. A dean might use it to
recruit a faculty member, host an event, support a new initiative or cover an unexpected expense.

Spending from a discretionary fund still has to follow the institution's financial policies, any
donor restrictions, and tax and audit rules. In practice, though, fewer people review an individual
decision than for a grant or a budget line. That is the purpose of such a fund: it lets a leader act
quickly. It also means that the main record of a decision may be an internal approval or an
expense report rather than a committee minute.

A discretionary fund is not a personal account. The officer controls spending in an official
capacity and the money belongs to the institution. When a record mentions one, the useful questions
are where the money came from, who approved the spending, and whether any donor terms applied.
