---
term: "Amended complaint"
short: "A revised version of a complaint that replaces the earlier one, filed to add or drop parties, claims or factual allegations."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["first amended complaint", "second amended complaint", "operative complaint"]
related: ["complaint-civil", "motion-to-dismiss", "allegation", "docket"]
---

An amended complaint is a new edition of the plaintiff's opening pleading. Plaintiffs amend to
correct errors, to respond to defects identified in a motion to dismiss, to add facts learned later,
or to change who is being sued and for what. Successive versions are numbered: first amended
complaint, second amended complaint, and so on.

In federal court a plaintiff may amend once without permission within a short period early in the
case. After that, amendment requires the other side's written consent or the court's leave, which
the rules say should be given freely when justice requires. Judges may refuse where amendment would
be futile, unduly delayed or unfairly prejudicial.

An amended complaint generally supersedes the one before it. The latest version is called the
operative complaint, and it is the one the defendant must answer and the court will test.
Allegations left out of the new version are no longer part of the case, although the earlier
document remains on the public docket.

This matters for anyone quoting a lawsuit. An allegation or a defendant that appears in the original
complaint may have been removed later, and the reason is usually not stated. Before relying on a
passage, check the docket for the most recent version and note which version is being cited.
