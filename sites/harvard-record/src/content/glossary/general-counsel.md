The general counsel is the senior lawyer employed by a university. The office of the general counsel
(often abbreviated OGC) is the in-house legal department, staffed by attorneys who specialize in
areas such as employment, research, real estate, tax and student affairs.

The general counsel usually reports to the president and has a direct line to the governing board.
The office advises on policy, reviews contracts and unusual gift agreements, responds to subpoenas
and government inquiries, supervises outside law firms, and guides the handling of complaints and
investigations. When a serious matter arises, the office commonly directs the preservation of
records and decides, with leadership, whether a review will be done internally or by an outside
firm.

The general counsel's client is the institution, not any individual employee, student or official.
That shapes everything the office does. Its advice is typically covered by attorney-client
privilege, which belongs to the institution and allows it to keep legal communications confidential.
As a result, much of what the office knows and writes is never made public unless the institution
chooses to waive privilege. The office is also not neutral in a dispute. Its duty is to protect the
institution's legal interests, which usually but not always align with full public disclosure.
