---
term: "Genetic information (legal definition)"
short: "In U.S. federal law, information about a person's genetic tests, the genetic tests of family members, and family medical history."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["gina", "hipaa", "covered-entity"]
---

Genetic information has a specific statutory meaning in the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination
Act. It includes information about an individual's genetic tests, the genetic tests of that
individual's family members, and the manifestation of a disease or disorder in family members, which
is to say family medical history. It also includes a request for or receipt of genetic services, and
participation in clinical research that includes genetic services, by the individual or a family
member.

A genetic test, for this purpose, is an analysis of human DNA, RNA, chromosomes, proteins or
metabolites that detects genotypes, mutations or chromosomal changes. Family history is included
because it works as a rough proxy for inherited risk. The HIPAA Privacy Rule was later amended to
state that genetic information is health information, and to prohibit most health plans from using
it for underwriting.

The legal definition is narrower than everyday usage in some ways and wider in others. It does not
include a person's sex or age. It does not include a disease the person already has, even one with a
genetic cause. It does include a relative's diagnosis, which many people would not think of as
genetic data about themselves. State laws often define the term differently, so the applicable
definition depends on which law is in play.
