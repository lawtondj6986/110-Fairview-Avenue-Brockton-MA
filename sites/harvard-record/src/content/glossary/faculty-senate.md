A faculty senate, council or assembly is the main organ of faculty participation in governance.
Forms vary. Some universities have a single senate for the whole institution, some include students
and staff in a university senate, and some have no central body and rely on the faculty of each
school meeting separately, often with an elected council that prepares its business.

These bodies act on matters within faculty authority, such as degree requirements, new programs,
academic calendars and standards for appointment. They also elect or nominate members of committees,
including those that hear grievances and conduct cases. Beyond formal powers, they serve as a forum.
Members can question the president, provost or dean, request information, pass resolutions, and
establish committees to study an issue. A vote of no confidence, the strongest expression available,
does not bind the governing board.

A faculty body's powers come from the institution's statutes and bylaws, and on most non-academic
matters its role is advisory. It typically has no authority to compel the administration to produce
documents or to overturn a decision about a gift or a personnel matter. Its minutes, reports and
resolutions can nonetheless be valuable records. They show what faculty were told, what they asked,
and when a concern first became a matter of open discussion within the institution.
