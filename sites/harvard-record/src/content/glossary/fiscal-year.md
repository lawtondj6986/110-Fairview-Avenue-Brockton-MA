---
term: "Fiscal year"
short: "The twelve-month period an organization uses for its budget, accounts and tax filings, which often does not match the calendar year."

A fiscal year is the accounting year an organization chooses. Many American universities use a
fiscal year that begins on July 1 and ends on June 30, which fits the academic calendar. The federal
government's fiscal year begins on October 1. A fiscal year is usually named for the calendar year
in which it ends, so a year running from July of one year to June of the next carries the later
year's number, though some organizations label it differently.

The choice matters whenever figures are compared. Annual fundraising totals, endowment returns,
budgets and Form 990 filings all follow the fiscal year. A Form 990 is labeled by the tax year in
which the fiscal year began, which can make a filing appear to be a year older than the period it
mostly covers.

A common confusion is to match a gift or payment to the wrong year. A pledge made in one fiscal year
may be paid in several later ones, and a campaign total may span many fiscal years. When a document
says "FY" followed by a year, check how the organization defines its year before lining it up with
calendar dates.
