---
term: "Emergency notification"
short: "The alert a college must send without delay once it confirms a significant emergency or dangerous situation that poses an immediate threat on campus."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["emergency alert", "mass notification"]
related: ["timely-warning", "clery-act", "annual-security-report"]
---

Emergency notification is the second alert duty under the Clery Act. An institution must notify the
campus community immediately upon confirming a significant emergency or dangerous situation that
involves an immediate threat to the health or safety of students or employees on campus. Examples
include an armed intruder, a fire, a gas leak, severe weather, or an outbreak of a serious
contagious illness.

Once officials confirm the situation, the institution must, without delay, decide what to say and
send the message, taking the safety of the community into account. The only permitted reason for
holding back is a professional judgment by responsible authorities that notifying would compromise
efforts to help a victim or to contain or respond to the emergency. The message can be directed to
the whole campus or to the affected segment. Institutions must describe their procedures in the
annual security report and test them at least once a year.

The trigger is broader than for a timely warning. A timely warning concerns Clery crimes that have
already been reported and pose a continuing threat. An emergency notification concerns any immediate
danger, criminal or not. If an institution sends an emergency notification about an event, it does
not have to issue a separate timely warning about the same circumstances, though it must provide
follow-up information as needed.
