---
term: "Restricted gift"
short: "A gift that the donor has limited to a particular purpose, time period or unit, which the institution is legally bound to honor once it accepts."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Donor-restricted gift", "Designated gift", "Restricted fund"]
related: ["unrestricted-gift", "donor-intent", "gift-agreement", "current-use-gift", "endowment"]
---

A restricted gift comes with a donor-imposed limit on how it may be used. The restriction may name a
purpose (a field of research, a scholarship, a building), a unit (a particular school, department or
laboratory) or a time (not to be spent before a certain date, or to be held permanently as
endowment).

Once an institution accepts a restricted gift, it must use the money within the restriction. It
tracks the money in a separate fund and reports on it in financial statements as net assets with
donor restrictions. Restrictions can be broad ("for the sciences") or narrow ("for the research of a
named professor"). A gift directed to a specific faculty member's work is still legally a gift to
the institution, which holds and administers the money, but in practice that faculty member usually
decides how it is spent within university rules.

A restriction limits purpose. It does not give the donor continuing control over decisions such as
hiring, admissions or research conclusions, and most gift policies say so. Changing a restriction
later generally requires the donor's consent or, if the donor is unavailable, a legal process under
state law. A restricted gift should also not be confused with a sponsored research award, which
carries deliverables and reporting duties.
