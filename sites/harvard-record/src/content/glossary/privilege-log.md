---
term: "Privilege log"
short: "A list of documents that a party has withheld from production on the ground of legal privilege, giving enough detail for the claim to be assessed."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["attorney-client privilege", "work product", "privilege claim"]
related: ["document-production", "discovery", "redaction", "subpoena"]
---

A privilege log accompanies a document production. When a party withholds or redacts a responsive
document because it is privileged, court rules require the party to say so expressly and to describe
the document in a way that lets others assess the claim without revealing the protected content. A
typical log entry gives the date, the author and recipients, the type of document, a brief
description of the subject, and the privilege asserted.

The two most common grounds are attorney-client privilege, which protects confidential
communications between a client and a lawyer made for the purpose of legal advice, and work product
protection, which covers material prepared in anticipation of litigation. The other side can
challenge an entry, and the judge can resolve the dispute, sometimes by reviewing the document
privately. Privilege belongs to the client, who may waive it.

Privilege is narrower than it may appear. It protects communications, not underlying facts, and
copying a lawyer on a message does not by itself make the message privileged. Communications made to
further a crime or fraud are not protected.

A privilege claim is not evidence of concealment. Seeking legal advice is ordinary, and logs in
large cases run to thousands of entries. The log is still informative: it shows who consulted
lawyers, when, and in general terms about what.
