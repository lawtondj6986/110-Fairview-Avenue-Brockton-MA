The federal Freedom of Information Act lets anyone request records from federal agencies, but it
lists nine exemptions that allow an agency to withhold some information. They cover, among other
things, classified national security material, information exempted by other statutes, confidential
commercial information, internal deliberations and legal advice, personal privacy, and certain law
enforcement records. A response letter or a redaction box usually cites the exemption by number,
such as "(b)(6)" for personal privacy.

An agency must release any part of a record that can reasonably be separated from the exempt part,
so most withholding is done by redaction rather than by holding back whole documents. Since a 2016
amendment, agencies are also expected to withhold only where they reasonably foresee harm from
release or where law prohibits disclosure. A requester can appeal a denial within the agency and
then sue in federal court.

An exemption is permission to withhold, not a finding that the hidden material is damaging. Privacy
exemptions often cover routine names and contact details. Exemptions also apply only to federal
agencies; state public records laws have their own, different lists.
