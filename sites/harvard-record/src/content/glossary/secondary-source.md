---
term: "Secondary source"
short: "An account that describes, analyzes or interprets events using primary sources or other evidence, written by someone who was not a direct participant."
category: "Records and sourcing"
related: ["primary-source", "tertiary-source", "reliability-grade", "attribution", "corroboration"]
---

A secondary source is one step removed from the events it describes. It reports on, summarizes or
interprets primary material. News articles, books, scholarly studies, documentaries and analytical
reports are the common forms.

Secondary sources do work that raw documents cannot. A good one gathers scattered records,
interviews people, supplies context, and explains what the material means. Their quality varies
widely, and it can be judged by a few features: whether the author identifies sources, whether
documents are quoted or linked so that claims can be checked, whether the outlet has a practice of
correcting errors, and whether the people discussed were given a chance to respond. This site
distinguishes between reporting that closely tracks primary documents or records on-the-record
statements, and broader analysis that is used for background only.

A secondary source can be more reliable than a primary one, for example when careful reporting
corrects a self-serving official statement. It can also add errors of its own through paraphrase,
compression or misreading. When several articles repeat the same claim, they often trace back to one
original report. Counting them as independent confirmation is a mistake. Where the underlying
document is available, it should be read and cited directly.
