term: "Inquiry (research misconduct)"
short: "The preliminary stage of a research misconduct proceeding, in which an institution gathers information to decide whether an allegation warrants a full investigation."

Federal rules for research funded by the Public Health Service divide misconduct proceedings into
stages. After an initial assessment of whether an allegation falls within the definition and is
specific enough, the institution conducts an inquiry. An inquiry is a fact-gathering step,
typically involving securing research records, interviewing the people concerned and reviewing the
evidence, to decide whether there is enough to justify an investigation. It does not decide whether
misconduct occurred.

If the inquiry finds that an investigation is warranted, the institution must notify the Office of
Research Integrity and begin a formal investigation, which weighs the evidence and reaches findings.
Respondents are informed and given an opportunity to comment at each stage. Many institutions apply
the same staged process to all research, whatever its funding. The federal rules were revised
recently, and timelines and terms in older documents may differ, so check the current text.

An inquiry is not a finding, and an investigation is not yet a conclusion. Reporting that a
researcher was "under investigation" when only an inquiry was opened overstates the record.
Proceedings are confidential, so news of them usually comes from a party or a later published
finding.
