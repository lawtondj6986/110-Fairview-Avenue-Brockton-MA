---
term: "Prospect research"
short: "The work of gathering and analyzing mostly public information about potential donors to estimate their wealth, interests and likelihood of giving."
category: "Fundraising and development"

Prospect research is a specialist function within a development office. Researchers collect
information about people and organizations who might give, and turn it into profiles and ratings
that gift officers use.

Sources are mainly public: property records, securities filings, foundation tax returns, business
registries, news archives, biographical directories and records of gifts to other charities.
Institutions also buy wealth screening services, which match a list of names against commercial data
and return estimated ratings in bulk. Researchers add what the institution already knows, such as
degrees, past giving, event attendance and notes from staff contacts. The output is typically a
written profile for a senior official before a meeting, or a rating stored in the donor database.

Prospect research is primarily about capacity and interest, not character. A profile can describe a
person's assets and philanthropy in detail while saying little about how the wealth was made or
about adverse history, unless the institution asks for that separately as due diligence. Research
also produces estimates, not facts. Wealth held privately or abroad is hard to see, and ratings can
be wrong by a wide margin. The field's professional body, Apra, publishes ethical guidance on
accuracy, confidentiality and lawful collection.
