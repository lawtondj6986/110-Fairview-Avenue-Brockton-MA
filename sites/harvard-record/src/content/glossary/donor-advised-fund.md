term: "Donor-advised fund"
short: "A giving account held by a sponsoring charity. The donor gets a tax deduction when contributing and may later recommend, but not legally direct, grants from the account."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["DAF"]

A donor-advised fund is a separately identified account at a sponsoring organization, which is
itself a public charity. Sponsors include community foundations and charitable arms of financial
firms. A donor makes an irrevocable contribution to the sponsor and can take the tax deduction at
that point. The donor, or people the donor names, then keeps advisory privileges: they may recommend
investments and grants to operating charities over time.

Legally the sponsor owns and controls the assets and could decline a recommendation, though sponsors
usually follow them when the recipient is an eligible charity. The receiving charity records the
grant as coming from the sponsor. The donor may be identified in the grant letter or may ask to
remain anonymous. Unlike a private foundation, an individual account has no annual payout
requirement under current federal law, and the sponsor's tax return reports its funds in aggregate.

For readers tracing gifts, this structure matters. A grant listed from a large sponsoring
organization tells little about whose money it was, and public filings alone often cannot connect a
specific account to a specific gift. A donor-advised fund is also not a bank account: once
contributed, the money cannot be returned to the donor or used to pay for personal benefits.
