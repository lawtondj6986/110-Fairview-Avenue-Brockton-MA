---
term: "Quasi-endowment"
short: "Money that an institution's board chooses to invest and manage like an endowment, even though no donor required it to be kept permanently."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Board-designated endowment", "Funds functioning as endowment", "Quasi-endowed fund"]

A quasi-endowment is a fund that behaves like an endowment by the board's choice. The money might
come from unrestricted gifts, from a bequest with no conditions, or from operating surpluses. The
board directs that it be invested alongside true endowment funds and that only a regular payout be
spent, so that it supports the institution over a long period.

Because the restriction comes from the board and not from a donor, the board can reverse it and
spend the principal. Accounting standards therefore report quasi-endowment as net assets without
donor restrictions, often described as board-designated. A true endowment, by contrast, is created
by donor restriction and its original value generally cannot be spent at the board's discretion.

The term matters when reading endowment figures. A headline endowment total often combines donor
endowments and quasi-endowment, and the share of each varies widely between institutions. Gifts
described as endowed in announcements may sometimes sit in quasi-endowment, and the reverse can also
happen. The notes to audited financial statements usually show the split.
