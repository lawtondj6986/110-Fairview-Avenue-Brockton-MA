---
term: "Quid pro quo harassment"
short: "Harassment in which a person with authority conditions a job or educational benefit on submission to unwelcome sexual conduct."
category: "Law and compliance"
related: ["hostile-environment", "title-ix", "title-vii", "quid-pro-quo-contribution"]
---

Quid pro quo is Latin for "something for something." In harassment law it describes a situation
where a person with power over another's employment or education makes a benefit depend on
submitting to unwelcome sexual conduct. The benefit might be a grade, a recommendation, a research
position, funding, a promotion, or simply keeping a place in a program.

The condition can be stated outright or implied by conduct. A single incident is enough, which
distinguishes this category from hostile environment harassment, where severity or repetition
usually matters. The 2020 Title IX regulations define it as an employee of the school conditioning
an aid, benefit or service on participation in unwelcome sexual conduct. Title VII applies a similar
idea to supervisors in the workplace.

In universities the concept matters because of steep dependencies. Graduate students and
postdoctoral researchers often rely on one supervisor for funding, authorship and references, so
pressure need not be explicit to be effective.

The term should not be confused with a quid pro quo contribution in tax law, which is a payment to a
charity partly in exchange for goods or services, or with the general use of the phrase to mean any
exchange of favors.
