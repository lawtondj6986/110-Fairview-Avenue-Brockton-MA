News reports use a small set of standard phrases to record what happened when a subject was asked to
respond. Each has a specific meaning.

"Declined to comment" means the person or office was reached and chose not to answer. "No comment"
is the same thing in the subject's own words. "Did not respond to a request for comment" means a
request was sent and no reply came by the time of publication, which may reflect a short deadline, a
wrong address or a deliberate choice. "Could not be reached" means the reporter was unable to make
contact. A responsible report specifies which occurred and, ideally, how and when the request was
made.

Institutions decline to comment for many reasons unrelated to the truth of the matter. These include
pending litigation or investigation, legal advice, privacy obligations concerning students and
employees, confidentiality terms in agreements, and a general policy of not discussing donors or
personnel.

Silence therefore has no evidentiary weight in either direction. It does not confirm a claim, and it
does not rebut one. What it does establish is that the subject had an opportunity to respond. A
careful account reports the non-response in neutral terms and draws no inference from it. See
`/methodology/` for this site's practice.
