term: "Responsible employee (mandatory reporter)"

A responsible employee is a member of staff who must tell the Title IX coordinator when they learn
of possible sexual harassment or sexual violence. The term comes from federal Title IX guidance
issued before 2020, which treated a school as being on notice once such an employee knew of an
incident. Many campus policies use the plainer label mandatory reporter.

The 2020 regulations changed the federal rule for colleges. A college is now deemed to have notice
when the Title IX coordinator, or an official with authority to take corrective measures, has actual
knowledge. Colleges remain free to require more employees to report, and most still designate
faculty, supervisors, coaches and residence staff. The designation matters to anyone deciding whom
to tell: a disclosure to a responsible employee will reach the institution, while a disclosure to a
confidential resource will not.

Three similar terms are often confused. A campus security authority has a duty under the Clery Act
to report crimes for statistical purposes, usually without names. A mandated reporter under state
law must report suspected abuse of children, and sometimes of other vulnerable people, to state
authorities. A responsible employee reports internally, to the school. One person can hold all three
roles at once.
