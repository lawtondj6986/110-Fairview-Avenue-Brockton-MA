---
term: "Authenticity versus accuracy"
short: "Authenticity asks whether a document is genuinely what it purports to be; accuracy asks whether what the document says is true."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["genuine versus true"]
related: ["provenance", "primary-source", "metadata", "official-record", "corroboration"]
---

Authenticity and accuracy are separate questions about a document. A document is authentic if it is
genuine: it was created by the person or body it appears to come from, at about the time indicated,
and has not been altered. A document is accurate if its contents correspond to the facts.

The two can come apart in either direction. An authentic email may contain a false statement, an
exaggeration, a joke, a plan that was never carried out, or a mistaken belief honestly held. An
authentic official report may contain errors. Conversely, a forged or doctored document may recite
some facts that are true. Establishing authenticity therefore settles only the first question.
Authenticity is assessed through provenance, metadata, consistency with other known records, and
confirmation by the author or the releasing body. Accuracy is assessed by corroboration from
independent evidence.

The distinction governs how careful writers describe documents. An authentic message supports the
statement that its author wrote those words on that date. It does not, without more, support the
statement that the events described took place. Writing "a message says X" is different from writing
"X happened". Official confirmation that a set of released documents is genuine is likewise not an
endorsement of every assertion made by the people whose words appear in them.
