---
term: "Ranking member"
short: "The most senior member of the minority party on a congressional committee or subcommittee, who leads that party's work on the panel."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["ranking minority member", "vice chair", "committee chair"]
related: ["congressional-oversight-letter", "subpoena", "document-production"]
---

Each committee of the U.S. House and Senate is led by a chair from the majority party. The ranking
member is the chair's counterpart from the minority party: the minority member who holds the top
position on that committee, chosen under the party's own rules, which weigh seniority among other
factors. Subcommittees have ranking members as well. A few committees use the title vice chair for
the same role.

The ranking member directs the minority's committee staff, speaks for the minority at hearings,
negotiates with the chair, and often issues separate statements, letters and staff reports. When
party control of the chamber changes, the ranking member is commonly in line to become chair.

The distinction matters for understanding the force of an inquiry. Under most committee rules, the
power to schedule hearings, call votes and issue subpoenas rests with the chair or the committee
majority. A ranking member acting alone can request documents and can release what is received, but
generally cannot compel production. A minority staff report is likewise the work of one party's
staff, not a report adopted by the committee.

None of this bears on reliability. Documents obtained and published by a ranking member are assessed
on their own terms. The label identifies who is asking and with what authority.
