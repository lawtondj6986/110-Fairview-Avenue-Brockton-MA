A biobank, also called a biorepository, is an organized collection of biological samples kept for
research, together with information about the people who gave them. Samples may include blood,
saliva, tissue, cell lines and extracted DNA. The linked data may include age, sex, medical history,
test results and genetic data. Biobanks range from a single laboratory's freezer to national
collections with hundreds of thousands of participants.

The value of a biobank lies in the link between sample and data and in consistent handling. A
well-run biobank tracks each sample from collection through storage and distribution, documents the
consent under which it was given, and controls who may receive samples or data. Outside researchers
usually apply for access and sign agreements that restrict use and forbid attempts to identify
participants. Samples are typically coded, with the key to identities held separately.

A biobank is not the same as a clinical laboratory, which tests samples for patient care, or a
commercial cell or sperm bank that stores material for the depositor's own use. What a biobank may
do with a sample depends on the consent given. Broad consent to future research is common, but it is
not unlimited.
