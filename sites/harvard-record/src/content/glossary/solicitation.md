Solicitation is the request itself: the point at which an institution asks a prospect for a specific
gift. In the standard fundraising cycle it follows cultivation and precedes stewardship.

For large gifts, the request is planned in detail. Staff decide the amount, the purpose, who will
ask (often a dean, the president, a trustee or a faculty member the donor respects) and when. The
request is usually made in person and followed by a written proposal. Negotiation over amount,
timing, terms and recognition may follow, ending in a gift agreement or in a decision by the donor
not to give. For small gifts, solicitation means mass appeals by mail, email, phone or online.

Charitable solicitation is also a regulated activity. Many U.S. states require organizations that
ask the public for donations to register and file reports, though the rules and their exemptions
(which often cover educational institutions) differ from state to state. In ordinary development
usage, solicitation has no negative meaning, unlike in some other legal contexts. A record showing
that someone was "solicited" means only that they were asked. Whether an institution asked a person
for money, or the person offered it unprompted, can still matter for understanding who initiated a
relationship.
