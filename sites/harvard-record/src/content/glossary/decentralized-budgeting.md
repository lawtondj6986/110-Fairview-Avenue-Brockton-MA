Decentralized budgeting treats each school or major unit of a university as financially
self-supporting. The traditional phrase "every tub on its own bottom" (a tub being a barrel that
must stand unaided) expresses the idea. The modern technical name is responsibility center
management.

Under this model a school receives the tuition its students pay, the research funding and indirect
cost recovery its faculty bring in, the income from its own endowment funds and the gifts made to
it. From that revenue it pays its salaries and operating costs and contributes to central services,
usually through a formula-based assessment. The dean is accountable for balancing the school's
budget. Universities sit at different points on this spectrum, and many use hybrids.

The model creates strong incentives for each unit to raise its own money, and it makes deans and
even individual research centers into fundraisers. It also distributes administrative authority and
record keeping. Schools may have their own development offices, appointment procedures and policies,
and central officials may have limited visibility into them. The result is that questions such as
who accepted a gift or who approved an affiliation can have different answers in different parts of
one university. Decentralization explains how that can happen. It does not settle whether central
leaders should have known.
