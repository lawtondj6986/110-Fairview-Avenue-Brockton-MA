A development office is the part of a university responsible for raising money from private sources:
individuals, foundations and companies. Many institutions use the broader name advancement, which
usually combines fundraising with alumni relations and sometimes communications.

In practice the office employs gift officers who each manage a list of potential donors, researchers
who assess those donors, and staff who record gifts, issue receipts and report back to donors on how
money was used. At large universities there is often a central office plus separate fundraising
teams inside individual schools. The central office usually sets policy, runs the shared donor
database and handles the largest gifts. The school teams work with deans and faculty on their own
priorities. How much control the center has over the schools varies widely.

A development office does not normally decide how a university spends its money, and it does not
negotiate research grants or contracts, which go through a separate sponsored programs office. It is
also not the only route by which donors meet a university. Faculty members, deans and presidents
often have their own relationships with donors, and those contacts are not always recorded in
development office systems.
