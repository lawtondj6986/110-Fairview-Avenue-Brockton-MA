A start-up package is the set of resources offered to a faculty recruit, especially in laboratory
sciences and medicine. It commonly includes a sum of research money to be spent over a few years,
laboratory and office space, major equipment, salary support for staff or students, and sometimes
summer salary or relief from teaching. The terms are usually set out in an offer letter.

The money can come from several sources: a school's central funds, a department's budget, the
payout from endowed funds, unrestricted gifts or a dean's discretionary fund. In some fields the
package is large enough that recruiting a single scientist is a significant budget decision for the
school. Retention packages offered to keep a faculty member who has an outside offer work in a
similar way.

A start-up package is not a grant. It is internal money and is not peer reviewed in the way a
federal award is, though it is subject to institutional spending rules. It is also meant to be
temporary: the recruit is expected to win outside funding to sustain the laboratory. When a gift is
described as supporting a recruitment, it may be one component of a start-up package rather than its
whole.
