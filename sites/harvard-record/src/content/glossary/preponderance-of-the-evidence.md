---
term: "Preponderance of the evidence"
short: "A standard of proof met when the evidence shows that a claim is more likely true than not true."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["more likely than not"]
related: ["clear-and-convincing-evidence", "burden-of-proof", "civil-vs-criminal"]
---

Preponderance of the evidence is the lowest of the three common standards of proof. The
decision-maker asks whether, taking all the evidence together, the claim is more likely true than
not. It is often described as anything more than a 50 percent likelihood, though no actual
calculation is made.

It is the ordinary standard in civil lawsuits. Most colleges also use it in student and employee
disciplinary proceedings, including Title IX cases. Federal guidance issued in 2011 told schools to
use it for sexual violence complaints. The 2020 Title IX regulations instead let each school choose
between this standard and the higher clear and convincing standard, provided the school applies the
same one to complaints against students and against employees.

The standard is about the weight and persuasiveness of evidence, not the number of witnesses or
documents. A finding under this standard also means much less than a criminal conviction, which
requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Someone can be found responsible in a campus process, or
liable in a civil suit, on evidence that would not support a criminal charge. The reverse inference
is also wrong: the absence of a prosecution does not show that a civil or campus finding was
mistaken.
