Biomedical informatics is the study and practice of managing and using information in health care
and biomedical research. It covers electronic health records, systems that support clinical
decisions, standards that let different systems exchange data, methods for analyzing large sets of
patient records, and the combination of clinical data with genetic and other laboratory data.

The field has several branches. Clinical informatics focuses on information systems used in patient
care. Public health informatics deals with population data such as disease surveillance.
Translational bioinformatics connects molecular data to clinical questions. University departments
and centers of biomedical informatics typically do a mix of method development, analysis of health
data, and training.

The term is often confused with bioinformatics. The two overlap, particularly where genomic data
meet medical records, but bioinformatics is centered on molecular sequence data and biomedical
informatics on clinical and health data. Because the field works with records about people, privacy
rules, de-identification methods, data use agreements and ethics review are a routine part of the
work. A project's place in an informatics department does not by itself indicate whether it involved
identifiable patient data.
