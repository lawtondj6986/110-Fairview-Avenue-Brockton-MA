A denial is a statement rejecting a claim. Denials come in several forms and should be read closely.
A specific denial addresses the claim directly, as in "I never attended that meeting". A general
denial rejects an allegation in broad terms without addressing the particulars. A partial denial
contests some elements and leaves others unaddressed. A statement that sounds like a denial but,
read literally, does not contradict the claim is sometimes called a non-denial denial. Declining to
comment is not a denial, and it is not an admission either.

Careful reporting records a denial accurately. It notes who made it, whether it came from the person
or a representative, when, and exactly what it covered. It quotes the wording where precision
matters, and it places the denial close to the claim it answers. This site's rules require that
denials and responses be included wherever a person or institution has made one.

A denial is a fact about what someone said. It does not settle the truth of the matter in either
direction. It neither disproves a documented claim nor counts as evidence of guilt. Where a claim
rests on weak sourcing and is specifically denied, a careful writer may conclude it should not be
repeated at all. Denials in legal pleadings are formal positions in litigation and are read in that
light.
