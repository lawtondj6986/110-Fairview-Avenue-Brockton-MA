In the 2020 Title IX regulations, a formal complaint is a document filed by a complainant, or signed
by the Title IX coordinator, that alleges sexual harassment against a named respondent and asks the
school to investigate. It is the step that starts the school's grievance process.

Filing triggers specific duties. The school must give both parties written notice of the
allegations, investigate, let each side review the evidence, and reach a written determination
through the procedures the regulations describe. A school must dismiss a formal complaint from the
Title IX process if the alleged conduct would not meet the regulatory definition of sexual
harassment even if proven, or did not occur in the school's program or activity in the United
States. The school may still address the conduct under another policy.

A formal complaint is different from a report or disclosure. A person can tell the school what
happened and receive supportive measures without filing one. This is why the number of formal
complaints at an institution is always lower than the number of reports, often by a wide margin.

It is also different from a complaint that opens a civil lawsuit, and from a complaint filed with a
federal agency about the school itself.
