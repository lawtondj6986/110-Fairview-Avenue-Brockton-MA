Net assets are what remains when an organization's liabilities are subtracted from its assets.
Under the accounting standards that apply to American nonprofits, net assets are split into two
classes. Net assets with donor restrictions are the portion a donor has limited, for example to a
particular purpose, to a future period, or to be held in perpetuity with only the investment return
available for spending.

Before a change in the accounting standards that took effect in the late 2010s, nonprofits reported
three classes: unrestricted, temporarily restricted and permanently restricted. Older financial
statements and many articles still use those terms. The two restricted classes were combined into
the current single class, with notes that explain the kinds of restriction.

When a restriction is satisfied, for example when money is spent on the stated purpose, the amount
is released from restriction and moves to net assets without donor restrictions. Only a donor can
create a donor restriction. Limits a board places on its own money, such as designating it to act
as an endowment, do not count here. A large restricted balance therefore does not show what the
institution is free to spend.
