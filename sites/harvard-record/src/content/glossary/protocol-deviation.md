---
term: "Protocol deviation"
short: "Any departure from the approved research protocol; serious ones are often called violations and must be reported to the ethics board."
category: "Research ethics"
aliases: ["protocol violation", "noncompliance"]
related: ["research-protocol", "institutional-review-board", "research-misconduct", "informed-consent"]
---

A protocol deviation is a departure from the study plan that an ethics board approved. Examples
include enrolling someone who did not meet the eligibility criteria, using an outdated consent form,
missing a required safety test, collecting a sample the protocol did not provide for, or sharing
data in a way the protocol did not describe.

Institutions classify deviations by seriousness. Minor deviations that do not affect participants'
safety or rights, or the integrity of the data, are usually logged and reported periodically.
Significant ones, sometimes called protocol violations, must be reported promptly. The board may
require corrective action, re-consent of participants, additional monitoring, or suspension of the
study. U.S. regulations require institutions to have procedures for reporting serious or continuing
noncompliance to federal authorities for the research those regulations cover. Terminology varies
between institutions and sponsors.

A deviation is not automatically research misconduct. The federal definition of misconduct is
limited to fabrication, falsification and plagiarism. Most deviations are errors or lapses handled
as compliance matters. Conducting human research with no approved protocol at all is a more serious
form of noncompliance than deviating from one. Deliberately concealing deviations or falsifying
study records can cross into misconduct.
