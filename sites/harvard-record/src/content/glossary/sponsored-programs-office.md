term: "Sponsored programs office"
short: "The university office that reviews and submits research proposals, accepts awards on the institution's behalf and oversees compliance with sponsors' terms."

A sponsored programs office administers externally funded research. It is the institutional
counterpart to the principal investigator, handling the legal and financial side of grants and
contracts.

Before an award, the office checks each proposal for compliance with the sponsor's rules and the
institution's policies, confirms the budget and indirect cost rate, and submits it under an
authorized signature, since investigators generally may not commit the institution themselves. After
an award, the office or a companion post-award unit negotiates terms, sets up accounts, monitors
spending, files financial reports and closes out the project. It also confirms that required
approvals are in place, such as review of human subjects research and disclosure of financial
conflicts of interest. Some universities have one central office, while others split the work among
central, school and department levels.

The office deals with sponsored awards and not with gifts. Money classified as a gift goes through
the development office and gift accounting, and usually bypasses the reviews that sponsored programs
staff perform. Classification of a given payment is therefore a decision with consequences, and
institutions normally have written criteria and a process for doubtful cases. A search of sponsored
programs records will show a funder's grants and contracts. It will not show gifts from the same
source, and the reverse is true of gift records.
