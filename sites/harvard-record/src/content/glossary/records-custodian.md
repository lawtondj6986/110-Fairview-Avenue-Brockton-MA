The term custodian has a few related meanings. Under public records laws, a records custodian or
records access officer is the official responsible for maintaining an agency's records and
responding to requests. Requests are often addressed to that office, and response letters are signed
by it. In court, a custodian of records may provide a sworn statement that documents are true copies
of records kept in the regular course of business, which helps them be admitted as evidence.

In discovery, a custodian is any person whose email, files and devices are collected and searched.
Lawyers negotiate a list of custodians and search terms, and productions are often organized by
custodian. Knowing who the custodians were tells a reader whose files were searched and, by
implication, whose were not.

A custodian keeps records but is not necessarily their author or the person who knows what they
mean. A statement that "no responsive records were found" depends on how and where the custodian
searched. A missing document may sit with a custodian who was not included, or have been disposed of
under a retention schedule.
