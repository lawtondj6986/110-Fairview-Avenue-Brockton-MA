Access control is the technology that enforces a campus access policy. Most universities issue each
member an identification card containing a magnetic stripe, chip or wireless credential. Card
readers at doors check the credential against a database of permissions and unlock the door if entry
is allowed.

Permissions are assigned by status and by request. An administrator adds a person to an access group
for a building, floor or laboratory, usually on the authority of a department or laboratory head,
and sets an expiration date where the appointment is temporary. The system typically writes a log
entry each time a card is presented, noting the card, the door, the time and whether entry was
granted.

Card records are useful evidence, but they are limited. A log shows that a card was used, not who
carried it. It usually records entries and not exits. It does not capture people who walk in behind
a cardholder or who are admitted as guests. Logs are also kept only for a period defined by policy
or system capacity, which can be short, so older data may no longer exist. The fact that a person
was issued a card shows that someone authorized access. It does not show how often the card was used
or whether the person visited at all.
