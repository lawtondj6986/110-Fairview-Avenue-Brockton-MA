---
term: "Gene"
short: "A stretch of DNA that carries the instructions for making a functional product, usually a protein."
category: "Genetics and biomedical research"

A gene is a segment of DNA that carries the instructions for making a functional product. For most
genes the product is a protein, a molecule that does work in the cell. Some genes instead produce
RNA molecules that have jobs of their own. Humans have about 20,000 protein-coding genes.

A typical human gene is made of exons, the parts that code for the protein, separated by introns,
which are removed before the protein is made. Nearby regulatory sequences control when, where and
how strongly the gene is active. People carry two copies of most genes, one inherited from each
parent. Different versions of the same gene are called alleles.

Popular writing often speaks of "a gene for" a trait or a disease. That phrasing is accurate for a
limited set of conditions in which a change in a single gene has a large effect. It is misleading
for most common traits, such as height, or for most common diseases, which are influenced by many
genes acting together with environment. Everyone has the same set of genes. What differs between
people is the particular variants they carry within and around those genes.
