Public record has a legal meaning and a looser everyday meaning. In law, a public record is a
document made or received by a government body in connection with public business that people are
entitled to inspect under an open records statute. The federal government and every U.S. state have
such laws. Each defines which bodies are covered and which records are exempt, for instance to
protect personal privacy, ongoing investigations or legally privileged material. Court files are
generally open under separate rules.

In everyday use, "on the public record" means that information has been openly published or stated,
so that anyone can find and cite it. This site uses "the public record" in that broader sense: the
body of documents and reporting available to any reader. Its rule of relying on public sources only
means that every claim should be traceable by the reader.

Several distinctions follow. The records of private institutions, including private universities,
are generally not public records in the legal sense, although the institutions may publish reports
voluntarily or file documents with regulators that become public. A leaked document is available but
was not lawfully released. And the public record is always incomplete. That no public record of
something exists is a statement about the record, not about the world.
