term: "Prevalence versus reported incidents"
short: "Prevalence is the share of a population that experienced something in a given period. Reported incidents are the cases that reached an official channel. The two measure different things."

Prevalence is the proportion of people in a defined group who experienced a particular thing during
a defined period. In campus safety it is estimated from anonymous surveys, for example the
percentage of undergraduates who experienced nonconsensual sexual contact since enrolling. Reported
incidents are counts taken from administrative records: Clery Act statistics, police logs, or the
number of reports received by a Title IX office.

The two differ in unit, source and scope. Prevalence counts people, while incident data count events
or reports. Surveys reach experiences that were never disclosed to anyone in authority. Official
counts include only what was reported, and each source adds its own filters, such as the Clery Act's
geographic limits and offense definitions. Research has consistently found that most sexual assaults
are not reported to police or campus officials, so reported counts are far below what surveys imply.

For this reason the two figures cannot be used to check each other, and neither is wrong because it
differs from the other. A low reported count alongside a high survey estimate may indicate barriers
to reporting. A rising reported count may indicate growing trust in the reporting system and not
more misconduct.
