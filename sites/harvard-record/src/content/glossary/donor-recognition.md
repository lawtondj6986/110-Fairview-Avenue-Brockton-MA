Donor recognition is how an institution acknowledges a gift beyond the formal receipt. It ranges
from a name in an annual list of donors to a building bearing the donor's name.

Common forms include honor rolls published in reports or online, giving societies with membership
levels tied to gift size, plaques and donor walls, named rooms, funds and positions, announcements
and press releases, and invitations to events with institutional leaders. The form of recognition
for a significant gift is usually set out in the gift agreement. Recognition policies define what
each level of giving earns and who approves it.

Recognition is not a legal entitlement beyond what an agreement provides, and tax rules distinguish
simple acknowledgment from benefits with real market value. It is also not the same as an
appointment or title. A place on a donor list does not make a person a member of the university
community, and it confers no right to use campus facilities. The public side of recognition matters
for another reason. Being seen as a supporter of a respected university carries social value, and
that value does not depend on the size of the gift. A reader should separate recognition the
institution chose to give from associations a donor claimed for themselves.
