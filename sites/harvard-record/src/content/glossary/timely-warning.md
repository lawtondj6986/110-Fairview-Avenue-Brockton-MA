A timely warning is one of two alert duties in the Clery Act. An institution must issue one when a
crime covered by the act is reported to campus security authorities or local police, occurred within
the institution's Clery geography, and is considered by the institution to represent a serious or
continuing threat to students and employees. The warning must be issued in a way that reaches the
community promptly and helps people protect themselves.

The institution decides case by case whether the threat test is met. Relevant factors include the
nature of the crime and whether a suspect is still at large. The regulations set no fixed deadline,
but the warning should go out as soon as the pertinent information is available. Warnings usually
describe the type of crime, the time and general location, and safety advice. They must not include
the names of victims.

A timely warning differs from an emergency notification, which covers any immediate danger and not
only crimes. The absence of a warning also does not mean no crime was reported. Many reported crimes
are judged not to present a continuing threat, for example when a suspect is already in custody or
the report concerns an incident long past.
