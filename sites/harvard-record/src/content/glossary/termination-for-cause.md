---
term: "Termination for cause"
short: "Ending an employee's or faculty member's appointment because of misconduct or serious failure to perform, as opposed to ending it for budget reasons or at the close of a term."
category: "University governance"
aliases: ["Dismissal for cause", "Adequate cause", "Revocation of tenure", "Dismissal of tenured faculty"]
related: ["tenure", "faculty-sanctions", "faculty-handbook", "faculty-conduct-committee", "separation-agreement", "administrative-leave"]
---

Termination for cause means dismissal based on the person's own conduct or performance. For tenured
faculty it is the main way an appointment without limit of time can be ended against the person's
will, apart from financial exigency or the closing of a program. Faculty handbooks usually define
what counts as adequate cause, such as serious professional misconduct, research misconduct or
persistent neglect of duty.

Policies modeled on long-standing guidance from the American Association of University Professors
call for written charges, a hearing before a faculty committee with the right to counsel and to
present evidence, a record of the proceedings, and a decision by the governing board after
considering the committee's report. Institutions vary in how closely they follow that model.
Dismissal of tenured faculty through the full process is uncommon.

Many careers end in other ways that are not terminations for cause: resignation, retirement, a
negotiated separation agreement, non-renewal of a fixed-term contract or a sanction short of
dismissal. A departure announced as a resignation can follow the start of a dismissal process, and a
reader should not assume either way without a document that says which occurred.
