---
term: "Major gift"
short: "A gift large enough, by an institution's own threshold, to be sought through personal contact by a gift officer instead of mass appeals."
category: "Fundraising and development"

A major gift is a gift above a size that the institution has chosen as the point where individual
attention is justified. There is no standard threshold. A small college and a large research
university may set figures that differ by an order of magnitude or more, and an institution may
raise its figure over time.

What defines the category in practice is the method. Smaller gifts are raised through annual fund
mailings, email, phone programs and online giving. Major gifts are raised one donor at a time, by a
gift officer who meets the prospect, learns their interests, involves faculty or deans, and presents
a tailored proposal. The process can take months or years. Most institutions report that a small
number of large donors account for the bulk of dollars raised, which is why major gift work receives
a large share of staff and leadership time.

"Major gift" is an internal classification, not a public or legal term. It says nothing about
restrictions, purpose or the donor's standing. It should also be distinguished from principal gift,
a higher tier that many institutions use for their very largest donors, and from lead gift, which
describes a gift's role in a campaign and not simply its size.
