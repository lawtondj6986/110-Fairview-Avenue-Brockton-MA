A determination letter is the IRS's written recognition that an organization is exempt from federal
income tax. For a charity it typically confirms that the organization is described in section
501(c)(3) and states whether it is classified as a public charity or a private foundation, which
determines how donations are treated and which further rules apply.

Organizations usually receive the letter after applying, and many keep it on file to show donors and
grantmakers. The IRS also maintains a public online search tool where a reader can check an
organization's exemption status, find copies of some determination letters, see whether exemption
has been automatically revoked for failing to file, and locate recent Form 990 filings.

A determination letter shows status at the time it was issued. It does not show that the
organization is well run, that a particular gift was deductible, or that the organization still
holds the same classification today. Some organizations, such as churches and certain units of
government, can be exempt without one. Universities that are public bodies may rely on a different
legal basis for exemption.
