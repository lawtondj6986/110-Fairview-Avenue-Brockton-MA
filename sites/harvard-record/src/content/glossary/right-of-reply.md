---
term: "Right of reply"
short: "The principle that a person or organization facing criticism or allegations should have a fair opportunity to respond, and that the response be reported."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["opportunity to respond", "request for comment", "right of response"]
related: ["denial", "correction", "attribution", "on-the-record", "defamation"]
---

Right of reply is the practice of giving the subject of a critical report the chance to answer
before publication, and of including the answer. Journalism ethics codes treat it as a matter of
fairness and of accuracy, because the subject may have information that changes the story. In
practice it means contacting the person or organization, describing the claims specifically enough
that they can respond, allowing reasonable time, and reporting the substance of any reply, or the
fact that none was given.

In the United States this is an ethical norm, not a general legal duty. Courts have held that the
government cannot compel a newspaper to print a reply. Some other countries do provide a statutory
right of reply or correction in certain media. Many publications also offer a route after
publication, such as letters, corrections or an added statement.

For a reference work that draws on published sources, the equivalent duty is to include responses
and denials that are on the record, and to update entries when a new response appears. This site's
editorial rules require that. Reporting a reply does not mean endorsing it, and it does not require
giving every statement equal weight regardless of evidence. "Did not respond" and "declined to
comment" also carry no implication of admission.
