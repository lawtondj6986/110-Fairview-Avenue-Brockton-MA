---
term: "Mirror site"
short: "A website that hosts copies of documents or pages originally published somewhere else, often by an unofficial third party."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["mirror", "unofficial archive", "document repository"]
related: ["provenance", "archive-snapshot", "link-rot", "document-release", "tertiary-source"]
---

A mirror site reproduces content that was first published elsewhere. Mirrors are set up for
convenience, to preserve material that might be taken down, or to make a large release easier to
search. Some are run by libraries or established organizations with stated procedures. Many are run
by individuals or anonymous groups. Crowd-edited wikis and searchable databases built on top of
released documents are closely related.

Mirrors can be useful for finding material. Their weakness is provenance. A reader usually has no
means of confirming that a mirrored file is complete and unaltered, that it came from the release it
claims to come from, or that the labels, summaries and search indexes added by the operator are
correct. Mirrors may also republish material that the original source withdrew or corrected, or that
exposes private individuals.

The usual scholarly practice is to treat a mirror as a finding aid and to cite the original
publication. This site follows that rule: unofficial mirrors and wikis are not cited as sources, and
documents from official releases are cited through the original or through established reporting
until the original can be linked. A mirror differs from a web archive snapshot, which is a
timestamped capture of the original page made by an archiving service.
