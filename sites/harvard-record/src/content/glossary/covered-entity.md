Covered entity is the term that sets the reach of the HIPAA rules. There are three kinds: health
plans, including insurers and employer group plans; health care clearinghouses, which convert
billing data between formats; and health care providers who transmit health information
electronically in connection with standard transactions such as insurance claims. A business
associate is an outside person or company that handles protected health information on a covered
entity's behalf, and it is bound by many of the same rules through law and contract.

A university is often a hybrid entity. Its teaching hospital, faculty medical practice or employee
health plan may be covered components, while its academic departments, research laboratories and
development office are not. Rules written for the hospital do not automatically follow information
into the rest of the institution, although separate limits apply when a hospital shares patient
information for fundraising.

The practical point is that the question is who holds the information, not what the information is
about. The same diagnosis is protected health information in a hospital chart and not
HIPAA-regulated in a personal email, a court filing or a laboratory notebook kept outside a covered
component. Other laws, such as research regulations and state privacy statutes, may still apply.
