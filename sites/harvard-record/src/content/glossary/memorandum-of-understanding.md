A memorandum of understanding, or MOU, records how two or more organizations intend to work
together. It is usually less formal than a commercial contract and often states that it creates no
legally enforceable rights, though its legal effect depends on its wording and on the parties'
intent.

In campus safety, the most common MOU is between a college and a local or state police agency. It
may cover which agency responds to which locations, which one leads the investigation of serious
crimes such as sexual assault, how information and evidence are shared, how each side notifies the
other, and how a criminal investigation is coordinated with the college's own Title IX process.
Colleges also sign MOUs with rape crisis centers, hospitals and prosecutors. The Clery Act requires
the annual security report to state whether the institution has written agreements with police
agencies for the investigation of alleged crimes. Some states require such agreements by statute.

The term is used widely outside safety as well, for research collaborations, exchange programs and
gift arrangements.

An MOU describes intended practice. Its existence does not show that the practice was followed in a
particular case. MOUs involving a public agency can often be obtained through a public records
request, which makes them a useful source.
