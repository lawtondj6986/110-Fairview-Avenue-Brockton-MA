term: "Appointment (academic)"

An academic appointment is the official grant of a position in a university's teaching or research
ranks. Every title, from professor to lecturer to research associate, exists because an appointment
was made under the institution's rules.

Appointments are classified in several ways. Ladder or tenure-track ranks (assistant, associate and
full professor) lead to or carry tenure. Non-ladder ranks include lecturers, instructors, professors
of the practice, adjunct and clinical faculty, research scientists and postdoctoral fellows,
generally on fixed terms. Visiting appointments are temporary posts for people based elsewhere. A
joint appointment is shared between two units, and a courtesy or secondary appointment gives a title
in a second unit without salary or vote. Approval levels rise with rank. A term appointment may need
only a chair and dean, while a tenured appointment requires review up to the president or governing
board.

Not every title is an appointment in this formal sense. Honorary titles, advisory board seats and
many affiliate statuses are conferred outside the appointments process. Because approval for junior
and unpaid titles is delegated downward, the fact that a person held an appointment does not show
that senior officials reviewed or knew of it. The appointment letter and the approving signatures
are the best evidence of who decided.
