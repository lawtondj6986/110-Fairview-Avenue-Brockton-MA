---
term: "Without admission of liability"
short: "A standard settlement term stating that the party paying or agreeing to act does not concede that it did anything wrong."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["no admission of wrongdoing", "neither admits nor denies", "non-admission clause"]
related: ["settlement", "consent-decree", "consent-judgment", "finding-vs-allegation"]
---

Most settlement agreements, consent decrees and administrative resolution agreements contain a
sentence saying that the agreement is not an admission of liability or wrongdoing. The clause means
that the paying party resolves the dispute while continuing to deny the claims, or at least without
conceding them. Some regulatory settlements use the formula that the party neither admits nor denies
the allegations.

The clause has a practical function. Without it, a defendant might fear that settling one claim
would be used as proof against it in other lawsuits. Rules of evidence already restrict the use of
settlement offers to prove liability, and the clause reinforces that protection. It also makes
compromise easier, since neither side has to win the argument about what happened.

The clause should be read for exactly what it says. It does not show that the allegations were
false, and it does not show that they were true. It leaves the factual question unresolved. Some
agreements go further and do contain admissions, such as an agreed statement of facts, and a guilty
plea in a criminal case is an admission by definition. Readers should therefore check whether a
particular document includes a non-admission clause, an agreed statement of facts, or both, and
report it accordingly.
