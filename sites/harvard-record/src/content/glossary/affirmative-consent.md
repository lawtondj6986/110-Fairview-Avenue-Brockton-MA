---
term: "Affirmative consent"
short: "A standard for sexual consent that requires a knowing, voluntary and mutual agreement to engage in sexual activity, shown by words or actions, instead of the mere absence of refusal."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["yes means yes", "affirmative consent standard"]
related: ["incapacitation", "sexual-assault-clery", "nonconsensual-sexual-contact", "preemption"]
---

Affirmative consent is a way of defining consent in conduct policies and some laws. Under it,
consent is an active, voluntary agreement to take part in a specific sexual activity. Silence,
passivity or lack of resistance does not by itself amount to consent. Consent can be given by words
or by clear actions, can be withdrawn at any time, and does not carry over automatically from a
previous occasion or an existing relationship. A person who is incapacitated cannot give it.

Many colleges adopted this definition in their sexual misconduct policies during the 2010s, and some
states require colleges to use it. Federal Title IX and Clery Act regulations do not impose any
particular definition of consent. They leave the definition to institutions and to state law, while
requiring that policies say what definition is used.

The standard applies in campus disciplinary processes. It is usually not the test in criminal law,
where most state statutes define sexual offenses through force, threat, incapacity or lack of
consent in terms that differ from campus policy. Conduct can therefore violate a college policy
without being a crime.

Affirmative consent defines what must be shown. It does not shift the burden of proof onto the
accused, which remains with the institution under the applicable standard of evidence.
