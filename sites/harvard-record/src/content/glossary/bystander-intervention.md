Bystander intervention is a prevention approach that treats everyone in a community as able to help
reduce harm, not only potential victims and perpetrators. The Clery Act regulations define it as
safe and positive options that an individual may carry out to prevent harm or intervene when there
is a risk of dating violence, domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking. It includes recognizing
situations of potential harm, understanding the institutional and cultural conditions that make
violence easier, overcoming barriers to intervening, and choosing safe and effective options.

Colleges covered by the Clery Act must include bystander intervention in the prevention and
awareness programs they provide to incoming students and new employees. Training programs commonly
teach a small set of approaches: directly addressing the situation, creating a distraction, bringing
in someone with more authority, and checking in with the affected person afterward. Many are
delivered as workshops or online modules.

Evaluation research has found that some programs improve knowledge, attitudes and stated willingness
to intervene. Evidence that they reduce the number of assaults is more limited and mixed. The
requirement to offer training is also a requirement about programming. An institution's compliance
with it shows that the training exists, not that the campus is safer or that bystanders are legally
obliged to act.
