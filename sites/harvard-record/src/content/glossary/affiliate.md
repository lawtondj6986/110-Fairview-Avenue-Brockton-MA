Affiliate is a broad term for someone who belongs to a university in a limited way. It includes
visiting scholars, research collaborators from other institutions, unpaid associates of a center,
adjunct or courtesy appointees whose main job is elsewhere, contractors, volunteers and retired
staff.

Institutions create affiliate statuses mainly for practical reasons. A person needs a record in the
identity system to receive an email account, a card, library rights or computing access. A
sponsoring department submits a request, an administrative office enters the person under an
appropriate category, and the status runs until an end date or renewal. Some affiliate titles pass
through an academic appointments office. Many do not and are handled as an administrative
transaction, sometimes under a human resources label for non-employees. Standards for approval, such
as a required degree or a written purpose, vary among schools.

The word carries weight outside the institution that it may not carry inside. Internally, an
affiliate may be little more than a database entry. Externally, "affiliated with" a university
suggests endorsement. Affiliation does not establish employment, payment, academic standing or
regular presence, and the reverse also holds: a person may be present and involved without any
recorded affiliation. Good practice in describing a record is to state the exact title, the unit
that granted it, and the dates.
