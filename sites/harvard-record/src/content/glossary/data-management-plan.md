A data management plan (DMP) sets out how a research project will handle its data from collection to
long-term preservation. Typical contents are the types and amounts of data expected, the formats and
documentation that will make the data usable by others, where the data will be stored and backed up,
who is responsible, how confidential information will be protected, which data will be shared, where
and when, and how long the data will be kept.

Many public and private research funders require a plan as part of a grant application, and some
have made data sharing plans a general condition of funding in recent years. For research with human
participants, the plan must be consistent with what participants were told in the consent form and
with privacy law. Genomic data are commonly deposited in repositories that release individual-level
data only to approved users.

A data management plan is a statement of intent written before the work begins. It is not a record
of what was done, and compliance has historically been checked unevenly. It is also distinct from a
data use agreement, which is a contract governing a specific transfer of data between parties, and
from a research protocol, which governs the study as a whole. Plans typically address data. Physical
samples are usually covered by separate agreements.
