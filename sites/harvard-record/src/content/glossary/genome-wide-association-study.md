---
term: "Genome-wide association study (GWAS)"
short: "A study that scans genetic markers across the genomes of many people to find variants that occur more often in those with a trait or disease than in those without it."
category: "Genetics and biomedical research"
aliases: ["GWAS", "Association study", "Genome-wide significance"]
related: ["polygenic-score", "polygenic-trait", "snp", "genotyping-array", "heritability", "statistical-significance"]
---

A genome-wide association study compares the DNA of large numbers of people, often tens or hundreds
of thousands, to see which genetic markers are associated with a trait. Participants are usually
genotyped on arrays that measure hundreds of thousands of single nucleotide polymorphisms, with
further variants estimated statistically. Each marker is tested for association with the trait,
such as height, blood pressure or the presence of a disease.

Because so many markers are tested, the threshold for calling a result significant is set very
strictly, to reduce false positives. Findings are usually reported as a list of genomic regions,
each contributing a small effect. The results are the raw material for polygenic scores, and they
can point biologists toward genes worth studying.

An association is not a cause. The marker found may simply sit near the variant that matters, and
effects can reflect ancestry or environment that the study did not fully account for. Results from
one population may not transfer to others. A GWAS describes averages across many people; it does not
diagnose or predict outcomes for an individual with much accuracy for most traits.
