term: "Stewardship report"
short: "A periodic report from an institution to a donor describing how a gift or fund was used, its financial position and, where relevant, who benefited from it."

A stewardship report is a document a development or donor relations office sends to a donor to
account for a gift. For an endowed fund it commonly shows the fund's market value, the amount made
available for spending during the year, what was spent and on what, and a short narrative about the
students, faculty or programs supported. For a current-use gift it may describe the outcome of the
project the gift paid for.

Reports are usually prepared once a year, often after the fiscal year closes, and are written for
the donor rather than the public. They are part of the relationship-building work described as
stewardship, and they are also a practical way for an institution to show it is honoring the terms
of a restricted gift.

A stewardship report is not an audit. It is prepared by the institution about itself, and its
figures may be rounded, pooled or presented in a favorable light. When one appears in a record,
treat it as the institution's own account of how it used the money. Compare it with the gift
agreement and, where possible, with audited financial statements or public filings.
