For Clery Act statistics, sexual assault is an umbrella term for four offenses, defined by reference
to the Federal Bureau of Investigation's crime reporting program.

Rape is the penetration, no matter how slight, of the vagina or anus with any body part or object,
or oral penetration by a sex organ of another person, without the consent of the victim. Fondling is
the touching of the private body parts of another person for the purpose of sexual gratification
without consent, including where the victim cannot consent because of age or temporary or permanent
mental incapacity. Incest is sexual intercourse between people related to each other within the
degrees in which marriage is prohibited by law. Statutory rape is sexual intercourse with a person
under the statutory age of consent.

The rape definition is not limited by the sex of either person and does not require force.

These are counting definitions. State criminal codes use different names and elements, such as
sexual battery or criminal sexual conduct in various degrees. A number labeled sexual assault in one
source therefore cannot be set beside a number from another source without checking how each defines
it. The regulations do not define consent, which is left to state law and institutional policy.
