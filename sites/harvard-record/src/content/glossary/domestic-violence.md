Domestic violence is one of the categories added to Clery Act reporting in 2013. The regulations
define it as a felony or misdemeanor crime of violence committed by any of the following: a current
or former spouse or intimate partner of the victim; a person with whom the victim shares a child; a
person who lives or has lived with the victim as a spouse or intimate partner; a person similarly
situated to a spouse under the domestic or family violence laws of the jurisdiction; or any other
person against a victim who is protected from that person's acts under those laws.

The act must be a crime of violence, and the relationship between the people is what places it in
this category. Because the last parts of the definition refer to local law, the category's reach
differs somewhat from state to state.

The term is narrower in Clery reporting than in everyday speech. Roommates who are not in an
intimate relationship generally do not fall within it merely because they share housing. Emotional
or financial abuse, which many advocates and some state laws treat as domestic abuse, is not counted
unless it amounts to a crime of violence. An incident between dating partners who do not fit the
listed relationships is counted as dating violence instead.
