Prospect management is the set of rules and records by which a fundraising office organizes its work
with potential donors. Moves management is an older name for the same idea: each planned contact is
a "move" intended to bring the prospect closer to giving.

The system rests on a cycle of stages, commonly identification, qualification, cultivation,
solicitation and stewardship. Each prospect is assigned to a manager, given a stage and a strategy,
and tracked in the donor database. After each meaningful contact the fundraiser writes a contact
report describing what was discussed and the next step. Managers hold regular meetings to review
portfolios, settle competing claims on the same prospect and decide who will make the request. Staff
performance is often measured by counts of visits, proposals submitted and dollars raised.

For someone reading institutional records, prospect management explains the form the evidence takes.
Contact reports, strategy notes and stage changes are the paper trail of a donor relationship. The
system covers only what staff enter into it, however. Contacts handled personally by a faculty
member, a dean or a president may not appear, and a decentralized university may run several systems
that do not share information. An empty record therefore does not prove there was no relationship.
