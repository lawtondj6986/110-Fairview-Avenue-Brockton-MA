---
term: "Exome"
short: "The portion of the genome made up of exons, the segments of genes that code for proteins; it is a small fraction of all DNA."
category: "Genetics and biomedical research"
related: ["exome-sequencing", "gene", "genome", "whole-genome-sequencing"]
---

The exome is the part of the genome made up of exons, the segments of genes that are translated into
proteins. It accounts for only about one to two percent of human DNA. Despite its small size, it
contains a large share of the variants currently known to cause disorders that follow from a change
in a single gene.

The exome is studied as a unit for a practical reason. Reading a small, well-understood portion of
the genome is cheaper than reading all of it, and changes in protein-coding sequence are easier to
interpret than changes elsewhere. A variant that alters or truncates a protein has a more
predictable effect than one in a region whose function is unclear.

The exome is not the whole story. Regulatory DNA outside the exons affects how genes are used, and
some disease-causing variants lie there. Large rearrangements of DNA can also be missed when only
exons are read. A statement that someone's exome was analyzed therefore means that a targeted,
informative portion of their DNA was read. It does not mean the entire genome was examined.
