---
term: "On the record, on background and off the record"
short: "Journalistic terms for the conditions under which a source speaks: fully quotable by name, usable without naming the source, or not for publication."
category: "Records and sourcing"

These phrases describe agreements between a reporter and a source about how information may be used.
On the record means that everything said can be quoted and attributed to the speaker by name. On
background, or not for attribution, generally means the information can be published but the source
is described only in agreed general terms, such as "a university official". Deep background usually
means the information can be used without attributing it to any source. Off the record means the
information is not to be published, although practice differs on whether it may be used to seek
confirmation elsewhere.

These are conventions, not legal rules, and their meanings vary between news organizations and
between individuals. For that reason the terms are supposed to be agreed before the conversation,
not claimed afterward. Many outlets require an editor to know the identity of an unnamed source and
require a reason for granting anonymity.

For a reader, the distinction affects weight. An on-the-record statement can be attributed, checked
and answered by the person who made it. An unnamed source's account depends on trust in the outlet's
judgment. This site treats on-the-record statements reported by established outlets as stronger
material than anonymous accounts. In ordinary speech, "on the record" also simply means stated
publicly, which is a different sense.
