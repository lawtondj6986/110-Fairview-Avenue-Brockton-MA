A court record is the official file of a legal case. It includes the docket, which is a
chronological list of everything filed and every action the court takes. It also includes the
parties' pleadings (such as a complaint and an answer), motions and briefs, exhibits attached to
them, transcripts of hearings and testimony, and the court's orders and judgment.

Court records are generally open to the public, in keeping with a long tradition that courts operate
in the open. Access is not total. Judges can seal documents or order redactions to protect matters
such as the identities of minors or victims, trade secrets or personal identifiers. Material
exchanged between the parties in discovery is usually not part of the public file unless it is filed
with the court.

The essential reading skill is to tell the types of document apart. A complaint contains
allegations, which are one side's unproven claims. A sworn declaration or deposition is testimony
that has not necessarily been tested. An order or judgment is the court's own decision. A settlement
resolves a case, usually without any finding about who was right. Being named in a court record, as
a witness, in an exhibit or in passing, does not mean a person was accused of anything.
