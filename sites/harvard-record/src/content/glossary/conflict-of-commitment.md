---
term: "Conflict of commitment"
short: "A situation in which a faculty or staff member's outside activities take time or loyalty away from the duties they owe their institution."
category: "University governance"
aliases: ["Outside activities", "Outside professional activities", "Conflict of effort", "External commitments"]

A conflict of commitment concerns time and primary allegiance. It arises when outside work, such as
consulting, a position at a company, an appointment at another institution or a large personal
venture, interferes with a person's obligations to their university employer.

Universities permit and often encourage outside professional activity, within limits. Policies
usually allow full-time faculty a set amount of time for consulting, require prior approval for
certain roles such as management positions in companies or appointments elsewhere, and require
annual disclosure of outside activities. Review is typically done by the department chair or dean,
with a central office handling difficult cases. Federal research funders also require investigators
to disclose other positions and sources of research support in their proposals.

The term is distinct from conflict of interest, though the two often occur together and are
frequently covered by one policy and one disclosure form. A conflict of interest involves a
financial or personal stake that could bias a person's judgment. A conflict of commitment involves
divided time and effort, even where no money is at stake. Both systems depend on self-reporting.
Unpaid roles, informal advisory relationships and personal ties to a funder may not fit the form's
categories, so their absence from a disclosure does not necessarily mean they were concealed. It may
mean the form did not ask.
