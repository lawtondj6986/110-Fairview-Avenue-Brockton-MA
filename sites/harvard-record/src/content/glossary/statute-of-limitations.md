---
term: "Statute of limitations"
short: "A law that sets the maximum time after an event within which a lawsuit or criminal charge may be brought."
category: "Law and compliance"
aliases: ["limitations period", "tolling", "lookback window"]
related: ["civil-vs-criminal", "motion-to-dismiss", "complaint-civil"]
---

A statute of limitations is a deadline for starting legal proceedings. Once the period has run, a
civil claim can be dismissed and a criminal charge can be barred, whatever the strength of the
evidence.

The length varies with the type of claim and the jurisdiction. Each state sets its own periods for
civil claims and for crimes, and federal law sets others. The most serious crimes, such as murder,
typically have no limit. The clock usually starts when the event occurs, but under a discovery rule
it may start when the injured person knew or should have known of the injury. The period can be
paused, or tolled, for reasons such as the plaintiff being a minor. Many states have lengthened the
periods for sexual abuse claims, and some have opened temporary windows that revive claims that had
already expired.

A case that ends on limitations grounds has not been decided on its merits. The court has not found
that the events did not happen. For the same reason, the fact that no charge or suit was ever filed
may reflect the passage of time and nothing more. Because these laws change often, always check the
rule in force for the place and date in question.
