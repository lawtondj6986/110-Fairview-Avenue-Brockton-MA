A donor database, often called a CRM (constituent or customer relationship management system), is
the central record of an institution's fundraising. It holds a record for each alumnus, donor,
prospect and organization the institution tracks.

A typical record contains biographical details, family and business relationships, degrees, a full
gift and pledge history, capacity and affinity ratings, the assigned gift officer, the prospect's
stage and strategy, event attendance, and contact reports written by staff after meetings and calls.
It also carries codes that control handling, such as anonymous, do not solicit or do not contact.
Gifts are entered by a gift processing team, which is also the source of receipts and of the totals
the institution reports.

The database is the institution's memory of a donor relationship, and it is the first place an
internal review will look. Its limits are equally important. It contains what staff chose to enter,
in the words they chose. Contacts handled by faculty or senior officials outside the development
office may be missing. Large universities may run more than one system, or have moved between
systems and lost detail in the transfer. A statement that "no record was found" is only as strong as
the search and the system behind it.
