A Dear Colleague letter is a form of agency guidance. Federal agencies, including the Department of
Education, use these letters to tell schools how the agency interprets existing statutes and
regulations and what it expects to see when it reviews compliance. The name comes from the
salutation.

Guidance letters do not go through notice-and-comment rulemaking, so in formal terms they do not
create new legal obligations. In practice they carry weight, because the agency that wrote the
letter is the one that investigates. Schools have often rewritten policies in response to a single
letter. Because guidance is easy to issue, it is also easy to withdraw. Title IX guidance on sexual
violence, for example, was issued, rescinded and replaced several times between 2011 and the present
as administrations changed.

When reading an older institutional policy, it helps to know which guidance was in force at the
time. A policy that looks unusual today may have followed the guidance of its day.

The phrase has a second meaning. In Congress, a Dear Colleague letter is correspondence one member
circulates to other members, typically to seek cosponsors or signatures. That kind of letter has no
regulatory effect.
