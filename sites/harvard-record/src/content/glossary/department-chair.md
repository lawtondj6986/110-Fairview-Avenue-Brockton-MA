A department chair is the professor who leads a department, such as chemistry or economics. The
chair is normally a tenured member of the department, appointed by the dean, often after consulting
the department's faculty, for a renewable term of a few years.

The chair's duties are administrative. They include organizing teaching assignments, managing the
department's budget and staff, running faculty searches and promotion reviews, mentoring junior
colleagues, assigning space, and approving routine requests such as visiting appointments, leaves
and some expenditures. The chair is often the first person to receive a complaint about a member of
the department and is usually required to pass serious matters to the dean or a designated
university office.

The chair's power is limited. Chairs are colleagues of the people they supervise and typically
return to the ordinary faculty when their term ends. They generally cannot discipline a tenured
professor on their own authority, and they have little control over research groups funded by a
professor's own grants and gifts. In clinical departments of medical schools, the equivalent
position may be a department head or chief with greater and longer-lasting authority. In records, a
chair's signature on an appointment form shows that a required approval was given. It does not by
itself show how much the chair knew about the person being appointed.
