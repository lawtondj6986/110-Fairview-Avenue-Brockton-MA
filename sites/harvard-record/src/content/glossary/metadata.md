---
term: "Metadata"
short: "Information about a document or file, as distinct from its content, such as author, dates, sender, recipients, file type and edit history."
category: "Records and sourcing"
aliases: ["document properties", "email headers"]
related: ["provenance", "authenticity-vs-accuracy", "duplicate-and-thread", "redaction", "archive-snapshot"]
---

Metadata is data about data. For an email it includes the sender, recipients, date and time, subject
line and technical routing information. For a word-processing file or PDF it can include the author
name recorded by the software, the creation and modification dates, and the program used. For a
photograph it may include the camera, the time and sometimes the location. Archives and databases
also add descriptive metadata of their own, such as titles, custodians and index terms.

Metadata is valuable for dating documents, linking them to people and accounts, reconstructing
sequences of events, and testing authenticity. In litigation, parties often must produce defined
metadata fields along with documents. When records are released as scanned images or flattened PDFs,
most of the original metadata is lost. What remains may describe the scanning or release process and
not the original document.

Metadata must be read critically. Dates can reflect when a file was copied or converted, not when it
was written. An author field may show the owner of a computer or a template, not the writer. Time
zones cause apparent discrepancies. Metadata can also be edited. It is evidence to be weighed
together with the content and provenance of a document. The term also has a privacy sense, since
files shared carelessly can reveal more than their visible text.
