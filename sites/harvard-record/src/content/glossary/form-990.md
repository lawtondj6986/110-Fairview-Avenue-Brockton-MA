Form 990 is the return that most organizations exempt from federal income tax must file each year.
It is an information return, not a tax bill. It reports revenue, expenses, assets, program
activities, governance practices, and the pay of officers, trustees and the highest-paid employees.
Attached schedules cover topics such as fundraising, transactions with insiders, foreign activities
and grants made.

The law requires both the Internal Revenue Service and the organization to make the return available
for public inspection, and several free online databases republish the filings. Private foundations
file a different version, Form 990-PF, which lists every grant paid during the year with the
recipient and amount. This makes foundation returns one of the few places where an outside reader
can see individual gifts to a university.

The form has well-known limits. A public charity's list of major contributors, Schedule B, is
generally withheld from the public copy, so a university's own return does not reveal who gave.
Returns are filed many months after the fiscal year ends. Governmental units, including most public
universities, and churches are not required to file, though a public university's affiliated
foundation normally is. Figures are self-reported and are not audited by the IRS before release.
