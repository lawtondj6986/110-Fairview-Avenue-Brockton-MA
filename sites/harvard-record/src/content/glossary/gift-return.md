Refusal means turning down a gift before acceptance. Return means giving back a gift after it was
accepted. They are different decisions with different difficulties.

An institution is free to refuse any offered gift, and gift acceptance policies usually list grounds
for doing so: unacceptable conditions, costs that exceed the benefit, concerns about the source of
funds or damage to reputation. Refusals are rarely announced. Returning an accepted gift is harder.
The money may have been spent. If it was restricted or endowed, state charity law may limit what the
institution can do with it, and returning charitable assets to a private person can require legal
review or the involvement of the state attorney general. Tax questions can arise for the donor as
well. Institutions therefore sometimes choose alternatives: stopping further payments on a pledge,
removing a name, or redirecting an equivalent sum to a different charitable purpose.

Public statements on these matters use varied wording that should be read exactly. "We will accept
no further gifts" says nothing about past gifts. "Unspent funds" excludes what was already used.
"Redirected" means the institution kept the value and applied it elsewhere. An announcement that a
gift was returned also does not establish when the institution first learned of the concern that
prompted it.
