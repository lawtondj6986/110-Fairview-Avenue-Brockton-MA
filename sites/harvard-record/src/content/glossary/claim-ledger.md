A claim ledger is a working tool for keeping published statements tied to their evidence. Each row
holds a single checkable statement of fact, an identifier that never changes, the source that
supports it, a grade for how reliable that source is, how far the evidence goes (for example, a
stated plan versus a documented action), and when the source was last checked. Compound sentences
are split until each row can be checked against one passage in one document.

In practice the ledger makes errors easier to catch and to fix. If a source turns out to be wrong
or a link breaks, every claim that depends on it can be found at once. When a correction is made,
the ledger records the change, so the page and any downloads stay consistent. Statements that
something is absent from a document are claims too and belong in the ledger.

A ledger is not a substitute for judgment, and an entry in it does not make a claim true. It
records what the researcher relied on and how strong that support is. This site's own ledger and the
guide to keeping one are linked from /ledger/ and /guides/.
