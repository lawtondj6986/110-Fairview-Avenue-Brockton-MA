---
term: "Mediation"
short: "A confidential process in which a neutral person helps the parties to a dispute negotiate their own settlement, without the power to impose a decision."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["Mediator", "Court-ordered mediation", "Alternative dispute resolution", "ADR"]
related: ["mandatory-arbitration", "settlement", "informal-resolution", "ombuds", "docket"]
---

In mediation, a trained neutral, the mediator, meets with the parties, often separately as well as
together, to help them find terms both can accept. The mediator may point out risks, suggest
options and carry offers back and forth, but cannot decide the case. If the parties reach agreement,
they usually sign a settlement; if they do not, the dispute continues.

Courts frequently order or encourage mediation in civil cases, and a docket may show that a case was
referred to a mediator or that a mediation session was held, followed some weeks later by a notice
of settlement or dismissal. Universities use mediation in employment disputes, some student matters
and, under some policies, as a form of informal resolution in misconduct cases. Ombuds offices may
offer it informally.

What is said in mediation is generally confidential and, in many jurisdictions, cannot be used later
as evidence; the details depend on state law and court rules. A docket entry showing failed
mediation says nothing about the merits or about which side refused to settle. Mediation is
distinct from arbitration, where the neutral does make a binding decision.
