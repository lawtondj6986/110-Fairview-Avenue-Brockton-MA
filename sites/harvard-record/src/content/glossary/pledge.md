---
term: "Pledge"
short: "A donor's promise to give a stated amount in the future, often paid in installments over several years."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Gift commitment", "Multi-year pledge"]

---

A pledge is a commitment to make a gift at a later date or over a period of time. Large gifts to
universities are commonly structured as pledges, with payments spread across several years.

A pledge is normally documented in a signed pledge form or gift agreement that states the total, the
payment schedule and the purpose. Institutions count pledges toward campaign totals when they are
made, which is why an announced gift figure can be much larger than the cash received so far. Under
U.S. nonprofit accounting rules, an unconditional written promise to give is generally recorded as a
receivable. A conditional pledge, which depends on some future event, is treated differently and is
usually not recorded as revenue until the condition is met.

A pledge is not the same as money received. Donors sometimes stop paying, renegotiate or are
released from a pledge, and an institution may itself decline further payments. Whether a charitable
pledge can be enforced in court depends on state law and on the facts, and institutions rarely sue
donors. When reading a record, it helps to separate three figures: the amount pledged, the amount
paid and the amount actually spent.
