An advisory board is a body of outsiders invited to counsel part of a university. Members are
commonly alumni, donors, business leaders and experts in the field. A visiting committee is a more
formal variety, appointed to review a department or school periodically and report to the governing
boards or the administration.

These bodies meet a few times a year. They hear presentations from the dean or director, comment on
strategy, and may write a report. Many also have an explicit or understood fundraising purpose.
Members are often expected to give and to help find other donors, and a seat on a board is a
standard tool of cultivation and recognition. Appointment is usually by a dean, a director or the
president, depending on the level, and the rules are local.

An advisory board does not govern. It cannot hire, fire, set budgets or bind the institution, and
its members owe none of the legal duties that trustees do. Membership nevertheless brings regular
contact with academic leaders, early knowledge of plans and a title that can be cited publicly. It
is one of the clearest examples of access granted without formal authority. Membership lists are
sometimes published and sometimes not, and past lists can be hard to reconstruct once a website is
updated.
