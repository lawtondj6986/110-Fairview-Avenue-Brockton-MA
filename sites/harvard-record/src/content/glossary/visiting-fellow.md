A visiting fellow or visiting scholar is a person from outside the institution who is given a
temporary affiliation with one of its units. The person is not a student working toward a degree and
usually is not a paid employee.

The category exists mainly for academics on sabbatical, researchers from other universities or
industry, and practitioners who want to spend time in a scholarly setting. Appointment procedures
are generally lighter than for faculty. A sponsoring professor or a center director proposes the
visitor, and a department chair or dean's office approves, usually for a term of a year or less that
may be renewed. Typical privileges include an identification card, library borrowing, an email
address, building access and sometimes a desk. Typical limits are no salary, no teaching in one's
own name and no claim on university funds. Requirements such as a doctoral degree or a current
position elsewhere vary and can sometimes be waived.

A visiting title does not mean the person was vetted as a faculty candidate would be, or that the
central administration was aware of the appointment. Because these affiliations are approved locally
and give real access and a real credential, they are a point where the judgment of one unit can
attach the institution's name to an individual.
