---
term: "Conflict of interest committee"
short: "A standing committee that reviews disclosed outside interests of faculty, staff or trustees and decides whether they create a conflict and how it should be managed, reduced or eliminated."
category: "University governance"

A conflict of interest committee is the body an institution relies on to judge disclosed financial
and other outside interests. Faculty and senior staff typically file periodic disclosures of
consulting, equity, board seats and similar ties. Staff in a compliance office screen them and send
the significant cases to the committee, which is often made up of faculty with administrative and
legal support.

For research funded by the Public Health Service, federal rules require institutions to review
investigators' significant financial interests, decide whether they are related to the research and
amount to a financial conflict, and adopt a management plan where they do. Typical management steps
include public disclosure in papers, independent monitoring of the research, limits on the
investigator's role, or divestment. Similar committees, or a board committee, may review trustees'
interests and institutional conflicts, such as a company that is both a sponsor and a donor.

A committee's decision that an interest is manageable is not a finding that no conflict exists. It is
a judgment that the risk can be reduced to an acceptable level. Committee records are usually
confidential, though some institutions publish summary policies or statistics.
