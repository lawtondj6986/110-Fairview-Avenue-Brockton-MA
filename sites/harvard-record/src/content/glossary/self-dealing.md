Self-dealing, in tax law, refers to transactions between a private foundation and its disqualified
persons, such as its substantial contributors, foundation managers and their families and
businesses. Section 4941 of the tax code treats most such transactions as prohibited: sales,
exchanges or leases of property, loans, the furnishing of goods or services, payment of
compensation beyond what is reasonable for personal services, and transfers of the foundation's
income or assets for an insider's benefit.

Unlike the rules for public charities, the self-dealing rules generally apply even if the deal is on
fair terms. There are exceptions, including reasonable pay for services that are needed to carry out
the foundation's purposes. Violations trigger excise taxes on the insider and on managers who
knowingly took part, and the transaction must be undone.

The rule matters for donor research because a foundation controlled by a donor cannot be used to pay
for benefits to that donor, such as event tickets or obligations the donor has personally pledged.
Self-dealing is a technical term with a specific legal meaning. It should not be used loosely to
describe any conflict of interest, and it does not apply to public charities.
