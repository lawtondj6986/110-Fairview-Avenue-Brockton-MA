term: "Lab manager"
short: "A staff member who runs the day-to-day operations of a research laboratory, such as ordering, budgets, safety compliance, equipment and scheduling, under the direction of the principal investigator."

A lab manager keeps a research laboratory working. Typical duties include purchasing supplies,
tracking spending against grants, maintaining equipment, keeping safety and training records,
onboarding new members, managing animal or chemical inventories, and handling paperwork with central
offices. In a small lab the manager may also do research; in a large one the role can be mostly
administrative and may supervise other staff.

Lab managers are usually staff employees rather than faculty, and they report to the principal
investigator who leads the lab. Because they handle orders, schedules and records, they often appear
in a laboratory's documents, such as purchase approvals, training logs and correspondence with
vendors and research offices.

A lab manager is not the person responsible for the research itself. Scientific direction, grant
compliance and final decisions normally rest with the principal investigator and, above that, with
the department and institution. A lab manager's signature on a routine form usually reflects
delegated administrative authority, not an independent decision. Titles vary widely between
institutions, and the same work may be done by a research administrator or a senior technician.
