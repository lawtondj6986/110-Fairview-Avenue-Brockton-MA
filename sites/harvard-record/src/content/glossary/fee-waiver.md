Agencies may charge for processing records requests. Under the federal Freedom of Information Act,
fees depend on the requester's category. Commercial requesters can be charged for search, review and
duplication; educational and noncommercial scientific institutions and representatives of the news
media are usually charged only for duplication beyond a free amount; others fall in between.

A fee waiver is a separate request to have charges waived or reduced. Under the federal statute, an
agency should grant it when disclosure is in the public interest because it is likely to contribute
significantly to public understanding of the government's operations or activities, and is not
primarily in the requester's commercial interest. Requesters usually explain in the request how they
will share the information. A denied waiver can be appealed.

A fee waiver does not affect whether records are released, only who pays. Agencies also commonly
ask a requester to agree to pay up to a stated amount, and a request can be paused until fees are
settled. State public records laws set their own fee rules, which vary considerably, so the current
state statute should be checked.
