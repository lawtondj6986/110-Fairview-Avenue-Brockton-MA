term: "Data use agreement"
short: "A contract that sets the terms on which one party may use data provided by another, such as permitted purposes, security measures, limits on re-identification and rules on publication."
category: "Research ethics"

A data use agreement governs the transfer of a dataset from a provider, such as a hospital, a
biobank or another research group, to a recipient. It usually limits use to a stated research
project, names who may access the data, requires security safeguards, bars attempts to identify
individuals or contact them, controls onward sharing and sets out how results may be published and
when data must be destroyed or returned.

Some agreements are required by law. The HIPAA Privacy Rule allows a covered entity to share a
limited data set, which has direct identifiers removed but may keep dates and some geographic
details, only under a data use agreement. Large genomic repositories commonly use a controlled
access model in which researchers apply, have their request reviewed and sign a data use
certification before downloading data.

A data use agreement is a promise between institutions. It is not a consent document and does not
replace participants' consent, and it does not make data anonymous. Its protections depend on
recipients following it and on the provider's ability to enforce it. A material transfer agreement
plays the same role for physical samples.
