A Federalwide Assurance, or FWA, is an agreement between a research institution and the Department
of Health and Human Services. By filing it with the Office for Human Research Protections, the
institution promises that human subjects research supported by HHS, and by other agencies that
accept the assurance, will be reviewed by a registered institutional review board and conducted
according to federal rules. Each assurance has a number, and grant applications and IRB documents
often cite it.

The assurance names an institutional official who is responsible for compliance and lists the IRBs
the institution relies on. Institutions may choose to apply the same protections to all of their
human research regardless of funding. Whether they do is set out in their own policies, which are
worth checking before assuming that an unfunded study had the same review.

An FWA is a promise, not a certificate of good practice. Having one does not show that a particular
study was reviewed or approved. Research that receives no federal funding may fall outside the
assurance, although FDA rules and state law can still apply. When oversight problems arise, the
assurance is one basis on which federal officials can act.
