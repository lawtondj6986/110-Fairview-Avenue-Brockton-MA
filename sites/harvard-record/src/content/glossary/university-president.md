---
term: "President (university)"
short: "The chief executive of a university, appointed by and answerable to the governing board, and its principal public representative."
category: "University governance"

---

The president is the senior officer of a university. The governing board selects the president, sets
the terms of the appointment and can end it. In some public systems the equivalent officer is called
chancellor, and titles are used differently from one system to another.

The president is responsible for the institution as a whole: strategy, budget, senior appointments,
relations with government and the public, and fundraising. In practice, academic matters are largely
delegated to the provost and deans, and the president's own time is heavily committed to external
work. Presidents are usually the lead fundraiser for the largest gifts, meeting personally with
principal donors. Senior officers such as the provost, the chief financial officer, the general
counsel and the head of development typically report to the president.

A president's authority is real but bounded. The board holds ultimate legal authority. The faculty,
by long custom, controls curriculum and has the main voice in academic appointments. In a
decentralized university, deans control their own budgets and may raise their own funds. A president
may therefore be unaware of arrangements made within a school or laboratory, and may also be unable
to direct them easily. Whether a president knew of, approved or could have prevented a particular
act is a factual question, not something the title answers.
