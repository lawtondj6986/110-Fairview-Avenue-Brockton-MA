Section 117 is a disclosure rule. It requires an institution of higher education that receives
federal financial assistance to file a report with the Secretary of Education when it receives gifts
from, or enters into contracts with, a foreign source above a dollar threshold within a calendar
year. The threshold has long stood at $250,000, counted alone or in combination. Institutions owned
or controlled by a foreign source must also report. Reports are filed twice a year.

A foreign source includes a foreign government, a legal entity created under foreign law, a person
who is not a U.S. citizen or national, and an agent acting for any of these. Reports generally give
the amount, the date and the country, with more detail for gifts from governments and for restricted
or conditional gifts. The Department of Education publishes the reported data. If an institution
fails to comply, the Department can ask the Department of Justice to seek a court order.

The section requires disclosure only. It does not prohibit foreign gifts or cap them. The public
data also have limits: many entries do not name the individual donor, and reporting practices have
varied between institutions and over time. Congress has considered changes to the threshold and
scope, so check the current statute.
