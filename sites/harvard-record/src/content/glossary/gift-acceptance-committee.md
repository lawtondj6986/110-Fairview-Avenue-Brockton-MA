term: "Gift acceptance committee"
short: "A group of senior officials that reviews gifts that are large, unusual or potentially controversial and decides or recommends whether to accept them."

A gift acceptance committee is a standing body that considers gifts falling outside routine
approval. Not every institution has one, and some created theirs only recently.

Membership usually includes senior development staff, the general counsel or a deputy, a finance
officer and often the provost or a representative of academic leadership. Some include faculty
members. A gift reaches the committee when a policy requires it (for example, real estate, a gift
with unusual conditions, or a donor flagged in due diligence) or when a staff member chooses to
refer it. The committee may approve, approve with conditions, ask for more information or decline.
At some institutions it only advises, and the president makes the final decision.

The committee sees only what is brought to it. A gift that is small, split into parts, routed
through a foundation or other third party, or handled directly by a faculty member may never be
referred. The committee's records are internal and seldom public. Its existence therefore shows that
a review mechanism was available, not that a particular gift was reviewed. A reader should look for
evidence of an actual referral and decision before concluding that a gift was approved through this
route.
