---
term: "Nondisparagement clause"
short: "A contract term in which one or both parties agree not to make negative statements about the other, common in settlement, separation and some commercial agreements."
category: "Legal procedure"
aliases: ["Non-disparagement clause", "Mutual nondisparagement", "No-disparagement provision"]
related: ["nondisclosure-agreement", "separation-agreement", "settlement", "declined-to-comment", "defamation"]
---

A nondisparagement clause restricts what a party may say about the other. It differs from a
confidentiality clause. An NDA limits sharing particular information; a nondisparagement clause
limits critical statements in general, even ones based on public facts or opinion. Clauses may be
one-way, binding only the departing employee, or mutual. A mutual clause binding an organization
often covers only named officers or an official statement, since an institution cannot easily
control what every employee says.

Such clauses are routine in separation agreements and settlements. Their enforceability depends on
the law that applies. Federal labor law has been interpreted to restrict broad nondisparagement terms
in some agreements with non-supervisory employees, federal law limits clauses signed before a sexual
assault or harassment dispute, and several states restrict them in other settings. These rules have
shifted in recent years, so the current position should be checked.

A nondisparagement clause can explain why a person declines to comment, but silence alone is not
evidence that such a clause exists. The clause also does not stop truthful testimony under subpoena
or reports to government agencies.
