Stalking was added to Clery Act reporting in 2013. The regulations define it as engaging in a course
of conduct directed at a specific person that would cause a reasonable person to fear for their own
safety or the safety of others, or to suffer substantial emotional distress.

Each element has its own definition. A course of conduct means two or more acts. These can include
following, monitoring, observing, surveilling, threatening, or communicating to or about a person,
or interfering with a person's property. The acts can be done directly, indirectly or through third
parties, and by any method or device, so online conduct is included. A reasonable person means a
reasonable person in similar circumstances and with similar identities to the victim.

Because stalking is a pattern, counting it raises practical questions. The regulations tell
institutions to record it at the first location within Clery geography where the conduct occurred or
where the victim first became aware of it, and to count it in each year in which the conduct is
reported.

The Clery definition is used for statistics and campus policy. Each state has its own criminal
stalking statute with different elements, so conduct counted by an institution may or may not be
chargeable as a crime.
