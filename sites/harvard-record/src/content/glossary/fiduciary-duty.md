term: "Fiduciary duty"
short: "The legal obligation of a trustee or director to act with care, loyalty and fidelity to an organization's purposes when making decisions on its behalf."

A fiduciary is someone trusted to act for another. Members of a nonprofit's governing board are
fiduciaries of the institution. Their duties are usually described in three parts. The duty of care
requires them to act in good faith and with the attention an ordinarily prudent person would use,
including being informed before deciding. The duty of loyalty requires them to put the institution's
interests ahead of their own and to handle conflicts of interest openly. The duty of obedience,
recognized in many states, requires them to keep the institution faithful to its mission and to
the law.

In practice, these duties are why boards adopt conflict of interest policies, have trustees disclose
outside interests and recuse themselves, keep minutes, and rely on committees and professional
advice. State law sets the duties and the state attorney general can usually enforce them on behalf
of the public. Courts generally defer to good-faith board decisions under the business judgment rule.

A breach of fiduciary duty is a legal conclusion, usually reached by a court or regulator. Poor
outcomes, controversial decisions or a donor serving on a board do not by themselves show a breach.
