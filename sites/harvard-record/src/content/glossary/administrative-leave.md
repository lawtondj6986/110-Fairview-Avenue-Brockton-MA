Administrative leave takes an employee out of the workplace for a period at the institution's
direction. For faculty it typically means relief from teaching, supervision of students and
sometimes research, and may include limits on access to campus.

Leave is usually described as an interim or precautionary measure, not a punishment. It is imposed
by a dean or provost when allegations are serious enough that the person's continued presence might
pose a risk, interfere with an investigation or disrupt the unit. Paid leave is the norm at this
stage, because no finding has yet been made. Unpaid leave or suspension without pay is generally
treated as a sanction, and for tenured faculty it normally requires the procedures set out in the
faculty handbook. The details are governed by institutional policy, employment contracts and, at
public universities, constitutional due process requirements.

Being placed on leave is not a finding of wrongdoing, and announcements often say so. Leave also
does not end the employment relationship: the person keeps their appointment and title unless and
until further action is taken. Terms vary. One person on leave may be barred from campus and from
contact with students, while another continues to run a laboratory remotely. A reader should look
for the specific conditions imposed and not infer them from the phrase.
