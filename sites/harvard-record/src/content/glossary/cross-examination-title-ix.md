short: "The questioning of a party or witness by the other side, which in campus sexual harassment hearings is usually carried out by an advisor rather than by the parties themselves."

In a courtroom, cross-examination is the questioning of a witness by the party who did not call
them, meant to test their account. In campus sexual harassment cases under the 2020 federal Title IX
regulations, cross-examination takes place at the live hearing and must be conducted by each party's
advisor, never by the party in person. Before a question is answered, the decision-maker decides
whether it is relevant and explains a decision to exclude it.

The regulations limit some questions. Questions about a complainant's prior sexual behavior are
generally excluded, with narrow exceptions. Under the 2020 rule as first issued, statements by a
person who refused to be cross-examined could not be relied on; that provision was later struck down
by a court and the Department stopped enforcing it. Rules issued in 2024 relaxed the requirement,
but those rules were themselves vacated in early 2025. Readers should check the current text.

Cross-examination in this setting is not the same as in a criminal trial, where the rules of
evidence and constitutional rights apply in full. A party's choice not to take part, or not to answer,
is not an admission.
