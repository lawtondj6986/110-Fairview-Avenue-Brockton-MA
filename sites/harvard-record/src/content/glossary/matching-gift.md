---
term: "Matching gift"
short: "A gift made on condition of, or in proportion to, gifts from others, most often an employer matching its employees' donations or a donor offering a challenge."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Corporate match", "Employer match", "Challenge gift", "Challenge grant"]
related: ["corporate-foundation", "capital-campaign", "gift-counting-standards", "pledge", "lead-gift"]
---

A matching gift is one tied to another gift. The term covers two common arrangements.

In an employer matching program, a company or its foundation agrees to match charitable donations
made by its employees, up to a limit set by the company and usually at a stated ratio. The employee
gives, submits a request, and the employer sends its own payment to the same institution. In a
challenge or matching grant, a donor promises to give a certain amount if the institution raises a
specified sum from others, sometimes by a deadline or for a particular purpose. Institutions use
challenges to motivate new donors, since each gift appears to count for more.

For record keeping, the two halves of a match are separate gifts from separate donors. Under
standard counting practice, the employee is the donor of the original gift and the company is the
donor of the match, though an institution may give the individual recognition credit for both. That
means a person's recognition total can exceed what they personally paid. A challenge gift is
conditional until the condition is met, so it may be announced before it is certain. Announced
campaign figures sometimes include matching funds that came from the institution's own resources,
which is not new money.
