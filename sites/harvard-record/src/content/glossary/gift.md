term: "Gift"

A gift is money or property given voluntarily to an institution without the donor receiving goods or
services of substantial value in exchange. The absence of an exchange is what separates a gift from
a payment, a contract or a sponsored research award.

A donor may attach conditions about purpose, such as support for a particular field, scholarship or
building. Those conditions make the gift restricted, but it is still a gift so long as the donor
does not control the work, own the results or require specific deliverables. Universities record
gifts through the development office, issue a written acknowledgment and, for larger amounts, sign a
gift agreement. In the United States, gifts to qualifying charitable organizations, which include
most colleges and universities, may be deductible for the donor under federal tax law, subject to
limits and substantiation rules.

A gift does not buy a legal right to direct the institution. It also should not be confused with a
pledge, which is a promise to give later, or with a grant, which usually arrives through a formal
application and reporting process. The same donor can be the source of gifts, grants and sponsored
research at the same time, each recorded in a different system.
