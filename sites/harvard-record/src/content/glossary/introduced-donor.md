---
term: "Introduced donor"
short: "A donor who comes to an institution through the recommendation or personal connection of someone else, such as another donor, a trustee or a faculty member."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Third-party introduction", "Referred donor", "Donor referral", "Peer introduction"]

An introduced donor is one whose relationship with the institution began with a third party. Someone
already connected to the institution brings the new person in, vouches for them or encourages them
to give.

Introductions are a routine and valued source of new prospects. Trustees and campaign volunteers are
expected to open their networks. Existing donors bring friends and business associates. Faculty
members introduce people who have shown interest in their research. The development office usually
records who made the introduction, and some institutions give informal credit to the introducer in
their records, even though the gift legally belongs to the person who made it.

Introductions raise two questions for anyone reading a record. The first is attribution. When one
person encourages or arranges gifts from others, internal notes may describe those gifts as
"directed by" or "credited to" the introducer. That is a statement about influence, not about whose
money it was, and the two should be kept apart. The second is vetting. An introduction from a
trusted person can reduce the scrutiny a new donor receives, and due diligence on the new donor does
not necessarily extend to the person who made the introduction. Whether the introducer was
separately reviewed, or held any status at the institution, is a distinct question.
