A spending policy sets how much money flows from an endowment to the programs it supports each year.
The amount distributed is called the payout or distribution. Many institutions calculate it as a
percentage of the endowment's average market value over several past years, which smooths out good
and bad market years. Others adjust the previous year's payout for inflation, or combine methods.

The board sets the policy and reviews it periodically. In most states the governing law is the
Uniform Prudent Management of Institutional Funds Act (UPMIFA), which asks boards to spend prudently
in light of factors such as the fund's purpose, the institution's resources, general economic
conditions and the need to preserve the fund over time. Some states add further rules, so the
current state text should be checked. Each endowed fund receives its share of the payout, which
must then be spent within any donor restrictions on that fund.

The payout rate is not the same as the endowment's investment return. A fund can earn a high return
and pay out a modest, stable amount, or pay out in a year of losses. A large endowment also does not
mean a large pool of freely spendable money, since much of the payout is restricted.
