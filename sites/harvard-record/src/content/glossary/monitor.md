term: "Monitor (court or agency)"
short: "An independent person or firm appointed under a court order, settlement or agency agreement to check and report on whether an organization is carrying out required reforms."
category: "Legal procedure"

A monitor is an outside overseer installed to verify compliance. Monitors are named in consent
decrees, settlement agreements, deferred or non-prosecution agreements and some agency resolution
agreements. Their mandate is set by that document. It typically includes reviewing policies and
records, interviewing staff, testing whether new procedures work, and filing periodic reports with
the court, the agency or both. The organization being monitored usually pays the monitor's costs.

Monitorships last for a fixed period or until the organization meets defined benchmarks. A monitor's
reports can be among the most detailed public accounts of how an institution is implementing a
reform, though some are filed under seal or kept confidential under the agreement. Agencies
sometimes use the term loosely for their own staff who track a resolution agreement.

A monitor is not an investigator of past wrongdoing and does not decide liability. A monitor's
favorable report shows that agreed steps were taken during the review period, not that every
underlying problem has been solved. The end of a monitorship is also distinct from the end of the
agreement's other obligations, which may continue.
