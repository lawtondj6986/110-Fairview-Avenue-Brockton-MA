A reliability grade is a short label attached to a source that tells the reader how strong that kind
of source is. Grading schemes are used in intelligence analysis, in evidence-based medicine and in
some reference works. Their purpose is to make the basis of each claim visible and to stop weak
material from being presented with the same confidence as strong material.

This site uses four grades, set out in full at `/methodology/`. In summary, A1 is a primary official
record speaking for itself. A2 is reporting by an established outlet that closely follows primary
documents or records an on-the-record statement. B1 is secondary analysis with clear sourcing, used
for background. B2 is thin or unverified material, such as a single uncorroborated account, which is
always flagged in the text. The site requires an A1 or A2 source for a factual claim about a named
living person.

A grade describes the type of source. It is not a judgment of the people the source discusses, and
it does not certify that every statement in the source is true. A complaint filed in court is graded
as a primary record of what was alleged, and remains an allegation. A grade is also separate from
the evidence state of a claim, which records whether something was merely proposed, was documented
as done, or was confirmed.
