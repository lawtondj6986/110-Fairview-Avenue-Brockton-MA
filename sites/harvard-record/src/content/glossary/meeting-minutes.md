Minutes are the formal record of a meeting. They usually list members present and absent, guests,
the items on the agenda, motions made and votes taken, and short summaries of discussion. They are
typically drafted by a secretary or staff member and approved by the body at a later meeting, which
gives them official status.

For a researcher, minutes can establish when a body considered an issue, what it decided and who
was in the room. Faculty meeting minutes at some institutions are published or held in university
archives. Governing board minutes of private universities are generally not public, although public
universities may be subject to open meetings and public records laws, which vary by state. Many
boards discuss sensitive matters in executive session, which may be minuted separately or only
briefly.

Minutes are a summary, not a transcript. They are often written to record decisions while saying
little about debate, and the wording of approved minutes can be negotiated. The absence of an item
from minutes does not show that it was never discussed. Draft minutes and approved minutes can
differ, so check which version a document is.
