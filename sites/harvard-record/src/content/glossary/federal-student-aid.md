Federal Student Aid, often shortened to FSA, is the part of the Department of Education that
administers the student aid programs created under Title IV of the Higher Education Act. A college
that wants its students to receive federal grants and loans signs a program participation agreement
and must meet conditions set by the statute and regulations.

Those conditions include the Clery Act's campus safety disclosure requirements. FSA has been
responsible for reviewing institutions' compliance with them, often through program reviews that
examine crime statistics, policy statements, timely warnings and records. A review can end in a
report of findings, required corrective actions and, in some cases, a fine. The Department publishes
some final program review determinations and fine actions.

FSA is a different office from the Office for Civil Rights, which enforces Title IX and other civil
rights laws. Its campus safety reviews ask whether an institution followed reporting and disclosure
rules, not whether a particular crime occurred or a particular investigation reached the right
result. How the Department organizes Clery enforcement has changed over time, so current
arrangements should be checked.
