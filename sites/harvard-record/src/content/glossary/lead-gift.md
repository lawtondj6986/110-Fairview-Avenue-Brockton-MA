---
term: "Lead gift"
short: "A large early commitment to a campaign or project that supplies a substantial share of the goal and is used to encourage others to give."
category: "Fundraising and development"
aliases: ["Leadership gift", "Cornerstone gift", "Anchor gift", "Nucleus gift"]

A lead gift is the largest, or one of the largest, gifts to a campaign or project, secured near the
start. Its purpose is partly financial and partly persuasive. It shows other donors that the effort
is credible and sets the scale for the gifts that follow.

Campaign planners typically build a gift table showing how many gifts of each size are needed to
reach the goal, with one or a few lead gifts at the top. These are sought first, during the quiet
phase, from the prospects with the greatest capacity and the closest ties, often trustees. For a
building or a new institute, the lead gift usually carries the principal naming. Many institutions
will not publicly announce a campaign or start construction until lead gifts are in hand.

A lead gift is defined by its role, so the same amount might be a lead gift for a departmental
project and an ordinary major gift in a university-wide campaign. Because so much depends on it, a
lead donor may have more influence over the shape of a project at the planning stage than later
donors do, for instance over scope or emphasis. That is a matter of negotiation before acceptance.
After acceptance, the usual rule applies: the institution controls the program.
