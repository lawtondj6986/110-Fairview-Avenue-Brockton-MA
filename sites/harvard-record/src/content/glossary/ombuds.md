---
term: "Ombuds"
short: "An independent, impartial and confidential office that helps members of an institution raise and resolve concerns informally, without conducting formal investigations."
category: "Campus safety"
aliases: ["ombudsman", "ombudsperson", "organizational ombuds"]

An ombuds is a designated neutral within an organization. In universities, the ombuds office is a
place where students, faculty or staff can discuss a problem in confidence, learn what options and
policies apply, and get help resolving it through coaching, shuttle diplomacy or mediation. The
office also commonly reports general trends to senior leaders without identifying anyone.

Professional standards for organizational ombuds rest on four principles. The office is independent
of ordinary management lines. It is impartial and does not take sides. It is confidential, to the
extent the law allows. It is informal, meaning it does not investigate, adjudicate, keep official
records for the institution, or impose outcomes.

These features have consequences. Telling the ombuds about a problem is generally not notice to the
institution, so it does not start a formal process or create an official record. Someone who wants
the institution to act must use a formal channel. The ombuds is also not an advocate for the
visitor, and confidentiality is usually a matter of policy and practice instead of a legal privilege
recognized everywhere. It typically yields where there is an imminent risk of serious harm.

Whether a campus ombuds counts as a confidential resource for Title IX and Clery purposes depends on
the institution's own designation.
