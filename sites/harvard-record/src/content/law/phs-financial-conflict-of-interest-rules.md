## What it does

Subpart F of 42 C.F.R. Part 50 is titled "Promoting Objectivity in Research." Its aim is to give a reasonable expectation that the design, conduct and reporting of research funded by the Public Health Service will be free from bias caused by an investigator's financial interests. A parallel rule at 45 C.F.R. Part 94 applies to research contracts. The rules were substantially revised in 2011.

The system has three steps.

Disclosure. Each investigator must disclose to the institution their significant financial interests, and those of their spouse and dependent children, that reasonably appear related to the investigator's institutional responsibilities. Disclosure is due no later than the time of applying for funding, at least once a year during the award, and within 30 days of acquiring a new interest.

Review. A designated institutional official decides whether a disclosed interest relates to the funded research and, if so, whether it is a financial conflict of interest. That term means a significant financial interest that could directly and significantly affect the design, conduct or reporting of the research.

Management and reporting. If there is a conflict, the institution must put a management plan in place. A plan might require public disclosure in papers and talks, independent monitoring, changes to the research plan, limits on the investigator's role, or sale of the interest. The institution must report the conflict to the funding agency before spending the award funds, and each year afterward.

A significant financial interest is defined with a dollar threshold. For a publicly traded entity, it exists when payments received in the previous twelve months plus the value of any equity together exceed $5,000. For an entity that is not publicly traded, it exists when payments exceed $5,000 in the previous twelve months, or when the investigator holds any equity interest at all. Income from intellectual property rights counts once income is received. Investigators must also disclose reimbursed or sponsored travel related to their institutional responsibilities, with exceptions.

Institutions must also have a written policy and post it publicly, train investigators before they do funded research and at least every four years, and extend the requirements to subrecipients.

There is a public access provision. Before spending funds, the institution must make information about the financial conflicts of interest of senior and key personnel available to the public, either on a public website or by written response to any request within five business days. The information includes the person's name, title and role, the name of the entity, the nature of the interest, and its approximate value in ranges.

## Who it covers

The rule applies to institutions that apply for or receive Public Health Service research funding, mainly from the National Institutes of Health, and through them to investigators. An investigator is anyone responsible for the design, conduct or reporting of the research, whatever their title. That can include collaborators and consultants.

Other federal research funders have their own conflict of interest policies, with different thresholds and procedures.

## What it does not do

The rule is about the personal financial interests of individual investigators. That focus sets its limits.

It does not address institutional conflicts of interest, such as an institution's own equity in a company whose product its researchers are testing, or the influence of a major donor on institutional decisions.

It does not cover gifts to the institution. A donation made to a university to support a laboratory is not the investigator's personal financial interest, even though it may matter a great deal to the investigator's work. Such gifts are governed by the institution's gift policies and, in federal grant applications, by separate rules about disclosing other research support.

It does not count certain income. Excluded are salary and other payments from the investigator's own institution, income from mutual funds and retirement accounts where the investigator does not control the investment decisions, and income from seminars, lectures, teaching or service on advisory or review panels for United States government agencies, United States institutions of higher education, academic teaching hospitals, medical centers and affiliated research institutes. Payments from foreign institutions and foreign governments are not within these exclusions and must be disclosed if they meet the threshold.

It does not prohibit financial interests. It requires disclosure and management. Most conflicts are managed, not eliminated.

It does not make the investigator's disclosures to the institution public. Only identified conflicts of senior and key personnel on funded projects fall within the public access provision.

It does not cover non-financial interests such as personal relationships, intellectual commitments or reputational stakes.

## How it is enforced

Enforcement runs through the funding agency. If an investigator fails to disclose, or the institution fails to review or manage an interest in time, the institution must carry out a retrospective review of the research to determine whether it was biased, document the review, and, if bias is found, notify the agency and submit a mitigation report.

The agency may ask for records at any time. It may impose special award conditions, suspend funding, or take other enforcement action available under grant rules. For clinical research testing the safety or effectiveness of a drug, device or treatment, where a conflicted investigator did not have the conflict managed or reported, the institution must require the investigator to disclose the conflict in each public presentation of the results and to request an addendum to earlier presentations.

Knowing false statements to a federal agency can carry separate civil or criminal consequences under other laws.

## Common misreadings

A disclosed interest is not a conflict. A conflict exists only after the institution determines that the interest could directly and significantly affect the research.

A managed conflict is not a violation. It is the system working as designed.

A disclosure line in a journal article follows the journal's policy, which is usually broader than this rule.
