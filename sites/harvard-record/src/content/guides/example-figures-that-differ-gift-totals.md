This worked example applies the guide on [figures that differ](/guides/method-figures-that-differ/)
to numbers already in this site's record. It adds no facts. Each figure below restates a row in the
[claim ledger](/ledger/), with its caveat. Two cases are covered: one where sources round the same
total differently, and one where two figures look like a conflict but count different things.

## Case 1: one total, several roundings

[Ledger F-01](/ledger/#F-01) records that Epstein's gifts to Harvard totalled about $9.1 million
between 1998 and 2008 [@harvard-2020-report]. The row is graded A1 and sourced to the General
Counsel's report and the President's message that released it [@bacow-2020]. Its caveat reads:
University-wide total. Some coverage gives $9,179,000 or "nearly $9.2 million."

### Step 1: identify what each figure counts

All three figures describe the same thing: the total of Epstein's own gifts to the whole University
over one decade. None is a figure for a single program, and none includes money from other donors.
The period, the giver and the recipient are the same in each.

### Step 2: decide which figure the page uses, and say why

The site uses "about $9.1 million" because that is the figure in the row's A1 sources. It keeps the
qualifier "about," as the method requires. It does not silently replace the figure with a more
precise one from coverage, and it does not drop the coverage figures either. The caveat carries
them wherever the total appears, and the [Program for Evolutionary Dynamics
page](/program-for-evolutionary-dynamics/) states them beside the total.

One point deserves care. $9,179,000 rounded to one decimal place is $9.2 million, not $9.1 million.
The figures are close, and they describe the same total, but a reader doing the arithmetic will see
that "about $9.1 million" is not a rounding of $9,179,000. The honest course is the one the ledger
takes: keep the A1 wording, show the other figures, and do not claim to have reconciled them.

### Step 3: keep the total separate from its parts

[Ledger F-02](/ledger/#F-02) records that a $6.5 million gift in 2003 established the Program for
Evolutionary Dynamics [@harvard-2020-report]. That gift falls inside the 1998 to 2008 period. It is
part of the story the total tells, not an addition to it. Adding $6.5 million to $9.1 million would
count one gift twice.

## Case 2: $7 million and $7.5 million

Two figures for introduced donors sit close together on the [donor pathways
page](/donor-pathways/). They do not conflict.

### Step 1: trace each figure to its row

[Ledger D-01](/ledger/#D-01) records that between 2010 and 2015, donors Epstein had introduced gave
$7.5 million for Martin Nowak's work [@harvard-2020-report]. It is graded A1.

[Ledger D-04](/ledger/#D-04) records that about $7 million of the gifts for Professor Nowak's work
came from Leon Black, his wife and their family foundation [@axios-2020-black]. It is graded A2, and
its caveat reads: whether the report itself names the donors must be confirmed in the PDF.

### Step 2: compare the definitions

The two figures count different groups. D-01 counts all introduced donors. D-04 counts one donor
family. The words "of the gifts" in D-04 place the $7 million inside the $7.5 million, and the donor
pathways page says so in terms. The site's [index of names](/named/) gives the period for that
subset as 2011 to 2015, inside the 2010 to 2015 period of D-01. The figures differ because the
second is a part of the first.

The two rows also differ in grade. The total comes from the institutional report. The attribution to
one family comes from reporting on the report, and the ledger flags that the attribution has not yet
been checked against the report's text. A page that uses both figures should keep both grades
visible.

### Step 3: carry the responses with the figures

[Ledger D-03](/ledger/#D-03) records that the donors denied that Epstein directed their gifts, and
the review found no evidence that he did [@crimson-2020-report]. Ledger D-05 records that a
representative of Leon Black confirmed the introduction and said the funds were not provided by
Epstein [@axios-2020-black]. Any sentence that gives the $7 million or $7.5 million should be read
with these rows. An introduction is documented. Direction of the money is not.

### Step 4: watch for a third $7.5 million

[Ledger D-06](/ledger/#D-06) records that the Black family foundation's 2018 gift of $7.5 million
for military and veteran fellowships has no public documented connection to Epstein
[@hks-2018-black]. Its state is nothing on record, and its caveat notes that the gift itself is
documented by the school's announcement. The amount matches D-01 exactly. The donor overlaps with
D-04. The year, the purpose and the recipient programs are different. A matching number is not
evidence that two gifts are the same, and the site does not treat this gift as part of the
introduced-donor pathway.

## Doing the arithmetic in the open

The donor pathways page gives $9.5 million from introduced donors. That figure is a sum, and the
sum should be shown: $7.5 million for Professor Nowak's work (Ledger D-01) plus $2 million for
George Church's work in the same period (Ledger D-02) equals $9.5 million. Both rows come from the
same A1 source and cover the same years, which is why adding them is sound.

What should not be added is $9.1 million and $9.5 million. The first is Epstein's own gifts from
1998 to 2008. The second is other donors' gifts from 2010 to 2015. Different givers, different
periods. A combined figure would describe nothing the record counts.

## Wording that follows

- "Harvard's review counted about $9.1 million in gifts from Epstein to the University between 1998
  and 2008. Some coverage gives $9,179,000."
- "Of the $7.5 million that introduced donors gave for Professor Nowak's work, reporting attributes
  about $7 million to Leon Black, his wife and their family foundation. A representative said the
  funds were not provided by Epstein."

Each sentence names the source, the period and what is counted. For the general rules, see [how to
read a donation statistic](/guides/read-a-donation-statistic/).
