Prospective students, current students and families are entitled to ask a college how it handles
reports of sexual harassment, assault and other misconduct, and what support it gives to people
affected. Colleges that receive federal funding have obligations in this area under federal law,
including [Title IX](/law/title-ix/) and the [Clery Act](/law/clery-act/), which are explained in
the [law explainers](/law/). The details of those rules have changed more than once in recent years,
so this template asks the institution to describe its current practice instead of assuming one.

The questions are general. They are not legal advice, and they are not a substitute for help in an
urgent situation. Anyone in immediate danger should contact emergency services. General orientation
is on the [campus safety page](/campus-safety/).

## Who to ask

- An admissions office can answer general questions and should be able to direct you to the right
  office.
- A student-affairs office or dean of students deals with student support and conduct.
- The Title IX coordinator is the official responsible for the institution's response to sex-based
  discrimination and harassment. Institutions covered by Title IX are required to designate one and
  to publish contact details.
- The campus safety or police department is responsible for the annual security report and the crime
  log.

## The template

```text
Subject: Questions about reporting options and student support

Dear [Office name / Title IX Coordinator / Dean of Students],

I am [a prospective student / an admitted student / a current student /
a parent of a prospective student] at [or: considering] [institution
name]. I would like to understand how the institution handles reports
of sexual harassment, sexual assault, stalking and relationship
violence, and what support is available. I would be grateful for
answers to the questions below, or links to where they are published.

Where to find things
1. Where are the current policies on sexual misconduct and
   non-discrimination published? When were they last revised?
2. Where can I read the most recent annual security report?
3. Who is the Title IX coordinator, and how can that person be
   reached?

Making a report
4. What are the ways to make a report (in person, online, by phone),
   and can a report be made anonymously?
5. Which staff are confidential resources, who can keep what they are
   told private, and which staff are required to pass a report on to
   the institution?
6. What is the difference between telling the institution about an
   incident and filing a formal complaint? Can someone receive support
   without filing a formal complaint?
7. How does reporting to the institution relate to reporting to the
   police? Will the institution help a student who wishes to do either,
   both or neither?

Support
8. What supportive measures are available (for example, changes to
   housing, classes or schedules, or no-contact arrangements), and are
   they available to both the person reporting and the person
   responding?
9. What counseling, medical and advocacy services are available, on
   campus and nearby? Is there a cost, and is there a limit on
   sessions?
10. Is support available outside office hours?

Process
11. What are the main steps after a formal complaint is filed, and
    about how long does each usually take?
12. Who investigates, and who decides the outcome? What training do
    they receive?
13. What standard of evidence is used?
14. May each party have an adviser, and may the adviser be a lawyer?
15. What appeal rights exist?
16. What protections exist against retaliation, and how is a concern
    about retaliation raised?

Scope
17. Do the policies cover incidents off campus, during study abroad,
    in affiliated programs, internships or research placements, and
    online?
18. Do the policies cover conduct by people who are not students or
    employees, such as visitors, donors, volunteers or contractors?
    How would a concern about such a person be raised?
19. How do the procedures differ when the person complained about is a
    faculty or staff member?

Information and prevention
20. Does the institution publish statistics on reports received and
    their outcomes, beyond the annual security report?
21. What prevention and awareness programs are provided for students
    and employees, and are they required?
22. Has the institution conducted a campus climate survey, and are the
    results published?

Thank you for your help. I would appreciate a reply by [date].

[Full name]
[Email address]
```

## Notes on adapting it

Choose your questions. Twenty-two is the full list. For a campus visit or a short email, pick five
or six. Questions 5, 6, 8, 11 and 18 often reveal the most about how well a process is understood by
the people who run it.

Do not share personal details. This template is for learning about process. Do not include details
of any incident in a general enquiry. Some staff are required to pass on what they are told about an
incident, which is why question 5 comes early. If you need to talk about something that happened,
start with a confidential resource.

## Reading the answers

- Specific and consistent answers are a good sign. Compare what you are told with the published
  policy. Differences are worth a follow-up question.
- A policy describes what is supposed to happen. In the terms used on the [methodology
  page](/methodology/), it is stated intent. It does not show how cases are handled in practice.
- Statistics need context. Published crime figures count reports that fall within defined categories
  and locations. A low number can reflect few incidents or few reports. A higher number can reflect
  a campus where people feel able to report. A guide in the Reading documents series covers
  [how to read a security report](/guides/read-a-campus-security-report/).

## What these questions cannot tell you

They cannot tell you how any individual case would be handled, or whether an institution is safe.
They establish what the institution says its process is, and whether its staff can explain it. For
the federal record of complaints and resolutions involving an institution, see the [civil-rights
records request template](/guides/template-foia-civil-rights-office-records/) in this series.
