This worked example applies the [three-state discipline](/guides/method-three-state-discipline/) to
one document already in this site's record: an invoice sent in June 2014 and reported from the
Justice Department release. It adds no facts. Every statement below restates a row in the
[claim ledger](/ledger/), and each row's caveat travels with it. The invoice is set out line by line
on the [Harvard Medical School page](/harvard-medical-school/).

## Step 1: identify the document and its author

The invoice came from Joseph Thakuria, then a physician at Massachusetts General Hospital who was
not directly employed by Harvard (Ledger G-05) [@cnn-2026-thakuria]. That ledger row carries two
caveats that belong in any paragraph naming him: the hospital says it has no record of approving the
studies described, and no wrongdoing is alleged.

Two features of the document matter before any line is sorted. First, the site cites it through
CNN's reporting, graded A2, because the [methodology](/methodology/) cites documents from the
release through established reporting until the original can be cited directly. Second, an invoice
is written by the party asking to be paid. Under the method, a record made by the person proposing
work is evidence of intent unless something else shows the act.

## Step 2: split the document into separate claims

One invoice, several claims. The method says to split a compound claim and give each part its own
state. The ledger does this in two rows.

[Ledger G-03](/ledger/#G-03) records that the invoice listed an initial $2,000: $1,000 for exome
sequencing and $1,000 for fibroblast sequencing [@cnn-2026-thakuria]. [Ledger G-04](/ledger/#G-04)
records that the same invoice priced further work to a total of $193,400, including a $160,000 item
called "The Venus Project" [@cnn-2026-thakuria].

The ledger does not say whether the $193,400 total includes the initial $2,000. This example
therefore does not subtract one figure from the other, and it does not describe the difference as an
amount of anything.

## Step 3: find the most advanced thing the record shows

Take the two $1,000 lines first. The record shows more than a price for them. Dr Thakuria said a
$2,000 check was provided for the sequencing. That statement is what places these lines at
documented activity. The caveat on Ledger G-03 says so in plain terms: payment rests on his
statement. The activity therefore rests on one person's account, carried by one outlet, and the
sentence on the page should let the reader see that.

Now take everything else. For those lines the record shows a price and nothing after it. Ledger
G-04 is stated intent, and its caveat reads: no record of payment or performance. Dr Thakuria
described the idea as preliminary and unfunded [@cnn-2026-thakuria]. In his own account, as the
site's [index of names](/named/) records it, he said the $2,000 check covered sequencing, that
Epstein was a research subject, and that the larger project was a preliminary idea that never went
anywhere.

A priced proposal is not a payment. A $160,000 line on an invoice is a figure someone wrote down. It
is not $160,000 of work, and it is not evidence that work began.

## Step 4: test each line against verified implementation

Verified implementation needs an official finding or an independent result that confirms the
activity and what it produced. Apply each half of that test in turn.

- An official finding. None of the ledger rows about the invoice cites one. The hospital's position,
  recorded in the caveat to Ledger G-05, is that it has no record of approving the studies. That is
  a statement about the hospital's records. It is not a finding about the work.
- An independent result. [Ledger G-10](/ledger/#G-10) records that no public record shows a shared
  genetic database, a research output or an operational program connecting the DNA-related items,
  and its caveat marks this as a statement of absence across the sources reviewed
  [@cnn-2026-thakuria].

Neither half is met for any line. The sequencing therefore stops at documented activity, and the
priced proposals stay at stated intent. No line reaches the third state. That is not a judgement
that nothing happened. It is a statement of how far the public record goes.

## Step 5: write each sentence at its own level

The state should be visible in the verb, not only in a label beside the sentence.

- For the sequencing: "Dr Thakuria said a $2,000 check covered exome and fibroblast sequencing."
  The verb "said" keeps the payment attached to the person who reported it.
- For the proposals: "The same invoice priced further work to a total of $193,400. We found no
  record of payment or performance." The verb "priced" describes what the document does.
- Avoid any sentence that describes the priced items as a project that took place, or that gives
  $193,400 as an amount spent. Both would move a proposal up a state with no source for the move.

Volume does not change this. Repeating the $193,400 figure in several places does not make it more
than a price, and the size of the number is not evidence of anything except what was written on the
page.

## What this example does not show

The states describe the record. They say nothing about what anyone intended or why the invoice was
written, and this example offers no view on that. The reporting states that Dr Thakuria is not
accused of wrongdoing, and nothing in the ledger rows used here suggests otherwise.
