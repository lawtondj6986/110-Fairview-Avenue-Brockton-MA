 This worked example applies the guide on [avoiding false
patterns](/guides/method-avoiding-false-patterns/) to the [DNA and genetics record](/dna-genetics/).
It adds no facts. Each statement restates a row in the [claim ledger](/ledger/), with its caveat.
The question is simple: several DNA-related items appear in the record, and the site does not
present them as one undertaking. This example shows why.

## Step 1: list the items, one row each

Laid out by date, the items are these.

- November 2012. Southern Trust Company was described to a U.S. Virgin Islands tax-incentive
  commission as consulting in "biomedical and financial informatics" (Ledger V-01)
  [@source-2022-settlement]. Stated intent.
- 2013. An attempt to prioritize Epstein's tissue sample caused a dispute in George Church's lab,
  and objections stopped special treatment (Ledger G-01) [@stat-2026-tissue]. Documented activity.
  Professor Church confirmed the episode and said he had not personally pressed for priority.
- June 2014. An invoice from Joseph Thakuria listed an initial $2,000 for exome and fibroblast
  sequencing ([Ledger G-03](/ledger/#G-03)) [@cnn-2026-thakuria]. Documented activity, with the
  caveat that payment rests on his statement that a $2,000 check was provided. The same invoice
  priced further work to a total of $193,400 (Ledger G-04). Stated intent, with no record of payment
  or performance.
- September 2016 to summer 2017. Emails describe 23andMe consumer kits sent or ordered as gifts
  (Ledger G-06, G-07, G-08) [@nypost-2026-kits]. Documented activity. The caveats say receiving a
  kit implies nothing about the recipient.
- Undated. Epstein told people of a wish to have women inseminated at his New Mexico ranch (Ledger
  G-09) [@nyt-2019-seed]. Stated intent, graded B2 because the fullest account is second-hand. The
  newspaper reported no evidence the idea was acted on.

## Step 2: ask what the items share

Set side by side, the items share a subject (DNA, broadly) and one person. That is all the list
itself shows.

They do not share a date: they spread over about five years. They do not share actors. The kits were
handled by staff for named recipients. The sequencing involved a hospital physician. The
lab dispute involved a university laboratory. The informatics description was given to a government
commission. They do not share an evidence state either. Some are documented activity, some are
stated intent, and one is a relayed remark graded B2.

## Step 3: look for a document that joins them

The method's central test is whether any source connects the items. Adjacency in a list is not a
connection. A source has to say what the connection is.

[Ledger G-10](/ledger/#G-10) answers the question for this record. No public record shows a shared
genetic database, a research output or an operational program connecting these items
[@cnn-2026-thakuria]. Its caveat marks it as a statement of absence across the sources reviewed.
That is a limit on the search, not a finding that nothing could exist, and it is written that way.

The [methodology](/methodology/) turns this into a rule: no inference of coordination. Two facts
side by side are not presented as connected unless a source connects them.

## Step 4: check the strongest-sounding item

The informatics description is the item most likely to be read as a frame for the others. The
ledger treats it with care. Ledger V-01 is stated intent: it records what the company was described
as doing. [Ledger V-03](/ledger/#V-03) records that the territory's Attorney General alleged that
Southern Trust made fraudulent misrepresentations to obtain tax benefits and performed no visible
informatics services [@usvi-complaint]. The caveat says this is an allegation in a complaint, and
that the case settled without a ruling.

So the description does not show genetic work, and the allegation against it does not show genetic
work either. Neither supplies the missing connection.

## Step 5: calibrate with an outside case

The page offers one outside reference point. Ledger G-11 records that the Repository for Germinal
Choice opened in 1980 and produced about 215 children [@plotz-2005]. It is graded B1 and its caveat
says it is background calibration only. Its use here is limited: it reminds the reader how far a
stated plan can sit from an operational one. It says nothing about any item in this record.

## Writing the items honestly

The [DNA and genetics record](/dna-genetics/) describes each item in its own section, at its own
state, and closes with an assessment that the items sit side by side and do not connect. A heading
such as "the DNA program" would assert in four words what no source states. So would verbs such as
"linked" or "part of," which name a relationship without saying what it is.

Useful wording keeps each item separate:

- "The record shows one $2,000 sequencing job, according to the physician who sent the invoice."
- "Staff bought and sent consumer test kits as gifts."
- "A business description given to a tax commission was later alleged in a complaint to be false.
  The case settled without a ruling."

## Fairness to the people in the list

A list of items invites a reader to treat everyone in it as part of one story. The people here
appear in very different capacities. The reporting states that Dr Thakuria is not accused of
wrongdoing. Professor Church has said he did not press for special treatment of the sample. The
people named as recipients of test kits are named only as recipients, and they are not implicated in
anything. Jaron Lanier, whose account is relayed in the ranch reporting, appears only as a source.
Keeping the items apart keeps each person in the role the record gives them.
