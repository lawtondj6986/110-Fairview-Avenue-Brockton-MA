This worked example applies the [three-state discipline](/guides/method-three-state-discipline/)
and the site's rules on numbers to one item on the [Harvard Medical School
page](/harvard-medical-school/): a donor rating reported from the school's development records. It
adds no facts. Each statement restates a row in the [claim ledger](/ledger/), with its caveat. The
rule it applies comes from the site's style guide: a capacity rating is not a gift.

## Step 1: read the row exactly

[Ledger D-08](/ledger/#D-08) records that Harvard Medical School's development records generated a
$75 million "active" donor rating for Epstein in July 2012 [@crimson-2026-hms-rating]. It is graded
A2 and its state is documented activity.

The caveat is longer than the claim, and every part of it matters: a capacity estimate, not a gift,
pledge or solicitation; single outlet; Harvard declined to comment.

## Step 2: name the kind of record

A development office is the part of a university that raises money. Its records hold estimates of
what prospective donors could give, as one input to planning. The caveat calls this rating exactly
that: a capacity estimate. It is a number a system or a staff member assigned to a person. It is not
a number of dollars that moved.

So the documented activity in D-08 is the generation of a rating. That is the act the record shows.
The row does not record anyone acting on the rating.

## Step 3: list what the rating does not show

The caveat names three things, and each one is a separate step up that the record does not take.

- Not a gift. No money is recorded as arriving.
- Not a pledge. No promise to give is recorded.
- Not a solicitation. No request for a gift is recorded.

A page that described the rating as Harvard "seeking" $75 million, or as Epstein "worth" $75 million
to the school, would add one of these steps. Neither is in the row. The word "active" is quoted from
the reporting and should stay in quotation marks, because the ledger does not define what the label
meant inside the system.

## Step 4: set the rating beside the gift record, carefully

The rating is reported for July 2012. [Ledger F-03](/ledger/#F-03) records that Harvard accepted no
gifts from Epstein after his 2008 conviction [@harvard-2020-report]. Its caveat says the decision
was made by the President in 2008 and was not formally recorded at the time. The dates differ by
four years: 2012 minus 2008.

Placing these two rows together is legitimate because both concern the same person and the same
question, whether Harvard took his money. What the combination supports is narrow: a rating was
reportedly generated after the decision not to accept his gifts, and the institutional review found
no gifts accepted after that decision. It does not support a claim that the decision was set aside,
or that anyone at the school planned to seek a gift. The caveat on F-03, that the decision was not
formally recorded at the time, is a fact about the record of the decision. It does not explain the
rating, and this example offers no explanation.

## Step 5: compare with money the record documents

The same page records money that did arrive, and the contrast shows what a documented gift looks
like.

Ledger F-06 records that before 2008, Epstein's foundation gave $686,000 to support George Church's
research [@stat-2019-church]. Its caveat says the figure is attributed to a footnote of the report
and must be checked in the PDF. [Ledger D-02](/ledger/#D-02) records that between 2010 and 2015,
introduced donors gave $2 million for Professor Church's work [@harvard-2020-report]. The donors
denied that Epstein directed their gifts, and the review found no evidence that he did (Ledger
D-03).

Those rows describe gifts given. D-08 describes an estimate of capacity. The three figures should
never be added together, and the $75 million should never appear in a sentence about amounts given.

## Step 6: weigh the source

D-08 rests on one outlet, and the source register notes that the reporting is single-outlet and that
Harvard declined to comment. A single-outlet report is still graded A2 if the outlet tracks the
record, but the page has to say it is single-outlet, and the ledger caveat does. Declining to
comment is not a denial, and it is not an admission. It means no response is on the record.

If the underlying development record, or a statement from the school, is later published, the row
can be regraded and its state checked again. Until then the row stays where it is.

## Wording that follows

- "The Crimson reported that Harvard Medical School's development records generated a $75 million
  'active' donor rating for Epstein in July 2012. A rating of this kind is an estimate of giving
  capacity, not a gift, pledge or solicitation. Harvard declined to comment."
- Avoid "Harvard valued Epstein at $75 million," "Harvard targeted a $75 million gift" and any
  sentence in which the rating is the subject of a verb of giving or asking.
