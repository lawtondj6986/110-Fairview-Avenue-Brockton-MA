A grade describes the kind of source you are holding. It is not a verdict on the people the source discusses, and it is not a measure of how interesting the source is. This site uses four grades, defined at `/methodology/`. This guide shows how to apply them consistently.

## The four grades

- A1, primary official record. An official document speaking for itself: an institutional report, a court filing, a government release, a statute, or hearing minutes.
- A2, established reporting that tracks the record. Reporting by an established outlet that quotes or closely follows primary documents, or that records an on-the-record statement.
- B1, secondary analysis. Books, scholarship or analysis with clear sourcing and context. Useful for background and calibration.
- B2, thin or unverified. A single uncorroborated account, a second-hand recollection, or material you could not check against a primary document.

## Five questions to ask

Take the source in hand and answer these in order.

1. Who produced it? An official body acting in its official role, a news organization, an author, or an unknown party.
2. How close is it to the event? A record made at the time by a participant or an office is closer than a later retelling.
3. Can you see what it rests on? Look for quoted documents, named speakers, footnotes or exhibits.
4. Can you open the underlying material yourself? If the source cites a filing, try to obtain the filing.
5. Is there a correction process? Established outlets and official bodies publish corrections. Anonymous pages usually do not.

A source that is official and speaks for itself is A1. A source from an established outlet that shows its documentary basis is A2. A sourced work of analysis is B1. Anything you cannot trace is B2.

## Grade the claim's path, not just the container

One document can carry material of different grades. Three rules deal with this.

First, when a well-sourced article relays a weaker account, the weaker grade applies to that account. If a careful newspaper story includes one anonymous recollection, the story may be A2 overall, but that recollection is B2.

Second, a primary record of an allegation is A1 for what it says, not for whether it is true. A civil complaint is an official court filing. It is first-rate evidence that the plaintiff made those allegations on that date. It is not a finding. Always describe its contents as allegations.

Third, an institution's report about itself is A1 as a record of what the institution found and said. It is still the institution's own account. Note who commissioned it, what it was asked to examine, and what it says it did not examine.

## A worked example

Suppose you have four items about a hypothetical gift to a research center.

- A report published by the university's general counsel that lists the gift, the date and the amount. Official body, own records, published under its name. Grade A1. The amount and date can be stated as findings of the report.
- A national newspaper article that quotes the report and adds an on-the-record statement from a university spokesperson. It tracks the record and names its speaker. Grade A2. The statement can be quoted as the university's response.
- A book chapter that discusses the gift in the context of university fundraising, with endnotes. Sourced analysis. Grade B1. Use it for context. Do not use it as the only basis for a new factual claim about a named person.
- A forum post saying a former staff member "heard" the gift was larger. Unknown author, second-hand, no document. Grade B2. If you mention it at all, flag it in the text, and do not use it for a claim about a named living person.

Now suppose the newspaper article also says, citing "a person familiar with the matter", that a second gift was discussed. That sentence is B2 within an A2 article. It also describes stated intent, not activity.

## Common traps

- Prestige is not grade. A famous outlet repeating an unsourced claim has not raised the claim's grade.
- Many outlets are often one source. Trace each story back. If ten articles all rest on one filing, you have one A1 document and ten pointers to it.
- A screenshot is not the document. An image of a page, with no route to the original, is B2 until you find the original.
- Press releases are official but partial. A release is A1 for what the issuer announced. It is not independent confirmation of the facts announced.
- Wikis and unofficial mirrors are leads. A separate guide in this series explains why.

## What follows from a grade

On this site, a factual claim about a named living person needs an A1 or A2 source. B1 sources supply background. B2 material is always flagged in the text and never carries a claim about a named person by itself. You may set your own thresholds, but set them before you start and apply them to every claim, including the ones you would like to be true.

## Record the reasoning

Write one line beside each grade saying why you chose it, for example "A1: court filing obtained from the court's docket" or "B2: single anonymous account, no document". Grades assigned without a reason tend to drift upward over time. A recorded reason lets a second reader disagree with you usefully, and lets you regrade quickly when a better source appears.
