This worked example applies the site's rules on allegations, set out in [writing about named
people](/guides/method-writing-about-named-people/) and [grading a
source](/guides/method-grading-a-source/), to the U.S. Virgin Islands material on the [DNA and
genetics record](/dna-genetics/). It adds no facts. Each statement restates a row in the [claim
ledger](/ledger/), with its caveat.

## Step 1: put the documents in order

Four ledger rows cover the material. Read them by date, and note what kind of record each rests on.

- [Ledger V-01](/ledger/#V-01). In November 2012 Southern Trust Company was described to a
  tax-incentive commission as consulting in "biomedical and financial informatics"
  [@source-2022-settlement]. Graded A2, from local reporting. State: stated intent.
- Ledger V-02. The company received a ten-year package of tax incentives in 2013
  [@source-2022-settlement]. Graded A2. State: documented activity.
- [Ledger V-03](/ledger/#V-03). The territory's Attorney General alleged that Southern Trust made
  fraudulent misrepresentations to obtain the benefits and performed no visible informatics
  services [@usvi-complaint]. Graded A1, from the complaint. State: stated intent. Caveat: an
  allegation in a complaint; the case settled without a ruling.
- [Ledger V-04](/ledger/#V-04). The case settled in November 2022 for more than $105 million; the
  government said more than $80 million in tax benefits would be returned [@usvi-2022-settlement].
  Graded A1, from the government's release. State: verified.

## Step 2: separate the grade of the complaint from the truth of its contents

The complaint is graded A1. That can look like a contradiction: a top-grade source whose contents
are not treated as established. It is not one. The [methodology](/methodology/) says a primary
record of an allegation is graded A1 for what it says and is always described as an allegation. The
source register makes the same point in its note on the complaint: allegations are not findings.

So the complaint is excellent evidence of one thing: that the Attorney General made these
allegations in a court filing. It is not evidence that a court accepted them. The ledger records
this by placing V-03 at stated intent. A complaint is something said, in a formal setting, by a
party to a case.

## Step 3: choose the verb

The attribution table in the site's style guide gives the wording: "The complaint alleges that ..."
Never "the lawsuit shows." Applied here, the sentence is: "The Attorney General alleged that
Southern Trust made fraudulent misrepresentations to obtain the tax benefits." The subject is the
person who made the allegation. The verb is "alleged." The paragraph then says that the case settled
without a ruling.

Verbs to avoid follow from the same rule. "Found," "established" and "showed" would each turn the
allegation into a finding. "Admitted" would assert something the ledger does not record.

## Step 4: read the settlement for what it records

V-04 is the only row in this group at the verified state. What it verifies is the settlement: that
the case ended in November 2022, for more than $105 million, and that the government said more than
$80 million in tax benefits would be returned. Those are facts about how the case ended, recorded by
the government that brought it.

The settlement does not convert the complaint into a finding. The caveat on V-03 states that the
case settled without a ruling, so no court decided whether the allegations were true. A reader
should be able to see both facts in the same paragraph.

V-04 carries its own caveat, and it must travel with the figures: whether the $80 million is inside
or in addition to the $105 million must be confirmed from the release. Until it is, the page gives
both figures as the release gives them and does not add them. A total of $185 million would be
arithmetic the record does not support. The guide on [figures that
differ](/guides/method-figures-that-differ/) covers this case.

## Step 5: keep the description at stated intent

V-01 needs as much care as V-03. A description given to a tax-incentive commission is a statement
about what a company does. It is not evidence that the company did it. The ledger therefore places
the "biomedical and financial informatics" description at stated intent, and the later allegation
that the company performed no visible informatics services does not move it in either direction. It
remains a description, later alleged to be false, in a case that settled without a ruling.

The narrow point for the genetics material follows from [Ledger G-10](/ledger/#G-10): no public
record shows a shared genetic database, a research output or an operational program connecting the
DNA-related items, a statement of absence across the sources reviewed [@cnn-2026-thakuria]. The word
"biomedical" in a business description does not supply one.

## Step 6: look for a response

The style guide asks for a denial or response in the same paragraph as the allegation it answers.
The site's [index of names](/named/) records no published response for Southern Trust Company in the
sources reviewed, so this example cannot quote one. If a response from the company is
located, it belongs beside the allegation, and the change is recorded in the update log.

## What the page can say

Put together, a fair paragraph reads like this: "In 2012 Southern Trust Company was described to a
tax-incentive commission as an informatics consultancy, and in 2013 it received a ten-year package
of tax incentives. The territory's Attorney General later alleged that the company made fraudulent
misrepresentations to obtain the benefits. The case settled in November 2022 for more than $105
million, without a ruling."

Every clause in that paragraph has a ledger row, and none is written above its state. For the
general approach to these documents, see [how to read a civil
complaint](/guides/read-a-civil-complaint/) and [how to read a settlement
announcement](/guides/read-a-settlement-announcement/).
