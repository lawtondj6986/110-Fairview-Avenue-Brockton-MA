## What it means

A demolition clause, sometimes called a redevelopment clause, gives the landlord the right to terminate a lease before expiration in order to tear down, substantially renovate, or redevelop the property. It sets a notice period and may set conditions and compensation. It turns a fixed lease term into one the landlord can shorten.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Older retail strips and one-story commercial buildings near commuter rail stations and downtowns are redevelopment candidates, especially where towns have adopted multifamily zoning under the MBTA Communities Act or smart growth districts. Landlords in Quincy, Weymouth, Brockton, and Braintree increasingly want this flexibility. A restaurant or medical tenant investing in a build-out needs to see the clause before it prices the lease.

## In practice

A restaurant signs a 10-year lease in Quincy and spends $300,000 on its build-out. The lease allows termination for redevelopment on 9 months' notice after year 4. The tenant negotiates repayment of unamortized improvements. If notice lands at the end of year 5, half the term remains, and the landlord owes $150,000.
