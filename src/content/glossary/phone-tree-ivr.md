## What it means

A phone tree, formally interactive voice response (IVR), is the automated system that answers a call, plays a recorded menu, and routes the caller based on key presses or spoken words. It can play hours and directions, send calls to different people or departments, offer a callback, or hand off to voicemail. Done well it is invisible; done badly it is the reason people hang up.

## Why it matters for a local business

A new customer in Rockland calling three roofers at lunch will give each one about 20 seconds. A four-level menu loses that call. On the other hand, a Taunton auto dealership with sales, service, and parts genuinely needs routing, and a flat line that rings everyone is worse. ICS configures phone handling in Lead Engine as a short menu or none, with an AI receptionist taking the open-ended calls and every unanswered call triggering a text-back within seconds.

## In practice

A Weymouth HVAC company had a five-option tree with a 40-second greeting. Call tracking showed 22 percent of callers hung up during the menu. ICS cut it to one line, "press 1 if you have no heat or no AC right now, otherwise stay on the line," and routed everything else to the office with text-back on misses. Menu abandonment fell to 4 percent, and the emergency option let the dispatcher catch 31 same-day jobs over the first winter, averaging $640 each.
