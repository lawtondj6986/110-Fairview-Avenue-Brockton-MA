## What it means

Hard costs are the costs of physically building something: labor, materials, site work, structure, mechanical and electrical systems, and finishes. Soft costs are everything else the project requires that is not a physical part of the building: architectural and engineering fees, permits and impact fees, legal and title work, surveys, environmental reports, construction loan interest, insurance, marketing, and the developer's or project manager's fee. Lenders, appraisers, and tax advisors treat the two categories differently, which is why budgets separate them.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore owner-users building out a flex unit in Rockland or converting a Brockton storefront to a clinic tend to budget hard costs carefully and forget the soft costs until the invoices arrive. Permit and site plan review fees vary by town, and an engineer's stamp is often required for anything touching structure, sprinklers, or septic under Title V. Soft costs also determine how much of a project an SBA 504 or 7(a) loan will finance, so the breakdown goes into the loan package, not just the contractor's bid.

## In practice

A contractor buys a 9,000 square foot flex building in Hanover for $1.35 million and plans an office and showroom conversion of 3,000 square feet. Hard costs bid at $115 per square foot, $345,000. Soft costs include $31,000 in architectural and engineering fees, $6,500 in permit fees, $9,000 in legal and survey, $12,000 in construction-period interest, and $8,000 in contingency and project management, totaling $66,500, about 19 percent of hard costs. The all-in budget of $411,500 goes to the lender, and the breakdown later feeds a cost segregation study. Talk to your CPA about how each category is depreciated.
