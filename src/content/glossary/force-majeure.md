## What it means

Force majeure is a clause that suspends or extends a party's obligations when an event outside its reasonable control makes performance impossible or late. It lists qualifying events and sets the remedy, most often a day-for-day extension. It does not usually cancel the contract or forgive money owed.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Construction here slips for ordinary reasons: winter storms, utility hookup delays, long waits for switchgear and rooftop units, and inspections that back up. In a lease with landlord's work, the force majeure clause decides whether those delays push the delivery date for free or cost the landlord rent credits. In a purchase, it can move a closing date when a registry or town hall is closed.

## In practice

A landlord in Weymouth must deliver a medical suite by March 1 or give two days of free rent for each day late. A February blizzard stops work for 6 days and the landlord gives written notice within the 10 days the lease requires. Delivery on March 7 is on time. Delivery on March 15 is 8 days late, worth 16 days of rent.
