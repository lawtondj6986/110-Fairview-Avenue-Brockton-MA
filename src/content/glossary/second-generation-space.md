## What it means

Second-generation space has been previously occupied and built out, usually for the same or a similar use, leaving infrastructure a new tenant can reuse. It is the opposite of raw shell and is especially valuable for restaurants, medical, and salon uses whose fit-outs are expensive and heavily permitted.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Across the South Shore, the fastest and cheapest restaurant, medical, or salon openings we have seen reused second-generation space in downtown Brockton, Whitman, Rockland, and along Route 18. A new operator who insists on virgin space pays for infrastructure the last tenant already bought.

## In practice

A new restaurant leases a former restaurant space in Rockland with hood, grease trap, walk-in, and restrooms in place. Fit-out costs $90,000 instead of the $300,000 a raw shell would have required, and the opening is four months earlier.
