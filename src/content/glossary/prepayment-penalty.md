## What it means

A prepayment penalty is a contractual charge for paying off a commercial loan, in whole or part, before its maturity date. Lenders include it because they priced the loan on the expectation of receiving interest for the full term. Common forms are a fixed percentage of the balance, a step-down schedule that declines each year, yield maintenance tied to Treasury rates, and defeasance. Most commercial loans have some form of penalty; most residential loans in Massachusetts do not. The note and loan agreement spell out the terms.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Owner-users on the South Shore often outgrow buildings faster than they expect. A contractor who finances a 6,000 square foot shop in Rockland and needs 15,000 square feet four years later will face the penalty when the building sells or refinances. SBA 504 borrowers, who are a large share of local owner-users, carry a ten-year declining penalty on the debenture portion. When ICS runs a seller net sheet, the payoff letter with the penalty is one of the first numbers we ask for, because it can move net proceeds by tens of thousands.

## In practice

A Rockland owner has a $900,000 bank loan at 6.5 percent with a 5-4-3-2-1 step-down and sells in year three. The penalty is 3 percent of the roughly $840,000 balance, about $25,000. The owner also has a $700,000 SBA 504 debenture in year four; its penalty is a declining percentage of the debenture rate applied to the balance, roughly $20,000 at that point. Combined, about $45,000 comes off the net proceeds, which the seller factors into the asking price.
