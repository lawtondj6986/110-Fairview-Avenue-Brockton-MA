## What it means

Cost per click is the price an advertiser pays for a single click on a pay-per-click ad, most often on Google or Meta. It is set by auction: your bid, the competition for that keyword, and the platform's judgment of your ad's relevance. CPC is an input cost, not a result. On its own it tells you nothing about whether the click became a customer.

## Why it matters for a local business

Competition for "plumber Quincy" is thicker than for "plumber Hanson," and CPC reflects that. A Brockton contractor paying $28 a click with a landing page that converts 4 percent of visitors is paying $700 per lead. The same clicks sent to a focused page with a QuoteBrain calculator converting 12 percent cost $233 per lead. ICS treats CPC as one number in a chain that ends at booked work; Lead Engine reporting shows cost per click, cost per lead, and cost per booked job side by side.

## In practice

A Weymouth electrician was averaging $19 CPC on 180 clicks a month, $3,420 in spend. Adding negative keywords (jobs, apprentice, salary, school) cut wasted clicks by a third and dropped average CPC to $16. Sending the remaining clicks to a service-specific landing page lifted conversion from 5 to 11 percent. Spend fell to about $2,400, leads rose from 9 to 16 a month, and cost per lead dropped from $380 to $150.
