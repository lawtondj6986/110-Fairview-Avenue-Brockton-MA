## What it means

An as-is clause provides that the buyer takes the property in its present condition, without reliance on seller representations about its physical state, and often with a disclaimer of implied warranties. It is standard in commercial sales and shifts the burden of discovery to the buyer's due diligence.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial buildings are sold as-is, and most are decades old. That combination makes the inspection period the buyer's only protection. We treat an as-is clause as a reason to extend diligence and bring in specialists, not as a reason to skip anything.

## In practice

A buyer of an as-is Stoughton industrial building commissions a roof inspection, a Phase I, and a mechanical review during a 45-day window. The roof has five years left. The buyer negotiates a $35,000 credit against a $1,100,000 price and closes.
