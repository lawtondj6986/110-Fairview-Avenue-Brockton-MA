## What it means

A nonconforming use is a use of land or a building that lawfully existed before a zoning bylaw or amendment made it noncompliant, and that may continue because Chapter 40A, Section 6 protects it. The same section protects nonconforming structures, such as a building that sits inside a setback the town later widened. Most people say "grandfathered." The protection covers continuing the existing use, not expanding it, changing it, or rebuilding after abandonment, each of which requires some form of local approval.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Much of the South Shore's small commercial stock predates modern bylaws. Auto repair shops in residential zones along Route 27 in Brockton, contractor yards in Whitman and Hanson, and corner retail in Quincy neighborhoods survive as nonconforming uses. The grandfathered status is often the main thing being sold: without it, the lot might be worth half as much as a house lot. Confirm the status in writing from the building department, check for gaps in use, and price the risk that a future buyer or lender will ask the same questions.

## In practice

A Brockton auto repair shop sits in a residential zone and has operated since 1978. The owner wants to add a 1,500 square foot bay. The buyer's attorney obtains a zoning determination letter confirming the nonconforming status, and the ZBA grants a Section 6 finding after one hearing with conditions on outdoor storage and hours. The addition costs about $220,000 and the shop's value rises from $650,000 to roughly $900,000, a gain that would not exist without the grandfathered use.
