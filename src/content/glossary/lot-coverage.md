## What it means

Lot coverage is the share of a lot's area that may be occupied by buildings, expressed as a percentage in the zoning bylaw's dimensional table. Many bylaws also set a separate impervious coverage limit that includes pavement. A lot coverage limit of 30 percent on a 40,000 square foot lot allows a 12,000 square foot footprint. Coverage works with setbacks, parking requirements, and landscaping rules to define the buildable envelope.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

For single-story commercial product, which is most of what trades in Brockton, Rockland, Hanover, and Plymouth, lot coverage and parking are the controls that matter. A contractor who needs a 15,000 square foot shop plus outdoor storage will find that a 35 percent coverage cap and a 70 percent impervious cap set the minimum lot size before anyone talks about price. Where coverage is already maxed, a seller's "expandable" claim is usually wrong, and the only path is a variance or a pervious paving credit if the bylaw offers one.

## In practice

A Hanover lot of 60,000 square feet sits in a district with 35 percent building coverage and 65 percent total impervious coverage. The maximum footprint is 21,000 square feet. The remaining impervious allowance, 18,000 square feet, covers roughly 50 parking spaces and a drive aisle. A 20,000 square foot flex building needing 60 spaces will not fit without a reduction in building size or a parking relief special permit. The buyer offers $100,000 less to reflect the smaller building.
