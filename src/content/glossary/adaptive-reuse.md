The building inspector decides what applies, so meet with the department before you close, not after."

## What it means

Adaptive reuse is the conversion of an existing building to a use it was not designed for while keeping most of the structure. Common examples are a shoe factory turned into loft apartments, a vacant bank branch turned into a dental office, or a church turned into a brewery. It sits between a renovation (same use, better finish) and a teardown. The economics depend on whether the existing shell is worth more than the cost to adapt it.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Brockton has more than a century of shoe-industry masonry mills, many of them half empty, and several have become residential or mixed-use. Along Route 18, Route 139, and Route 44 the smaller version is just as common: closed bank branches, former pharmacies, and empty restaurant pads converted to medical, veterinary, daycare, or self-storage. Zoning rarely anticipated the new use, so a special permit or variance is often part of the project, and the Planning Board will want parking and site plan review.

## In practice

An owner-user buys a vacant 6,500 square foot bank branch in Weymouth for $1.4 million and converts it to an urgent care clinic. Demolition, new HVAC, sprinklers, accessibility, and medical fit-out run $190 per square foot, about $1.24 million, plus $60,000 in permitting and design. All-in cost lands near $2.7 million, or about $415 per square foot, roughly 20 percent below the quote for ground-up medical on a comparable lot, and the drive-thru lane becomes the patient drop-off.
