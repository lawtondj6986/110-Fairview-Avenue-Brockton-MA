## What it means

A perc test is a soil evaluation required under Title 5 of the Massachusetts Environmental Code before a septic system can be designed. A soil evaluator digs deep observation holes to log soil layers and find the estimated seasonal high groundwater, then runs a percolation test that times how fast water drains through the soil. The results set whether a system is feasible, what type it must be, and how large the leaching area has to be for the design flow. A Board of Health agent witnesses the test.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Large parts of Hanover, Pembroke, Hanson, Halifax, Plympton, Kingston, Duxbury, and much of Plymouth have no public sewer, so a commercial building's capacity is capped by what the soil will take. Clay, high groundwater, and ledge are common. A restaurant, daycare, or medical office generates far more wastewater per square foot than a warehouse, and a lot that passes for one may fail for the other. A failed or marginal perc test is a deal-killer or a $60,000 to $150,000 alternative system. Test during the due diligence period, not after.

## In practice

A buyer in Pembroke plans a 3,000 square foot restaurant with 80 seats on an unsewered lot. Title 5 design flow is 35 gallons per seat per day, or 2,800 gallons daily. The perc test shows 25 minutes per inch, acceptable, but groundwater at 42 inches forces a mounded system. The engineer prices it at $95,000 against $40,000 for a conventional field, and the buyer negotiates a $50,000 price reduction before the inspection contingency expires.
