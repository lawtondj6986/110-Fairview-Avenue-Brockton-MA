## What it means

Pass-throughs are building operating expenses that the landlord pays and then bills back to tenants, usually in proportion to the square footage each tenant occupies. The big three are real estate taxes, property insurance, and common area maintenance, which is why a triple net lease is called "net of" those three. Pass-throughs are estimated monthly and reconciled annually. The lease defines what is included, what is excluded, how the tenant's share is calculated, and whether any category is capped.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Pass-throughs on the South Shore have risen faster than base rent in recent years. Property insurance premiums in coastal towns like Marshfield, Scituate, and Plymouth jumped sharply, snow removal is unpredictable, and towns reassess commercial property on their own schedules. A tenant who signs at $18 net in Hanover and budgets $5 in pass-throughs can find itself paying $7.50 two years later with no change in base rent. The lease's exclusions and caps are where a tenant's broker earns the fee.

## In practice

A 2,500 square foot tenant in a 40,000 square foot Weymouth plaza pays $20 per square foot net, $50,000 a year, plus a 6.25 percent share of pass-throughs. In year one the building's taxes are $140,000, insurance $38,000, and CAM $110,000, a total of $288,000, so the tenant's share is $18,000, or $7.20 per square foot. In year three insurance rises to $56,000 and taxes to $152,000; the tenant's share climbs to $19,875. Its negotiated 4 percent cap on controllable CAM saves it about $600 that year when landscaping and management costs spike.
