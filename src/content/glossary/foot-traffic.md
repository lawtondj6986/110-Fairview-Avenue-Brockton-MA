## What it means

Foot traffic is the count of pedestrians passing a storefront or entering a store or center over a day, week, or year. It is reported as visits, unique visitors, visit frequency, and dwell time. Conversion, the share of visitors who buy, connects it to sales.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore retail is car-based, so foot traffic here usually means visits to a center, driven by the anchor. A grocery store may pull over a million visits a year and the inline shops live on the spillover. True walk-by traffic exists in a few places: Quincy Center, downtown Plymouth near the waterfront, and Hingham Square. Summer changes the numbers in coastal towns, so a July count in Plymouth says little about February.

## In practice

A tenant compares two 1,400-square-foot spaces. One, in a Hanover grocery-anchored center, shows about 1.1 million annual center visits at $34 a square foot. The other, a freestanding strip in the same trade area, shows 240,000 at $24. The $14,000 a year rent difference buys more than four times the visits. At a 2 percent capture rate and a $19 ticket, the better site supports about $330,000 more in sales.
