## What it means

A go-dark clause governs what happens when a retail tenant stops operating its business in the premises while the lease is still in force. From the tenant's side, it is the right to close the store and keep paying rent without being in default. From the landlord's side, it is usually paired with a continuous operation covenant that prohibits going dark, or a recapture right that lets the landlord take the space back and re-lease it if the tenant closes. The clause matters because a dark storefront hurts the whole center even if the rent keeps coming.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

A dark anchor or dark inline unit in a Route 139 or Route 3 plaza drags down traffic for every neighbor, and co-tenancy clauses can let other tenants cut rent or leave when it happens. South Shore landlords with a single national tenant in Hanover or Kingston should know whether that tenant has a go-dark right before they buy or refinance, because lenders will ask. Local tenants rarely get a go-dark right, but they can often negotiate a recapture-and-release instead: close, give notice, and walk away.

## In practice

A regional pharmacy chain leases a 12,000 square foot end cap in a Plymouth plaza at $24 per square foot, $288,000 a year, with a go-dark right after year five. In year six it closes the store, keeps paying, and lists the space for sublease. Two inline tenants invoke co-tenancy clauses and reduce rent by 25 percent, costing the landlord about $42,000 a year. The landlord exercises its recapture option, terminates the pharmacy's lease, and re-leases the end cap to a fitness operator at $19 per square foot, trading $60,000 in annual rent for a lit, trafficked plaza.
