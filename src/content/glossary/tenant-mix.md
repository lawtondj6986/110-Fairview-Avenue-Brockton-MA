## What it means

Tenant mix is the lineup of businesses in a shopping center or mixed-use property and the way their uses interact. A good mix draws customers for different reasons at different times of day, cross-shops them between stores, and avoids tenants who cannibalize one another. It is shaped by the anchor, by exclusive use clauses that protect existing tenants, and by the landlord's leasing choices over years. Tenant mix is a leasing strategy for landlords and a site-selection question for tenants deciding whether a center's neighbors will help or hurt.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore plazas on Route 139, Route 53, and Route 18 are mostly neighborhood and community centers anchored by a grocer, a pharmacy, or a hardware store, and their health depends on the service tenants around them: dentists, nail salons, pizza, insurance, fitness. A tenant choosing between a Hanover plaza and a Rockland plaza should look at who draws traffic at its own peak hours. A landlord that leases to a third pizza shop or a vape store next to a pediatric dentist to fill a vacancy can lose two renewals to gain one lease.

## In practice

A 60,000 square foot Marshfield plaza anchored by a 32,000 square foot grocer has 28,000 square feet of inline space across 11 units. The landlord's mix: a pharmacy, a bank branch, a dry cleaner, a nail salon, a pediatric dentist, a sub shop, a liquor store, a fitness studio, and three vacant units. A tutoring center offers $24 per square foot for 1,800 square feet; a second nail salon offers $27. The landlord takes the tutoring center, since the first nail salon holds an exclusive and would have had grounds to reduce rent or terminate, and the tutoring center brings 60 families a week at 3 p.m., the grocer's slow hour.
