## What it means

A reference check, in our work, is a direct conversation with a past customer, supplier, lender, or partner of a business under review. The caller says who they are and why they are calling. The references usually come from the counterparty itself, sometimes supplemented by names that appear in public records such as permits or contract awards.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Records show what was filed. People explain what it was like. Did the contractor finish on time? Were change orders fair? Would they hire them again? Supplied references are curated, so we weigh them accordingly and compare what they say to the documents. Every call is logged with date, name, and what was said, and marked as an interview, not a record.

## In practice

A property owner is choosing a general contractor for a $900,000 build-out near Route 3. The contractor supplies 4 references. We reach 3 within 5 business days. Two report on-time delivery. One describes a 7-week delay and $40,000 in disputed change orders. Permit records confirm that timeline. The owner tightens the schedule and change order terms in the contract. The fourth reference never called back, and the report says so.
