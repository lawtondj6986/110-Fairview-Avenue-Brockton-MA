## What it means

An approval not required plan, or ANR, is a plan dividing land in a way that does not count as a subdivision under Massachusetts law, typically because each lot has the required frontage on an existing way. The owner submits it to the planning board, which endorses it "approval under the subdivision control law not required." The statute gives the board 21 days to act.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

ANR is the fast route for splitting commercial and industrial land along existing roads: carving a pad site off a larger parcel on Route 139 in Hanover, dividing a long frontage lot in Rockland, or cleaning up a boundary in Brockton. There is no public hearing, which saves months. The shortcut has limits, and frontage that exists only on paper can sink it.

## In practice

An owner holds 4 acres with 420 feet of frontage where zoning requires 150 feet and 40,000 square feet per lot. A surveyor draws two lots with 210 feet each for about $5,000. The board endorses the plan in three weeks and it is recorded. One lot sells for $650,000. The buyer still needs site plan review and a wetlands filing.
