## What it means

Pre-leasing is the process of securing signed leases from tenants for a building that is still in planning, under construction, or undergoing major renovation. Developers pre-lease to reduce lease-up risk, to satisfy construction lenders who condition funding on a minimum occupancy commitment, and to prove the project's rents to equity partners. Pre-leases typically include delivery deadlines, outside termination dates if the project stalls, and detailed work letters because the space does not yet exist.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

New commercial construction on the South Shore is limited and mostly build-to-suit or small: a 20,000 square foot flex building in Rockland, a medical office pad in Pembroke, a retail strip at a Route 3 interchange. Local lenders rarely fund speculative multi-tenant projects without a meaningful pre-lease. For ICS developer clients, pre-leasing is what turns a permitted site into a financeable project. For tenant clients, a pre-lease on a new building is a chance to lock in the right space at favorable terms, provided the lease protects them if delivery slips.

## In practice

A developer plans a 15,000 square foot medical office building in Pembroke at $32 per square foot NNN with a $5.2 million total cost. The bank requires 50 percent pre-leasing. ICS brings a physical therapy group for 4,500 square feet and a dental practice for 3,200 square feet, 51 percent of the building, each with a $60 per square foot allowance and six months free rent. In exchange the leases carry an outside delivery date 18 months out with tenant termination rights and a $250 per day late-delivery credit after month 14.
