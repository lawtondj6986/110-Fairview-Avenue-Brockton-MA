## What it means

Mezzanine financing is a layer of capital between the senior mortgage and the owner's equity. It is structured as a loan to the entity that owns the property, secured by a pledge of that entity's ownership interests rather than a mortgage on the real estate. Because it is subordinate to the first mortgage, it carries a higher interest rate and often fees or a share of upside. Senior lenders govern the arrangement through an intercreditor agreement that limits the mezz lender's rights and cure periods.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Mezz is rare on deals under $5 million, which describes most South Shore commercial trades, because the legal cost of the structure does not scale down well. Where it shows up is in larger multifamily and mixed-use projects in Quincy, Brockton, and Weymouth, where a developer needs to fill a gap between a 60 percent senior construction loan and the equity it has raised. For a local owner or investor, the practical lesson is to recognize it on a seller's capital stack: a seller carrying expensive mezz is often motivated to close on time.

## In practice

A developer builds a $20 million mixed-use project near a Quincy Red Line station. The bank lends $12 million at 60 percent loan-to-cost. The developer has $5 million of equity. A mezzanine lender fills the $3 million gap at 12 percent with a 2 percent origination fee, secured by a pledge of the ownership entity. Blended cost of debt rises from about 7 percent to roughly 8 percent, and the developer keeps a larger share of the equity than a joint venture partner would have taken.
