## What it means

An appraisal contingency conditions the buyer's obligation to close on the property appraising at or above an agreed value, usually the purchase price. If it does not, the buyer may terminate, renegotiate, or proceed with more equity. It protects a financed buyer from a gap the lender will not fund.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore commercial appraisals work from thin comparable data, and two appraisers can land $100,000 apart on the same building. A buyer's broker who already holds the comparable sales can make a credible challenge when a value comes in low, which is often faster than renegotiating.

## In practice

A $1,250,000 contract appraises at $1,180,000. The buyer invokes the contingency, the seller reduces the price by $40,000, the buyer brings $12,000 more cash, and the deal closes two weeks later than planned.
