## What it means

A false positive is a hit that is not a match: a lawsuit against a different company with a similar name, a sanctions list entry for a namesake abroad, a news story about another John Murphy. Every broad search produces them. The failure is not generating a false positive but reporting one as a finding. Clearing them, using public records only, is a core part of careful research.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Common South Shore surnames and generic LLC names make false positives routine. A search for a Brockton contractor can return dozens of court entries, most for unrelated parties across the Commonwealth. ICS shows its work: raw hits, how each was cleared, and what remains. A finding that survived that process is one a client can repeat to an attorney or a lender.

## In practice

A sanctions and litigation screen on a Plymouth seller's principal returns 23 hits. ICS clears them on public attributes: 14 are in other states with no Massachusetts tie, 6 share a name but a different middle initial on filings, 2 are the subject's own small claims as plaintiff. One remains: a dismissed 2020 contract suit. The brief reports 1 finding and 22 cleared hits.
