## What it means

An asset search is a structured sweep of public records to identify what a person or company owns and what is already pledged or encumbered. It covers real property, business interests, recorded liens, judgments, and bankruptcy filings. It is a records exercise. It does not include bank account lookups, credit reports, or anything obtained by pretending to be someone else.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Before you sue, lend, partner, or accept a personal guaranty, you want to know whether there is anything behind the signature. A guarantor with three Brockton multifamily buildings looks strong until the registry shows each one mortgaged to the hilt and a recent transfer to a family trust. The asset search shows ownership and the encumbrances together, which is the only honest way to read either.

## In practice

A supplier is owed $140,000 by a Weymouth contractor and asks counsel whether a suit is worth filing. We search two registries, the corporate database, UCC filings, and court dockets. Result: one commercial condo with roughly $60,000 of visible equity, a blanket UCC lien held by a bank, and two prior judgments. Counsel uses the sourced memo to decide strategy. Total turnaround: about 5 business days.
