## What it means

A political action committee is an organization registered to raise and spend money in elections, separate from any single candidate's committee. In Massachusetts, PACs register with OCPF and file periodic reports listing every contributor over $50 and every expenditure. Federal PACs file with the FEC. Some PACs give directly to candidates; independent expenditure PACs spend on their own advertising instead.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A PAC is a map of relationships. When a trade association's PAC gives to eleven South Shore legislators, or a new committee forms in Weymouth two months before a ballot question, the filings show who organized it, who funded it, and who was paid. ICS uses only the public OCPF and FEC records. There is no need for anything else; the data is complete and lawful.

## In practice

A due-diligence brief on a Taunton contractor bidding for municipal work pulls the PAC filings. The owner gave $500 a year for six years to a PAC that in turn gave to three city councilors. That is legal and common. It is still a fact the client should know before a contract vote, and it took forty minutes of public-database work to establish.
