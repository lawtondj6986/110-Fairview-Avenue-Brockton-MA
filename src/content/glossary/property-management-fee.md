## What it means

A property management fee is the compensation paid to a third-party manager for the day-to-day operation of a property: collecting rent, paying bills, handling tenant requests, supervising vendors, and reporting to the owner. It is typically a percentage of effective gross income, with 3 to 5 percent common for retail and office and higher for small or management-intensive properties. In lease language it is often passed through to tenants as part of operating expenses, subject to any cap.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial properties under $5 million are self-managed by the owner or a family member, which is why so many broker pro formas show no management line. Buyers from outside the area, and every lender, will add one back. Third-party management on the South Shore runs 4 to 6 percent for small multi-tenant retail and office in Brockton, Weymouth, and Hanover, with single-tenant NNN buildings sometimes as low as 2 to 3 percent or a flat monthly fee. Whether the tenant leases allow the fee as a pass-through is a line worth checking in the lease abstract.

## In practice

A six-tenant retail building in Rockland collects $312,000 in effective gross income. The seller's pro forma shows NOI of $214,000 with no management. A buyer's underwriting adds a 4.5 percent fee, $14,040, dropping NOI to about $200,000. At a 7 percent cap rate that is a $200,000 difference in value, $3.06 million versus $2.86 million. The leases let the landlord pass through a management fee up to 4 percent of base rent, so roughly $11,000 of it is recoverable, and the parties settle near $2.95 million.
