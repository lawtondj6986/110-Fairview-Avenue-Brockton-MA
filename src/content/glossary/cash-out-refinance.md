## What it means

A cash-out refinance pays off the current mortgage with a new, larger loan. After the old balance and closing costs are paid, the owner keeps the remainder in cash. The owner keeps the property and takes on more debt against it.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Plenty of owners in Brockton, Weymouth, and Quincy bought their buildings 15 or 20 years ago and now have a small balance against a much higher value. A cash-out refinance is one way to fund a second location, new equipment, or a partner buyout without selling. The constraint is usually debt service coverage, especially when the building is leased to the owner's own business at a below-market rent.

## In practice

An owner's Rockland building appraises at $1,800,000 with $500,000 still owed. The bank lends 70 percent, or $1,260,000. After paying off the $500,000 and about $25,000 of costs, the owner nets roughly $735,000. The new payment has to clear the bank's coverage test, often around 1.25 times.
