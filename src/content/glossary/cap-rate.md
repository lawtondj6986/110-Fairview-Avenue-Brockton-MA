## What it means

Cap rate is the ratio of a property's net operating income to its price. A building that nets $70,000 a year and sells for $1,000,000 trades at a 7 percent cap rate. It is the fastest way to compare two income properties with different prices, and it is the number investors quote first.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

On the South Shore, most sub-$3M commercial buildings are bought by the people who will occupy them, so cap rate is often less important than what the mortgage costs versus the rent they would otherwise pay. Still, every seller should know the cap rate their asking price implies, because the first investor who looks at the listing will compute it in thirty seconds.

## In practice

A Brockton retail building with $84,000 of net operating income listed at $1,200,000 implies a 7 percent cap rate. At a 6.5 percent cap the same income supports $1,292,000; at 8 percent it supports $1,050,000. That spread is the negotiation.
