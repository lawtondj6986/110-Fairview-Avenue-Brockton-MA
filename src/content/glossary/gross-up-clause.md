## What it means

A gross-up clause allows the landlord to calculate variable operating expenses as if the building were fully, or near fully, occupied, typically at 95 or 100 percent. The purpose is to keep a tenant's proportionate share of expenses consistent regardless of how many other tenants are in the building. It matters most in leases with a base year or expense stop, where the tenant pays increases over a baseline, because a baseline set during a vacancy period understates the true cost of operating a full building.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Multi-tenant office buildings in Quincy, Braintree, and Norwell have run with meaningful vacancy since 2020, so a tenant signing today often has a base year measured in a partly empty building. Without a gross-up, that tenant can see operating expense pass-throughs jump by thousands of dollars a year as the building leases up, with no increase in actual service. South Shore landlords generally include the clause, but the drafting varies. ICS reviews it in every office lease to confirm it applies to variable expenses only and that the base year is grossed up the same way as later years.

## In practice

A tenant leases 5,000 square feet, 10 percent of a 50,000 square foot Braintree office building, with a base year when the building is 60 percent occupied. Actual janitorial and utilities that year are $150,000. Grossed up to 95 percent occupancy they would be $237,500. Two years later the building is 95 percent leased and those costs are $245,000. Without the gross-up, the tenant's share of the increase is 10 percent of $95,000, or $9,500. With it, the share is 10 percent of $7,500, or $750.
