## What it means

Loan-to-cost is the ratio of a loan to the total cost of acquiring and building or renovating a property. A $3 million budget with a $2.1 million loan is 70 percent LTC, leaving 30 percent equity from the borrower. Construction and bridge lenders use it to make sure the borrower has real money at risk and to cap exposure if the project runs over. It is paired with loan-to-value on the completed project and with debt service coverage on the stabilized income.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial deals are small, under $5 million, and many are owner-user renovations: a contractor buying a tired Brockton warehouse and putting $400,000 into it, or a medical group converting retail in Hanover. Local banks and credit unions size those loans on LTC, usually 75 to 80 percent for a strong owner-user and lower for an investor. SBA 504 financing can push effective LTC to 90 percent, which is why it is the dominant structure for owner-users here. Know the number before you negotiate price.

## In practice

A Brockton buyer acquires a 15,000 square foot warehouse for $1.4 million and budgets $500,000 for roof, HVAC, and office fit-out, $60,000 in soft costs, and a $40,000 contingency, for total cost of $2 million. A bank offers 75 percent LTC, a $1.5 million loan, requiring $500,000 of equity. Through SBA 504, a $1 million first mortgage plus an $800,000 debenture gets to 90 percent LTC and cuts the equity to $200,000.
