## What it means

Adverse media screening is a systematic search of published news, regulatory releases, court reporting, and trade press for negative information about a named person or entity. The screen covers enforcement actions, litigation, bankruptcies, fraud allegations, and reputational events. It is a public-source method: nothing is obtained by pretext, and the output is a graded list of hits, not a verdict.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A counterparty on a Route 24 industrial deal can look clean in corporate filings and still have a 2019 wage-theft settlement covered in the Enterprise. Adverse media surfaces what registries do not. ICS runs it on companies and their public-facing principals only, never on private individuals, and never for employment, credit, or tenant screening decisions.

## In practice

A buyer asks ICS to screen a seller entity and its two managers before signing a $2.4 million purchase and sale on a Taunton flex building. The screen returns 31 raw hits; 26 are name collisions and 3 are duplicates. Two remain: a 2021 Attorney General consumer protection settlement and a local story on a stalled Weymouth project. Both go in the brief with source grades and a 48-hour corroboration pass.
