## What it means

Docket monitoring is the practice of checking one or more public court dockets on a set schedule and reporting anything new: a motion, a hearing date, a judgment, a new case naming the same party. It can follow a specific case or run a recurring name search for new filings.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A dossier is a snapshot. Deals and campaigns keep moving after it is delivered. A lis pendens filed the week before closing, or a new suit against a counterparty mid-negotiation, changes the picture. Monitoring keeps the snapshot current for the period that matters, and it does so using only records the court already makes public.

## In practice

A client has a 60-day due diligence period on a Plymouth retail property whose seller is in a partnership dispute. We check the Superior Court docket twice a week. On day 41, a motion for a preliminary injunction over the sale proceeds appears. The client's attorney has it within 24 hours and extends the closing date instead of finding out at the table. Each check is logged with the date and time, including the 11 checks that found nothing new, so the record shows the docket was actually watched.
