## What it means

Section 3A of Chapter 40A, the Zoning Act, was added in 2021 and is usually called the MBTA Communities Act. It requires every city and town served by the MBTA to have at least one zoning district where multifamily housing is allowed as of right, at a minimum gross density of 15 units per acre, with no age restrictions and no unreasonable size limits. State guidelines set each community's required district area and minimum unit capacity, and many districts must sit within a half mile of a transit station. The Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities determines compliance. The Supreme Judicial Court upheld the law in early 2025, and towns have been adopting or revising districts since.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Nearly the whole South Shore is covered, from Quincy's Red Line stops to the commuter rail towns down Route 3 and Route 24. Where a town drew its district is now public, and land inside one carries a different value than land outside. Owners of tired strip retail or single-story office near a station in Abington, Whitman, or Kingston may be sitting on multifamily land. Towns that fought the law and lost grant eligibility are a different risk profile for a buyer counting on public money for infrastructure.

## In practice

A one-acre lot with a 6,000 square foot office building near a commuter rail station sits inside the town's new 3A district. At 15 units per acre it supports 15 apartments as of right, and the local bylaw may allow more. If a developer will pay $60,000 a door, the land is worth roughly $900,000 to a residential buyer, against $650,000 as an office. Confirm the district map and any site plan review conditions before you list.
