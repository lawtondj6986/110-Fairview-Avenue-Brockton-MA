## What it means

A property tax abatement is a reduction in a property's tax bill granted by the local Board of Assessors after the owner challenges the assessed value, the classification, or a clerical error. The owner files an application for the fiscal year, the assessors have three months to act, and a denial or a deemed denial can be appealed to the Appellate Tax Board. The tax must generally be paid on time while the abatement is pending. This is different from a rent abatement, which is a lease concession, and from a tax exemption or TIF agreement negotiated with the town.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Commercial tax rates on the South Shore vary widely. Brockton, Taunton, Weymouth, Braintree, and Quincy have split rates where commercial property pays considerably more per thousand than residential, so an over-assessment costs more. Assessors revalue every year and tend to lag market changes in both directions: a Brockton office building that lost its anchor tenant may be assessed at pre-vacancy income for a year or two. ICS pulls the assessor's property record card for every property it analyzes and flags assessments that look high against income and comps. The filing itself is worth a conversation with your attorney or a tax representative.

## In practice

A 14,000 square foot office building in Brockton is assessed at $2.1 million. After losing a 5,000 square foot tenant, its NOI falls to $118,000, which supports about $1.6 million at a 7.5 percent cap rate. The owner files an abatement application by February 1 with income statements and three comparable sales averaging $125 per square foot, about $1.75 million. The assessors reduce the value to $1.7 million. At a commercial rate near $30 per thousand, the saving is about $12,000 for the year.
