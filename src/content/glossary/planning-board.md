## What it means

A Planning Board is the municipal body created under Chapter 41, Section 81A, with authority over subdivision control and, in most towns, site plan review and a share of special permits under the zoning bylaw. Members are elected or appointed depending on the town. The board holds public hearings, takes input from the town engineer, fire department, and Conservation Commission, and issues written decisions that are filed with the town clerk and, for permits, recorded at the registry of deeds.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

In towns like Hanover, Rockland, Abington, and Pembroke, the Planning Board is the board a commercial buyer will actually face. It controls the site plan, which means parking count, driveway location, landscaping, lighting, drainage, and signage. It meets once or twice a month, and a missed submission deadline costs a month. The board's peer-review engineer is paid by the applicant. Knowing the board's habits, what it waived last year and what it will not waive, is worth more than a polished rendering.

## In practice

A Rockland owner wants to convert a 9,000 square foot warehouse to a gym with a 40-space lot. The bylaw requires site plan review and a special permit from the Planning Board for the change of use. The application goes in by the 15th, the hearing opens five weeks later, and the board continues it once for a stormwater revision. Approval with conditions comes at the third meeting, about 90 days after filing, and the 20-day appeal period runs before the permit records.
