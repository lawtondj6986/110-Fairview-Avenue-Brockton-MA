## What it means

The due diligence period is the time after signing when the buyer can investigate the property and, if something disqualifying turns up, terminate and recover the deposit. Everything a buyer learns later costs money to fix; everything learned inside the window is leverage.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

On the South Shore the three inspections that most often change a deal are the Phase I environmental report, the Title V septic inspection in towns without sewer, and a zoning confirmation that the intended use is actually allowed at that address. Start all three the day the agreement is signed.

## In practice

A buyer in East Bridgewater discovers during due diligence that the septic system failed Title V. The seller agrees to a $38,000 credit, and the deal closes on schedule with the repair funded.
