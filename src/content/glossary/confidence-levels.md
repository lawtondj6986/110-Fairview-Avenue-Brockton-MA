  - q: "What does moderate confidence mean in a report?"
    a: "That the finding is credibly sourced and plausible but has gaps: a single source, an inference across records, or evidence that could support an alternative reading. High confidence means multiple independent, reliable sources agree. Low confidence means the finding is possible but thinly supported and should not drive a decision alone."
  - q: "Why not just report facts?"
    a: "Because most useful findings are judgments assembled from facts, and the reader needs to know how much weight each will bear. A report that states everything with equal certainty is either overclaiming or underclaiming, and the reader cannot tell which."

## What it means

Confidence levels are explicit statements of analytic certainty attached to each judgment in an intelligence product, grounded in the quality and quantity of sources and the strength of the reasoning. They are the honest companion to source grading: grades describe the inputs, confidence describes the conclusion.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Every ICS brief states confidence for every finding, which is how a South Shore client knows that the seller's identity is high confidence, the undisclosed lawsuit is high confidence, and the suspected related-party sale is moderate and worth a direct question before closing.
