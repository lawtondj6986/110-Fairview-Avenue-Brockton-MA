## What it means

A balloon payment is the remaining principal owed at the end of a loan whose term is shorter than its amortization. Because the scheduled payments were calculated as if the loan would run its full amortization, a large balance remains when the term ends.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Many South Shore commercial loans written in the low-rate years of the early 2020s carry five- and seven-year terms that are now coming due. Owners facing those balloons at higher rates are a meaningful part of today's seller pool, and a planning conversation two years early would have changed some outcomes.

## In practice

A $1,000,000 loan at 4 percent with a 7-year term and 25-year amortization leaves a balance of about $800,000 at maturity. Refinancing that at 7 percent raises the annual payment from $63,300 to about $67,900 on the smaller balance.
