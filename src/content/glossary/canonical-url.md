## What it means

A canonical URL is the one address a site declares as the authoritative version of a page, set with a link tag in the page head. Sites generate duplicates constantly: with and without "www," with tracking parameters, with trailing slashes, under http and https. The canonical tag tells search engines and AI crawlers which copy to index and credit, so ranking signals are not split across copies.

## Why it matters for a local business

A Rockland plumber's homepage reachable at four addresses is four weak pages instead of one strong one. Service area pages for 41 towns multiply the problem. Every JobSite build sets one canonical per page, forces https and a single host, and strips UTM parameters from the canonical so paid traffic does not create indexed duplicates. It costs nothing and protects the rankings a business is paying to earn.

## In practice

A Weymouth dental practice had its homepage indexed under 3 addresses and its "Invisalign" page under 2, one with a Facebook tracking string. After canonicals and a redirect to the https www host, duplicate indexed pages dropped from 38 to 19 in 6 weeks, and the Invisalign page moved from position 14 to 6 for its target search in the town. No new content was written.
