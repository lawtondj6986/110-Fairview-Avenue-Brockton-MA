## What it means

Source citation is the practice of attaching to every factual statement a precise reference to the record that supports it, in enough detail that an independent reader can locate and verify that record. In public-records research, a citation names the custodian, the document, its identifier, and when it was retrieved. Where a record may change or disappear, the citation also points to a preserved copy.

## Why it matters in a dossier

ICS works entirely from lawful public and licensed sources, and citation is how that is proven. A reader of a Deep Dossier can see that the finding about a Brockton LLC rests on a Secretary of the Commonwealth filing retrieved on a specific date, a Plymouth County deed at a specific book and page, and a docket entry in a specific court. There is nothing in the file that cannot be traced to a record anyone could request. That is also what makes a finding usable by the client's attorney.

## In practice

A due-diligence brief states that a seller's affiliate was sued twice in Plymouth Superior Court. Each statement carries the docket number, filing date, parties, and disposition, plus the date the docket was viewed. When the seller's attorney disputes one case, the buyer's counsel pulls the docket in five minutes and confirms it. The dispute ends there, because the citation did its job.
