## What it means

A town meeting warrant is the document that calls a Massachusetts town meeting and lists, article by article, what voters or town meeting members will decide. Zoning amendments, budgets, borrowing, land acquisitions, and bylaw changes all appear as articles. Cities such as Brockton and Quincy have councils instead and do not use warrants.

## Why it matters in a dossier

In a town, the warrant is where land use rules and public spending change. A rezoning article can alter what a parcel is worth. A citizen petition shows organized opposition before it reaches a ballot. Past warrants, with the recorded votes, give a town's decision history going back generations. For property and ballot research in towns like Hanover or Rockland, we read them first.

## In practice

A client owns 3 acres on Route 139. The spring warrant, posted 3 weeks ahead, has 42 articles. Article 27 would rezone a corridor including the client's parcel to allow mixed use by special permit. Zoning changes need a two-thirds vote in most cases. The client attends, the article passes 212 to 64, and the parcel's use options widen. The warrant, the motion, and the certified vote all go in the file.
