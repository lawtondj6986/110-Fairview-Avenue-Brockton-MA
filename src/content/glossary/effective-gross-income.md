## What it means

Effective gross income is the income a property is realistically expected to collect in a year. The calculation starts with potential gross income, the rent if every space were leased at its scheduled or market rate, subtracts an allowance for vacancy and credit loss, and adds other income such as parking, signage, late fees, and expense reimbursements. EGI is the figure you subtract operating expenses from to get net operating income, so an error here flows straight into the NOI and the value.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Seller pro formas on South Shore listings tend to show potential gross income with a token 3 percent vacancy. Real vacancy for strip retail in Brockton or older office in Quincy has often run higher, and a buyer who underwrites EGI at the seller's number will overpay. Expense reimbursements are the other place to check: a triple net rent roll in Hanover might show $4.50 per square foot in CAM recoveries that the leases do not actually support. ICS rebuilds EGI from the leases, not the brochure, for Buyer Brief clients.

## In practice

A 20,000 square foot retail plaza in Brockton has a scheduled rent roll of $18 per square foot, or $360,000 in potential gross income, plus $70,000 in expense reimbursements. The buyer applies a 7 percent vacancy and credit loss factor, $30,100, and adds $4,000 in signage and ATM income. EGI comes to $403,900. The seller's offering memorandum showed $425,000. At a 7.5 percent cap rate with $160,000 in operating expenses, that $21,100 difference moves the value by about $281,000.
