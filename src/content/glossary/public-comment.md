## What it means

Public comment is the input that residents, businesses, and organizations give a government body before it decides something. It includes letters to a planning board, testimony at a hearing, and written comments on a proposed regulation. The comments are collected into the record for that decision.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Comment records show how a fight actually went. A company's own letter to a regulator is a signed statement of its position. A trade group's testimony shows what it wanted. For a developer, the last project's comment file predicts the next project's objections. Our focus stays on organizations, officials, and applicants. A neighbor who wrote a letter is not a research subject.

## In practice

A client plans a 40-unit mixed-use proposal in Weymouth. We pull the comment files from 2 similar projects heard in the last 5 years: 86 letters and 3 hearing videos. Traffic comes up in 61 percent of comments, building height in 34 percent. The client commissions a traffic study before filing and drops a story from the design. Only applicants, officials, and organized groups are named in our summary. Individual residents are counted by topic and not identified.
