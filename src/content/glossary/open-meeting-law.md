## What it means

The Open Meeting Law, M.G.L. c. 30A, sections 18 through 25, requires most state and local public bodies, including select boards, planning boards, zoning boards, and school committees, to post meeting notices at least 48 hours in advance, deliberate in open session except for narrow executive-session purposes, and keep minutes that are public records. It is enforced by the Attorney General's Division of Open Government, which investigates complaints and issues determinations.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Minutes, agendas, and recordings are among the richest public sources on any property or project. The Rockland Planning Board's minutes record who spoke for a special permit, what conditions were debated, and when. Posted notices date the chronology. An executive session on a land acquisition is itself a signal. Every ICS land-use brief pulls them, lawfully and without a records request, because the law already makes them public.

## In practice

A buyer wants to know whether a Hanover parcel's approved site plan faced opposition. Planning Board minutes across 5 meetings show 3 continuances, 11 abutters speaking against, a traffic peer review costing the applicant $14,500, and a 4-1 vote with 9 conditions. The approval is solid, but 2 conditions carry annual compliance costs the pro forma had not included.
