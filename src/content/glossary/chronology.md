## What it means

A chronology is the ordered list of every dated event in a research question: filings, deed transfers, court dates, permits, news items, and corporate changes, each tied to its source. Timeline analysis is the work of reading that list for gaps, clusters, and sequences that a pile of documents hides. It is built from public records and licensed databases only.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Order reveals motive. A Plymouth seller who formed a new LLC two weeks before a 21E notice, then transferred the parcel into it, tells a different story than the same facts out of sequence. ICS builds a chronology for every Deep Dossier and due-diligence brief because it is the fastest way to see what the documents are not saying.

## In practice

A lender asks about a borrower's record on a Taunton redevelopment. The chronology shows a permit approved in March, a mechanic's lien recorded in June for $186,000, a lien discharge in August, and a refinance in September. Read alone, the lien looks alarming. Read in sequence, it is a 60-day contractor dispute that was paid in full. The brief says so, with all four documents cited.
