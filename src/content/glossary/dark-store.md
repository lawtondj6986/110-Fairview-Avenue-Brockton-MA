## What it means

Dark store has two meanings. In leasing, it is a store that has closed to the public while the tenant still holds the lease and pays rent. In logistics, it is a retail-format space with no walk-in customers, used to pick and pack online orders for delivery or curbside pickup.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Both versions appear here. A dark big box at a center off Route 24 can sit for years because the tenant is paying and has no reason to give the space back, while the inline shops lose traffic. Buyers of retail centers should read every anchor lease for an operating covenant and every small-shop lease for co-tenancy remedies. Fulfillment-only use also raises a zoning question: a town may treat it as warehouse, not retail.

## In practice

A buyer looks at a 96,000-square-foot center in Brockton where a 38,000-square-foot anchor went dark with six years left at $11 a square foot. The $418,000 of rent is still paid. But four inline leases, totaling 9,200 square feet, allow rent to drop to 50 percent after 12 months of anchor closure. That is roughly $101,000 of annual income at risk, and the buyer prices it in.
