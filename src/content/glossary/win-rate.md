## What it means

Win rate is the percentage of qualified opportunities that end in a sale, calculated as deals won divided by deals won plus deals lost. It excludes leads that were never real opportunities, such as wrong-service calls. It is the single best measure of how well a business sells once it has a real prospect in front of it.

## Why it matters for a local business

A local business usually competes for each job against two or three others, and small changes in win rate have large effects. A Brockton electrical contractor bidding 40 qualified jobs a month at $2,400 each who moves from a 30 to a 40 percent win rate adds four jobs and $9,600 a month without a new lead. Lead Engine tracks won and lost in the CRM by source, town, and service, so the owner can see that Hanover kitchen jobs win at 48 percent and Plymouth basement jobs at 22 percent, and bid accordingly.

## In practice

A Weymouth landscaping company tracked 160 qualified opportunities in a season: 54 won, 106 lost, a 34 percent win rate. Lost deals skewed toward quotes delivered more than three days after the site visit. The company set a 24-hour quote rule and added a proof page with before-and-after photos to each proposal. The following season, win rate rose to 45 percent on a similar opportunity count, about 18 more jobs at a $5,600 average.
