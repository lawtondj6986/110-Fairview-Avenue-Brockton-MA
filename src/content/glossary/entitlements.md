## What it means

Entitlements are the discretionary and administrative approvals a property needs before a project can be built or a use can begin: zoning relief, site plan approval, environmental permits, subdivision approval, utility connections, and ultimately building permits. "Entitled land" is land with these approvals in hand, which makes it worth far more than raw land with the same zoning. The entitlement process is where time, cost, and risk concentrate in development.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

On the South Shore, entitlements run through three local bodies more often than not: the Planning Board for site plan review, the Zoning Board of Appeals for special permits and variances, and the Conservation Commission for an order of conditions under the Wetlands Protection Act and local bylaws. Each meets once or twice a month, each has its own filing deadlines and abutter notice requirements, and each can continue a hearing. Marshfield, Plymouth, and Duxbury projects almost always touch wetlands buffers; Brockton and Quincy projects more often need parking or dimensional relief.

## In practice

A buyer contracts to purchase 3 acres in Hanover for a 24,000 square foot flex building at $1.5 million, contingent on entitlements. The timeline: Conservation Commission order of conditions, four months; Planning Board site plan review, three months, overlapping; ZBA special permit for a reduced buffer, two months. Consultants and legal fees total about $110,000. Eleven months later the buyer closes on an entitled site a lender will finance at 70 percent of cost, and the land is worth roughly $2.1 million with permits.
