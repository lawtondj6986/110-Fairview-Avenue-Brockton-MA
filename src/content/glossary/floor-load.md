## What it means

Floor load, or floor load capacity, is the amount of weight a floor is designed to support, expressed in pounds per square foot for distributed loads. Slabs on grade are also judged by thickness, reinforcement, and the soil beneath. Point loads from rack legs and machine feet are a separate check.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The old multi-story shoe factories and mills in Brockton, Rockland, and Whitman were built for machinery and often have strong upper floors, but freight elevators limit what gets up there. Newer flex buildings sometimes have thin slabs meant for light storage. A machine shop, a brewery with full tanks, or a distributor with high racks has to match the floor to the load before signing.

## In practice

A fabricator plans to install a 30,000 pound press with a 6 by 10 foot base in a Taunton flex bay. That is 500 pounds per square foot on a slab the plans show as 5 inches, lightly reinforced. The engineer specifies an isolated reinforced pad, about $18,000, which the tenant negotiates into the landlord's work.
