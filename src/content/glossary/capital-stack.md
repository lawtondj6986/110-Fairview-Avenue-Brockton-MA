## What it means

The capital stack is the full set of money sources behind a property, arranged by priority of repayment. Senior debt sits at the bottom of the risk scale and is paid first. Equity sits at the top of the risk scale and is paid last. Each layer's cost reflects its place in line.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most deals here have a simple stack: one bank loan and the owner's cash. It still pays to draw it. A Brockton owner-user using SBA 504 has three layers. An investor bringing in two partners on a Plymouth retail building has a stack inside the equity. Knowing who is ahead of you tells you what a bad year costs you.

## In practice

A $2,000,000 flex building in Hanover is bought with a $1,300,000 bank loan (65 percent), a $300,000 seller note in second position (15 percent), and $400,000 of buyer equity (20 percent). If value drops 20 percent to $1,600,000, both loans are still covered on paper. The buyer's $400,000 is gone.
