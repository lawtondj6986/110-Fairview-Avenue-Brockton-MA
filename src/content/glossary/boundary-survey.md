## What it means

A boundary survey is a field measurement by a Massachusetts-licensed land surveyor that locates a parcel's property lines, corners, and monuments and compares them to the recorded deed and plans. The surveyor sets or recovers iron pins and bounds and produces a stamped plan showing the lines, any encroachments, and visible evidence of easements. It is distinct from a mortgage plot plan, which is a sketch for the lender and does not certify the lines.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Older South Shore commercial lots were often described by reference to stone walls, trees, and neighbors who are long gone, and the Plymouth County Registry of Deeds holds plans going back to the 1800s. Shared driveways, encroaching parking lots, and dumpster pads over the line are routine on Route 18 and Route 53 properties. A survey before the purchase and sale agreement is signed lets you negotiate a cure, an easement, or a price adjustment. After closing you own the problem.

## In practice

A buyer of a 1.2-acre industrial lot in Rockland orders a boundary survey for $4,800. The survey shows the neighbor's loading area paved 11 feet over the line along 140 feet of frontage, about 1,540 square feet. The buyer negotiates a recorded easement in the neighbor's favor in exchange for a $35,000 price reduction and an agreement that the neighbor maintains the pavement, rather than fighting over it later. The lender accepts the survey and closes on schedule.
