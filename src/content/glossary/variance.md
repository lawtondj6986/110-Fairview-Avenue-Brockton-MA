## What it means

A variance is relief from a dimensional or use requirement of the zoning bylaw, granted by the zoning board of appeals when enforcing the rule literally would cause a hardship related to the land itself. Under Massachusetts law the standard is strict and the relief is narrow.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

On tight South Shore lots, the common ask is dimensional: a few feet of setback, a parking count, a sign size. Those are sometimes granted. A use variance, asking to run a business the district does not allow, is rarely granted and often not even available under the town's bylaw.

## In practice

A Whitman owner wants to add a drive-through lane that would reduce the side setback from 20 feet to 14. The board grants a dimensional variance based on the lot's unusual triangular shape. A request to open a use the district bans would have been denied.
