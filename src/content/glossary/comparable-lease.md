## What it means

A comparable lease is a recently signed lease for space similar in type, size, location, and condition to the space being valued, used as evidence of market rent. Appraisers, brokers, and lenders rely on lease comps to set asking rents, test a tenant's counteroffer, or support the market rent assumption in an appraisal. A useful comp captures the whole deal, including free rent, allowances, escalations, and who pays expenses, not just the face rate.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore retail and office rents vary sharply over short distances: a Route 53 end cap in Hanover, a Route 18 inline space in Abington, and a Main Street storefront in Brockton can differ by $15 per square foot within ten miles. Lease comps are the only way to know where a specific space sits, and because leases are not recorded, the comps live with the brokers who closed them. ICS tracks South Shore lease comps by town and corridor and uses them in every tenant representation and landlord pricing engagement.

## In practice

A Hanover landlord asks $28 per square foot NNN for a 2,000 square foot inline retail space. Three comps from the past twelve months within two miles: $24 NNN with three months free on a five-year term, $26 NNN with no free rent, and $25.50 NNN with a $10 per square foot allowance. Adjusted to a net effective basis, the comps cluster near $24.50. The tenant counters at $25, and the landlord has little data to argue past $26.
