## What it means

An off-market deal is a property transaction that occurs without the property being publicly marketed through a listing service, broker blast, or signage. The seller may be approached directly by a buyer or buyer's broker, may quietly tell one or two brokers to find a buyer, or may be responding to a life event such as retirement, death of a partner, or a loan maturity. The term covers a spectrum from truly unsolicited purchases to "pocket listings" that a broker shows selectively before going public.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore commercial market is small and relationship-driven. Many owners of Brockton industrial buildings, Rockland flex space, and Route 53 retail have held for 20 or 30 years and never listed anything. The best buildings often change hands between people who know each other, with the broker's role being to find the owner and make the approach. ICS runs targeted owner outreach for buyer clients using public records, a Buyer Brief that defines the criteria, and a short, honest letter. Response rates are low, but a single answered letter can save a buyer years of waiting for the right listing.

## In practice

An owner-user needs 12,000 to 18,000 square feet of industrial space with a loading dock within 15 minutes of Route 24 in Brockton. Nothing is listed. ICS pulls 140 owner records in Brockton, Avon, Stoughton, and West Bridgewater for buildings that fit, mails letters, and follows up by phone. Six owners respond; two are serious. The buyer closes on a 14,500 square foot building in Avon for $1.85 million, about $128 per square foot, roughly 8 percent under what two comparable listed buildings sold for that year.
