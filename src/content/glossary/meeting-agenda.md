## What it means

A meeting agenda is the public notice of what a board, committee, or council expects to discuss. In Massachusetts, the Open Meeting Law requires the topics to be listed with enough detail that the public can tell what is coming. Items often cite a street address, an applicant name, or a case number.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Agendas are an early warning system built from public notices. A zoning hearing on the parcel next to your client's site, a license transfer for a competitor, a vote to put a debt exclusion on the ballot: each shows up on an agenda first. Past agendas also give a fast index for finding the right minutes.

## In practice

For a client holding 3 commercial properties, we check agendas for the planning board, zoning board, conservation commission, and select board in 2 towns each week: 8 boards, about 30 minutes. In month 4, a zoning board agenda lists a special permit hearing for a drive-through 200 feet from one property. The client attends, with counsel, 6 days later. Without the weekly check, the first notice would have been a construction fence. Each week's review is logged, including the weeks with nothing to report.
