## What it means

Redaction is the practice of blacking out or removing portions of a record that fall under a legal exemption while releasing the remainder. Under the Massachusetts Public Records Law, a records access officer must segregate exempt material from non-exempt material and cite the specific exemption for each redaction. Redactions in electronic files should be done in a way that actually removes the text, not simply covers it.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Redactions are information too. A police log entry with a name removed still shows a date, a location, and a category of call. A town contract with pricing blacked out still shows the vendor, the term, and the signers. ICS reads what remains and appeals what seems over-withheld. It never attempts to recover redacted text by technical means from a file an agency released; if the agency made an error, the right move is to tell them.

## In practice

A Plymouth department releases 140 pages of email about a sewer extension with 31 redactions, each citing the personal privacy exemption. Twelve of them cover a developer's business email address, which is not personal information. An appeal to the Supervisor of Records takes 23 days and produces an unredacted release of those twelve. The developer's correspondence with the board was the point of the request.
