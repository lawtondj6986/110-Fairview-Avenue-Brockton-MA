## What it means

The Wetlands Protection Act, Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 131, Section 40, requires anyone who proposes to remove, fill, dredge, or alter a wetland resource area to file with the local Conservation Commission and receive an Order of Conditions. Resource areas include bordering vegetated wetlands, banks, land under water, bordering land subject to flooding, coastal beaches and dunes, and the 200-foot Riverfront Area. Work within 100 feet of most resource areas is also subject to review. MassDEP writes the regulations and hears appeals of local decisions.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Few regions in the state have more jurisdictional wetland per commercial acre. The Taunton, North, and Jones Rivers and their tributaries, salt marsh from Quincy to Plymouth, and the bogs and swamps of Pembroke, Hanson, and Halifax put a large share of developable land inside a buffer or riverfront area. A parcel's useful area is often a fraction of its deeded acreage. Before you price land in Hanover, Kingston, or Plymouth, have a wetland scientist flag the line and tell you what is actually buildable.

## In practice

A 5-acre industrial lot in Halifax backs onto a perennial stream. The 200-foot riverfront area and a 100-foot wetland buffer cover roughly 2.2 acres. Under the Act the project can alter no more than 10 percent of the riverfront area, about 9,500 square feet. The buildable envelope shrinks to about 2.5 acres, enough for a 25,000 square foot building and yard, and the purchase price is renegotiated from $900,000 to $650,000.
