## What it means

Life safety systems are the building components that protect occupants during a fire or emergency: automatic sprinklers, fire alarm and detection, emergency and exit lighting, means of egress including stairs and exit doors, fire-rated walls and doors, smoke control, and kitchen suppression. The Massachusetts State Building Code and fire code set the requirements by use and occupancy, and the local building inspector and fire department enforce them through plan review, inspections, and the certificate of occupancy.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Much of the South Shore's commercial inventory predates sprinkler requirements: downtown Brockton blocks, Route 18 strip retail from the 1970s, and converted houses used as offices in Hingham and Scituate. A change of use, which is what most adaptive reuse and owner-user purchases involve, can trigger full sprinklers, a new fire alarm, and a second means of egress. Towns on municipal water can supply a sprinkler system; a building on a private well in Pembroke or Plympton may need a storage tank and fire pump, which adds six figures. Check water supply before you check anything else.

## In practice

A buyer plans to convert an unsprinklered 11,000 square foot former office building in Abington to a daycare and medical office. The building department confirms the change of use triggers sprinklers throughout and a monitored fire alarm. Quotes: $5.50 per square foot for sprinklers, $60,500, plus $9,000 for a new water service, $28,000 for the alarm system, and $14,000 for emergency lighting and rated corridor doors, about $111,500 total. The buyer negotiates a $90,000 price reduction before signing the purchase and sale agreement.
