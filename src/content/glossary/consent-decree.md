## What it means

A consent decree, or consent judgment, is a settlement that a court enters as an order. The parties agree to the terms, the judge approves them, and the court keeps the power to enforce them. They are common in environmental, consumer protection, civil rights, and antitrust cases brought by government agencies.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A decree is a public, court-stamped document with obligations that may still be running. If a company you are about to buy, lease to, or partner with is under one, you inherit the context and sometimes the obligations. We report the terms, the duration, and whether compliance filings appear on the docket. We do not characterize a no-admission settlement as a finding of guilt, because it is not one.

## In practice

A buyer is evaluating an auto repair business near Route 18. The search turns up a consent judgment from 6 years ago with the state over waste handling: a $45,000 payment and 3 years of reporting. The docket shows the reporting period closed without further action. That fact goes to the buyer's attorney and environmental consultant, who fold it into the Phase I scope.
