## What it means

A good-guy guaranty is a personal guaranty with an exit. The principal of a tenant business guarantees the lease personally, but the guaranty terminates if the tenant gives the required notice, vacates, surrenders the premises in the condition the lease requires, and is current on all rent and charges through the surrender date. If the tenant leaves on those terms, the "good guy" is released and the landlord's only recourse for future rent is against the tenant entity. If the tenant stays in default or leaves owing money, the guaranty stays in force.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial tenants are small businesses, and most landlords want a personal guaranty from the owner. A full guaranty on a five-year lease can mean personal exposure of several hundred thousand dollars. The good-guy structure gives the landlord what it most needs, a tenant who will not squat and will leave the space clean, while giving the owner a way out if the business fails. ICS recommends it as the middle ground for tenant clients in Brockton and Weymouth who would otherwise refuse to guarantee at all.

## In practice

A restaurant owner signs a five-year lease on a 2,800 square foot space in Rockland at $30 per square foot, $84,000 a year, with a good-guy guaranty requiring 90 days notice. In month 20 the restaurant is failing. The owner gives notice, pays rent through month 23, cleans out the space, and returns the keys. The remaining 37 months of rent, about $259,000, are owed by the LLC alone, which has no assets. Under a full guaranty the landlord could have pursued the owner personally for that balance, less whatever it recovered by re-leasing.
