## What it means

A turnkey delivery means the landlord builds out the space to an agreed set of plans and specifications at its own cost and hands it over ready for the tenant to move in and operate. The tenant turns the key and opens. It contrasts with an allowance deal, where the landlord contributes a fixed dollar amount and the tenant manages and pays for the construction, and with an as-is or vanilla shell delivery, where the tenant does all the work. In a turnkey deal the landlord recovers the cost through rent, so the rate is higher than it would be for the same space delivered as-is.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Turnkey deals suit South Shore tenants who are good at their business and bad at construction: a physical therapist opening a second office in Kingston, a franchisee opening in a Brockton strip. Landlords with in-house construction teams offer it because they can build cheaper than a tenant can and mark it up through rent. The risk for the tenant is a thin spec and a slow schedule. The risk for the landlord is scope creep once the tenant sees the walls go up. A detailed exhibit protects both.

## In practice

A landlord offers a 2,400 square foot office suite in Kingston two ways: as-is at $19 per square foot, or turnkey to the tenant's plan at $26 per square foot on a seven-year lease. The build-out, new carpet, paint, lighting, six private offices, and a kitchenette, is estimated at $85 per square foot, $204,000. The $7 per square foot premium is $16,800 a year, $117,600 over the term, well below the construction cost, because the landlord expects the improvements to serve the next tenant too. The tenant takes turnkey and preserves its cash for hiring.
