## What it means

A lease buyout is an agreement to terminate a lease before its natural expiration in exchange for a payment. Most often the tenant pays the landlord to be released from the remaining term. Sometimes it runs the other way: a landlord pays a tenant to vacate so the space can be redeveloped, combined with an adjacent unit, or leased to a higher-paying user. The buyout agreement should release both parties, settle the security deposit, set a surrender date and condition, and address any personal guaranty.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Small business tenants on the South Shore outgrow space, downsize, or close more often than their five-year leases anticipate, and a buyout is cleaner than a default or a sublease to an unknown third party. The leverage depends on the market. A flex bay in Rockland that will re-lease in 30 days at higher rent should cost little to exit; a second-floor office suite in Brockton that may sit for a year will cost more. Landlords sometimes initiate buyouts too, especially when a national tenant wants a Route 139 end cap that a local tenant occupies.

## In practice

A tenant with 32 months left on a 3,500 square foot Weymouth office lease at $24 per square foot, about $224,000 of remaining rent, wants out. The landlord has a prospect willing to take the space at $26 within 60 days. The tenant proposes $40,000. The landlord counters at $75,000, citing two months of downtime, a $12,000 commission, and $20,000 in paint and carpet. They settle at $58,000 plus forfeiture of the $14,000 security deposit, and the tenant is released, guaranty included, on a surrender date 45 days out.
