## What it means

Attornment is a tenant's agreement to accept a successor as its landlord. If the building is sold or a lender forecloses, the tenant continues under the existing lease and owes rent and performance to the new owner. It is the "A" in SNDA: subordination, non-disturbance, and attornment.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

A buyer or lender on a leased building in Plymouth or Taunton is pricing the income. Attornment is what keeps that income attached to the building when ownership changes hands. Most local landlord lease forms include it. What is often missing is the non-disturbance half, which protects the tenant's build-out and its location if the landlord's loan goes bad.

## In practice

A dental practice in Rockland has seven years left on a lease at $6,500 a month and has put $250,000 into the space. The landlord defaults and the bank forecloses. Because the practice signed an SNDA, it attorns to the bank, pays the same $6,500, and keeps its seven years. Without non-disturbance, the outcome would depend on lien priority and the bank's choice.
