## What it means

Gross building area is the total enclosed floor area of a building, measured from the outside face of the exterior walls on every floor, including mechanical rooms, stairwells, elevator shafts, corridors, and interior walls. It excludes unenclosed areas such as open parking, loading docks without a roof, and exterior balconies. GBA is the measurement most often used by assessors, appraisers, and in whole-building sale comparables, and it is always larger than rentable or usable area.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Square footage on South Shore commercial listings is unreliable. Older buildings in Brockton, Taunton, and Quincy have been added onto, had mezzanines built in, and had basements finished without updated records. The assessor's card may show 14,200 square feet, the listing 16,000, and a laser measurement 15,100. Since buyers and lenders think in price per square foot, a 1,800 square foot discrepancy on a $150 per foot building is $270,000 of imagined value. ICS reconciles GBA against the assessor's record and a field measurement before advising on price.

## In practice

A Taunton industrial building is listed at 24,000 square feet for $2.4 million, $100 per square foot, which looks cheap against comparable sales at $115. The assessor's card shows 21,600 square feet of GBA. A measurement confirms the 24,000 figure includes a 2,400 square foot unheated open-sided shed that no appraiser would count. On 21,600 square feet, the asking price is $111 per foot, roughly in line with the market, and the "discount" disappears. The buyer adjusts the offer accordingly.
