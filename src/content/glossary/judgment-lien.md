## What it means

A judgment lien is an encumbrance on real property arising from a court judgment against the owner. In Massachusetts it is created by recording a levy of execution, or earlier a writ of attachment, at the Registry of Deeds in the county where the property sits. It attaches to the debtor's interest in the real estate and must be satisfied or released before the owner can deliver clear title.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Judgment liens tie the court record to the land record. ICS runs the court search and the Registry search together, because a judgment in Brockton District Court is only half the story until you know whether it was levied on the debtor's Route 28 commercial lot. For a buyer, the lien is a closing problem the seller must solve. For anyone evaluating a counterparty, a string of recorded levies is a clear public signal about how that party handles obligations.

## In practice

A seller presents a Taunton retail building as free and clear. The Bristol County Registry index shows a 2017 levy of execution for $64,500 in favor of a roofing contractor, never released. The seller says it was paid. Without a recorded release, the title examiner will not pass it. The buyer's attorney makes a recorded release a condition of closing, which takes the seller three weeks to obtain.
