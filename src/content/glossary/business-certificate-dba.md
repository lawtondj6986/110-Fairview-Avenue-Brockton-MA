## What it means

A business certificate, commonly called a DBA for "doing business as," is the Massachusetts filing required of anyone conducting business under a name other than their legal name. It is filed with the clerk of each municipality where the business is conducted, costs a modest fee set by the town, and is valid for four years. The certificate names the owners and their home addresses and is open to public inspection.

## Why it matters in a dossier

The DBA is often the only record tying a storefront name to a human being. A sign on Route 139 that says "South Shore Roofing" may be a sole proprietor whose name appears nowhere in the Secretary of the Commonwealth database. The Rockland or Hanover town clerk will have the certificate. ICS checks clerk records in each town where a subject operates, because there is no central index, and treats the information as public-records data, not as a lead for contact.

## In practice

A buyer of a Brockton mixed-use building wants to know who really runs the ground-floor tenant. The lease names a trade name. The Brockton city clerk's business certificate, filed in 2023 for $50, lists two owners, one of whom also owns the delinquent tenant two doors down according to a court records search. That connection changes how the buyer underwrites the rent roll.
