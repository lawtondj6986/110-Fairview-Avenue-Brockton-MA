## What it means

A referral program is a repeatable system for turning satisfied customers into a source of new ones. It has three parts: a moment and a script for asking, a simple way for the customer to make the introduction (a link, a code, a text), and a reward or thank-you that arrives when the referred person becomes a paying customer. It replaces hoping for word of mouth with asking for it.

## Why it matters for a local business

Referred customers on the South Shore close faster, haggle less, and leave better reviews, because they arrive trusting you. A Hingham landscaper who gets 20 percent of new accounts from referrals is paying far less to acquire them than through ads. ICS adds a referral step to the Proof Engine sequence: after the review request lands and the customer says yes, a second message asks whether a neighbor could use the same work, with a tracked link so credit is automatic.

## In practice

A Rockland painting company launched a program in March: a text after every completed job with a personal referral link, $100 off the referrer's next project and $100 off the new customer's first one, paid at job completion. Over nine months, 212 completed jobs produced 61 referral clicks, 38 estimates, and 24 signed jobs averaging $4,100. Rewards cost $4,800 against roughly $98,000 in work, about one tenth of the company's cost per customer from paid ads.
