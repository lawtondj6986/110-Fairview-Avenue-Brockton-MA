## What it means

Tenant representation is the brokerage service in which a licensed real estate broker or salesperson acts solely on behalf of a tenant seeking to lease, renew, or relocate commercial space. The tenant rep owes its duties to the tenant, not the landlord, and is compensated from the commission the landlord pays at lease signing. It is the leasing equivalent of buyer representation in a sale. Because listing brokers represent landlords, a tenant without its own representation negotiates against a professional with no one on its side of the table.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial tenants are small businesses signing a lease every five to ten years against landlords and listing brokers who negotiate leases every month. The information gap is wide: a dentist in Hanover does not know that the building across the street gave six months free rent, or that the market rate for second-generation office in Quincy dropped $3 a foot last year. ICS provides tenant representation across the South Shore under Massachusetts license #9082020, using Market sheets to benchmark every proposal, and is paid by the landlord in the usual way.

## In practice

An insurance agency with a lease expiring in Braintree asks ICS to compare renewal against relocation. The landlord's renewal offer is $29 per square foot on 3,600 square feet with no concessions. ICS surveys eleven buildings, tours five, and brings two proposals at $26 and $27 per square foot with three months free rent and $15 per square foot in refresh allowances. The current landlord matches at $26.50 with two months free and new carpet and paint. Over a five-year term the tenant saves roughly $62,000 against the original renewal offer, and pays no fee.
