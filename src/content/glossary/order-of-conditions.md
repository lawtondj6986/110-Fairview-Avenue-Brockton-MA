## What it means

An Order of Conditions is the decision a Conservation Commission issues after a public hearing on a Notice of Intent under the Wetlands Protection Act. It approves, approves with conditions, or denies the proposed work. Approvals come with standard conditions set by MassDEP plus special conditions written for the site, such as erosion controls, limits on work seasons, stormwater maintenance, and replication of any wetland filled. The Order is recorded at the registry of deeds and runs with the land until a Certificate of Compliance is issued.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

With so many South Shore commercial sites touching a buffer zone or riverfront area, Orders of Conditions are routine, and the conditions themselves shape the budget and schedule. A requirement for a two-to-one wetland replication area or a stormwater system maintained forever is a cost most buyers do not model. An open Order from a prior owner is one of the most common title surprises we see in Hanover, Marshfield, and Plymouth, usually because someone built a parking lot in 2009 and never closed it out.

## In practice

A Marshfield owner expands a parking lot by 30 spaces inside the 100-foot buffer. The Order of Conditions requires silt fence and haybales during construction, a deep-sump catch basin with annual cleaning, and a 2,000 square foot planted buffer restoration. Construction cost rises about $35,000 and the owner must file for a Certificate of Compliance when done. The lot is built in one season, and the recorded Certificate clears the title for a sale four years later.
