## What it means

Last-mile logistics is the final stage of the delivery chain, moving goods from a local distribution point to homes and businesses. The real estate it requires differs from a regional distribution center: smaller buildings on larger sites, close to population, with heavy parking for delivery vans, multiple loading positions, good highway access, and increasingly electric vehicle charging capacity. Parcel carriers, grocery delivery, furniture and appliance delivery, and the contractors who serve them are the tenants.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore sits between Boston's dense last-mile ring and the big-box distribution corridor along Route 24 and Interstate 495 in Taunton, Mansfield, and Brockton. Carriers and their delivery partners want sites in Brockton, Rockland, Weymouth, Hanover, and Plymouth that can reach the coastal towns in under 40 minutes. Sites with industrial zoning, a large paved yard, and no wetlands constraints are scarce, and towns have grown wary of 24-hour van traffic through residential neighborhoods. Expect the Planning Board to condition hours and truck routes.

## In practice

A delivery contractor leases a 22,000 square foot building on 3.5 acres off Route 24 in Brockton. The building itself is ordinary, 24-foot clear with four drive-in doors, but the 2.4-acre paved yard holds 85 vans and 60 employee cars. Rent is $14.50 per square foot NNN, about $319,000 a year, roughly 30 percent above comparable buildings without the yard. The tenant spends $240,000 on 40 Level 2 chargers and asks for a seven-year term to amortize them.
