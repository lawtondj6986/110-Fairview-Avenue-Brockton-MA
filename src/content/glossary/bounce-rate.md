## What it means

Bounce rate is the percentage of sessions in which a visitor looks at one page and leaves without clicking, calling, submitting a form, or moving to another page. In current analytics tools it is often reported as the inverse of engagement rate. It is a symptom, not a diagnosis: it tells you the page failed, not why.

## Why it matters for a local business

Most local traffic arrives on a phone from a Google search, often while the person is standing in a driveway with a problem. If a Brockton roofing page takes six seconds to load or opens with a stock photo and no phone number, the visitor is gone. JobSite pages are built for a fast first screen: service, town, proof, and a tap-to-call button. That structure is what keeps bounce low for a service business, more than any design choice.

## In practice

A dental office in Quincy had a landing page with a 78 percent bounce rate and about 900 visits a month. The page weighed 6 MB and the booking button sat below three paragraphs. After compressing images, cutting the page to 1.1 MB, and moving the booking button into the first screen, bounce dropped to 52 percent and online bookings rose from 14 to 31 a month. Same traffic, same ad spend, about $5,000 a month in added production.
