## What it means

The sales comparison approach estimates value by analyzing recent sales of similar properties and adjusting their prices for differences in location, size, condition, date of sale, and terms. The adjusted prices, usually expressed per square foot of building or per acre of land, indicate a value range for the subject. It is the primary approach for land, owner-user buildings, and small properties where buyers think in price per square foot rather than cap rate.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Owner-user sales dominate the South Shore market below $3 million, and those buyers compare price per square foot, not NOI. The Plymouth County Registry of Deeds records every sale with the deeds excise stamps, so prices are public, but the useful details, such as whether the building was vacant, what condition it was in, or whether the sale was between related parties, are not. ICS verifies South Shore comps with the brokers involved before relying on them, since one unverified distressed sale can skew a whole analysis.

## In practice

A 7,500 square foot free-standing retail building on Route 139 in Marshfield is appraised using four sales: $205, $228, $240, and $262 per square foot. The $205 comp was a vacant estate sale, adjusted up 10 percent for condition of sale; the $262 comp is a newer building in Hingham, adjusted down 12 percent for location and age. The adjusted range tightens to $226 to $238, and the appraiser concludes $232 per square foot, or $1.74 million.
