## What it means

An overlay district is a zoning layer drawn over one or more base districts. Properties inside it are subject to both the base rules and the overlay's rules. Overlays are used to protect resources, such as water supplies and flood plains, or to encourage particular development, such as downtown housing.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Overlays are where much of a parcel's value or constraint hides. Brockton, Quincy, and other communities have used smart growth and downtown overlays to allow denser mixed-use projects. Towns served by the MBTA have adopted multifamily districts, some as overlays. Water resource and flood overlays in towns like Hanover and Plymouth can limit impervious coverage or prohibit uses like fuel storage.

## In practice

A buyer looks at a 1.5 acre parcel zoned general commercial, where housing is not allowed. A multifamily overlay permits 15 units an acre by right, about 22 units. A separate aquifer overlay caps impervious coverage at 40 percent, about 26,000 square feet. Both rules apply. The site plan has to fit 22 units and parking inside that limit.
