## What it means

A bridge loan is short-term debt, usually one to three years, used to acquire or hold a commercial property while the borrower completes a plan that will qualify it for cheaper permanent financing or a sale. Bridge lenders underwrite the plan and the exit as much as the current income. Loans are typically interest-only, carry higher rates and origination fees than bank debt, and are sized to loan-to-cost rather than to stabilized value.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Value-add deals on the South Shore are often too small or too messy for the banks that want a stabilized, leased asset: a half-vacant Brockton industrial building, a Quincy office that is converting to medical, a Weymouth retail strip with an anchor on a month-to-month tenancy. Local banks and credit unions sometimes offer short-term structures, but private bridge lenders fill the gap. ICS helps buyers map the exit first. A bridge without a credible path to a term loan or sale is how investors lose buildings.

## In practice

An investor buys a vacant 18,000 square foot flex building in Avon for $1.9 million with a 12-month bridge at 10.5 percent interest-only, 70 percent loan-to-cost on the $2.4 million total budget. Interest runs about $14,700 a month. The investor leases three units at $11 per square foot NNN over eight months, generating $198,000 of NOI, then refinances with a local bank at 65 percent of a $2.6 million appraised value. The bridge cost roughly $210,000 in interest and fees, against roughly $700,000 of value created.
