## What it means

An appraisal gap is the difference between the contract price and the value the lender's appraiser reports, when the appraisal comes in lower. Lenders lend against the lower of the two numbers. The gap is the extra cash someone has to produce, or the price someone has to give up, for the deal to close.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Owner-user buildings in Brockton, Rockland, and Hanover trade thinly. An appraiser may have three or four usable sales from the past two years, some of them in different towns. When buyers compete for a clean flex building, the winning price can run ahead of what those older sales support. The gap shows up three weeks into the deal, usually after the buyer has spent money on inspections.

## In practice

A buyer agrees to pay $1,400,000 for a 10,000 square foot building in Hanover with a loan at 75 percent. The appraisal comes back at $1,300,000. The loan drops from $1,050,000 to $975,000, so the buyer needs $75,000 more cash on top of the original $350,000. The parties settle at $1,350,000 and the buyer covers the rest.
