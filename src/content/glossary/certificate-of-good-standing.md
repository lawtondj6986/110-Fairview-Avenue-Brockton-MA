## What it means

A certificate of good standing is a dated document from a government agency stating that an entity has met its obligations to that agency. In Massachusetts, the Secretary of the Commonwealth issues one confirming an entity is legally organized and current on annual reports. The Department of Revenue issues a separate one confirming tax compliance. Each is a snapshot as of its date and says nothing about debts, lawsuits, or reputation.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Good standing is a floor, not a verdict. An entity that is not in good standing cannot reliably sign a lease, transfer a liquor license, or close a loan, so the check belongs at the front of any counterparty file. ICS verifies corporate status directly in the public database rather than accepting a photocopy, and asks the counterparty for the DOR certificate when a deal depends on it. Private tax information is never sought by other routes.

## In practice

A Quincy landlord is about to sign a 7-year lease with an LLC tenant. The free lookup shows the LLC was administratively dissolved in 2024 for failing to file two annual reports. Reviving it costs the tenant the back fees plus about $100 and a few days. The landlord makes revival a condition of signing, which is a cheap fix for a problem that would have been expensive at eviction.
