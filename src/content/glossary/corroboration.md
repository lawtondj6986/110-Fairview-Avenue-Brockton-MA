## What it means

Corroboration is the rule that no single source makes a finding. A fact seen in one record becomes a finding only when a second source, independent of the first, confirms it. A news story is corroborated by the docket it describes. A tip is corroborated by the deed. Until then it is a lead and is labeled as one. Every source is public or licensed; none is obtained by deception.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Clients act on dossiers: they walk from deals, reprice offers, and brief attorneys. An uncorroborated error about a Hanover landlord or a Quincy counterparty can cost real money and real reputation. ICS grades every finding by the number and quality of independent sources behind it, and the brief says plainly which items are single-source.

## In practice

A reporter's story says a Taunton developer "walked away from three projects." The MassCourts search finds one breach-of-contract suit, dismissed. The Registry shows two parcels sold at a loss, roughly 18 percent below purchase price, within 14 months. The finding becomes: one litigated dispute, two distressed sales, no record of a third. That is narrower than the headline, and it is what the client can rely on.
