## What it means

Assessed value is the figure a city or town assigns to a parcel to compute its property tax. In Massachusetts the standard is "full and fair cash value," and every community revalues on a Department of Revenue schedule, with interim adjustments in between. Tax due equals assessed value divided by 1,000 times the tax rate, and many towns run a split rate that charges commercial and industrial property more than residential.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The split rate is the story here. Brockton, Quincy, Weymouth, and Braintree all tax commercial property at a higher rate than homes, and the gap changes every December when the rate is set. A buyer who models taxes off the seller's last bill, in a town that just revalued or that reassesses after a sale, can be off by thousands a year. Pull the assessor's card and the current rate before you underwrite, and ask whether the town is due for revaluation.

## In practice

A 12,000 square foot flex building in Rockland is assessed at $1.4 million. At a commercial rate near $20 per thousand, that is about $28,000 a year. If the sale closes at $2.1 million and the assessor catches up to the price, the bill moves toward $42,000, a $14,000 hit to NOI. At an 8 percent cap rate that is roughly $175,000 of value. Underwrite the post-sale tax, not the current one.
