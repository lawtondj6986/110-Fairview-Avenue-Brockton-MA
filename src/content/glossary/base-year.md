## What it means

In a base year lease, the landlord pays the building's operating expenses up to the amount spent in a stated year, usually the first calendar year of the term. In every later year the tenant pays its proportionate share of any increase above that base. The structure sits between a full gross lease and a triple net lease: the tenant is protected from year-one costs but exposed to inflation, tax reassessments, and insurance jumps after that.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Base year leases are standard in multi-tenant office buildings in Quincy, Braintree, and along Route 3. The detail that bites South Shore tenants is property tax. Towns revalue on their own schedules, and a Quincy reassessment can move a building's tax bill 15 percent in one year, all of which lands above your base. Timing matters too: a lease that starts in November gets a base year with two months of real data and ten months of estimates.

## In practice

A tenant takes 5,000 square feet, 10 percent of a 50,000 square foot Braintree office building. Base year operating expenses come in at $9.00 per square foot, or $450,000 for the building. In year three, expenses run $9.80, a $40,000 increase. The tenant's 10 percent share is $4,000 for that year, billed on top of base rent. Had the base year been grossed up from 70 percent occupancy to 95 percent, the baseline would have been closer to $9.40 and the tenant's bill about $2,000.
