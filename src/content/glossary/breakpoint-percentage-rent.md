## What it means

The breakpoint is the gross sales figure at which percentage rent begins. A natural breakpoint equals base rent divided by the percentage rate, so the tenant pays overage only once sales exceed the level the base rent already implies. An artificial breakpoint is any other number the lease states.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Percentage rent shows up in grocery-anchored centers and restaurant leases along Route 3 and Route 139. The breakpoint decides whether the clause ever costs anything. Three details move real money: what counts as gross sales, since online orders, gift cards, and delivery fees are often argued over, whether the breakpoint steps up with each rent escalation, and how it is prorated in a partial year. Have your attorney read the definition of gross sales closely.

## In practice

A 3,000-square-foot restaurant in Hanover pays $28 a square foot, or $84,000 a year, with 6 percent percentage rent over a natural breakpoint of $1,400,000. Sales reach $1,650,000. Overage is 6 percent of $250,000, or $15,000, for total rent of $99,000. If base rent rises 3 percent and the breakpoint stays fixed, the tenant pays more on both ends.
