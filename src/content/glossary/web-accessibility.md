## What it means

Web accessibility means a site works for people who use screen readers, keyboard navigation, magnification, or captions, and for people with limited vision, hearing, motor control, or cognition. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define testable criteria at levels A, AA, and AAA: text alternatives for images, sufficient color contrast, keyboard operability, readable structure, labeled forms. WCAG 2.1 AA is the common target in the United States.

## Why it matters for a local business

About one in four adults has a disability, and the South Shore skews older than the state average. A dental office in Weymouth or a restaurant in Fort Lauderdale that a screen reader cannot read loses those customers and their families. There is also legal exposure: accessibility suits against small business websites are routine, and settlement plus remediation commonly runs $10,000 to $30,000. Talk to your attorney about your obligations. JobSite builds to WCAG 2.1 AA from the start.

## In practice

A Hanover physical therapy practice had a site with 41 unlabeled images, a booking form with no field labels, and gray-on-white text at a 2.6:1 contrast ratio. The rebuild fixed all three, added keyboard focus states, and captioned 2 videos. Form completions rose about 14 percent over the next quarter, mostly on mobile, and a demand letter 5 months later was answered with the audit report.
