## What it means

The building envelope is the physical shell that keeps water, air, and temperature where they belong: roof, exterior walls, windows, doors, and foundation. Envelope condition drives energy cost, interior damage, and the biggest capital repairs a building will need. In zoning, the same phrase can mean the buildable volume on a lot.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Much of the industrial and retail stock in Brockton, Rockland, and Taunton dates from the 1960s through the 1980s. Freeze and thaw cycles, coastal wind, and snow load are hard on flat roofs, block walls, and old sealants. An envelope problem rarely shows on a sunny walk-through. It shows in ceiling stains, efflorescence on block, and heating bills.

## In practice

A buyer under agreement on a 20,000 square foot warehouse in Brockton hires a roofer during due diligence. The membrane is 22 years old and replacement is quoted at about $14 a square foot, or $280,000. The buyer uses the report to negotiate a $150,000 price reduction and budgets the remainder as a year-two capital item.
