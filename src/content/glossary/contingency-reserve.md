## What it means

A contingency reserve is an amount carried in a construction or renovation budget to pay for costs that cannot be identified in advance: unforeseen site conditions, design gaps, code interpretations, and price changes. It is distinct from a replacement reserve, which is an operating-period set-aside for future capital items. A contingency that goes unused is returned to the owner or applied to the loan; one that is exhausted means the owner funds the rest from equity.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial renovation involves buildings from the 1950s through the 1980s along Route 18, Route 139, and Route 3A, with original electrical, undocumented additions, and septic systems that may not pass. Site work surprises are routine: ledge in Rockland and Hingham, high groundwater in Marshfield and Plymouth, and contaminated fill in Brockton industrial areas. A 5 percent contingency on these projects is optimism, not planning, and the lender's inspector will say so.

## In practice

An owner-user renovates a 9,000 square foot former auto dealership in Weymouth into a veterinary hospital. Hard costs bid at $1.35 million; the owner carries a 12 percent contingency of $162,000 in the bank budget. During demolition the crew finds a buried hydraulic lift and oil-stained soil, $48,000 to remove and dispose, and the fire department requires a larger water service, $31,000. The contingency covers both, leaving $83,000 for the rest of the job, and the owner never has to write a surprise check.
