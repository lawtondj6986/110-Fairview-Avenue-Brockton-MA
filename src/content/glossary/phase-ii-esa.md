## What it means

A Phase II Environmental Site Assessment is an intrusive investigation that follows a Phase I when records or site conditions suggest a possible release. An environmental consultant drills soil borings, installs groundwater monitoring wells, and samples soil, water, and sometimes indoor air or building materials, then compares the results to state standards. In Massachusetts the results are read against Chapter 21E reporting thresholds, and a hit above them triggers notification to MassDEP and oversight by a Licensed Site Professional. The report either closes the question or defines a cleanup.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore's commercial corridors were built on gas stations, auto body shops, machine shops, and shoe factories. Brockton, Whitman, and Rockland industrial buildings routinely carry a Phase I recognized environmental condition, and the Phase II is what tells a buyer whether it is a $15,000 study or a $400,000 problem. Lenders here, particularly SBA 504 lenders, often will not fund a flagged property without one. The due diligence period must be long enough to drill, wait for lab results, and read the report, usually four to eight weeks.

## In practice

A buyer of a former machine shop in Whitman gets a Phase I that flags a floor drain and a 1980s heating oil tank. The Phase II, at $18,000, puts six borings and two wells on the site and finds petroleum in soil near the tank above reportable levels but no groundwater impact. The seller reports to MassDEP, hires an LSP, removes about 60 tons of soil for $45,000, and closes the release before the buyer's extended due diligence period ends.
