## What it means

An environmental escrow is a portion of the purchase price held by a third party at closing to fund the cleanup of a known or suspected release, or to secure the seller's indemnity for one. It lets a sale close before the environmental work is finished. The escrow agreement defines what triggers a release of funds, who performs the work, what level of closure ends the arrangement, and where any shortfall or surplus goes. It usually pairs with an environmental indemnity in the purchase and sale agreement.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Brockton, Rockland, Whitman, and Taunton have a century of shoe factories, tanneries, machine shops, and gas stations, and a Phase I or Phase II assessment on an older industrial property often finds something. Under Massachusetts Chapter 21E, the response action runs through a Licensed Site Professional and MassDEP rather than a government cleanup, which makes the timeline more predictable but still a year or more. An environmental escrow lets the owner-user or investor close, start using the building, and leave the seller carrying the cleanup it created. Talk to an environmental attorney before signing the escrow terms.

## In practice

A Phase II on a former auto repair building in Brockton finds petroleum in soil near an old waste oil tank. The LSP estimates $90,000 to remove the tank, excavate 200 tons of soil, and file a Permanent Solution Statement. The buyer and seller agree on a $1.1 million price with $160,000 held in escrow for 18 months. The seller completes the work for $104,000, MassDEP accepts the filing at month 14, and $56,000 returns to the seller.
