## What it means

Open Graph tags are a set of meta tags in a page's head, introduced by Facebook and now read by most platforms including LinkedIn, iMessage, Slack, and many messaging apps, that define how a link unfurls when shared: the title, description, preview image, URL, and content type. X reads a similar set of card tags. Without them, platforms guess, often picking a logo fragment or no image at all.

## Why it matters for a local business

Most local referrals happen in a text thread or a neighborhood Facebook group. "Who do you use for HVAC in Rockland?" gets a pasted link. If that link unfurls as a clean image, a plain title, and one sentence that says what you do and where, it gets tapped. If it shows a broken image and the words "Home | index," it does not. JobSite sets Open Graph tags on every page, with a per-page image for services and towns.

## In practice

A Weymouth landscaper's homepage link, when shared, showed no image and the title "Home." After adding a 1200 by 630 pixel photo of a finished patio, a title with the town and service, and a 140-character description, the same link shared in a town Facebook group drew 212 clicks over a weekend, against roughly 30 for a similar post 2 months earlier. Nothing else about the site changed.
