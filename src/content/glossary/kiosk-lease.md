## What it means

A kiosk lease covers a small, freestanding retail unit, typically 50 to 300 square feet, placed in a mall common area, a shopping center sidewalk, or a parking lot. Terms are short, often month to month up to a year or two, and the agreement is frequently written as a license with relocation and termination rights for the owner.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Kiosks include the cart in a mall corridor, the drive-up coffee hut in a parking field, and the key-cutting or parcel locker unit by a grocery entrance. For an owner, a parking lot kiosk is income from land that earns nothing. But it can collide with existing leases: anchor tenants often hold no-build zones, parking ratio requirements, and exclusive use rights. It may also need site plan review. Check the anchor leases and the zoning before signing a kiosk.

## In practice

A center owner in Brockton licenses a 160-square-foot drive-up coffee kiosk in the outer parking field for $2,400 a month plus 6 percent of sales over $40,000 a month, on a one-year term with 60-day termination. It removes 9 parking spaces. The owner first confirms the remaining ratio still meets the grocery anchor's 4.5 per 1,000 requirement. Annual base income is $28,800.
