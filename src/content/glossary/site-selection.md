## What it means

Site selection is the structured process of identifying and evaluating locations for a business or investment against a defined set of requirements. It starts with the criteria: size, zoning, access, parking, visibility, labor, utilities, cost, and timing. It moves to screening the market for parcels or buildings that meet them, then to scoring the finalists on fieldwork and data, and it ends with a recommendation that weighs the trade-offs. Done well, it produces a defensible answer rather than a gut feeling about a building someone happened to show you.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore is a collection of 40-odd towns with different zoning bylaws, tax rates, permitting cultures, and traffic patterns, and a site that is perfect in Hanover may be unbuildable in Norwell two miles away. Route 3 and Route 24 corridors behave differently for retail than for industrial. Labor comes from Brockton and Taunton for industrial, from the coastal towns for medical and office. ICS maintains Market sheets on each town and uses them to narrow a search before any site visits, so a client evaluates five real options instead of touring twenty.

## In practice

A home services contractor with 22 trucks needs a 12,000 to 15,000 square foot shop with yard space, within 20 minutes of most of its customers in Hingham, Norwell, and Scituate. ICS screens 14 towns for industrial zoning that permits contractor yards and outdoor vehicle storage, finds candidate sites in Rockland, Hanover, Abington, and Pembroke, and scores seven on drive time, yard area, purchase price per square foot, and permitting path. The Rockland site wins at $1.95 million for 13,800 square feet on 1.6 acres, with a special permit for outdoor storage estimated at 90 days.
