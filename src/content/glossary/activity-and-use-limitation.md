## What it means

An Activity and Use Limitation is a deed notice filed at the registry of deeds under the Massachusetts Contingency Plan, the regulations that implement Chapter 21E. It is recorded when a release has been cleaned up to a level that is safe only if certain uses or activities are kept off the site. The AUL lists permitted uses, prohibited uses, and obligations such as maintaining a pavement cap or following a soil management plan. It runs with the land and binds every future owner.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Former gas stations, machine shops, and dry cleaners along Route 18, Route 28, and Route 139 are where AULs show up most. Many of these sites are fine for a contractor's yard or a retail strip and wrong for a daycare or apartments, and the AUL says which. A title search may not flag it clearly, so your Phase I should pull MassDEP's site file and the recorded instrument. ICS flags AULs in every Buyer Brief because they shape what a buyer can do and what a lender will finance.

## In practice

A Brockton auto-body building sells with an AUL that allows commercial and industrial use, bars residential and childcare use, and requires the asphalt cap stay intact. The buyer planned a service garage, so the AUL cost nothing. Two years later a developer offers $300,000 more to build 20 apartments, and the deal dies because terminating the AUL would mean excavating roughly 800 tons of soil at $150 to $200 a ton.
