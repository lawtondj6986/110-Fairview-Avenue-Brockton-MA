## What it means

A strip center is an unenclosed row of attached retail units with a shared parking field in front and direct customer entry from the lot. They range from a 6,000 square foot three-tenant building to a 50,000 square foot neighborhood center with a small grocery or pharmacy anchor. Units are described as end caps, which have exposure on two sides and often support drive-thrus, and inline spaces between them. Rents, CAM, and sales are quoted on gross leasable area.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Strip centers are the dominant retail form on the South Shore's commercial corridors: Route 18 in Weymouth and Abington, Route 53 in Hanover and Norwell, Route 139 in Marshfield and Pembroke, Route 3A in Hingham and Cohasset, and Route 44 and Route 123 in Taunton and Brockton. Most are owned by local families or small partnerships and trade in the $2 million to $10 million range, the heart of ICS's advisory work. Traffic counts, visibility, and curb cuts drive rent far more than finish, and a center on the going-home side of the road with a signalized entrance commands a premium.

## In practice

A 24,000 square foot strip center on Route 139 in Pembroke has nine tenants, with a 4,200 square foot pizza and liquor end cap at $30 per square foot NNN and inline spaces averaging $22. Gross rent is about $570,000, recoveries cover $185,000 of expenses, and NOI after a 5 percent vacancy and 4 percent management allowance is roughly $515,000. At a 7.5 percent cap rate the center is worth about $6.9 million, or $286 per square foot of GLA.
