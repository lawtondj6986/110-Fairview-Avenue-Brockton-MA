## What it means

Code violation records document instances where a public official found a property out of compliance with the state building code, sanitary code, fire code, or local zoning, and the steps that followed: the notice, the deadline to correct, reinspections, fines, and any court action. They are kept by the issuing department and are public. A clean property has a file with routine inspections; a troubled one has a trail of orders.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Violations reveal how a property has been maintained and how its owner responds to authority. A Brockton mixed-use building with 14 unresolved orders over six years tells a buyer about deferred maintenance and about the seller. A restaurant tenant with repeated health failures tells a landlord about the rent roll's durability. ICS pulls violation files from every relevant department, judges them against the local base rate, and reports both.

## In practice

A buyer underwrites a Route 18 strip center at a 7.2% cap rate. Fire department records show three orders since 2022 for a blocked egress and an uninspected sprinkler system, with the latest still open. The cure costs about $35,000 and the reinspection must pass before a tenant can renew its occupancy permit. The buyer negotiates a $45,000 credit and a seller obligation to close the order before the closing date.
