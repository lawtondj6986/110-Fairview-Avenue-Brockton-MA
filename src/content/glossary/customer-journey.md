## What it means

The customer journey is the full path a customer travels with your business: discovering you, contacting you, receiving a quote, booking, getting the work done, paying, and coming back or sending a friend. Each stage has its own conversion rate and its own point of failure. Mapping it shows where revenue is lost and in what quantity.

## Why it matters for a local business

A local business usually sees only the stages it touches by hand. The stages in between, such as the 48 hours after a quote goes out, are where most jobs are lost. ICS maps the journey at the start of every Lead Engine build so the automations land on the weak points: missed-call text-back at inquiry, quote follow-up at decision, review requests at completion. A Plymouth plumber does not need more leads if 40 percent of his quotes die in silence.

## In practice

A Quincy auto shop mapped one month: 210 calls, 160 answered, 120 quoted, 70 booked, 68 completed, 9 reviews. The largest leak was quote to booking, with 42 percent lost. A three-text follow-up over five days lifted booked jobs from 70 to 89 the next month, about $7,600 in added revenue from leads the shop had already paid for. The review step was fixed next.
