## What it means

A corporate guaranty is a written promise by a business entity, usually the tenant's parent company or an affiliated operating company, to pay rent and perform lease obligations if the tenant defaults. It serves the same function as a personal guaranty but shifts the exposure from an individual to a company. Its strength depends entirely on the guarantor's creditworthiness and on the drafting: whether it is full or capped, whether it covers the whole term or burns off, and whether the guarantor waives defenses such as requiring the landlord to pursue the tenant first.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Multi-unit operators are a growing share of South Shore retail and medical tenants: a dental group opening a fourth office in Hanover, a fitness franchise in Brockton, a regional urgent care in Plymouth. Each location is typically a single-purpose LLC with no assets beyond the lease and fixtures. The landlord's real security is the guaranty from the parent. ICS checks the guarantor's standing with the Secretary of the Commonwealth and asks for two years of financials before advising a landlord client to accept a corporate guaranty in place of a personal one or a larger security deposit.

## In practice

A regional physical therapy company with eleven locations leases 3,800 square feet in Weymouth at $26 per square foot NNN, roughly $99,000 a year, through a new single-location LLC. The landlord asks for a personal guaranty from the founder. The company counters with a guaranty from the parent, which shows $4 million in annual revenue, capped at 18 months of rent, about $148,000, and burning off after 36 months of timely payment. The landlord accepts, with a two-month security deposit of $16,500 as additional cover.
