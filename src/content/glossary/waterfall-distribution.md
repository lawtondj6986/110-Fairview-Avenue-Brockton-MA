## What it means

A waterfall distribution is the set of rules in a partnership or LLC operating agreement that dictate how cash from operations and from a sale or refinance is divided among the equity holders. Cash flows down through tiers: usually return of invested capital, then a preferred return to the limited partners, then one or more splits between investors and the sponsor that tilt toward the sponsor as returns climb. The sponsor's disproportionate share at the upper tiers is the promote, or carried interest.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Small syndications are increasingly how South Shore multifamily, flex, and mixed-use deals get bought: a local sponsor raises $1 million to $3 million from a dozen investors, many of them business owners in Brockton, Hanover, or Plymouth who want real estate exposure without running a building. The waterfall is where the money actually goes, and two deals with the same pro forma IRR can pay investors very differently. ICS reviews sponsor track records and the entity filings behind them as part of due-diligence briefs; the waterfall math itself is a question for your attorney and CPA.

## In practice

Investors put $2 million into a Brockton flex building deal with an 8 percent preferred return, then a 70/30 split, then 50/50 above a stated IRR hurdle. The property sells in year five for a $1.4 million profit after return of capital. Simplified, the first $800,000 covers the 8 percent pref over five years. The next $450,000 goes 70/30 under the first split tier, and once the hurdle is met the remaining $150,000 splits 50/50. Investors receive about $1.19 million; the sponsor about $210,000.
