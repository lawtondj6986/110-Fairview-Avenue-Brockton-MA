## What it means

A betterment assessment is a charge a Massachusetts city or town imposes under Chapter 80 on parcels that receive a special benefit from a public improvement, most often sewer, water, sidewalks, or road reconstruction. The town divides the project cost among benefited parcels by a formula such as frontage, area, or units, records the assessment at the registry of deeds, and adds it to the tax bill. It can be paid in a lump sum or apportioned over up to 20 years with interest, and the unpaid balance is a lien.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Sewer extensions are the big one here. Towns such as Hanover, Pembroke, Marshfield, and parts of Plymouth have run or planned sewer projects funded in part by betterments, and the per-parcel charge on a commercial lot can reach five or six figures. The betterment is a cost, but the sewer it pays for often raises the property's value by more, by unlocking restaurant, medical, or multifamily use that septic would not allow. Pull the collector's records for both recorded and pending betterments before you underwrite.

## In practice

A Marshfield owner with a 4,000 square foot retail building receives a $48,000 sewer betterment, apportioned over 20 years at 5 percent, roughly $3,900 a year on the tax bill. When the building sells two years later, the municipal lien certificate shows a $43,000 unpaid balance. The buyer's lender requires it paid at closing, so the seller's net proceeds drop by $43,000. The buyer then leases to a 40-seat cafe that the old septic system could never have served.
