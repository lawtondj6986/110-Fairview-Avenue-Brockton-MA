## What it means

A due-on-sale clause, also called an acceleration clause on transfer, gives the lender the right to declare the entire loan balance due if the borrower sells or transfers the property without consent. In commercial loans it usually reaches transfers of ownership interests in the borrowing entity too. It is the lender's control over who owes it money.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Three common local moves run into it: deeding a building from personal names into an LLC, bringing in or buying out a partner, and selling with owner financing wrapped around an existing bank loan. Family-owned buildings in Brockton and Weymouth change hands between generations this way all the time. Most community banks will work with a borrower who asks first.

## In practice

Two brothers own a Taunton building with $600,000 owed at 4.25 percent. One sells his half to the other and they record a new deed without calling the bank. The mortgage treats that as a transfer. The bank can demand $600,000 or reprice the loan at current rates. A consent request beforehand might have cost a $1,500 fee.
