## What it means

A pop-up lease is a short-term occupancy agreement for a temporary store, often written as a license. Rent is usually a flat fee, a percentage of sales, or both. The space is taken as-is with little or no landlord work, and the tenant brings movable fixtures.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

For a landlord, a pop-up puts income and activity in a space that would otherwise sit dark while it is marketed. For a business, it tests a location before committing to five years. Seasonal demand makes it practical here: holiday retail from October through December, and summer concepts in Plymouth, Hull, and Scituate. Watch for conflicts with existing tenants' exclusive use clauses, and confirm the use is allowed without a new permit.

## In practice

A landlord in Hingham has a 1,600-square-foot space vacant for seven months at an asking rent of $36 a square foot. A holiday gift retailer takes it from November 1 to January 15 for $3,500 a month plus 8 percent of sales over $45,000 a month, with a 30-day landlord termination right after December 26. The landlord collects about $8,750 in base rent and the tenant signs a three-year lease in February.
