## What it means

An executive summary is the short, self-contained section at the front of a report that gives a decision-maker the conclusion, the confidence attached to it, the principal supporting findings, and the recommended next step. It is written last, after the analysis, but placed first. In intelligence-style products it follows the bottom-line-up-front convention: the answer comes before the reasoning.

## Why it matters in a dossier

The person who commissioned a Deep Dossier is usually about to make a decision: sign the purchase and sale agreement, hire the vendor, enter the race. They need the answer, how sure ICS is of it, and what it means for them, on one page. The 80 pages of court dockets, Registry printouts, and OCPF exports behind it are there for their attorney and for anyone who wants to check the work. Every ICS brief leads with a one-page summary, and every fact in it is cited to the appendix.

## In practice

A buyer's brief on a Hanover industrial property opens: "No material undisclosed risk found. Confidence: high. Two items for counsel: a 2009 easement affecting the rear 40 feet and a lapsed HIC registration on the seller's contractor affiliate. Recommend proceeding to the 45-day diligence period with the easement reviewed by title counsel." The buyer read 90 words and knew what to do.
