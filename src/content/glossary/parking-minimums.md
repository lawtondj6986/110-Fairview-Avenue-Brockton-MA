## What it means

Parking minimums are the zoning requirements that set the fewest off-street parking spaces a property must provide for a given use, expressed as spaces per square foot of floor area, per seat, per employee, or per dwelling unit. They determine how much of a lot can be building versus pavement and often decide whether a use can go into an existing building at all. Some towns have begun reducing or removing minimums in dense districts, but most South Shore bylaws still carry them.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Parking is the quiet constraint on South Shore reuse. A 1980s office building on Route 53 in Hanover parked at 4 spaces per thousand cannot become medical at 5 or 6 per thousand without relief, and a vacant restaurant on Route 3A cannot become a larger restaurant without more pavement. Brockton and Quincy have relaxed minimums in their downtown and MBTA Communities districts to encourage reuse near commuter rail; most suburban towns have not. The Zoning Board of Appeals sees more parking relief requests than almost any other kind.

## In practice

A dental group wants to buy a 6,000 square foot former insurance office in Weymouth with 24 parking spaces, 4 per thousand square feet. Medical office requires 1 space per 175 square feet, 35 spaces. The site can fit 29 after restriping and removing a landscaped island. The group applies for a special permit with a parking study showing peak demand of 26 cars at a comparable practice, plus a shared parking agreement for 6 spaces at the adjacent bank. Approval takes four months and about $18,000 in engineering and legal fees.
