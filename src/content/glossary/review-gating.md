## What it means

Review gating is the practice of asking customers how they feel before asking for a review, then sending only satisfied customers to a public review site while routing unhappy ones to a private form. The goal is an inflated public rating. Google's Business Profile policies prohibit it, and the FTC's 2024 rule on fake and suppressed reviews makes it a federal compliance question as well.

## Why it matters for a local business

Many review-request tools sold to small businesses still do this by default, and the owner may not know. A Weymouth dentist whose vendor "filters" reviews is exposed on two fronts: Google can remove reviews or act against the profile, and the FTC rule allows civil penalties for suppressing negative reviews. Proof Engine does not gate. It asks every customer, sends the same link to everyone, and uses the feedback question only to alert the owner that someone needs a call. An honest 4.6 is more durable and more convincing than a filtered 4.9.

## In practice

A Brockton auto shop's previous vendor had pushed 4- and 5-star responders to Google and 1- to 3-star responders to a private form. Its profile showed 4.9 stars on 70 reviews while the private forms held 40 complaints no one read. ICS replaced the flow with an ask-everyone sequence. The rating settled at 4.6 on 190 reviews within a year, the shop fixed the two issues the complaints repeated, and call volume from the profile rose anyway. If you suspect a current or past vendor has been gating on your behalf, talk to counsel.
