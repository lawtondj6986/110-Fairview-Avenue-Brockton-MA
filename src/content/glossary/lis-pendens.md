## What it means

A lis pendens, Latin for "suit pending," is a notice recorded in the land records stating that litigation is underway whose outcome could affect title to, or the right to use, the described real estate. In Massachusetts it requires a judge's endorsement before recording. Once recorded, anyone acquiring an interest in the property takes it subject to the result of the case.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A lis pendens is one of the few places where the court record writes itself into the Registry, which makes it a natural link in a property brief. ICS checks the Registry index for the parcel and the parties' names, then pulls the referenced docket. The pleadings often reveal more about a property than the offering memorandum: a disputed purchase agreement, a contested easement, a partnership breaking up. All of it is public and lawful to read.

## In practice

A Weymouth retail pad is offered off-market at an attractive price. The Norfolk County Registry shows a memorandum of lis pendens recorded 7 months ago. The Norfolk Superior Court docket reveals a former partner suing to enforce a right of first refusal on the parcel. The buyer now knows why the price is attractive and that no closing is possible until the case resolves or settles.
