## What it means

Findings of fact are the factual conclusions a decision-maker reaches after hearing evidence. In a bench trial the judge writes them, usually alongside rulings of law. Administrative agencies and local boards issue them too: a Massachusetts zoning board must state the reasons for its decision. They are public unless the case is sealed.

## Why it matters in a dossier

They are among the strongest sources a researcher can cite. A neutral party heard both sides and put its conclusions in writing. When a dossier says "the court found," that carries far more weight than "the plaintiff alleged." We grade sources accordingly, and we check whether the findings were later appealed or reversed before relying on them.

## In practice

A subject was sued 3 times. Two cases settled with no findings. The third went to a 4-day bench trial, and the judge's 22-page decision found that the subject's company had billed for work it did not perform and awarded $64,000. The dossier quotes that decision directly, cites the docket number, and reports the other 2 cases as resolved without findings. We also confirm no appeal was filed within the deadline, which means the findings stand as the final word.
