## What it means

Metadata is the structured information embedded in or attached to a file that describes the file rather than its visible content. Document metadata includes author, timestamps, software, and edit history. Image metadata can include device and location. Record-system metadata includes when a filing was received and by whom. Massachusetts treats metadata in electronic public records as part of the record, which can be requested in native format.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Metadata authenticates and dates. A "2019" consultant report whose PDF was created in 2023, or a town memo whose author field names a developer's employee, is a finding. ICS asks for electronic records in their native format so the metadata survives, reads it with standard tools, and records what it shows alongside the visible content. It never alters metadata, and it treats a mismatch as a question to investigate, not a conclusion.

## In practice

A zoning applicant submits a traffic study dated March 2024 to a Hanover board. The PDF released by the town under a records request shows a creation date of October 2024 and an author field matching the applicant's engineer, not the independent firm named on the cover. That is not proof of anything improper. It is enough for the abutters' attorney to ask, on the record, who wrote the study and when.
