## What it means

A plot plan is a drawing prepared by a registered land surveyor showing a parcel's boundary lines and dimensions, existing and proposed structures, setback distances to each line, and often easements, driveways, and the street. It is the document a building inspector uses to confirm zoning compliance before issuing a permit and the document a buyer uses to check that what is being sold matches what is on the ground. A certified plot plan carries the surveyor's stamp; a mortgage plot plan is a lighter version done for a lender.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Older commercial lots in Brockton, Weymouth, and Rockland were often laid out from 19th-century plans and sold by reference to stone bounds that are long gone. Buildings sit over lines, fences wander, and shared drives have no recorded easement. A plot plan during due diligence surfaces these before they become a lender's problem. It also tells you quickly whether an expansion fits inside the setbacks and coverage limits, which is the question most owner-users are actually asking when they look at a building.

## In practice

A buyer of a 12,000 square foot warehouse in Weymouth orders a certified plot plan for $2,800 during the inspection period. It shows the building's rear wall 9 feet from the line where the bylaw now requires 20, a nonconformity that is grandfathered, and a neighbor's dumpster pad 4 feet over the side line. The attorney obtains a zoning determination letter on the setback and a recorded license agreement for the pad, and the lender accepts both.
