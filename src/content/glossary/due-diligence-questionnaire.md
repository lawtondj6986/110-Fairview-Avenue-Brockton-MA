## What it means

A due diligence questionnaire, or DDQ, is a document sent openly to the other side of a deal asking for written answers about its ownership, history, and legal exposure. The counterparty knows who is asking and why. The answers are usually signed, and sometimes backed by representations in the final contract.

## Why it matters in a dossier

It is the lawful, direct way to get information that is not public. We never obtain private information by pretext. We ask for it, in writing, through the client. Then we verify what can be verified. The dossier reports three things: what they said, what the record shows, and where the two differ.

## In practice

A client plans to bring a partner into a $2 million Taunton redevelopment. The partner's DDQ lists 3 prior entities and answers "none" for litigation in the last 10 years. Our search finds a fourth entity and 2 civil suits, one ending in a $90,000 judgment. The client raises it. The partner's explanation, and how fast it arrives, becomes part of the decision. Both the signed questionnaire and the court dockets go into the file, side by side, so the client's attorney can draft representations that match the facts.
