## What it means

Geofencing is location-based advertising that defines a virtual boundary around a physical place and serves ads to mobile devices detected inside it, or shortly after they leave. The fence can be a radius around a store, a polygon traced around a stadium, or a dozen competitor locations at once. Ads then follow those devices in apps and on websites for a set window, often 30 days.

## Why it matters for a local business

For a South Shore business, geofencing is a scalpel rather than a shotgun. A Brockton tire shop can fence the three competitor lots on Route 28 and the Brockton Fairgrounds during an event weekend. A Quincy restaurant can fence the hotels near Marina Bay. It is rarely the first dollar ICS recommends spending; Google Business Profile, reviews, and search ads come first. But for a client with a working Lead Engine and a specific venue to capture, it is a useful add.

## In practice

A Hanover kitchen remodeler fenced the South Shore Home Show at the Hanover Mall site for one weekend and the two big-box home improvement stores on Route 53 for the following 30 days. The campaign cost $1,400 and delivered about 48,000 impressions. It produced 212 landing page visits, 19 QuoteBrain estimate requests, and 3 signed projects averaging $38,000. The remodeler now repeats the home show fence every spring.
