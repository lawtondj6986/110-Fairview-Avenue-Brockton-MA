## What it means

As-built drawings are the final record set of a construction project, marked up or redrawn to reflect every field change made during the work. Where the original construction documents show design intent, as-builts show the actual locations of walls, doors, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, and structural members as installed. They are typically delivered by the general contractor at project close-out, often as a condition of final payment, and become part of the building's permanent file.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

A large share of South Shore commercial inventory is older: 1970s and 1980s flex buildings along Route 24 in Brockton and Avon, converted mills in Taunton, strip retail on Route 139 in Hanover and Marshfield. Many have changed hands several times without anyone keeping drawings. When a tenant wants to demise a 12,000 square foot bay into two units, or a buyer plans to add a drive-in door, the absence of accurate as-builts turns into change orders. ICS asks for as-builts in every due-diligence request list and flags their absence as a cost item, not a deal killer.

## In practice

An owner-user buys a 15,000 square foot Rockland warehouse and plans to add a 2,000 square foot office mezzanine. The seller delivers a 1986 permit set but no as-builts. The architect orders a field survey for $5,500 and discovers the main electrical feed and a sanitary line run under the exact spot planned for the stair. Relocating the stair costs $4,000 in redesign. Discovering it after framing would have cost closer to $25,000 and three weeks.
