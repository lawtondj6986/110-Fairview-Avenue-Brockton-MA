## What it means

Sales per square foot is annual gross sales divided by the square footage of the store, usually the leased area. It lets an owner compare tenants of different sizes and lets a tenant compare locations. It says nothing about profit on its own, since margins differ by category.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

For a landlord or a buyer of a retail center, it is the best early signal of which tenants will renew, which can absorb an increase, and which are at risk. Many leases require tenants to report sales, and those reports belong in due diligence. For a tenant, it is the test of whether a space is too big. A shop in Weymouth doing $180 a square foot in 2,500 square feet might do the same sales in 1,600 and cut rent by a third.

## In practice

A buyer reviews a strip center in Taunton. A 1,500-square-foot sandwich shop reports $690,000 in sales, or $460 a square foot, paying $36 all in: an 8 percent occupancy cost. A 4,000-square-foot gift store reports $520,000, or $130 a square foot, paying $30 all in: 23 percent. The buyer underwrites the first as a likely renewal at higher rent and the second as a vacancy.
