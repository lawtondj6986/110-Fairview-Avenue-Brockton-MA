## What it means

Underwriting is the disciplined analysis of a property's income, expenses, leases, physical condition, market position, and financing to decide whether to buy or lend, and at what price or loan amount. For a buyer it produces a stabilized NOI, a return on cost and on equity, and a maximum price. For a lender it produces a loan amount constrained by loan to value, debt service coverage, and debt yield. Good underwriting is a list of assumptions, each defended by a document or a comp.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore commercial deals are small enough that nobody else will underwrite them for you. The seller's broker supplies a one-page setup, the local bank orders an appraisal after you are under agreement, and the surprises come from the leases, the taxes, and the roof. ICS underwrites each South Shore acquisition for a client the way a lender will: tenant by tenant, with a reassessment at the purchase price, which in Brockton, Taunton, or Weymouth can add $20,000 or more to the tax line, and with the rollover schedule laid against market rent. The Buyer Brief packages that work for the client and the bank.

## In practice

A seller lists a 15,000 square foot mixed-use building in Quincy at $3.9 million with a pro forma NOI of $290,000, a 7.4 percent cap rate. Buyer underwriting: actual rents are $18,000 lower, taxes rise $24,000 after reassessment, a 4 percent management fee costs $14,500, and reserves add $3,000. Adjusted NOI is $230,500. At the same 7.4 percent cap, the supported price is about $3.1 million, and a 70 percent loan at 7 percent on a 25-year schedule covers debt service at 1.25 times, right at the bank's floor.
