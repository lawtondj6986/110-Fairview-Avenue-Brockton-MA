## What it means

A service level agreement is a documented promise about performance, stated in numbers and measured over a period. Between a vendor and a client it covers things like uptime, response time, and resolution time, with remedies if the standard is missed. Inside a business it sets the bar for how fast and how consistently the team handles leads, quotes, and customer issues.

## Why it matters for a local business

The internal SLA is the one that makes money. A Brockton roofing company that commits to a five-minute first response to every lead, and actually measures it, will outperform a competitor with better crews and a two-day callback habit. ICS builds response-time SLAs into Lead Engine with alerts when a lead crosses the line unanswered, and the JobSite hosting agreement states uptime, backup, and support response times in plain numbers so the client knows what was promised.

## In practice

A Quincy auto repair shop set three internal SLAs: respond to every new inquiry within 5 minutes, send every estimate within 4 business hours of inspection, and follow up on every open estimate at 48 hours. The Lead Engine dashboard tracked each one. In the first month the shop hit 61 percent on response time; by month three it was at 93 percent. Over that stretch, estimate approvals rose from 58 percent to 71 percent, worth about $14,000 a month in additional approved work.
