## What it means

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) is the federal law governing automated calls and text messages. For marketing texts it requires prior express written consent from the recipient, respects the national Do Not Call registry, restricts sending to roughly 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time (some states are stricter), and requires that opt-out requests such as STOP be honored promptly. Massachusetts and Florida add their own rules on top.

## Why it matters for a local business

Texting is the highest-response channel a local business has, and it is also the one with statutory damages per message. A Brockton auto shop that uploads its customer list and blasts a promotion without documented consent is a lawsuit waiting for a plaintiff. ICS builds consent capture into every Lead Engine form and booking flow, stores the consent record with each contact, enforces quiet hours by the recipient's time zone, and processes STOP automatically. Replies to a customer who just called are treated differently from marketing, but the line is narrow. None of this is legal advice; talk to counsel before launching any texting program.

## In practice

A Quincy dental practice wanted to text 2,400 patients about a whitening promotion. Only 1,350 had checked the marketing consent box at intake. ICS sent the promotion to those 1,350, with opt-out language, between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m., and kept the rest on an appointment-reminder-only flow. 62 patients booked. Twenty-one people replied STOP and were removed within seconds. The practice's attorney reviewed the consent language first, which took one short meeting.
