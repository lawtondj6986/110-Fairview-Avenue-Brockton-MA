## What it means

An expansion option is a lease provision giving the tenant the right, but not the obligation, to lease additional space, usually adjacent or in the same building, at a future point in the term. The clause specifies which space, when the option can be exercised, how much notice is required, what the rent and concessions will be, and whether the expansion space's term runs coterminous with the original lease. Landlords often pair it with a right of first offer on space the tenant has not reserved.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Growing South Shore businesses, a medical group in Plymouth or a contractor in Rockland, often lease the space they need today and then outgrow it in three years with no room to move. In a tight flex market, the adjacent bay is rarely available when you want it. An expansion option costs the tenant little to ask for and costs the landlord flexibility, so expect to trade for it: a longer initial term, a slightly higher rent, or a shorter exercise window. A fixed option with a hard date is worth far more than a ROFO.

## In practice

A software company leases 4,000 square feet on the second floor of a Quincy office building for seven years at $28 per square foot. The lease grants a fixed option on the adjacent 2,500 square foot suite, exercisable by written notice between months 24 and 36, at the then-escalated rent of the original space with a prorated improvement allowance. In month 30 the tenant exercises. It adds 2,500 square feet at $29.70 per square foot, with a $37,500 allowance, and avoids a relocation that would have cost an estimated $150,000 and six months of disruption.
