## What it means

A lot line adjustment relocates the common boundary between two neighboring parcels. One owner conveys a piece of land to the other, and no additional lot is created. It requires a survey plan, a deed, and usually a planning board endorsement before recording.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Small adjustments solve everyday commercial problems: a parking row that sits partly on the neighbor's land in Rockland, a loading area in Brockton that is five feet short for trucks, a building addition in Hanover that would break the side setback. Older parcels were often laid out before current zoning, so one fix can expose another shortfall. Check both lots against the dimensional table first.

## In practice

A contractor wants a 2,400 square foot addition but would end up 8 feet from the side line where zoning requires 20. The neighbor sells a 15 by 200 foot strip, 3,000 square feet, for $30,000. Survey, plan, and legal work run about $7,000. Both lots still meet minimum area and frontage, and the neighbor's lender signs a partial release.
