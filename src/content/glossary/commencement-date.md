## What it means

The commencement date is the day the lease term begins and the lease becomes fully effective. It sets the clock for the lease term, option deadlines, escalation dates, and renewal notices. Depending on the lease it may be a fixed calendar date, the date the landlord delivers possession, or the date the landlord substantially completes its work and obtains a certificate of occupancy. It is not always the day the tenant moves in or the day rent starts.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Delivery delays are common when a landlord is doing the build-out, and permit timing in South Shore towns varies more than tenants expect. A floating commencement date tied to delivery protects the tenant but makes every other date in the lease float with it, so both sides should sign a commencement date memorandum once the real date is known. ICS insists on this for clients in multi-tenant deals in Quincy and Braintree, where a renewal notice missed by a week because of a fuzzy start date can cost a tenant its option.

## In practice

A tenant signs a five-year lease for 6,000 square feet in Rockland on March 1 with a target delivery of June 1. The landlord's HVAC contractor runs late and the space is delivered July 10. Because the lease defines commencement as the delivery date, the term now runs July 10, 2026 to July 9, 2031, the 12-month renewal notice is due July 9, 2030, and the first rent escalation shifts to July 2027. Both parties sign a one-page memorandum confirming the dates.
