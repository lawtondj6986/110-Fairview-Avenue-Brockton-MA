## What it means

In a sale-leaseback an owner-occupant sells its property to an investor and simultaneously signs a long-term lease to remain. The seller converts illiquid equity into cash and a predictable rent expense; the buyer acquires a leased investment property with a tenant it already understands.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

For a South Shore business owner nearing retirement or funding a growth step, a sale-leaseback of a building bought twenty years ago can unlock significant equity. Local investors seeking stable income are a ready buyer pool, and the transaction is one we structure from both chairs.

## In practice

A Brockton manufacturer sells its 20,000 square foot building for $2,100,000 and signs a fifteen-year NNN lease at $150,000 a year. The owner redeploys the equity into equipment; the investor receives a 7.1 percent return from a tenant it knows.
