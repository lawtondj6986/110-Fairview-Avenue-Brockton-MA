## What it means

A civil docket is the chronological record a court keeps for a non-criminal case. Each entry has a date and a short description: complaint filed, answer filed, motion allowed, judgment entered. It identifies the parties, the attorneys, the case type, and the current status. It is a summary. The substance lives in the underlying filings.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A docket turns "they have been sued" into something useful. It shows who sued whom, over what, how long it took, and how it ended. A complaint is only an allegation. A dismissal, a default judgment, and an agreed settlement are three very different endings, and the docket is where you learn which one happened. We report the outcome, not just the caption.

## In practice

A search on a South Shore contractor returns 5 civil cases over 8 years in Plymouth County. Reading each docket: 2 were suits the contractor filed to collect unpaid invoices, 2 were dismissed by agreement within 6 months, and 1 ended in a $38,000 judgment against the company. The headline "5 lawsuits" would have misled the client. The dockets did not.
