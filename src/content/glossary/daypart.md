## What it means

A daypart is a defined segment of the operating day. Restaurants commonly track breakfast, lunch, afternoon, dinner, and late night. The term comes from broadcasting and is now standard in restaurant and retail analysis for breaking sales, traffic, and staffing into the hours when they actually occur.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Commuter patterns shape the dayparts here. Route 3 and Route 24 traffic heads north toward Boston in the morning and south in the evening, so the going-to-work side of a road such as Route 18 in Weymouth favors coffee and breakfast, and the going-home side favors takeout dinner and grocery. For a landlord, daypart mix across tenants determines whether the lot works and whether a parking variance or shared-parking study will hold up.

## In practice

A breakfast and lunch operator looks at a 2,200-square-foot end cap in Rockland. Hourly counts show 1,450 cars from 6 to 9 a.m. on the near side and 610 on the far side. The site is on the near side, with a curb cut. Projected sales are 58 percent breakfast and 34 percent lunch, so the tenant negotiates a 2:30 p.m. close and lower rent in exchange for no evening use.
