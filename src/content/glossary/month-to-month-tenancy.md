## What it means

A month-to-month tenancy is an occupancy arrangement that renews automatically each month with no fixed term, terminable by either landlord or tenant on proper written notice. In Massachusetts it is a form of tenancy at will. It arises when a lease expires and the parties agree to continue, when a short-term tenant never signs a lease, or when a lease itself converts to month-to-month after the term. Rent, use, and maintenance terms usually carry over from the expired lease unless changed in writing.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Month-to-month works for tenants waiting on a build-out elsewhere, seasonal businesses in Plymouth or Marshfield, and landlords in Brockton or Quincy who plan to redevelop or sell and do not want a long lease in the way. The risk runs both directions: a tenant can be told to leave with one month's notice and lose a location it has spent years building, and a landlord can lose a tenant the month before a sale closes. Buyers of multi-tenant buildings should treat month-to-month income as at risk when underwriting.

## In practice

A landscaping company's three-year lease on a 4,000 square foot Rockland yard and shop expires in March. It is buying its own property but the closing is uncertain. Landlord and tenant sign a one-page amendment converting to month-to-month at $5,200 a month, up from $4,800, with 60 days notice required from either side. The tenant closes on its building in July, gives notice August 1 effective September 30, and pays $31,200 for six extra months rather than signing a new lease it would have had to buy out.
