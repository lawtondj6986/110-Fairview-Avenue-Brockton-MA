## What it means

In-place rent is the contractual rent currently being paid under a property's existing leases. It is a fact, not an opinion: the rent roll shows it, the leases confirm it, and the bank statements prove it. It is distinct from market rent, which is what a new tenant would pay today, and from pro forma rent, which is what a seller hopes the next buyer will underwrite. Investors compare in-place rent to market to judge whether a property's income is at risk or has room to grow.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore commercial rents moved sharply between 2019 and 2024, particularly for flex and industrial space along Route 24 and Route 3. A building with five-year-old leases in Rockland or Avon may have in-place rents of $9 per square foot net while new deals are closing at $13. That gap is why sellers price on "market" and buyers should price on in-place, with a credit for the roll. The reverse happens in older office in Quincy and Brockton, where in-place rents signed before 2020 can sit above what the space would lease for today.

## In practice

A 24,000 square foot three-tenant flex building in Avon has in-place rents averaging $9.50 per square foot net, $228,000 a year, with leases expiring in 14, 26, and 40 months. Comparable new leases are signing at $13 net. The seller's offering memorandum prices the building on $312,000 of market rent at a 7 percent cap, $4.46 million. The buyer underwrites in-place income, a 7.5 percent cap on $228,000, about $3.04 million, then adds roughly $400,000 for the staged roll to market, and offers $3.45 million. The deal closes at $3.6 million.
