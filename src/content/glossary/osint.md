## What it means

Open-source intelligence is the discipline of collecting, verifying, and analyzing information from publicly available sources to answer a specific question. The craft is not in finding secrets; it is in knowing where public information lives, how to read it, and how to grade and connect it into findings someone can act on.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

ICS's intelligence practice applies OSINT to commercial questions on the South Shore and beyond: who really owns the building, what a counterparty's record looks like, what a competitor has filed, what a candidate's public record says. Every engagement is bounded by a published methods code and a written scope.

## In practice

A buyer asks who is behind an LLC selling a Brockton property. Registry of Deeds records, Secretary of the Commonwealth filings, and court dockets identify the principals, two prior entities, and a pending lawsuit that the seller's broker had not mentioned.
