## What it means

A records retention schedule lists categories of government records and assigns each a minimum retention period: one year, six years, permanent, or until superseded. In Massachusetts, the Municipal Records Retention Schedule governs cities and towns, and the Statewide Records Retention Schedule governs state agencies. An office may not lawfully destroy a record before its period runs, and municipalities need Records Conservation Board approval before any destruction.

## Why it matters in a dossier

The schedule tells you where the floor is. Building permits, deeds, board minutes, and licensing files are permanent in most categories, so a South Shore town should be able to produce the 1994 special permit for a Route 18 parcel. Routine correspondence may be gone after a few years, and that is lawful. ICS checks the schedule before writing a request so that the request asks for records that should exist and the response can be judged against the rule.

## In practice

A Hanover property's zoning history goes back to a 1987 variance. The town says the planning file cannot be found. The retention schedule lists zoning board decisions as permanent, so ICS asks the Registry of Deeds instead, where the decision was recorded in 1988 as required, and finds it in 20 minutes. The town file was mislaid; the record survived elsewhere.
