## What it means

Building class is a relative grading system that sorts commercial buildings, mainly office but also industrial and retail, into tiers. Class A is the newest or best-renovated space with high-quality systems, finishes, and location, commanding the top rents in a market. Class B is older or less amenitized but functional and well maintained, with rents below the top tier. Class C is older still, often with deferred maintenance, outdated systems, or weaker locations, serving tenants who prioritize cost. The letters are not standardized and shift by market.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore has very little true Class A by Boston standards. Most office in Quincy, Braintree, and Norwell is Class B: 1980s and 1990s buildings with updated lobbies and systems. Brockton and Taunton office and industrial stock skews B and C. That matters for pricing, because a listing that calls itself Class A at $32 per square foot may be a renovated B that should rent at $26. ICS uses Market sheets to benchmark asking rents by town and quality tier so clients compare buildings on what they are, not on the letter in the brochure.

## In practice

An investor compares two Braintree office buildings. The first, built in 2001 and renovated in 2019, is 40,000 square feet, 92 percent leased at an average $31 per square foot, asking $9.2 million at a 6.5 percent cap. The second, built in 1984 with original HVAC, is 38,000 square feet, 78 percent leased at $23 per square foot, asking $4.8 million at an 8.2 percent cap. The investor estimates $1.4 million to bring the second building to the first's standard and lease it to 90 percent at $28. The all-in cost of $6.2 million produces a stabilized yield near 9 percent, so the Class B deal wins.
