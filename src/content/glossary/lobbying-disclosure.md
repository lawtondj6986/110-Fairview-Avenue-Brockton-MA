## What it means

Lobbying disclosure is the public record of who is paid to influence government and by whom. In Massachusetts, lobbyists and their clients register with the Secretary of the Commonwealth's Lobbyist Division and file semiannual reports listing clients, compensation, the bills or agencies lobbied, and campaign contributions. Federal lobbying is disclosed under the Lobbying Disclosure Act through the House and Senate. Both are public and searchable online at no cost.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Development, licensing, and procurement on the South Shore often run through Beacon Hill. A Brockton cannabis applicant, a Plymouth energy project, a Taunton casino bid: each left a lobbying trail. The reports show who was hired, what they were paid, and which committees they worked. For ballot question and opposition research, lobbying records connect a public position to the money behind it without touching anyone's private information.

## In practice

A client preparing testimony on a Weymouth compressor-station bill asks who the other side has engaged. The Lobbyist Division shows 2 firms registered for the proponent, $148,000 in reported compensation over 18 months, the specific bill numbers, and $3,250 in lobbyist contributions to 4 legislators on the relevant committee. The chronology aligns the hires with each hearing date.
