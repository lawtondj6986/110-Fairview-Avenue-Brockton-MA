## What it means

CAM reconciliation is the year-end accounting for common area maintenance charges. During the year the tenant pays monthly estimates based on the landlord's budget. After year-end the landlord totals actual costs, applies each tenant's pro rata share, and compares it to what was collected. The tenant either receives a refund or credit, or owes the difference. Many leases apply the same process to real estate taxes and insurance.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Snow is the South Shore's CAM wildcard. A plaza on Route 139 in Marshfield might budget $25,000 for plowing and salt and spend $60,000 in a heavy winter, and that gap shows up in the spring reconciliation. Tenants in Plymouth and Hanover retail centers should read the statement line by line: management fees, capital items disguised as repairs, and shares that do not add up to 100 percent are common errors. ICS reviews these for tenant clients as part of a lease abstract.

## In practice

A 3,000 square foot tenant occupies 6 percent of a 50,000 square foot Marshfield plaza. It paid $4.50 per square foot in CAM estimates, $13,500 for the year. Actual CAM came in at $5.10 per square foot after a bad snow season, so the tenant's true share is $15,300. The reconciliation statement shows a $1,800 balance due, payable within 30 days. The tenant notices a new $4,000 roof repair in the pool, checks its lease, and finds roof repairs are excluded, trimming its bill by $240.
