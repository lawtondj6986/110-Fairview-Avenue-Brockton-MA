## What it means

Delivery condition is the lease provision describing the state of the premises when the landlord turns them over to the tenant: what systems are present and working, what finishes are complete, what the tenant must build itself, and what date the handoff occurs. It works alongside the work letter, which details the construction scope, and the commencement date, which sets when rent and the lease term begin. Vague delivery language is one of the most common sources of landlord-tenant disputes in the first year of a lease.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore landlords are often individual owners or small partnerships delivering second-generation space in Quincy, Weymouth, and Brockton. The former tenant's build-out may be 20 years old. "As-is" in those buildings can mean HVAC units past their life, undersized electrical, and a restroom that does not meet current accessibility code. A tenant that signs without defining delivery condition inherits those costs. ICS pushes for a specific list in every tenant engagement: demised, broom clean, systems in good working order, code-compliant restroom, and the landlord's warranty on HVAC for the first year.

## In practice

A physical therapy practice leases 3,200 square feet of former office space in Hanover at $24 per square foot. The initial draft says "as-is." ICS negotiates delivery as vanilla shell with new ceiling tile and LED lighting, HVAC certified by the landlord's contractor, and one ADA-compliant restroom. The landlord's cost is roughly $45,000; the tenant's build-out budget drops from $140,000 to $95,000. Rent commencement is tied to delivery plus 60 days, so a three-week landlord delay costs the tenant nothing.
