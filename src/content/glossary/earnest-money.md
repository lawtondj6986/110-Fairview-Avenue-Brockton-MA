## What it means

Earnest money is the deposit a buyer puts down to show it is serious. It is held in escrow, not paid to the seller directly, and is credited toward the purchase price at closing. The contract says when it is refundable and when it goes "hard," meaning at risk.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

In Massachusetts the deposit is commonly held by the listing brokerage or by one of the attorneys, as the contract provides. Sellers of scarce product, such as small industrial buildings in Rockland or Hanover, look hard at deposit size and how fast it goes non-refundable. A buyer can compete on those terms without raising the price, but only after sizing the risk.

## In practice

A buyer offers $1,200,000 for a building in Plymouth with $25,000 down at the letter of intent and another $35,000 at signing, $60,000 total, or 5 percent. The deposit is refundable for a 45-day due diligence period. On day 46 it goes hard. If the buyer walks on day 60 without a contractual out, the $60,000 is at stake.
