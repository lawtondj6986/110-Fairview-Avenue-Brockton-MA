## What it means

A punch list is the itemized record of work that remains incomplete, incorrect, or defective when a construction project reaches substantial completion. It is prepared during a walk-through by the owner, the architect, and the contractor, and it defines the remaining scope the contractor must finish before final payment and release of retainage. Items are typically minor: hardware, paint, caulking, cover plates, a door that sticks. Anything that prevents the space from being used for its intended purpose belongs in the definition of substantial completion itself, not on the punch list.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Tenant build-outs and landlord work on the South Shore are usually done by small general contractors who juggle several jobs. Once a job is "done" and the big check is paid, the crew moves to Plymouth or Quincy and the punch list sits for months. For ICS tenant clients, the lease and the construction contract should both tie money to the list: rent commencement to substantial completion, final payment to punch list completion, and a dollar-per-day incentive if it drags. A thorough walk-through at delivery is the cheapest leverage a tenant ever has.

## In practice

A contractor finishes a $180,000 build-out of 4,000 square feet of Brockton office space. At the walk-through the tenant's architect records 31 items: 12 paint and drywall touch-ups, 6 missing or loose cover plates, 4 doors out of adjustment, 3 ceiling tiles damaged, 2 light fixtures flickering, a leaking sink trap, and 3 data jacks unlabeled. Retainage held is $18,000, 10 percent of the contract. The contractor completes 29 items within 21 days; the final two take another three weeks, and the $18,000 releases on day 44.
