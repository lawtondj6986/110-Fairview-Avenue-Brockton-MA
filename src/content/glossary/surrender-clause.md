## What it means

A surrender clause sets out what the tenant must do when the lease ends: deliver the premises vacant and broom clean, remove its personal property and trade fixtures, repair damage caused by removal, and leave or remove alterations as the lease directs. It usually states that improvements become the landlord's property unless the landlord requires removal, defines "ordinary wear and tear," and sets the consequences for failing to comply, which include holdover rent and deductions from the security deposit. It also affects a good-guy guaranty, since a clean surrender is a condition of release.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Restoration costs catch South Shore tenants off guard, especially in flex and medical space. A landlord in Rockland may require a contractor to remove a mezzanine and patch the slab; a Hanover landlord may require a dental tenant to cap plumbing to six operatories and restore the floor. These can run $20,000 to $80,000 and land in the last month of the lease, when the tenant is paying for two spaces. Landlords should be specific too: a vague clause means a dispute over a security deposit instead of a clean turnover.

## In practice

A medical practice vacates 3,000 square feet in Braintree after a ten-year lease. The surrender clause says improvements remain unless the landlord elects removal in writing at least 90 days before expiration. The landlord elects removal of a lead-lined X-ray room and two sinks, a $14,000 job. The tenant also owes $3,200 to patch walls and remove low-voltage cabling. Because the tenant took dated photos at move-in showing stained carpet, the landlord drops a $9,000 carpet claim as wear and tear. The $25,000 security deposit is returned less $17,200.
