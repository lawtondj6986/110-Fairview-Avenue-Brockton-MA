## What it means

A reciprocal easement agreement is a recorded contract among the owners of two or more adjoining commercial parcels, most often in a shopping center that has been divided into separately owned lots. It grants each owner easements over the others for vehicle and pedestrian access, parking, utilities, drainage, and signage, and it sets rules for maintenance, cost sharing, insurance, building envelopes, use restrictions, and approval of changes. The REA runs with the land and binds all future owners and tenants of the parcels.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore shopping centers of any size have sold off pad sites to banks, fast food, pharmacies, and coffee chains: along Route 18 in Weymouth and Abington, Route 139 in Hanover and Marshfield, Route 44 in Raynham, and Route 3A in Kingston. Each of those pads sits inside an REA with the center. A buyer of the center inherits obligations to the pad owners; a buyer of a pad inherits a cost share and use limits. ICS pulls the REA from the Registry of Deeds in every retail acquisition and summarizes the practical constraints before the client signs a letter of intent.

## In practice

An investor buys a 1.1 acre pad site with a 3,500 square foot former bank branch in Abington for $1.6 million, planning a drive-through coffee tenant. The 1999 REA with the adjacent center grants parking and access easements but prohibits "restaurant uses with drive-through service" without the center owner's consent, and charges the pad 6 percent of the center's common area costs, about $14,000 a year. The investor negotiates consent for $40,000 and a modest increase in the cost share before closing. Without the review, the deal would have closed with a tenant it could not legally open.
