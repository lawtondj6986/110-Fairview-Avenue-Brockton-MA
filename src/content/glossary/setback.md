## What it means

A setback is the minimum distance a structure must be held from a property line, measured to the nearest wall or, in some bylaws, to the nearest projection such as a canopy. Zoning bylaws set front, side, and rear setbacks for each district, with extra depth where commercial land abuts residential, and sometimes separate setbacks for parking and signs. Along with lot coverage, height, and floor area ratio, setbacks define the buildable envelope on a lot.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Older commercial lots on Route 18, Route 28, and Route 139 were built close to the street under earlier rules, and many are shallow. A current 40-foot front setback and a 30-foot residential buffer at the rear can leave a 150-foot-deep lot with a 60-foot building zone. Loading docks, dumpster pads, and drive-through lanes all have to fit inside it. Towns like Hanover and Pembroke also require landscaped buffers inside the setback, which can take parking with it. Walk the site with the dimensional table in hand.

## In practice

A Whitman buyer plans a 7,500 square foot building on a 150-foot by 200-foot lot zoned for 30-foot front, 15-foot side, and 20-foot rear setbacks, with a 50-foot buffer where the rear line meets a residential zone. The envelope is 120 feet by 120 feet, or 14,400 square feet. The building fits, but 25 parking spaces and a 60-foot truck turning radius do not, and the plan shrinks to 6,000 square feet.
