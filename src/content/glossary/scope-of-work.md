## What it means

A scope of work is the document that defines a research engagement before it starts. It names the subject, frames the specific questions, lists the sources and methods, and sets the deliverable, timeline, and price. It also records the client's stated purpose, which determines what work is appropriate.

## Why it matters in a dossier

"Find out everything" is not a scope. It produces a long, expensive report that may miss the one question behind the request. A tight scope ties every search to a decision the client has to make. It also protects both sides: the methods are agreed in writing, and the limits on use are clear from day one.

## In practice

A client considering a $3 million acquisition of a Plymouth marina asks for a dossier on the seller. The scope sets 4 questions: ownership, litigation, liens, and regulatory history. It covers 2 principals and 3 entities, in Massachusetts and Florida, over 15 years, delivered in 10 business days for a fixed fee. Purpose: transaction due diligence, shared with the client's attorney only. Excluded in writing: employee backgrounds, personal finances of the principals, and any contact with the seller's staff. Anything new that comes up gets a written change, not a quiet expansion.
