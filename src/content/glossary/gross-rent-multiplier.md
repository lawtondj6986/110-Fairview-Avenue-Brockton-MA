## What it means

The gross rent multiplier is the purchase price divided by the gross scheduled annual rent. Because it ignores vacancy and operating expenses, it is a rough first pass, useful for sorting a list of listings before doing real underwriting on the few that survive.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

GRM is most useful on South Shore mixed-use buildings, where storefront and apartment rents are easy to total and expenses are harder to pin down. If two Whitman buildings show GRMs of 9 and 13, the 13 needs a reason before it gets a visit.

## In practice

A $1,100,000 building with $110,000 of gross rent has a GRM of 10. If comparable buildings trade at 9, the asking price implies either superior condition or room to negotiate.
