## What it means

A leasehold interest is the bundle of rights a tenant holds under a lease: exclusive possession and use of the premises for the term, subject to the rent and conditions in the lease. It is distinct from the landlord's fee interest, which is ownership of the land and building. When the contract rent is below market rent for the remaining term, the leasehold has positive value, which can be transferred through assignment or sublease. On a ground lease, the leasehold includes the tenant's ownership of the building it constructed, for as long as the lease runs.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Below-market leaseholds are common on the South Shore because many small landlords have not pushed rents in years. A restaurant in Quincy paying $22 per square foot in a market that now asks $35 holds real value, and that value is often the largest asset in a business sale. Ground leases on pad sites along Route 18 in Weymouth and Route 44 in Plymouth give national tenants control of a site for 20 to 50 years without buying it. ICS values the leasehold separately whenever a client is buying or selling a business that occupies leased space.

## In practice

A pizza shop owner in Weymouth is selling the business. The lease has nine years left on 2,200 square feet at $18 per square foot, $39,600 a year, with two five-year options at fixed 10 percent bumps. Market rent for the same space is $30 per square foot. The rent savings are about $26,400 a year, worth roughly $150,000 in present value over the nine years at an 8 percent discount rate. The business sells for $240,000; the broker and the buyer's CPA agree that more than half of that is the leasehold.
