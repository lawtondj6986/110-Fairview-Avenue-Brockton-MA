## What it means

A municipal lien certificate, or MLC, is a document issued by the town or city collector under Chapter 60, Section 23 that lists every charge constituting a lien on a parcel: real estate taxes, water and sewer, betterment assessments, and other municipal charges, with the amounts paid and outstanding. The buyer's attorney orders it before closing, the charges are prorated or paid off at the table, and the MLC is recorded at the registry of deeds with the deed. Recording cuts off the town's ability to collect unlisted charges from the new owner.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Every commercial closing in Brockton, Quincy, Rockland, or Plymouth runs through the MLC, but it does more than confirm taxes are current. It is where sewer betterments, water liens, and unpaid trash or fire-alarm fees surface. In towns with split tax rates, the MLC also shows the current commercial assessment and bill, which is a reality check on the seller's expense statement. We tell sellers to pull the MLC early so the net sheet is right before the purchase and sale agreement is signed.

## In practice

A Rockland owner is selling a 10,000 square foot industrial building for $1.6 million. The MLC, ordered three weeks before closing, shows the current quarter's taxes paid, an $1,800 unpaid water bill, and a $22,000 unpaid sewer betterment balance from a 2019 line extension. The buyer's lender requires the betterment paid off at closing. Seller net proceeds fall by $23,800, a number that should have been on the net sheet when the listing went live.
