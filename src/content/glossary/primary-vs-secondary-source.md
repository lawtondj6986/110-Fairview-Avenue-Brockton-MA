## What it means

A primary source is the original record created at the time of an event by a party to it: a deed, a filed complaint, a permit, a campaign finance report. A secondary source describes, summarizes, or interprets primary material: a news article, a commercial database summary, a book. Primary sources carry fewer layers of interpretation and error, so they are preferred for any fact a dossier will stand behind.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Most of what circulates about a South Shore business or official is secondary and often wrong in the details: the wrong year, the wrong entity, a settlement described as a judgment. ICS treats secondary material as a map to primary records and cites only the primary record. When a primary record cannot be obtained, the finding is downgraded in confidence and labeled as resting on secondary sources. The client sees which is which.

## In practice

A background article says a Quincy developer "lost a $3 million lawsuit" in 2021. The Norfolk Superior Court docket shows a case that settled for an undisclosed amount with no judgment entered, and a separate 2019 case in which the developer was the plaintiff and won. The dossier reports both from the dockets. The article was a useful lead and a poor source.
