## What it means

A certificate of occupancy, or CO, is issued by the municipal building department after final inspections confirm that a building or tenant space meets the building code, fire code, and zoning for its intended use. A new CO is generally required after new construction, a change of use, or a significant build-out. Without it, the space cannot legally be occupied, and most leases make it a condition of the commencement date.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Each South Shore town runs its own building department, and inspection scheduling varies: a final inspection can take a few days in one town and two or more weeks in another during a busy season. Change of use is the trap. Converting a former retail unit in Brockton to a medical office or a flex bay in Rockland to a gym may trigger a new CO, which may in turn trigger site plan review, parking requirements, or accessibility upgrades. Check with the building department before signing, not after.

## In practice

A physical therapy group leases 3,200 square feet of former retail space in Weymouth. The build-out finishes on schedule, but the fire department's final inspection flags two missing exit signs and an alarm panel that needs reprogramming. The fixes cost $1,400 and take nine days, and the CO is issued 11 days later than planned. Because the lease tied rent commencement to CO delivery, the tenant saves about $3,500 in rent it would otherwise have paid for a space it could not use.
