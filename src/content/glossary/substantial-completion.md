## What it means

Substantial completion is the stage of a construction project at which the work is sufficiently complete, in accordance with the contract documents, that the owner or tenant can occupy and use the space for its intended purpose, even though minor items remain on the punch list. It is a defined milestone in construction contracts and leases: it starts the warranty period, releases a portion of retainage, shifts responsibility for utilities and insurance, and often triggers the lease commencement date and rent. It is distinct from final completion, when every item is done and final payment is made.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

For South Shore tenants taking space with landlord build-out in Quincy, Weymouth, Hanover, or Brockton, the definition of substantial completion is the difference between paying rent on a finished space and paying rent on a construction site. Small landlords sometimes treat "the drywall is up" as done. The lease should require a certificate of occupancy or temporary certificate from the town building department, an architect's certification, and working HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and life safety systems. ICS writes those three tests into every tenant lease it negotiates.

## In practice

A landlord agrees to deliver 5,000 square feet of Weymouth medical office with substantial completion targeted for September 1, rent commencement 45 days later. On September 1 the contractor says the space is done, but the town has not issued a certificate of occupancy because the fire alarm has not been tested and two exam room sinks are not plumbed. Under the lease, substantial completion requires the certificate. The CO issues September 19, pushing rent commencement to November 3. At $30 per square foot, the 18-day delay saves the tenant about $7,400 and keeps the fixturing window intact.
