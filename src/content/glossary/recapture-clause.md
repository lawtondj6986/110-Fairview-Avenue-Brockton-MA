## What it means

A recapture clause gives the landlord the option to terminate the lease, in whole or for the affected portion, when a specified event occurs. The most common triggers are a tenant's request to sublease or assign, and a tenant ceasing to operate in retail space. Rather than consenting to a sublease and watching the tenant pocket the spread between its rent and the subtenant's, the landlord takes the space back and re-leases it directly. For the tenant, recapture is a release from future rent, but also the loss of any value the lease had.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Recapture is a real consideration in markets where rents have risen, which describes South Shore flex and industrial since 2020. A tenant paying $9 per square foot in Rockland on a lease signed in 2019 cannot assume it can sublease at $13 and keep the difference if the landlord holds a recapture right; the landlord will take the space and lease it at $13 itself. On the retail side, recapture is a landlord's answer to a dark store in a Route 3 plaza in Kingston, where the rent keeps coming but the center suffers.

## In practice

A distributor with 15,000 square feet in Avon at $8.75 per square foot net and four years remaining consolidates into another facility. It finds a subtenant at $12.50 and requests consent, expecting about $56,000 a year in spread. The landlord exercises recapture within the 30-day window, terminates the lease, and signs a new five-year lease with the same user at $12.50. The distributor saves $525,000 in remaining rent obligations and avoids four years of subtenant risk, but gives up $225,000 of potential profit. The clause was in the lease it signed in 2021; nobody had read it since.
