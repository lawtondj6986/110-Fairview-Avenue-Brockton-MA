## What it means

The cost approach estimates value as the market value of the land as if vacant, plus the current cost to construct the improvements new, minus accrued depreciation from physical wear, functional obsolescence, and external factors. It is one of the three standard appraisal approaches, alongside the income approach and the sales comparison approach. The logic is that a buyer will not pay more for an existing building than it would cost to buy land and build an equivalent one.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

On the South Shore the cost approach is most useful for new owner-user buildings, churches, schools, car washes, and self-storage, where sales comps are thin and the income is tied to the operator rather than the real estate. It also sets a ceiling: when construction costs in Plymouth County run $250 to $350 per square foot for new industrial and flex, an existing 1990s warehouse in Rockland selling for $140 per square foot looks cheap, which is why the market for second-generation industrial has been strong.

## In practice

An appraiser values a six-year-old 15,000 square foot medical office building in Hanover. Land: 2 acres at $650,000 per acre, $1.3 million. Replacement cost new at $320 per square foot, $4.8 million, plus $300,000 site work. Depreciation at 10 percent for age and minor functional items, $510,000. Indicated value: $5.89 million, within 4 percent of the income approach at $6.1 million, which gives the lender confidence in both.
