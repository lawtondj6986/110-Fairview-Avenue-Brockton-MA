## What it means

A non-recourse loan limits the lender's recovery to the collateral. If the property is worth less than the debt at foreclosure, the lender absorbs the shortfall and cannot collect it from the borrower personally. Lenders price that risk with lower leverage, tighter coverage tests, and carve-outs for bad acts.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The default loan for a building in Brockton or Hanover is a community bank or credit union mortgage with a full personal guaranty. Non-recourse becomes available as properties get larger and more stable: apartment buildings in Quincy, a leased retail center on Route 3. An investor with several properties cares because recourse on each one stacks up against the same personal balance sheet.

## In practice

Two lenders quote a $4,000,000 apartment building in Weymouth. A bank offers 75 percent, $3,000,000, with full recourse. An agency lender offers 65 percent, $2,600,000, non-recourse with standard carve-outs. The investor puts in $400,000 more equity to keep the loan off the personal guaranty ledger. If values fall 30 percent, the loss stops at the equity in that building.
