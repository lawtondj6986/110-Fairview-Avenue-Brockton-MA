## What it means

Stabilized value is the appraised or modeled value of a property assuming it has reached normal occupancy at market rents and expenses, with lease-up complete and concessions burned off. It is calculated by capitalizing the stabilized NOI at a market cap rate. Appraisals on development or repositioning projects usually report three numbers: as-is value, value at completion, and prospective stabilized value, each with a date. The gap between as-is and stabilized value is what the developer or investor is trying to capture.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Vacant and half-empty commercial buildings are where South Shore investors make money, and stabilized value is the number that justifies the purchase. A half-vacant Brockton retail strip or a dark Rockland industrial building is priced on its current income; the buyer is paying for what it will earn once filled. The risk is the time and cost to get there. Lenders in Plymouth and Norfolk counties will lend on stabilized value, but they want to see a lease-up plan and usually hold back funds until the tenants are paying.

## In practice

An investor buys a 60 percent occupied, 18,000 square foot Brockton retail strip for $1.5 million, where current NOI of $105,000 supports a 7 percent cap. The plan is to lease the remaining 7,200 square feet at $16 per square foot NNN over 12 months and spend $150,000 on facade and tenant improvements. Stabilized NOI reaches roughly $205,000 after a 5 percent vacancy allowance. At a 7.5 percent cap, stabilized value is about $2.73 million against $1.65 million all-in.
