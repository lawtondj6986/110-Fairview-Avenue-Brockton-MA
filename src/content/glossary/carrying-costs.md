## What it means

Carrying costs are the recurring expenses of owning a property during a period when it generates little or no income: debt service, property taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance, and professional fees. They matter most on vacant buildings, land awaiting permits, and value-add projects between purchase and stabilization. Carrying costs are the clock on a deal; the longer the timeline, the more the project has to earn to break even.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Permitting on the South Shore is not fast. A project needing Planning Board site plan review, a ZBA special permit, and a Conservation Commission order of conditions can run nine to eighteen months in Hanover, Marshfield, or Plymouth, and a buyer who closes before entitlements pays carry the entire time. Vacant industrial buildings in Brockton and Taunton are taxed at the commercial rate whether or not they earn, and Brockton's commercial rate is among the higher ones in the region. Plan the carry as a line item, not an afterthought.

## In practice

An investor buys a vacant 20,000 square foot building in Taunton for $1.8 million with $1.2 million of bank debt at 7.25 percent interest-only. Monthly carry: $7,250 interest, $3,900 taxes, $1,400 insurance, $1,100 heat and electric, $600 snow and upkeep, about $14,250 a month. Permitting and build-out take fourteen months before the first tenant pays rent, so carry totals roughly $200,000, which has to be added to the basis before the deal is judged.
