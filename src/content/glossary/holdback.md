## What it means

A holdback is a sum of money retained by one party, or held by a neutral escrow agent, pending the satisfaction of a stated condition. In a sale, the buyer's closing attorney holds part of the seller's proceeds until the seller completes an obligation that could not be finished before closing. In a construction contract, the owner retains a percentage of each payment, often 5 to 10 percent, until the punch list is complete. In a loan, the lender withholds proceeds until leasing or construction milestones are met. The holdback agreement spells out the condition, the deadline, and what happens if the condition fails.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore deals often close with loose ends: a Title V septic repair in Plymouth that cannot be finished before winter, a roof patch in Brockton waiting on a contractor, an estoppel certificate a tenant has not returned. Delaying closing can blow a buyer's rate lock or a seller's 1031 exchange timeline. A holdback lets the deal close while protecting the party that is owed performance. ICS structures the holdback amount at 125 to 150 percent of the estimated cost so the buyer is not short if bids come in high.

## In practice

A buyer is set to close on a $1.35 million Marshfield retail building on March 15. The seller agreed to replace two rooftop HVAC units, bid at $26,000, but the units are backordered until April. The parties close on schedule with $39,000 held in escrow by the closing attorney. The seller's contractor installs the units April 20, the buyer's inspector confirms, and the escrow releases the $26,000 invoice amount to the contractor and $13,000 back to the seller. If the seller had not performed by June 1, the buyer could have hired its own contractor and drawn on the escrow.
