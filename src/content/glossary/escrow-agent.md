## What it means

An escrow agent is a third party that holds money or documents for the parties to a transaction and releases them only when stated conditions are met. In a purchase, that usually means the buyer's deposit. After closing, it can mean funds held back for repairs, tax items, or environmental work.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most local deals name the listing broker or an attorney as escrow agent, and Massachusetts brokers have record-keeping and escrow account obligations for money they hold. The details still deserve a look: whose account, whether it earns interest and for whom, and what the agent does in a dispute. Those answers belong in the escrow paragraph of the purchase and sale agreement.

## In practice

A buyer deposits $75,000 with a title company on a $1,500,000 purchase in Brockton. At closing the seller has not finished a $20,000 roof repair. The parties sign a holdback agreement: the agent keeps $30,000 of sale proceeds, 150 percent of the estimate, and releases it when the buyer's inspector signs off within 60 days.
