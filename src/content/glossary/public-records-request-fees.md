## What it means

The Massachusetts Public Records Law allows a records access officer to charge a requester for the reasonable cost of producing records, within limits set by regulation at 950 CMR 32. The agency must respond within 10 business days, and if it intends to charge, it must provide a good-faith written fee estimate within that window. Fees cover copying, search, and redaction time, not the cost of reviewing records for the agency's own purposes.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Fee estimates are a tool some offices use to slow a request. A $1,400 estimate from a town for "all emails about the Route 139 project" is a signal to narrow, not to pay. ICS writes requests that name the record type, custodian, and date range, which keeps most municipal requests inside the two free hours. Brockton, at over 100,000 residents, falls under the two-free-hour rule; a town of 12,000 may charge from the first hour.

## In practice

A buyer wants every building permit and inspection report for a Quincy property since 2010. A request for "all records about the property" draws a $300 estimate. A revised request for permit applications, final inspection cards, and violation notices for one parcel number is produced electronically in eight business days at no charge. Same records, better question.
