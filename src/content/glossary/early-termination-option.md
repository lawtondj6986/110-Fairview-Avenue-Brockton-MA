## What it means

An early termination option is a lease clause allowing one party, almost always the tenant, to end the lease before the natural expiration date by giving advance written notice and paying a defined fee. It differs from a kick-out clause, which is usually tied to a performance test such as sales volume, and from a lease buyout, which is a one-off negotiation when no option exists. The option is only as good as its terms: the notice window, the exercise date, the fee formula, and what happens to the tenant's obligations after exercise.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Small businesses on the South Shore are often signing their first or second commercial lease and cannot predict where they will be in seven years. A dental practice in Weymouth or a contractor in Rockland may outgrow the space or close a second location. A termination right at year five of a ten-year lease gives the landlord a longer term on paper and gives the tenant a real exit if growth or the economy go the wrong way. ICS negotiates for it on behalf of tenant clients whenever the landlord asks for more than five years.

## In practice

A marketing firm leases 4,500 square feet of Quincy office at $28 per square foot, $126,000 a year, for ten years with a termination option effective at the end of year six. The landlord provided a $35 per square foot improvement allowance ($157,500) and paid a $37,800 commission. The termination fee is the unamortized balance of those costs at year six, about $78,000, plus four months rent of $42,000. The firm gives notice at month 63, pays about $120,000, and avoids four years of rent at roughly $540,000.
