## What it means

An assemblage is the purchase of two or more adjacent parcels, usually from different owners, to combine them into one development site. The combined parcel can support a larger building, better access, or a use that the individual lots could not. The value created by the combination, above the sum of the separate lot values, is called plottage. Assemblages are slow, quiet, and often done through options rather than outright purchases.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Much of the commercial frontage on Route 18 in Abington and Weymouth, Route 139 in Marshfield, and Main Street in Brockton was platted as small residential lots a century ago. A single 60-foot-wide lot cannot hold a modern retail or medical building with code parking, so the only way to build is to assemble three or four. The Planning Board will also treat the combined site as one lot for setbacks and lot coverage, which is where the extra value comes from. ICS runs ownership through the Registry of Deeds and the assessor database first, because a parcel held in an estate or a trust changes the approach.

## In practice

A buyer in Brockton options three adjacent lots on a Route 28 corridor at $350,000, $400,000, and $425,000, paying $10,000 per option for twelve months. Separately the lots are too small for anything but a house each. Combined, the 48,000 square foot site supports a 12,000 square foot medical building, and an appraisal values the assembled land at $1.6 million, about $425,000 above the $1.175 million purchase price. Each purchase agreement closes only if all three do.
