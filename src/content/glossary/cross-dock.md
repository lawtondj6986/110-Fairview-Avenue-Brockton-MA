## What it means

A cross-dock is a warehouse laid out with loading doors on two opposing sides. Inbound freight crosses the floor and goes directly onto outbound vehicles with little or no time in storage. The term also describes the operation itself, which can run inside any building with enough doors.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Deliveries bound for Cape Cod, the South Coast, and the southern Boston suburbs funnel down Route 24, Route 3, and Interstate 495. Carriers want transfer points near those interchanges in places like Brockton, Taunton, and Middleborough. True cross-dock buildings are scarce here, and new ones need large flat sites with room for trailers, which runs into wetlands, traffic review, and neighbors.

## In practice

A regional carrier compares two buildings in Taunton. One is 60,000 square feet, 300 feet deep, with 8 doors on one wall. The other is 40,000 square feet, 80 feet deep, with 40 doors split across two sides. The carrier takes the smaller building at a higher rent per square foot because it turns 40 trailers at once.
