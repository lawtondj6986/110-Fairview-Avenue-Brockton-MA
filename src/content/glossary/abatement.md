## What it means

Rent abatement is a period during which the tenant pays no rent or reduced rent. It comes in two forms. As a concession, it is the landlord's way of lowering the effective rent without lowering the face rate: two or three free months at the start of a five-year term. As a remedy, it is the tenant's protection when the landlord does not perform: the space is delivered late, the HVAC fails for weeks, or a fire takes half the premises out of service.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore landlords with a long-vacant unit in Brockton or Weymouth will often give abatement before they will cut the face rent, because the face rate sets the comps for the rest of the building. For tenants, the remedy version matters just as much. Older buildings along Route 18 and Route 139 have older roofs and rooftop units, and a lease with no abatement clause leaves you paying full rent while a space is unusable.

## In practice

A 4,000 square foot office tenant in Hanover signs a five-year lease at $22 per square foot gross, $88,000 a year. The landlord grants three months of abated base rent, worth $22,000, in exchange for the tenant accepting the space as-is. The tenant still pays its share of operating expenses during those months, about $1,800 a month, so the "free" period is not quite free. Spread over 60 months, the concession lowers the effective rent to about $20.90 per square foot.
