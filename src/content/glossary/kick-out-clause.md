## What it means

A kick-out clause is a lease provision that permits one party, most often a retail tenant, to terminate the lease before expiration if a specified condition occurs, typically gross sales falling below a stated dollar amount during a test period. The clause defines the test year, the sales threshold, how sales are reported and verified, the notice window to exercise, and any termination payment. It protects a tenant from being locked into a location that does not work while giving the landlord a measurable, limited exit rather than an open-ended one.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Retail on the South Shore is spread across strip centers, downtown storefronts, and highway pads in towns with very different traffic: Route 18 in Weymouth, Route 139 in Hanover, Main Street in Plymouth, Belmont Street in Brockton. A regional restaurant or fitness operator opening its first South Shore location cannot be sure a site will perform. A kick-out at the end of year three if sales are below a threshold lets them sign a ten-year lease they otherwise would not. ICS negotiates kick-outs for expanding tenant clients and, for landlord clients, insists the threshold be low enough that only a real failure triggers it.

## In practice

A fast-casual restaurant leases 2,600 square feet in a Hanover center at $34 per square foot, $88,400 a year, for ten years. The kick-out allows termination if gross sales in lease year three are below $1.1 million, with 180 days notice and a termination payment equal to the unamortized $90,000 improvement allowance, about $63,000 at that point. Year three sales come in at $1.04 million. The tenant gives notice in month 38, pays the $63,000, and closes in month 44, avoiding roughly $530,000 of remaining rent. The landlord re-leases the space in five months.
