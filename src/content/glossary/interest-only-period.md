## What it means

An interest-only period is a defined span, commonly one to five years at the start of a commercial loan, during which the borrower pays only the interest due and makes no principal payments. The balance stays flat. After the period ends the loan either begins amortizing, usually on a 20- to 30-year schedule, or comes due in a balloon. Interest-only is a feature of construction and bridge loans by design and is negotiated as a concession on permanent loans for stabilized property.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Local banks and credit unions that fund most South Shore commercial deals, under $5 million and often owner-occupied, offer interest-only mainly during construction or a lease-up period, then convert to amortizing. A contractor building a shop in Hanover or a medical group fitting out space in Weymouth will typically see 12 to 18 months of interest-only while the work is done. For an investor buying a vacant Brockton building to lease up, a year of interest-only can be the difference between carrying the asset and losing it.

## In practice

An investor borrows $1.5 million at 7 percent on a half-vacant Brockton retail building with 12 months of interest-only. Monthly payments are about $8,750 during lease-up. When amortization begins on a 25-year schedule, the payment rises to roughly $10,600, a $1,850 monthly jump. The new tenant signed in month nine at $14,000 a month of rent, so coverage holds. Had lease-up taken 18 months, the investor would have needed about $33,000 of additional reserves to bridge the gap.
