## What it means

A rent step is a predetermined increase in base rent that takes effect on a specific date, usually each lease anniversary. The lease states it as a percentage, a dollar amount per square foot, or a full rent schedule. It is one form of rent escalation, and the most predictable one.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

A half-dollar difference in annual steps looks small in a letter of intent and is not small over ten years. Landlords of industrial space in Brockton and Rockland have pushed for steeper steps when vacancy is tight. Tenants should model the full schedule, including option periods, because the rent in year 10 sets the starting point for the renewal conversation.

## In practice

A tenant leases 5,000 square feet at $15 a square foot with 3 percent annual steps. Year 1 rent is $75,000. Year 5 is $16.88 a foot, or $84,413. Over five years the tenant pays about $398,200, compared with $375,000 flat. The steps add roughly $23,200, about 6 percent of the total. At 2 percent steps the five-year total would be about $390,300.
