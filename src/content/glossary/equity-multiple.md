## What it means

The equity multiple is the sum of all cash distributions an investor receives over the life of an investment, including operating cash flow and net sale proceeds, divided by the total equity the investor contributed. A deal that takes in $500,000 and returns $950,000 has a 1.9x multiple. It is simple, hard to manipulate, and widely used in offering materials and partnership reporting, but because it ignores the timing of cash flows it is always read together with IRR.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore investment deals are small partnerships: a few partners buying a Brockton mixed-use building or a Plymouth retail strip, often with the sponsor putting in 10 to 25 percent of the equity. Partners who are not real estate professionals understand "I put in $100,000 and got back $190,000" far better than a discounted cash flow. ICS reports both metrics in Deal tools projections and in the pro forma for every investment property engagement, and warns clients when a high IRR is driven by a quick exit assumption rather than durable income.

## In practice

Three partners buy a 10,000 square foot Plymouth retail building for $1.8 million with $630,000 of equity including closing costs. Over six years the property distributes $31,000, $34,000, $38,000, $41,000, $44,000, and $47,000 in cash flow, a total of $235,000. It sells in year six for $2.25 million, netting $1.05 million after loan payoff and costs. Total returned: $1,285,000. Equity multiple: about 2.04x. The IRR works out to roughly 14 percent.
