## What it means

A stacking plan is a visual summary of a multi-tenant building, drawn floor by floor, with each tenant's suite shown as a block sized roughly to its square footage. Each block is labeled with the tenant name, rentable area, rent, and lease expiration, and blocks are color-coded by expiration year or by status: occupied, vacant, under negotiation. It turns the rent roll into a map. For a single-story plaza the same idea is drawn as a site plan with units instead of floors.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore multi-tenant buildings are two to four stories or single-story plazas with six to fifteen units, small enough that an owner can carry the whole picture in their head and often does, which is why the stacking plan never gets drawn. That is a mistake when the building is being sold, refinanced, or re-leased. A buyer looking at a Quincy office building wants to see that 40 percent of the rentable area rolls in 2027 before the lender does. ICS builds one for every multi-tenant property in a Buyer Brief and for landlord clients planning renewals.

## In practice

A three-story, 36,000 square foot office building in Braintree has 14 tenants. The stacking plan shows the second floor at 85 percent leased with three tenants, 9,800 square feet in total, all expiring between June and November 2027, two of them adjacent. The owner approaches the largest of the three, a 4,200 square foot accounting firm that has mentioned growth, 18 months early and offers a combined 7,600 square foot suite at $28 per square foot on a new seven-year term. The firm signs, the owner avoids three simultaneous vacancies, and the building's weighted average lease term rises from 2.4 years to 3.9.
