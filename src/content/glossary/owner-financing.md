## What it means

Owner financing, or seller financing, is a sale in which the seller lends part or all of the purchase price to the buyer, secured by a mortgage on the property. The buyer signs a promissory note with an interest rate, payment schedule, and maturity, often three to seven years with a balloon. The seller's mortgage is recorded at the registry of deeds, in first position if there is no bank, or in second position behind a bank loan. On default the seller forecloses like any lender.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Many South Shore commercial properties are owned by people in their sixties and seventies who built a business in the building and have no debt on it. A seller note lets them sell to a younger operator who has the cash flow but not the down payment, keep a steady income, and defer some tax. For a buyer, a seller note at a reasonable rate can replace expensive gap capital or get a deal past a bank that will only go to 70 percent. The note terms belong in the purchase and sale agreement, not a handshake.

## In practice

A Whitman owner sells a 7,000 square foot auto repair building for $1.1 million to a mechanic who has run a shop there as a tenant. A local bank lends $770,000 at 70 percent. The seller takes a $220,000 second mortgage at 7 percent, interest-only for five years with a balloon, and the buyer puts in $110,000. The seller earns about $15,400 a year in interest and reports the gain over time, and the buyer owns the building with 10 percent down.
