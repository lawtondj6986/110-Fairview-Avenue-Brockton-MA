## What it means

Defeasance is a way out of a fixed-rate loan that does not allow ordinary prepayment, most often a CMBS loan. The borrower replaces the property as collateral with securities, usually Treasuries, whose cash flows match the remaining loan payments. The lender's income continues unchanged and the property is freed to be sold or refinanced.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most local owners never see it, because community bank loans use step-down prepayment penalties. It appears on larger securitized loans: a grocery-anchored center in Plymouth, a hotel near Route 3, an apartment portfolio in Quincy. A buyer or seller of that kind of asset has to price defeasance before signing a letter of intent, and allow about 30 days or more to complete it.

## In practice

An owner wants to sell a Weymouth retail center with $8,000,000 remaining on a 5.0 percent CMBS loan and four years to maturity. Treasuries yield about 4.0 percent. The replacement securities cost roughly $8,280,000, so defeasance adds about $280,000 plus roughly $60,000 in fees to the cost of selling.
