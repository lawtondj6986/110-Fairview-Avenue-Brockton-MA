## What it means

The income approach values a property on the income it is expected to produce for an investor. In its simplest form, direct capitalization, stabilized net operating income is divided by a market-derived capitalization rate. In the discounted cash flow form, projected cash flows over a holding period plus the reversion at sale are discounted at a required rate of return. It is the primary approach for leased investment property and the one lenders weight most heavily.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Nearly every multi-tenant retail, office, and industrial sale on the South Shore is priced on the income approach, and the arguments are about the inputs. Buyers of Brockton and Taunton properties will insist on higher vacancy and management allowances than the seller shows; sellers of Hingham and Hanover properties will point to lower cap rates on Route 3 comps. ICS underwrites South Shore deals with a stabilized NOI that includes a management fee and reserves even when the owner manages the building, because the appraiser will.

## In practice

A 10,000 square foot three-tenant retail building in Weymouth collects $240,000 in gross rent and $60,000 in expense reimbursements. Stabilized: less 5 percent vacancy and credit loss, $15,000; less operating expenses, $62,000; less a 4 percent management fee, $11,400; less reserves at $0.20 per square foot, $2,000. NOI is $209,600. At a 7.25 percent cap rate the indicated value is about $2.89 million, roughly $289 per square foot.
