## What it means

The Zoning Board of Appeals is the local board created under Chapter 40A, Section 12, with three core jobs: hearing appeals from decisions of the building inspector or zoning enforcement officer, granting variances from the dimensional and use rules of the bylaw, and issuing the special permits the bylaw assigns to it. In most towns it also acts as the permit granting authority for Chapter 40B comprehensive permits. Decisions are filed with the town clerk, and a 20-day appeal period runs before they take effect.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Older South Shore commercial corridors, Route 18 in Weymouth and Abington, Route 139 in Marshfield, Main Street in Brockton, are full of lots laid out before current setbacks and parking rules. Reusing one often means a dimensional variance for frontage, setback, or parking, or a finding that an expanded nonconforming use is not more detrimental to the neighborhood. ZBA members are volunteers and the standard is strict, so a request framed around the lot's shape and history does far better than one framed around what the buyer would like to do.

## In practice

A Weymouth buyer wants to add a 1,200 square foot drive-through lane to a 1960s retail building that already sits 8 feet from the side line where the bylaw requires 20. The project needs a dimensional variance and a special permit for the drive-through. The ZBA hearing is noticed 14 days ahead, abutters are mailed, and the board approves after one continuance with hours limited to 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. Total time from filing to a recorded decision: about four months.
