## What it means

A demising wall is the partition that legally and physically separates one tenant's leased premises from the adjacent tenant or from building common areas. It defines where your space ends and where your rent is measured. Unlike interior partitions, a demising wall is usually a rated assembly that runs slab to deck, carries fire and sound ratings, and is considered part of the building shell rather than the tenant's improvements.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Splitting larger units is how South Shore landlords keep older flex and retail buildings leased. A 10,000 square foot bay in a Rockland or Avon industrial building that sat empty for a year can become two 5,000 square foot units with one new demising wall, and 5,000 square foot users are far more common than 10,000 square foot users in this market. The wall's location also changes the rentable area, the loading access, and which tenant gets the existing electrical service and restrooms, so the drawing matters as much as the price.

## In practice

A landlord in Avon agrees to split a 12,000 square foot flex bay for a 7,000 square foot HVAC contractor. The new demising wall runs 110 linear feet, slab to a 20-foot deck, at about $95 per linear foot installed, roughly $10,500 including fire caulking at the roof penetrations. The landlord also adds a second electrical meter and a restroom to the smaller side for another $28,000, and recovers the cost through a rent of $11.50 per square foot net rather than the $10.25 it had been quoting for the whole bay.
