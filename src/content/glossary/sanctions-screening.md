## What it means

Sanctions screening compares a person or entity against published government lists of parties with whom business is restricted or prohibited: the Treasury's OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list, the federal System for Award Management exclusions, state debarment lists, and comparable lists abroad. The lists are public. The hard part is clearing false positives and checking the owners behind an entity, not just the entity itself.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A South Shore landlord or seller can be bound by sanctions rules without knowing it: dealing with a listed party, or an entity 50 percent owned by one, is prohibited regardless of where the building sits. Foreign-owned buyers on Route 3 industrial deals and offshore LLCs in Fort Lauderdale condos both get screened. ICS screens entities and their disclosed owners from public lists and public filings only, and the result supports a commercial decision, never an employment, credit, or tenant one.

## In practice

A Quincy seller receives a $4.2 million cash offer from an LLC formed 6 weeks earlier. Secretary of the Commonwealth and Delaware filings name a manager; corporate records in a second jurisdiction show a 60 percent owner. OFAC and SAM return no match on either after 4 false positives are cleared on public attributes. The result goes in the brief as a dated negative finding with the lists and query dates cited.
