## What it means

Gross leasable area is the standard measurement of retail space: the total floor area designed for tenant occupancy and exclusive use, measured from the outside face of exterior walls to the centerline of shared demising walls, including any basements or mezzanines the tenant occupies. A shopping center's total GLA is the sum of its tenant spaces, excluding common areas like sidewalks, parking, and shared corridors. Rent, CAM shares, and sales per square foot are all quoted on GLA.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore strip centers on Route 18, Route 53, Route 139, and Route 3A are priced and compared on GLA, and the number drives more than rent. A tenant's pro rata share of CAM, taxes, and insurance is its GLA divided by the center's total GLA, so a landlord that understates total GLA or excludes a vacant anchor from the denominator shifts costs onto the small tenants. ICS checks the denominator in every retail lease it reviews for a tenant, because a 5 percent error in the center's GLA is a 5 percent error in every expense bill for the life of the lease.

## In practice

A Hanover strip center has 42,000 square feet of GLA across eleven tenants. A 2,100 square foot nail salon's pro rata share is 5 percent. Annual CAM, taxes, and insurance total $9.20 per square foot, or $386,400 for the center, so the salon owes $19,320 a year on top of $27 per square foot base rent. If the landlord's lease exhibit listed the center at 38,000 square feet, the salon's share would rise to 5.5 percent and its bill to about $21,350, a $2,000 a year difference nobody would notice without checking.
