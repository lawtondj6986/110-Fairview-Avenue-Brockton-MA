## What it means

Rent commencement is the date on which the tenant's obligation to pay base rent begins. It is distinct from the lease commencement date, when the term starts and the tenant takes possession, and from the opening date, when the tenant begins operating. The gap between commencement and rent commencement is the tenant's construction period or free rent period, or both. The lease should define rent commencement precisely, because every month of difference is a month of rent, and it should state whether operating expense pass-throughs begin on the same date or earlier.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore tenants doing their own build-out should tie rent commencement to delivery plus a fixed number of days, with the number sized to the real permit and construction timeline in that town. A dental fit-out in Hanover may need 150 days; a paint-and-carpet office in Braintree may need 30. Landlords prefer a hard date so a slow tenant does not get free rent indefinitely. The compromise is usually "the earlier of": a set number of days after delivery, or the day the tenant opens, whichever comes first.

## In practice

A veterinary clinic leases 3,500 square feet in Pembroke at $27 per square foot, $7,875 a month. The lease sets commencement on delivery, and rent commencement at the earlier of 120 days after delivery or the clinic's opening. Pass-throughs of about $1,900 a month start on commencement. The space is delivered May 1; permits take six weeks, construction ten weeks, and the clinic opens September 12. Rent commencement is August 29, the 120-day mark, so the clinic pays two weeks of base rent before opening, about $3,600, and $7,600 in pass-throughs during the build-out.
