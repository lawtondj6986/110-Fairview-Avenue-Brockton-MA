## What it means

Verification is the step where a claim is tested against a source that can actually settle it: the deed, the docket, the filing, the license record. It asks three things. Does the record exist? Does it say what the claim says? And is it about this subject, not someone with a similar name?

## Why it matters in a dossier

Clients act on what a dossier says: they sign, walk away, or go public with it. One unverified claim presented as fact can sink a deal unfairly or expose a campaign to a defamation problem. So every material statement carries a source and a status. If we could not verify it, we say so in the same sentence.

## In practice

A seller's offering memorandum says the building is 100 percent leased with 7 years left on the anchor lease. We check: the recorded notice of lease shows a term ending in 4 years, with two 5-year options. The corporate registry shows the tenant entity in good standing. Result: occupancy verified, the 7-year figure not verified. The buyer requests the lease and an estoppel certificate before the 30-day diligence period runs out. Each line carries its source.
