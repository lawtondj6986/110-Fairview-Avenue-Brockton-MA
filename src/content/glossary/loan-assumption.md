## What it means

A loan assumption is a transaction in which the buyer of a property takes over the seller's existing mortgage, becoming the borrower on the same note, rate, amortization, and maturity, with the lender's consent. Most commercial loans are not freely assumable; the lender reviews the new borrower's credit and experience, charges an assumption fee, and may require the seller to be released from liability or to remain as a guarantor. The buyer pays the seller the difference between the price and the loan balance in cash or with a lender-approved second loan.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Many South Shore owners locked in fixed-rate loans at 3.5 to 4.5 percent between 2019 and 2022 on Brockton industrial, Quincy retail, and Plymouth office buildings. Those loans are now below market by 250 to 350 basis points. A buyer who can assume one gets cash flow a new borrower cannot match, and a seller can market the loan as a feature rather than eat a prepayment penalty. ICS checks whether the existing debt is assumable at the start of a seller engagement, because it changes who the realistic buyers are and what the net proceeds look like.

## In practice

A seller owns a 16,000 square foot Brockton flex building with NOI of $168,000 and a $1.3 million loan at 4.1 percent fixed through 2031, payment about $7,600 a month. A buyer agrees to pay $2.3 million and assume the loan, bringing $1 million in cash. The assumption fee is $13,000. A new $1.3 million loan at 7 percent would cost roughly $9,200 a month, so the buyer saves about $19,000 a year in debt service, raising first-year cash-on-cash return from roughly 5 percent to nearly 7 percent.
