## What it means

Structured analytic techniques are documented procedures for organizing evidence and reasoning so that the steps are visible, repeatable, and open to challenge. They were developed in the intelligence community to counter known cognitive biases: anchoring on a first impression, seeking confirmation, and mistaking a vivid detail for a representative one. Common techniques include analysis of competing hypotheses, key assumptions checks, chronologies, and devil's advocacy.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A dossier is only as good as the reasoning between the records and the conclusion. The records are public and anyone can pull them; the value is in what they are judged to mean. ICS applies at least one structured technique to every Deep Dossier and names it in the methodology section, so a client's attorney or a campaign manager can see how the conclusion was reached and where it could be wrong. The techniques do not require more data. They require discipline with the data already in hand.

## In practice

A vulnerability study for a South Shore candidate produced 60 pages of public records and one headline finding. Before delivery, a hypotheses matrix tested the finding against three alternative explanations. One alternative fit the evidence as well as the headline did. The final brief reported both, rated the confidence as moderate rather than high, and recommended the candidate address the issue on their own terms.
