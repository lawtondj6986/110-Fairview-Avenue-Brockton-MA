## What it means

A building permit history is the chronological record of permits issued for a property: building, electrical, plumbing, gas, mechanical, and demolition, each with the applicant, the contractor, the scope and value of work, the inspection results, and whether the permit was closed out. The local building department keeps it. In Massachusetts those records are public and permanent, and many municipalities make them searchable online.

## Why it matters in a dossier

The permit history is the property's resume. It tells a buyer what was done and by whom, and it tells a dossier about the contractor as well, because the contractor's license number appears on each permit. ICS pulls the full history on any South Shore property brief and cross-checks the contractor names against license lookups and court records. A building with a $400,000 renovation and $12,000 in permitted work is a finding.

## In practice

A Quincy buyer is told a flex building got a "complete electrical upgrade" in 2021. The city portal shows one electrical permit that year, valued at $8,500, for a single panel, closed out after a passed inspection. There is no permit for the three new rooftop units the broker mentioned. The buyer's inspector confirms the units are unpermitted, and the price drops to reflect the cost of permitting them properly.
