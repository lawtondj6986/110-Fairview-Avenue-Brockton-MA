## What it means

A collection plan breaks a client's question into specific sub-questions, assigns each to the public or licensed sources most likely to answer it, and sets the order, the time budget, and the stopping point. It is written before any searching starts. It also states what is out of scope: private individuals, medical or credit data, anything requiring deception.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Without a plan, research sprawls and the bill follows. With one, a counterparty check on a Rockland industrial seller is 11 named sources, a 3-day window, and a clear line on what will not be touched. The plan is also the client's protection: it documents that every method was lawful before the first record is pulled, and that nothing feeds an employment, credit, or tenant decision.

## In practice

A buyer wants to know whether a Brockton seller has a history of failed closings. The plan lists MassCourts civil dockets in Plymouth and Brockton, the Registry of Deeds grantor index, Secretary of the Commonwealth filings for all related entities, and two years of local news archives. Budget: 14 hours. Stop rule: three consecutive sources with no new names. The brief lands on day four with 2 dropped deals confirmed and 1 pending suit.
