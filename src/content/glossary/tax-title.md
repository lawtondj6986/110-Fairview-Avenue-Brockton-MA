## What it means

Tax title is the lien interest a Massachusetts city or town acquires when property taxes go unpaid. Under M.G.L. c. 60, the collector can take the property for the municipality, or sell the lien, and record an instrument at the registry of deeds. The treasurer can later ask the Land Court to foreclose the owner's right to redeem. The rules have been amended recently, so talk to your attorney about current deadlines.

## Why it matters in a dossier

A tax taking is a dated, recorded sign of financial stress. An owner who lets taxes go unpaid on one building may be stretched across all of them. For a buyer, it is also a title item that must be cleared at closing. We report the taking, the amount, and whether a redemption or foreclosure followed.

## In practice

A client is weighing an off-market offer on a vacant commercial lot in Brockton. The registry shows an instrument of taking recorded 3 years ago for $14,200 in unpaid taxes, with no redemption recorded. A municipal lien certificate puts the current payoff near $27,000 with interest and charges. The client's attorney builds the payoff into the purchase and sale agreement.
