## What it means

A construction loan funds the hard and soft costs of building or substantially renovating a property. Unlike a permanent mortgage, it is advanced in draws as work progresses, carries a floating rate with interest-only payments, and matures in one to two years. The lender sizes it as a percentage of total project cost, typically 65 to 80 percent, and requires the borrower's equity to go in first. Repayment comes from a permanent loan once the building is complete and leased, or from a sale.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Local and regional banks headquartered on the South Shore do most of the construction lending for projects under $15 million: owner-user buildings in Rockland and Hanover, small flex buildings in Plymouth, and Brockton redevelopment. They want to see entitlements in hand, a fixed-price contract or a guaranteed maximum price, and a contingency. For owner-users, an SBA 504 loan can fund construction and then convert to permanent debt, which removes the takeout risk that worries most lenders.

## In practice

A contractor builds a 12,000 square foot flex building in Plymouth. Total cost is $3.6 million: $2.9 million hard, $400,000 soft, $300,000 land already owned. The bank lends 70 percent of cost, $2.52 million, at prime plus 1 with interest-only payments; the owner's land and $780,000 in cash go in first. Draws run monthly over ten months, and at substantial completion the loan converts to a 25-year SBA 504 structure with the bank in first position.
