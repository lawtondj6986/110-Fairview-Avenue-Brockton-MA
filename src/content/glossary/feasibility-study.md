## What it means

A feasibility study tests whether a proposed development or conversion is worth pursuing before serious money is spent. It combines a physical review of the site, a zoning and permitting analysis, a market study of demand and achievable rents or prices, and a financial model comparing total cost to value on completion. The output is a go, no-go, or redesign decision, and a list of the assumptions that would have to hold for the project to work.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Site conditions and permitting sink more South Shore projects than markets do. A feasibility study for a Plymouth or Marshfield site must address wetlands buffers and septic capacity early; one in Brockton or Taunton must address environmental history and parking relief; one in Hanover or Rockland must address ledge and traffic on Route 53 and Route 139. Highest and best use is often the real question: an owner who assumes retail may find that small-bay industrial or medical pencils better and permits faster.

## In practice

A Rockland owner considers replacing a 1960s 8,000 square foot office building with 20 small-bay industrial units totaling 30,000 square feet. The study finds the site holds the building at 30 percent lot coverage, septic flow is the constraint and limits the project to 26,000 square feet, and local small-bay rents support $16 per square foot NNN. Cost: $6.1 million. Stabilized NOI: $395,000, a 6.5 percent yield on cost against 7 percent exit caps. The owner redesigns toward a smaller phase rather than proceed as drawn.
