## What it means

Title insurance is an indemnity policy, paid once at closing, that covers loss from defects in title that existed before the policy date but were not excepted: forged deeds, unreleased mortgages, missed liens, errors in the public record, undisclosed heirs. A lender's policy is required by nearly every commercial mortgage; an owner's policy is optional and protects the buyer's equity. In Massachusetts the buyer's closing attorney typically examines title at the registry of deeds for 50 years and the title company issues the policy based on that examination.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Plymouth and Norfolk County titles run deep. Old mill lots in Brockton and Whitman, cranberry bog parcels in Carver and Plympton, and waterfront in Marshfield and Duxbury often carry chains with missing probates, ambiguous plan references, or unreleased mortgages from banks that no longer exist. A clean 50-year exam catches most of it, but not everything, and the cost to cure a defect can exceed the policy premium many times over. For an owner-user putting a decade of equity into a building, the owner's policy is cheap insurance.

## In practice

A buyer closes on a $1.8 million Brockton industrial property with a $1.3 million loan. The lender's policy costs about $3,500 and the owner's policy, issued simultaneously, roughly $1,800 more. Three years later a neighbor produces a 1972 easement for a shared drive that the title exam missed. The title insurer defends the claim and pays $40,000 to relocate the drive, a cost the owner would otherwise have carried alone.
