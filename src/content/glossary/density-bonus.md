## What it means

A density bonus is a zoning provision that allows a developer to build more than the base zoning permits, measured in dwelling units, floor area ratio, height, or lot coverage, in exchange for providing a public benefit. The benefit is most often affordable housing, but can be open space, infrastructure, green building standards, or historic preservation. The bonus is how a town pays for the benefit without spending its own money.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Density bonuses became more common on the South Shore as towns wrote MBTA Communities multifamily districts and inclusionary zoning bylaws. Quincy, Braintree, Weymouth, and Brockton all have provisions trading affordability for height or units in their denser districts, and Plymouth's village centers offer bonuses for mixed-use. For a developer the bonus often decides whether a site works: three extra units on a 30-unit project is a 10 percent revenue increase on the same land and much of the same site cost.

## In practice

A Weymouth site near the Route 18 corridor is zoned for 24 units by right. The bylaw offers one bonus market-rate unit for each affordable unit, up to 25 percent more density, by special permit. The developer provides six affordable units at 80 percent of AMI and receives six bonus units, for 36 total. The affordable units rent about $900 a month below market, roughly $65,000 a year in forgone rent, while the six bonus units add about $160,000 in annual rent on land already paid for.
