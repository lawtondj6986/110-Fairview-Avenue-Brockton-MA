## What it means

A commercial condominium divides a building into separately owned units, each with its own deed, plus an undivided interest in common areas such as the parking lot, roof, hallways, and systems. In Massachusetts it is created under Chapter 183A by recording a master deed, with an association of unit owners that collects fees and maintains the common elements. Office, medical, and small industrial condos are the usual forms.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Commercial condos are a common path to ownership for South Shore dentists, accountants, therapists, and contractors who need 1,500 to 5,000 square feet and cannot justify a whole building. Office condo parks in Hanover, Norwell, Pembroke, and Plymouth off Route 3 and Route 53 trade regularly, and small-bay industrial condos in Rockland and Hanover are in short supply. The documents matter as much as the unit: a master deed that bars medical use or limits signage will not bend for you later.

## In practice

A Hanover physical therapy practice buys a 2,400 square foot office condo for $540,000, about $225 per square foot, using an SBA 504 loan with 10 percent down, $54,000. Monthly debt service runs roughly $3,500 and condo fees $620, which covers snow, landscaping, roof reserve, and exterior insurance. Compared with the $4,600 a month it paid as a tenant, the practice's occupancy cost is similar, but it now builds equity and controls its space.
