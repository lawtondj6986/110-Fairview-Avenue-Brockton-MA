## What it means

Liquidated damages are an amount the parties agree to at signing as the compensation for a particular breach. The clause replaces a later argument over actual losses with a known number. In real estate it most often appears as the seller keeping the buyer's deposit, or as a daily charge for late delivery or holding over.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The clause sets the real downside of a deal. A buyer of a building in Hanover or Plymouth who signs with a deposit as liquidated damages knows the most it can lose by walking. A seller knows it cannot chase more, even if the next buyer pays less. In leases, the same idea shows up as holdover rent at 150 or 200 percent.

## In practice

A buyer signs to purchase a $2,000,000 property in Quincy with a $100,000 deposit, 5 percent, stated as liquidated damages and the seller's only remedy. After contingencies expire, the buyer cannot close. The seller keeps $100,000 and resells four months later for $1,900,000. The seller cannot recover carrying costs beyond the deposit.
