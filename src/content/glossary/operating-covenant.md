## What it means

An operating covenant is a lease clause requiring the tenant to open for business by a stated date and to continuously operate in the premises during specified hours for all or part of the lease term. It is the opposite of a go-dark right, which lets a tenant close while still paying rent. Operating covenants are most common in retail leases where the landlord, other tenants, or a lender depend on the tenant's presence to generate traffic. Remedies for breach range from damages to landlord termination and recapture.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

South Shore shopping centers in Hanover, Kingston, Weymouth, and Brockton depend on a handful of anchors and junior anchors to pull traffic for the smaller tenants. When a grocery or pharmacy closes a location but keeps paying rent to block a competitor, the inline tenants suffer and the center's value drops. Landlords and their lenders want operating covenants on anchors; anchors resist. For ICS landlord clients, the practical goal is a covenant to open and operate for an initial period plus a recapture right thereafter. For inline tenant clients, the goal is a co-tenancy clause triggered if the anchor goes dark.

## In practice

A landlord leases a 28,000 square foot junior anchor space in a Kingston center to a regional sporting goods chain at $14 per square foot NNN, about $392,000 a year. The tenant refuses a ten-year operating covenant. The parties agree to a covenant to open within 180 days and operate for 36 months, after which the tenant may go dark on 90 days notice, and the landlord may recapture the space within 12 months of closure. Four inline tenants totaling 11,000 square feet get co-tenancy rights tied to the anchor operating.
