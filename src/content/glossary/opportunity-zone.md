## What it means

An Opportunity Zone is a low-income census tract designated under the 2017 federal tax law, where investors who put realized capital gains into a Qualified Opportunity Fund can defer tax on those gains for a statutory period and, after a ten-year hold, exclude gain on the fund investment. The fund must invest in qualified property within the zone and, for an existing building, spend at least as much on improvements as it paid for the building, excluding land, within 30 months. Legislation in 2025 extended the program with new designations and revised rules. The details are technical and the penalties for getting them wrong are real.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Brockton is the center of gravity. Downtown, the Campello and Montello industrial areas, and tracts along Main Street and Route 28 were designated, which has drawn fund money into mixed-use and multifamily projects near the commuter rail stations. For a local owner-user, the practical angle is a different buyer pool: an investor with a large gain from a business or stock sale who needs a Brockton property to improve. For a seller, that buyer may pay for the location over the building. ICS notes OZ status in every Brockton Buyer Brief.

## In practice

An investor sells a business with a $1.5 million gain and, within 180 days, puts it into a Qualified Opportunity Fund that buys a $1.2 million Brockton mill building, of which $900,000 is the building and $300,000 is land. The fund must invest at least $900,000 more in improvements within 30 months. Tax on the original gain is deferred, and if the fund holds the renovated building past ten years, appreciation on the investment is tax free.
