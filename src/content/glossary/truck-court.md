## What it means

A truck court is the paved area adjacent to a building's loading docks where tractor-trailers and delivery trucks approach, turn, back into the dock, and sometimes stage. Its depth, measured from the dock face to the opposite edge of the maneuvering area, determines what size trucks the building can serve. Depth, pavement strength, separation from car traffic, and trailer parking are what industrial tenants and their logistics consultants inspect first on a tour.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Most South Shore industrial buildings were built for a different era of trucking. Buildings from the 1970s and 1980s in Rockland, Hanover, Avon, and Brockton often have 80 to 100 feet of court, fine for straight trucks and shuttle trailers but tight for 53-foot trailers, and the lot lines and wetlands leave no room to expand. Newer buildings along Route 24 and Interstate 495 in Taunton, Mansfield, and West Bridgewater are built with 130-foot courts and trailer parking as standard. A tenant with regular tractor-trailer deliveries will pay more for the new building or discount the old one.

## In practice

An investor compares two 40,000 square foot warehouses. The Rockland building has four docks and a 90-foot court bounded by a wetland buffer; the West Bridgewater building has six docks, a 135-foot court, and 12 trailer stalls. The Rockland building leases at $11.50 per square foot NNN to a cabinet distributor on straight trucks; the West Bridgewater building leases at $14 to a regional food distributor. Three-quarters of the $100,000 annual rent gap traces to the yard, not the box.
