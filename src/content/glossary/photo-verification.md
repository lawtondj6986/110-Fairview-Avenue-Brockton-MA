## What it means

Open-source photo verification is the process of testing a publicly available image against other public information to establish its origin, location, and date. It asks whether the photo is what it claims to be: the right place, the right time, unaltered, and not recycled from an older event.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Photos persuade faster than documents, which is why a wrong one does damage. A listing may show a building before the fire. A campaign mailer may use a picture from a different town. A "recent" site photo may be 6 years old. Before an image supports any statement in a report, it gets checked and graded like any other source.

## In practice

A broker's package for a Taunton warehouse includes 12 exterior photos described as current. Reverse image search finds 5 of them on a listing from 4 years ago. Street-level imagery dated 14 months ago shows a section of roof tarped and 2 loading doors boarded, neither visible in the package. The buyer orders a roof inspection and a fresh site visit before making an offer. The report shows each comparison side by side with its source and capture date, so the buyer can judge the match directly.
