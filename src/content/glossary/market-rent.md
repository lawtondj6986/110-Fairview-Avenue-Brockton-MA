## What it means

Market rent is the rent a space would lease for today, to a typical tenant, after reasonable exposure to the market, on terms normal for that property type. It is an estimate drawn from comparable signed leases, adjusted for differences in size, location, condition, term, and concessions. It is distinct from asking rent, which is what landlords hope for, and from in-place rent, which is what existing tenants pay. Appraisers, lenders, and renewal clauses all lean on it.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore is a collection of small submarkets, and market rent in one does not transfer to another. Flex space in Rockland and Hanover near the Route 3 and Route 53 corridor trades at a different number than similar space in Brockton or Taunton, and retail on Route 139 in Marshfield is not retail on Main Street in Brockton. Lease comps are harder to find than sale comps because they are not recorded, which is where a broker's deal flow earns its keep. ICS maintains lease comps by town for market sheets and tenant representation.

## In practice

A tenant's renewal option on 5,000 square feet of Hanover flex space sets rent at 95 percent of market. The landlord proposes $14 per square foot net, pointing to asking rents on two listings. The tenant's broker pulls four signed deals from the past 12 months at $11.75 to $13.00 net, two with three months of free rent, and argues for $12.25 net effective. The clause's tiebreaker sends it to a third broker, who lands at $12.60. The tenant pays 95 percent of that, $11.97 net, about $10,000 a year less than the landlord's opening number.
