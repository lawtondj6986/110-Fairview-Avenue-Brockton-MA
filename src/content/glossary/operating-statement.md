## What it means

An operating statement is the profit and loss report for a property over a month, a year, or a trailing twelve months. It lists income collected and the expenses of running the building and arrives at net operating income. Buyers, lenders, and appraisers all start from it.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Small owners in Brockton, Taunton, and Weymouth often keep books for tax purposes, not for a sale. The statement a buyer receives may mix in personal expenses, omit management because the owner does it, or show taxes from before a reassessment. Rebuilding the statement the way a lender will see it is the first real step in pricing a local investment property.

## In practice

A seller's statement for a six-unit retail building in Rockland shows $180,000 of income and $48,000 of expenses, for $132,000 of net operating income. The buyer adds a 4 percent management fee, $7,200, and $5,000 of reserves, and corrects taxes upward by $6,000. Adjusted income is $113,800. At a 7.5 percent cap rate, that is about $243,000 less value.
