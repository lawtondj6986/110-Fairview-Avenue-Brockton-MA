## What it means

A base rate is the underlying frequency of an event or condition in a relevant population. In analysis, it is the prior you should hold before examining a specific case. Neglecting it, by weighting a vivid specific detail more than the general frequency, is one of the most common reasoning errors. Good tradecraft states the base rate explicitly so the reader can judge how far a finding departs from it.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Public records are full of things that look bad out of context. Most Massachusetts LLCs miss an annual report at some point. Most commercial properties on Route 18 have a code violation in their file. Most contractors have been sued. ICS reports a finding against its base rate: three suits in two years when the trade averages one in ten is a signal; one in ten is Tuesday. This keeps a dossier honest and keeps a client from walking away from a sound deal over noise.

## In practice

A due-diligence brief on a Rockland restaurant tenant finds four Board of Health inspection failures in three years. Pulling the same records for the six nearest restaurants shows an average of 3.5 failures over the same period, all corrected on reinspection. The tenant is ordinary. The brief says so, with the comparison, and the landlord proceeds instead of losing a $42,000-a-year lease to a false alarm.
