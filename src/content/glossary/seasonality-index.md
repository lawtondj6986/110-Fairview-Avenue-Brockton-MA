## What it means

A seasonality index is a set of multipliers, one per month or week, that express how demand in that period compares to the average for the year. An index of 1.0 is average; 1.5 is 50 percent above; 0.5 is half. It is built from a business's own history, supplemented by search trend data, and is used to set ad budgets, staffing, inventory, and cash reserves ahead of time instead of reacting.

## Why it matters for a local business

South Shore trades live and die by the calendar. HVAC calls in Brockton spike in the first heat wave and the first cold snap. Landscapers in Hanover go from zero to overwhelmed in three weeks of April. Restaurants in Plymouth see summer tourists and a dead February. ICS uses the index when setting Lead Engine ad budgets and when scheduling reactivation campaigns, so the message to past customers lands two to four weeks before the index turns up, not after the phone is already ringing.

## In practice

A Weymouth HVAC company's three-year index showed June at 1.6, July at 1.7, January at 1.3, and April and October both at 0.5. The company had been spending the same $2,500 a month on ads all year. ICS shifted it: $4,000 in late May through July and December through January, $1,200 in the shoulder months with a maintenance-plan message. Annual spend stayed flat at $30,000. Booked service calls rose 14 percent, and the shoulder-month maintenance plans added 140 recurring customers.
