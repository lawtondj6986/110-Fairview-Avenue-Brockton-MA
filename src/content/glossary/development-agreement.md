## What it means

A development agreement is a negotiated contract between a developer and a city or town, sometimes a redevelopment authority, that fixes the terms of a project. It records what will be built, on what schedule, and what the developer will fund or provide. It gives both sides something enforceable beyond the conditions written into permits.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Larger projects here often carry one: downtown redevelopment in Brockton and Quincy, the former naval air station land in Weymouth, Rockland, and Abington, or a rezoning that goes to town meeting in Hanover or Plymouth. For a buyer of a site or a completed building, the agreement can carry obligations that run with the land. It is a public record, obtainable from the municipality.

## In practice

A developer proposes 180 apartments over 12,000 square feet of retail in a downtown. The agreement requires a $400,000 payment toward an intersection signal, 10 percent of units at affordable rents, and a building permit within 24 months or the approvals lapse. A later buyer inherits all three terms.
