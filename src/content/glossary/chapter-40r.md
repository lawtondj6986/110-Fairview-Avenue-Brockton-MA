## What it means

Chapter 40R is Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 40R, the Smart Growth Zoning Overlay District Act. A city or town adopts an overlay district that allows dense housing or mixed use as of right, with at least 20 percent of the units affordable, in a location near transit, a downtown, or an existing developed area. In return the state pays a zoning incentive payment on adoption and a density bonus payment per unit when building permits issue. A companion law, Chapter 40S, reimburses school costs tied to the new units.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Several towns south of Boston have used 40R, often around commuter rail stations on the Kingston, Middleborough, and Greenbush lines, and more are looking at it as a way to satisfy the MBTA Communities Act with state money attached. For a landowner inside a district, the overlay can double or triple what the base zoning allows without a special permit fight. For a commercial owner nearby, it means hundreds of new households walking past the storefront within a few years. Check whether a parcel is in or near an existing or proposed 40R district before pricing it.

## In practice

A 1.5-acre lot near a South Shore commuter rail stop is zoned for a single commercial building. Inside a 40R overlay at 20 units per acre it supports 30 apartments, six of them affordable, plus ground-floor retail. The town collects a one-time incentive payment and a per-unit bonus of about $3,000 at permit, and the land trades for roughly $40,000 per buildable unit instead of a flat commercial land price.
