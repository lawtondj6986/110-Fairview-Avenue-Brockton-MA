## What it means

Keyword cannibalization happens when multiple pages on one website target the same search intent. The search engine cannot tell which page is the authority, so it splits ranking signals between them and often ranks neither well. It is common on sites that publish many near-duplicate service or town pages.

## Why it matters for a local business

Local businesses are especially prone to it because the easy way to cover 41 towns is 41 copies of the same page with the town name swapped. Google sees thin duplicates, not local relevance. ICS service-area pages on JobSite are written to be distinct: a Hanover page talks about Hanover housing stock, permits, and jobs done there, and a Rockland page does the same for Rockland. One page per service per town, each saying something only that page says.

## In practice

A Plymouth roofing company had six pages mentioning "roof replacement Plymouth": a service page, two blog posts, a town page, a pricing page, and the home page. None ranked above position 9. ICS consolidated them into one service page and one pricing page, redirected the rest, and rewrote the blog posts around different questions. The service page reached position 3 within ten weeks and its monthly leads went from 4 to 15.
