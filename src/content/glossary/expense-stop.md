## What it means

An expense stop is a fixed amount of operating expenses, stated per square foot or as a total dollar figure, that the landlord agrees to pay. The tenant reimburses its pro rata share of any expenses above the stop. It is a tool for modified gross leases: the quoted rent looks like a gross rent, but the tenant carries the inflation risk above the stop. Unlike a base year, the stop does not move with actual first-year costs, so it can be set above or below what the building really spends.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Expense stops show up in older South Shore office buildings in Quincy, Braintree, and Brockton where landlords want to quote a simple gross rate against competing buildings. The number is only meaningful next to actual costs. A building on Route 18 quoting $20 gross with a $7 stop when real expenses run $9.50 is effectively charging $22.50 gross. Tenants comparing three buildings should convert each to an all-in occupancy cost before deciding; ICS does this in a side-by-side for tenant clients.

## In practice

A 3,000 square foot tenant signs a lease at $21 per square foot with an expense stop of $8 per square foot. In the first year, operating expenses come in at $8.60, so the tenant owes $0.60 per square foot above the stop, $1,800 for the year, on top of $63,000 in base rent. In year three, expenses reach $9.40 and the tenant's overage is $4,200. The landlord's alternative offer, $22 rent with a $9 stop, would have cost $1,200 more in year one and the same in year three: the price of predictability in a year when expenses stay flat.
