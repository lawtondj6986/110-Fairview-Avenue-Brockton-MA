## What it means

Web archives, chiefly the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, store dated snapshots of public web pages. A researcher can read what a company's site, a candidate's platform page, or a property listing said on a specific day, even after it was edited or deleted. Snapshots are public, timestamped, and citable. They cover only pages that were publicly accessible when captured; nothing behind a login is ever involved.

## Why it matters in a dossier

Websites are edited to fit the present. A Brockton contractor's "licensed and insured" claim, a Plymouth developer's "completed projects" list, a candidate's deleted position statement: the archive preserves the earlier version with a date. For chronology and corroboration work, a snapshot is often the only record of what a party represented to the public at the time. ICS uses it on businesses and public figures in public roles, not private individuals.

## In practice

A tenant in Hanover claims it has operated continuously since 2015 under the same ownership. Archived snapshots of its site show a different owner's name and a Boca Raton address from 2015 through 2019, and the current name appearing in March 2020 with no mention of the prior business. The landlord asks for 4 years of financials instead of 2 and a personal guaranty from the current owner.
