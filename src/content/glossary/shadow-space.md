## What it means

Shadow space is space that is leased and paid for but not occupied or needed by the tenant, and is likely to come back to the market through sublease, early termination, or a smaller renewal. It does not appear in vacancy statistics because the lease is still in force, so it is a hidden supply overhang. Shadow space grows after a tenant downsizes, merges, or shifts to remote work, and it shrinks as leases roll and the space is formally returned or re-leased.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Office space on the South Shore has carried meaningful shadow space since 2020. Professional firms in Quincy, Braintree, Hingham, and Hanover kept leases signed before remote work and now use half the floor, and a published vacancy rate of 12 percent may understate effective availability by several points. For an investor buying a multi-tenant office building on Route 3, shadow space is the difference between a stable rent roll and three tenants who all shrink by 40 percent at their next renewal. For a tenant, it is leverage: a landlord who knows the floor above is dark will negotiate.

## In practice

A buyer underwrites a 36,000 square foot office building in Braintree that shows 94 percent leased. On the tour, two of the five tenants, together 14,000 square feet, are visibly using less than half their space, and both leases expire within 30 months. The buyer models a 35 percent give-back from each at renewal, about 4,900 square feet, plus eight months of downtime and $25 per square foot in re-leasing costs. The adjustment lowers the offer from $5.4 million to $4.75 million, and the seller's broker does not dispute the tour.
