## What it means

Bad-boy carve-outs are the exceptions to a non-recourse loan. The lender agrees to look only to the property for repayment, except where the borrower commits certain acts. Those acts are listed in a carve-out guaranty, usually signed by a principal with real net worth.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Local banks here mostly lend with full personal guaranties, so carve-outs show up when a South Shore owner graduates to larger debt: an agency or CMBS loan on an apartment portfolio in Quincy, or a life company loan on a Route 3 retail center. Owners used to community bank paper sometimes read "non-recourse" and stop reading. The carve-out guaranty is where the personal exposure sits.

## In practice

An owner closes a $6,000,000 non-recourse loan on a Weymouth retail center. Two years later a partner records a $400,000 second mortgage without lender consent. The loan documents treat that as a full-recourse event, so the guarantor is now personally exposed to the whole $6,000,000 balance, over a $400,000 decision. A consent request to the servicer first would have avoided it.
