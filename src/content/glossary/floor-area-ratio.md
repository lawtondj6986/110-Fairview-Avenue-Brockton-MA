## What it means

Floor area ratio is the ratio of a building's total gross floor area to the area of the lot it sits on. A town that caps FAR at 0.4 in an industrial district allows 40,000 square feet of building on a 100,000 square foot lot, whether as one story covering 40 percent of the lot or two stories covering 20 percent. Together with height, setbacks, lot coverage, and parking, FAR defines the maximum building envelope and therefore the land's development value.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

Many South Shore towns regulate commercial districts with lot coverage, height, and parking rather than FAR, but Quincy, Braintree, and Brockton use FAR in their denser business and mixed-use districts, and several towns wrote FAR limits into their new MBTA Communities districts. Where FAR applies, it is often the number that decides whether a redevelopment pencils. A buyer who assumes "three stories on the whole lot" and later discovers an FAR of 1.0 has lost a third of the project before the first hearing.

## In practice

A 30,000 square foot lot in a Quincy business district carries a 1.5 FAR. The maximum building is 45,000 square feet. A developer planning ground-floor retail and three floors of apartments at 950 square feet per unit nets roughly 35 apartments after circulation and 8,000 square feet of retail. If a special permit allows FAR of 2.0, the same lot supports 60,000 square feet and about 50 units, which moves the land value by several hundred thousand dollars.
