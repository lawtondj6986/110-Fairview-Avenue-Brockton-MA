## What it means

A radius restriction is a covenant in a retail lease that prohibits the tenant, and often its affiliates, from operating another store under the same or a similar trade name within a stated distance of the leased premises. The radius is measured from the premises or the center boundary, and the clause typically defines what counts as a competing business, what happens to percentage rent if the tenant violates it, and whether the restriction survives assignment or sublease. It is the mirror image of an exclusive use clause: the exclusive protects the tenant from competitors in the center; the radius protects the landlord from the tenant competing with itself.

## Why it matters on the South Shore

The South Shore's trade areas are small and overlapping. A three-mile radius drawn from a Route 139 plaza in Hanover reaches into Rockland, Norwell, and Pembroke; a five-mile radius from a Brockton site covers most of Brockton, Abington, Whitman, and Avon. A growing local operator with one location who signs a five-mile restriction may find it cannot open its second store anywhere in its home market. ## In practice

A pizza operator leases 2,000 square feet in a Pembroke plaza with percentage rent of 6 percent above a $700,000 breakpoint and a three-mile radius restriction. Two years in, it wants a second location in Hanover, 2.4 miles away. The lease allows it only if the tenant pays the landlord percentage rent as though the two stores' sales were combined. The operator estimates $150,000 in cannibalized sales, roughly $9,000 a year in lost percentage rent to the landlord, and negotiates a flat $7,500 annual payment and a waiver of the restriction instead.
