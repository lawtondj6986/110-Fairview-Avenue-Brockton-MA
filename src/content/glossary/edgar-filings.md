## What it means

EDGAR is the Securities and Exchange Commission's free public system for filings by public companies, registered funds, and their officers and large shareholders. Annual and quarterly reports, 8-K event notices, proxy statements, insider trading forms, and ownership reports are all there, searchable by company or person, back to the 1990s. It is a primary source: the filer signs under penalty of law.

## Why it matters in a dossier

When a national tenant, a REIT, or a public lender shows up in a South Shore deal, EDGAR tells you what they told their own investors. A chain anchoring a Hanover plaza that disclosed 40 store closures in its last 10-K is a different tenant than its leasing agent describes. The same filings reveal officer histories, related-party deals, and litigation reserves, all public and all citable.

## In practice

A Rockland landlord is offered a 10-year lease by a publicly traded retailer. The latest 10-K lists the Northeast as a region under "store optimization review," the 10-Q shows same-store sales down 6.2 percent, and an 8-K discloses a $45 million credit facility amendment with tightened covenants. The landlord negotiates a 5-year term with options, a larger security deposit, and a corporate guaranty.
