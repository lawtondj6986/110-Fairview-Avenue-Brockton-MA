Every recorded deed, mortgage, lien, and easement on every parcel in Massachusetts sits in a county Registry of Deeds, and the index is online and free. Most owners have never opened it. Most buyers and tenants rely on what the other side tells them.

The registry is not hard to use. It is unfamiliar. It is indexed by name instead of address, every document runs in a direction, and the document type names are a vocabulary of their own. Learn those three things and you can rebuild the ownership and debt history of a South Shore building in about twenty minutes.

Here is the method, minute by minute.

## Minutes one to three: the right registry, the right side

Massachusetts records land by county, and some counties are split into districts. For most of the South Shore that means Plymouth County (Brockton, Rockland, Hanover, Whitman, Abington, Marshfield, Plymouth), Norfolk County (Quincy, Weymouth, Braintree, Randolph, Stoughton), and the Northern District of Bristol County (Taunton, Easton, Raynham). Search the wrong county and you will find nothing and conclude, wrongly, that nothing exists.

Each registry also has two sides. **Recorded land** is the ordinary system: documents are filed by book and page and indexed by name. **Registered land** is the Land Court system: the parcel has a certificate of title, documents carry document numbers, and the certificate lists what encumbers the land. A parcel is one or the other, and occasionally part of each. If your search on the recorded side comes up thin, check the registered side before you decide the record is clean. The glossary entry on [Land Court records](/glossary/land-court-records/) covers the difference.

Start with the town assessor's online database. Type the street address and write down three things: the owner name exactly as spelled, the book and page of the current deed, and the parcel ID.

## Minutes four to eight: grantor and grantee

Every document has a party giving something and a party receiving it. The registry indexes each document twice, once under each name.

| Document | Grantor (gives) | Grantee (receives) |
|---|---|---|
| Deed | Seller | Buyer |
| Mortgage | Borrower | Lender |
| Assignment of mortgage | Old lender | New lender |
| Discharge | Lender | Borrower |
| Tax taking | Owner | Town or city |
| Notice of lease | Landlord | Tenant |

The practical rule: to see what an owner has done to the property, search the owner as **grantor** from the day they bought it forward. That one search returns their mortgages, easements they granted, leases they noticed, and the deed out if they sold. Then search the same name as **grantee** to find how they acquired the property and anything granted to them.

Names are literal. "Fairview Realty LLC" and "Fairview Realty, L.L.C." may index separately. Search the shortest distinctive fragment, then read the results. For an individual with a common name, the property description inside the document is what confirms you have the right person, not the name alone.

## Minutes nine to fourteen: what each document type tells you

The type column in the results is a summary of the property's life. The ones that matter most on a commercial parcel:

- **Deed.** Who took title, from whom, when, and for how much. Massachusetts deeds state the consideration.
- **Mortgage.** The lender and the original principal. Not the current balance.
- **Assignment.** The loan changed hands. Several assignments in a short period can mean a loan sold into a servicing pool, or a loan in trouble.
- **Discharge.** The mortgage was paid off. No discharge means the mortgage is still of record.
- **Municipal lien certificate.** The town's statement of taxes and charges owed on a date. Usually recorded at a sale or refinance. See [municipal lien certificate](/glossary/municipal-lien-certificate/).
- **Instrument of taking.** The town has taken a tax title for unpaid taxes. A serious flag.
- **Lis pendens, attachment, execution.** A lawsuit touches the property, or a creditor has a claim or judgment against it. The [lis pendens](/glossary/lis-pendens/) entry explains what the notice does.
- **Notice of lease.** A long lease exists. In Massachusetts a lease longer than seven years is generally noticed on record to bind later buyers.
- **Easement, restriction, order of conditions.** Someone else has rights over the land, or the land carries a limit on use.
- **Trustee's certificate.** For a property held in trust: who the trustees are and what authority they have.

Open the image for anything you do not recognize. The first page usually says what it is in the first two lines.

## Minutes fifteen to eighteen: a worked chain on a Rockland flex building

A composite example. The assessor shows a 14,000 square foot flex building off Route 139 owned by an LLC, with a deed reference from 2017.

The grantor search on the LLC, 2017 forward, returns six documents:

1. **2017, deed in.** Consideration $1,450,000.
2. **2017, mortgage.** A regional bank, $1,087,500, recorded the same minute as the deed. That is 75 percent of the price.
3. **2021, mortgage.** A different lender, $400,000.
4. **2022, notice of lease.** A ten-year lease to a distribution tenant with two five-year options.
5. **2023, assignment.** The 2017 mortgage assigned to another institution.
6. **2025, instrument of taking.** The town, for unpaid fiscal 2024 taxes of $18,640.

No discharge appears for either mortgage. So the record shows $1,487,500 of original recorded debt against a $1,450,000 purchase, a long-term tenant with rights that survive a sale, and an owner who fell behind on taxes last year.

None of that proves distress. The first loan has been amortizing for nine years and the building may be worth far more than it cost. But a buyer or tenant now has four specific questions to ask, with book and page numbers attached, instead of a general feeling.

## Minutes nineteen to twenty: check the price with the stamps

Massachusetts charges a deeds excise of $4.56 per $1,000 of consideration in most counties. A few counties carry a different rate, so confirm for the county you are in. The excise paid is printed or stamped on the first page of the deed.

The arithmetic runs both ways. A deed showing $3,876 in excise is an $850,000 sale: $3,876 divided by $4.56, times $1,000. If the stated consideration and the stamps disagree, read the deed again.

A deed for "nominal consideration" or "less than $100" carries no stamps. That is a transfer without a sale: an owner moving a building into an LLC, a trust, or a family member's name. It is not a comparable sale, and it should never be used as one. The [deeds excise tax](/glossary/deeds-excise-tax/) entry has the detail, and the same check is how the comparable sales behind a [cap rate](/cap-rate/) estimate get verified.

## What the registry will not tell you, and where ICS stops

The registry is a record of documents, not of facts. It does not show current loan balances, unrecorded agreements, most short leases, or who owns an LLC. It can lag by a day. It contains errors, and discharges that were signed and never recorded. Whether a title is actually good is a question for a title examiner and an attorney, and before money moves you should talk to counsel.

ICS uses the registry as a public record, the same way anyone can. We do not pretext, meaning nobody calls a lender or a town office pretending to be the owner. We do not surveil private individuals, and a registry search on a building is not a license to build a file on a person's home, family, or private life. We do not obtain medical, credit, or private financial data. And nothing ICS produces is used for employment, credit, or residential tenant decisions.

## What to do this week

1. Look up your own building in the assessor's database and write down the owner name, book, and page.
2. Run the owner as grantor from the deed date forward and list every document by type and date.
3. Open each mortgage and note the lender and original amount, then look for a matching discharge.
4. Check the registered land side if the recorded side looks thin.
5. Divide the deed excise by $4.56 and confirm it matches the stated price.
6. If you are looking at a building to buy, start a free [Buyer Brief](/buyer-brief/) and bring the list with you.
