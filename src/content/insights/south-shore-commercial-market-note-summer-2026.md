The sub-$3M commercial market on the South Shore doesn't get research coverage — the national firms don't track it and the Boston brokerages don't work it. So here's what we're seeing on the ground in the Brockton corridor (Route 24, Route 28, downtown) and the surrounding towns.

## Three things happening right now

**1. Owner-user demand is outrunning inventory.** Contractors, medical groups, and service businesses that outgrew leased space during the last few years are shopping for buildings to own. Well-located flex and small industrial product moves fast — often before it's marketed properly, which means buyers without a broker actively hunting for them never see it.

**2. Sellers are leaving money on the table with weak marketing.** Most sub-$3M listings still go to market with a few phone photos and a two-line description. The gap between that and an institutional-quality package — offering memorandum, drone media, targeted digital campaigns — is worth real dollars at the closing table. Small buildings deserve big-building marketing.

**3. Financing is workable, but preparation wins.** SBA 504 remains the owner-user's best friend for this price band. The buyers winning deals arrive with financing framed, a clear use narrative for zoning conversations, and fast decision cycles.

## What to do with this

- **If you're buying:** get specific about corridor, size, and yard/parking needs, then get someone hunting off-market. Waiting for LoopNet is how you see only what everyone else passed on.
- **If you're selling:** demand a marketing plan in writing before you sign a listing agreement. Ask exactly how your building will reach owner-users in the trades and medical segments — they're the deepest buyer pool in this market.
- **If you're leasing:** landlords are increasingly open to shorter terms with options in older retail product. There are deals to be had for tenants who can demonstrate strong operations.
