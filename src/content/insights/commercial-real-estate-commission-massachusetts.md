Ask a commercial broker what their commission is and you'll usually get the same non-answer you get from every opaque industry: *let's talk about your property first*. Fair enough — but you can walk into that conversation already knowing how the money works. Here's the straight version.

## The typical ranges

- **Commercial sales:** total commissions commonly run about **4–6% of the sale price**, often drifting toward the lower end as the price climbs — a percentage point on a $2M building is real money, and both sides know it. There is **no legally fixed rate**; it's negotiable, always.
- **Commercial leases:** usually a **percentage of the total lease value** (frequently around 4–6% across the lease term) or a **set number of months' rent**. A five-year lease commission can rival a small sale commission — worth understanding before you sign.
- **Flat and hybrid fees:** on some assignments a flat fee or a reduced-percentage-plus-bonus structure makes more sense than a straight percentage. Good brokers will structure to the deal; it's a conversation, not a vending machine.

## Who actually pays

On a typical sale, **the seller pays the full commission at closing**, out of the proceeds — and the listing broker splits it with whatever broker brings the buyer. The buyer usually writes no commission check directly (though, like everything, it's priced into the deal). On leases, the **landlord typically pays**. That's why, as a seller, the commission is one of the biggest line items between your sale price and what you actually net — which you can model in about a minute with the [Seller Net Proceeds calculator](/seller-net/).

## What's actually negotiable (almost all of it)

The rate is negotiable. So is the structure, the **length of the listing**, whether it's **exclusive**, the **protection period** after it ends, and — the part most sellers skip — **what the commission actually buys**. "We'll put it in the listing service" and "we'll produce an offering memorandum, drone and interior media, a targeted campaign to qualified buyers, and property-level SEO" are very different levels of effort for the same percentage. Get the deliverables named.

## The honest frame

A commission isn't a tax on selling — it's a bet that representation nets you more than going alone, *after* the fee. On commercial property, where a mispriced building or a weak buyer pool costs far more than any commission, that bet usually pays. The question isn't "what's the rate" — it's "what does the rate buy, and does it clear its own cost?"
