Ask what opposition research costs and you'll get the industry's favorite answer: *it depends*. Ask who does it well and you'll get a shrug and a referral. For a business built entirely on other people's transparency, political research is remarkably allergic to its own.

Here's what the record — fittingly — actually shows.

## The going rates

- **Statewide races:** A Democratic consultant writing in *Campaigns & Elections* put the typical statewide opposition report at roughly **$10,000–$12,000**, with less for down-ballot statewide offices ([via Capitol Fax](https://capitolfax.com/2019/02/04/the-limits-of-oppo/)).
- **The new AI-assisted tier:** Newer research shops now deliver a 20-page book in days for around **$7,500**, with subscription "command center" options through Election Day — pricing that [drew skepticism from veteran researchers](https://oppofile.substack.com/p/the-6250-question), but signals where the economics are going.
- **The presidential scale:** Veteran operatives have described full-cycle research budgets of [tens of thousands per month — hundreds of thousands over a presidential cycle](https://www.kpbs.org/news/national/2007/02/06/opposition-research-know-thine-enemies). Not your race, probably, but it calibrates the ceiling.
- **Local and legislative races:** Here's the finding that matters most — **there is no published market**. No rate card exists for the select board race, the state rep primary, the mayoral contest. Which is why most local campaigns run on a volunteer with a search bar, or on nothing.

## Why "it depends" is half true

Scope really does move the number. Veteran researchers argue a serious book goes far past search engines — [courthouse records in multiple counties, student newspapers, microfilm](https://capitolfax.com/2019/02/04/the-limits-of-oppo/) — and that depth takes weeks, not days. And the practitioners worth hiring agree on what actually moves voters: [voting records, donors, business conduct, and saying one thing while doing another](https://www.kpbs.org/news/national/2007/02/06/opposition-research-know-thine-enemies) — not embarrassing personal trivia.

So the honest cost drivers are: how long the subject's paper trail is, how many jurisdictions it crosses, how fast you need it, and whether you want a standing desk through Election Day or a one-time book.

## The question under the question

Campaigns asking "what does oppo cost" are usually really asking: *is it worth it at my level?* Here's the honest math. A contested state rep race can turn on a few hundred votes. A documented contrast — a vote, a donor, a contradiction, sourced and usable — is frequently the difference-maker, and the self-audit version (knowing your own file first) has saved more candidates than it has ever embarrassed.
