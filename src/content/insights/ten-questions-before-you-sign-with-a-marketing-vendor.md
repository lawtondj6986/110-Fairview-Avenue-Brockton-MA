A deck is a promise about the future. A contract is a promise about the past: what was agreed. The gap between them is where most agency relationships go wrong, and it closes fast if you ask ten plain questions before you sign. Here they are, with the answers we give, because a vendor who will not answer them in writing has already told you something.

## 1. What exactly goes live, and by what date?

Not "a new website." A list: the pages, the quote tool, the tracking, the review requests, the profile work, with a date. Ours is published as [the first 90 days](/first-90-days/), phase by phase for each system. JobSite goes live by day 21 of kickoff. Lead Engine's response automation switches on in week one.

## 2. Who owns the website, the domain, the accounts, and the data?

You should, from day one, with the vendor as a manager who can be removed. If a vendor hosts your site on their account, owns your ad account, or keeps your leads in their CRM, you are renting your own business. Our answer is the [You-Own-Everything guarantee](/guarantees/): code, domain, accounts, data, yours, always.

## 3. How is the result measured, and against what?

A result is a number compared to a baseline that was written down before the work started. Ask what the baseline is, who measures it, and where you can see it. We measure the baseline at kickoff (response time, quote-to-booked, reviews per month), agree the 90-day target in writing, and report it monthly.

## 4. What happens if you miss, in dollars?

A guarantee without a consequence is a slogan. Ours: live in 21 days or a $1,000-per-week credit; under 60 seconds to first response, measured monthly, or the month is credited; 90 days to measurable improvement or we work at no monthly fee until there is. And one thing we will not guarantee, because nobody honestly can: revenue.

## 5. What does the first month cost me in time?

A good system runs without you; a good vendor tells you what they need and when. Ours is about five hours in the first month (a kickoff, two or three review calls, a launch walkthrough) and a thirty-minute review every month after. The [kickoff checklist](/first-90-days/) lists the access and material that start the clock.

## 6. What is in the monthly report?

Ask to see one. If it leads with impressions, keep asking. Ours leads with whether the month was good and why, measures the guarantee, shows leads by source and the pipeline from lead to booked, and ends with what we did and what we do next. There is [a sample](/monthly-report/sample/) on the site with invented numbers, labeled as such.

## 7. How long is the contract, and how does it end?

Month to month after the build, with everything staying yours, is the honest default. Market Lock is the exception by design: a twelve-month term because exclusivity has to mean something, with first right to renew.

## 8. Who actually does the work?

Ask to meet them. At ICS the person on the call is the person who builds the system and reads your report. There is no account manager and no hand-off.

## 9. What does it cost, before the call?

A vendor who publishes prices can be qualified in thirty seconds and has nowhere to hide on the written quote. Ours are on the [pricing page](/pricing/), and the [Proposal Builder](/proposal/) writes the proposal from them. The [Payback Calculator](/payback/) shows when each system pays back on your numbers, at both ends of the range.

## 10. What would you tell me not to buy?

The best answer to this question is specific. Ours: if your volume does not support Market Lock, we will say so and scope down; if your response time is the whole problem, fix it with the free [90-Day Playbook](/playbook/) first and call when the leads outgrow it.

Ask all ten. Write down the answers. Then sign the one that put them in writing before you asked.
