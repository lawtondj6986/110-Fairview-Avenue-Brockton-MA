A studio owner in Weymouth told us her trial class was her best salesperson. People loved it. They left sweaty and smiling and said they would be back. About a third of them joined.

The class was not the problem. The problem was the silence afterward. A visitor walked out at 7pm, went home, got busy, and by Thursday the feeling had faded. Nobody from the studio said a word. The front desk meant to call. The coach had three more classes.

Trial-to-member conversion is decided in the seven days after the class. This article lays out that week, day by day. It is one piece of the [fitness studio playbook](/industries/fitness-studios/playbook/), and it is the piece that pays fastest.

## Why trials go cold

A fitness inquiry is an impulse. So is the decision to join. The impulse is strongest in the two hours after a good workout and weaker every day after.

Three things kill it:

- No next step. The visitor leaves without a second class on the calendar.
- No personal signal. Nobody tells her she did well or that anyone noticed she was there.
- No reason to decide now. The offer is always available, so the decision is always later.

There is also a common mistake on the front end: selling the membership before the trial instead of after. A pitch at the desk before someone has taken a class is a price with nothing attached to it. The same price after a class she enjoyed is a different conversation.

## The week, day by day

Here is the sequence we build. Every message is short, in the studio's voice, and consented to when the trial was booked.

| When | What goes out | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 hours after class | Coach's note, membership link, offer deadline | Catch the feeling while it is fresh |
| Day 1 | Offer of a second class, two times to choose from | Get a next visit on the calendar |
| Day 3 | One answer to the most common objection | Remove the reason to wait |
| Day 5 | Short check-in from the coach or owner | Open a real conversation |
| Day 7 | Deadline reminder, sent once | Ask for the decision |

The first message is the one that matters most:

"Great work today, [Name]. Coach [Dana] said you picked up the rows fast. Founding rate holds until [Friday]: [link]. Questions? Reply here, a human answers."

The moment someone joins, the sequence stops. The moment someone replies, a person takes over. Nobody who signed up on day 2 should get a deadline reminder on day 7.

## The coach's note takes thirty seconds

The line "Coach Dana said you picked up the rows fast" is what separates this from a marketing blast. It has to be true.

So the coach supplies it. After class, the coach opens the roster on a phone or tablet, taps each trial visitor, and picks or types one observation. Good form on the squat. Kept pace in the last round. Asked a smart question about scaling. Thirty seconds a person.

If the coach skips it, the message goes out with a plainer line, and the owner sees on the weekly report which classes had no notes. That visibility usually fixes it.

The note also tells the visitor something no advertisement can: someone at this studio was paying attention to her. For a person deciding between a studio at $130 a month and a big-box gym at $25, that attention is the product.

## A worked example: 40 trials a month

Take a studio running 40 trial classes a month, with a membership worth about $1,560 over the first year, which is $130 a month.

| | No follow-up | Seven-day follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trials per month | 40 | 40 |
| Conversion | 35% | 60% |
| New members | 14 | 24 |
| First-year value per member | $1,560 | $1,560 |
| First-year value of the month's cohort | $21,840 | $37,440 |

Ten more members a month, from the same 40 people who already walked in. That is $15,600 in first-year value added per month, with no extra ad spend and no extra classes.

January makes the stakes larger. Inquiries roughly triple. A studio with 150 January inquiries that answers in 60 seconds should book about 105 trials. At 60 percent, that is around 60 new members from one month. At 35 percent, it is 37. The difference is 23 memberships, or about $36,000 in first-year value, decided by a week of follow-up.

## Day 3: answer the real objection

By day 3 the visitors who have not joined mostly fall into three groups, and each has one worry.

- **Schedule.** "I am not sure I can make it often enough." Answer with the class times that match the days she named at intake.
- **Ability.** "Everyone else was fitter than me." Answer with how scaling works and how many members started exactly where she is.
- **Price.** "It is more than my old gym." Answer with what is included, and with the freeze policy, so the commitment feels less risky.

The intake questions already tell you which group someone is in. She told you what she is training for, what days she can come, and whether she has trained before. Use it. One message, one objection, two or three sentences.

Do not send all three answers to everyone. A person worried about keeping up does not need a paragraph about pricing.

## The deadline has to be real

The founding rate that "holds until Friday" must end on Friday.

Studios get this wrong in both directions. Some never set a deadline, so there is no reason to decide. Others set one and then extend it for anyone who asks, and word gets around a small town quickly.

A workable policy: the intro rate is good for seven days from the trial. On day 7 one reminder goes out. After that the standard rate applies, and the visitor moves to a quieter list that hears about a new program or a challenge once a month.

Then the real work starts. A new member who reaches eight visits in the first thirty days tends to stay past a year. One who goes ten days without checking in gets a text: "Hey [Name], we have not seen you in a bit. Life happens. The [Thursday 6pm] class has a spot with your name on it. Want it?" Conversion gets someone in the door. The first month keeps her.

## What to do this week

1. Count last month's trials and how many joined within 14 days. That is your baseline conversion rate.
2. Check how long after class your first follow-up goes out today, if one goes out at all.
3. Have coaches write one specific observation for each trial visitor after class, starting tomorrow.
4. Offer every trial visitor a second class with two specific times before she leaves the building.
5. Set one intro offer with one deadline, and decide now that you will not extend it.
