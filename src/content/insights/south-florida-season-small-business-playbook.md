A pool company in Boynton Beach does roughly the same work in August as in February. The difference is who is home. In August the owner of the house is in Connecticut and the phone is quiet. In February the owner is on the patio, has guests coming, and wants the heater fixed by Friday.

That is the South Florida season. From November to April the customer base in much of Broward and Palm Beach County gets larger, older, wealthier, and less patient, all at once. A service business that treats those six months like any other six months leaves most of the year's profit on the table.

This is the season month by month: what changes, what to send, and where the money is lost. The full twelve-month version by trade is in the industry playbooks, and the current month is always at [/this-month/?market=fl](/this-month/?market=fl).

## Why season is a different business

Out of season, the problem is demand. In season, the problem is speed and capacity. Those need different systems.

A simple example. A handyman and small-remodel company in Delray Beach takes about 30 inquiries a week in September and books 12 of them. In January the same company takes about 70 a week with the same three crews. It can still only complete about 18 jobs. So 52 inquiries a week go somewhere else, and the owner has no idea which 18 he kept. Usually it is the first 18 who got a human on the phone, not the best 18.

In season the questions that matter are:

- Who answered first? A returning resident calls three companies from the car. The first one to respond gets the visit.
- Which jobs fill the schedule? A full calendar of $180 repairs is a worse January than a mix that includes four $6,000 projects.
- Who is on the list for next year? Every seasonal customer served this winter should be tagged so November 2027 starts with a message, not a hope.

Everything below follows from those three.

## November: the phone changes overnight

Season opens in the first two weeks of November. Seasonal residents come back to houses and condos that sat closed for six months, and they find what the summer did: a dead car battery, a tripped breaker, a green pool, an AC that ran all summer and now makes a noise.

Three things to do this month:

- **Send the welcome-back message.** One text or email to tagged seasonal customers in the first week: we are glad you are back, here is the one thing most houses need after a summer closed, reply to book. Keep it to three sentences.
- **Check the profile.** Hours, holiday hours for Thanksgiving week, the service list, and five new photos. A returning resident who has not called you since March will look you up first.
- **Answer everything inside a minute.** This is the month a missed call costs the most, because the caller is setting up vendors for the whole winter.

Restaurants, salons, and medical practices see the same pattern in a different form: the regulars come back and want their old table, their old Thursday slot, their annual visit. The business that calls them first keeps them.

## December: a short calendar and full houses

December has about three working weeks. Family arrives, rentals fill, and everything has a deadline: before the party, before the kids land, before the 24th.

Demand shifts from projects to urgency. Small repairs and emergency calls spike between the holidays, and the calls come at odd hours because the house is full. For an AC or plumbing company in a condo-heavy city like [Hallandale Beach](/broward/hallandale-beach/) or [Sunrise](/broward/sunrise/), the week between Christmas and New Year can be the busiest emergency week of the winter.

Two rules for December:

1. Publish holiday hours everywhere by December 10: the profile, the website, the voicemail greeting. Wrong hours on the 26th cost reviews.
2. Decide the after-hours answer before you need it. Either someone is on call, or an automated agent takes the details, quotes the emergency fee, and books the first morning slot. Voicemail is not a plan.

Gift cards and January pre-booking belong here too. A salon or studio that sells the January appointment in December starts the peak with a full book.

## January and February: the peak

These are the two months when everyone is here. Roads are full, restaurants are booked, and the trades are quoting two and three weeks out. In Wellington the equestrian season is running, which brings its own demand for barns, fencing, trailers, and veterinary care. In Palm Beach County the charity and gala calendar fills salons and caterers.

The mistake in the peak is to stop quoting because the schedule is full. A roofing or paver company that is booked through February should still quote every inquiry the same day and offer a March or April date. Many seasonal owners will take a date after they leave, because an empty house is the easiest house to work on.

Worked numbers for a painting contractor in [Boca Raton](/palm-beach/boca-raton/): 45 inquiries in January, average job $7,500. If slow replies lose 10 of them and normal close rate is 30 percent, that is 3 jobs, or about $22,500, gone in one month to response time alone. In season, speed is the cheapest revenue there is. ## March: the last full month

March is still peak, with two changes. Spring break fills the coast, so Las Olas, Atlantic Avenue, and the beach districts run at capacity. And seasonal customers start thinking about leaving, which creates a deadline: they want the treatment plan finished, the project closed out, the car serviced for the drive north.

This is the month to ask two questions of every seasonal customer:

- What do you want done while you are away? Remodels, exterior paint, flooring, and pool resurfacing are easier in an empty house, and summer is when crews are available.
- Can we put you on the calendar for November now? A standing appointment booked in March is worth more than any campaign in October.

It is also the first humid stretch of the year, so AC tune-up inquiries begin. Year-round customers should get that message in March, before the first hot week of May.

## April: the handoff nobody plans

By mid-April the seasonal residents are leaving and weekday volume drops. Most owners feel relief and then, around the second week of May, worry.

April is when next season is built:

- **Tag every seasonal customer** with the month they arrive and the month they leave. This is ten minutes a week at the front desk and it makes November work.
- **Ask for the review now.** A customer who was happy in February will still write two sentences in April. In July they will not.
- **Sell the summer.** Home watch visits, summer projects, storage, maintenance plans: anything that keeps revenue coming from an owner who is 1,200 miles away.
- **Turn to the locals.** Year-round customers carry May to October. Recall and reactivation campaigns to that list pay best starting now.

## The season on one page

| Month | What changes | The one thing to send |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November | Seasonal residents return | Welcome back, book your first visit |
| December | Guests, deadlines, emergencies | Holiday hours and the after-hours number |
| January | Peak volume | Same-day quotes with a real date |
| February | Peak volume, boat show and gala weeks | Confirmations and review requests |
| March | Spring break, departures begin | Summer project and November pre-booking |
| April | Residents leave | Review request and summer plan |

## What to do this week

- Send the welcome-back message to every seasonal customer you can identify, today, before Thanksgiving week.
- Set Thanksgiving and December holiday hours on your Google Business Profile and your voicemail.
- Call your own number at 7 p.m. and see what a returning customer hears.
- Start tagging seasonal customers with arrival and departure months at every booking.
- Decide what you will offer for the summer, so it is ready to sell in March.
