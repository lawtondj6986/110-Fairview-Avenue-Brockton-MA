A salon in Delray Beach has a client who comes every Thursday at 10 from November to April. She has done it for four winters. In April she says see you in the fall. In November she lands, cannot find the salon's number in a phone she replaced over the summer, searches, and books with a place that had an opening and answered in a minute.

Nobody did anything wrong. The salon simply waited to be remembered, and a seasonal customer has had six months to forget.

Seasonal residents are the most valuable returning customers a South Florida service business has. They come back on a known date, they buy a full winter of service, and they refer their neighbors in the building. Winning them back is not a marketing campaign. It is a list, three messages, and a habit in April.

## What a seasonal customer is worth

The reason to do this carefully is the arithmetic. Take a few typical cases, rough numbers:

- A salon client with a weekly blowout and monthly color: about 24 visits and $2,600 in a season.
- A pool service account, kept year-round by an owner who is only here in winter: about $1,800 a year plus repairs.
- A dental patient who does hygiene and the work they put off: $900 to $3,000 in a winter.
- An auto shop customer whose car comes out of storage: a battery, tires, and service, $600 to $1,200 each November.

Now the list. A business with 800 active customers in a coastal city might have 250 who are seasonal. If 20 percent of them do not come back each fall for no reason except that someone else answered first, that is 50 customers. At $1,500 each, the leak is about $75,000 a year, and it never shows up as a complaint or a bad review. It just looks like a slightly soft November.

## Build the list in spring

Reactivation in November depends on a record made in March. Every seasonal customer needs four things in your system:

- A tag that says seasonal.
- The month they usually arrive and the month they leave.
- A mobile number, with consent to text it.
- One note on what they usually buy first when they are back.

The front desk gets this by asking one question at checkout from February on: are you here year-round, or do you head north? Then the follow-up: when are you back?

The second half of the spring habit is the pre-booking. Before a seasonal customer leaves, offer the first November slot. A standing Thursday at 10 that is already in the calendar cannot be lost to a search result. In a typical service business, a meaningful share of seasonal regulars will take it if asked, and almost none will if not asked.

If you did not do this last spring, start now. Everyone who walks in this month and says they just got back is a record to tag.

## The three messages

Each message is short, comes from the business number they already know, and asks for a reply. No links required, no coupon.

**Message one: two weeks before arrival, mid to late October.**
Hi Carol, it is Dana at the salon. Are you back in November? I can hold your Thursday 10:00 starting the 12th. Reply YES and it is yours.

**Message two: arrival week, early November.**
Welcome back. Most pools need a filter clean and a chemistry reset after a summer closed up. We can do yours Tuesday or Thursday this week. Reply with a day.

**Message three: a reminder in late November to those who did not book.**
We still have you on our list for the winter. A few December times are left before the holidays. Reply here or call and we will set it up.

Then stop. Three messages over five weeks is service. Six is nagging. Anyone who replies that they sold the condo or are not coming this year gets thanked and untagged.

The first thing they buy differs by trade, and so does the script. The Florida versions are in the playbooks, for example [salons and wellness](/industries/salons-wellness/playbook/?market=fl) and [auto services](/industries/auto-services/playbook/?market=fl).

## A worked November for an auto shop

A repair shop on Federal Highway in [Pompano Beach](/broward/pompano-beach/) has 1,400 customers on file. After a winter of tagging, 310 are marked seasonal, most with a car that sits from May to October.

In mid-October message one goes to all 310: your car has been sitting since spring, we can check the battery, tires, and fluids the week you arrive, reply with your arrival date.

Typical results for a list like this, as a rough guide:

- About 95 reply with a date and get booked before they land.
- Message two in early November books roughly 45 more.
- Message three picks up about 20.

That is 160 cars, a little over half the list, booked without an ad. At an average first ticket of $420, it is about $67,000 in November and December, spread across the calendar by arrival date instead of landing in one frantic week. The other 150 customers are not lost. Many call on their own, and the shop's phone is free to answer them because the first 160 never had to call.

## Details that trip people up

A few practical points that come up in every install:

- **They are still up north when message one arrives.** That is the point, but mind the clock. Most are in the Eastern time zone, some are in Central, and Florida's rules set a texting window that ends at 8 p.m. in the recipient's local time. Mid-morning is safe everywhere.
- **Canadian numbers are different.** Many seasonal residents in Hollywood, Hallandale Beach, and south Palm Beach County are Canadian. Canada has its own consent law for commercial messages. Ask your attorney before texting those numbers and use email if in doubt.
- **Consent is per customer.** A marketing text needs written consent under Florida and federal rules. Collect it on the intake form, record it, and honor every STOP the same day.
- **Couples share appointments.** One message per household for home services, one per person for personal services.
- **Summer services are a separate offer.** If you check on a house or a boat while the owner is away, that is your trade's service. ICS's real estate brokerage is Massachusetts-licensed, and a Florida property matter is handled alongside a licensed Florida broker.

## Where it matters most

Every city in the two counties has seasonal residents, but the share varies widely.

The heaviest concentrations are the coastal condo towers of [Hallandale Beach](/broward/hallandale-beach/) and Hollywood, the communities of Boca Raton, Delray Beach, and Boynton Beach, the golf and club communities of Palm Beach Gardens and Jupiter, and Tamarac's established retiree neighborhoods. Wellington has its own version: households that follow the horses in for the winter show season and leave when it ends.

Family suburbs such as Pembroke Pines, Coral Springs, Cooper City, and Royal Palm Beach run on the school calendar instead. There the equivalent campaign is back to school in mid-August, not November.

A business that serves both kinds of city should run both calendars. The month's notes for each are at [/this-month/?market=fl](/this-month/?market=fl).

- Send the welcome-back message today to every customer you can identify as seasonal, even from memory.
