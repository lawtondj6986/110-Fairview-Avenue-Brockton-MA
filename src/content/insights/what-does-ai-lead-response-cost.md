Ask what an AI lead-response system costs and you'll get the same answer every marketing vendor gives: *it depends — let's book a demo*. Then the price appears only after a sales call, which tells you the number was never fixed to begin with.

Here's the honest version, with real ranges, so you can calibrate before anyone gets you on the phone.

## The market, in tiers

- **DIY widgets and tools — ~$50–$500/month.** Chat bubbles, missed-call-to-text apps, and shared "AI receptionist" platforms. Cheap, fast to turn on, and genuinely useful — but you're renting shared software, configuring it yourself, and it stops at capture. The lead gets a text; closing it is still on you.
- **Answering services — a few hundred dollars a month, or $1–$2/minute.** A human picks up after hours. Better than voicemail, but they don't know your business, don't quote, and don't follow up.
- **Marketing agencies — $2,000–$5,000+/month retainers.** Real capability, but bundled into a retainer where the lead system is one line item among many, and you rarely own the underlying build.
- **Custom, done-for-you systems — four to five figures to build, then a monthly.** A system designed around your business: answers every channel in under a minute, qualifies and books, chases the lead to a decision, and plugs into your calendar and CRM. The real cost — and the real payoff.

## What actually drives the number

Scope, honestly. The cost moves on four things: how many **channels** it covers (just missed-call-text, or phone *and* web *and* SMS *and* automated follow-up); whether it only **captures** leads or also **qualifies and books** them; how deeply it **integrates** with the tools you already run; and whether you're renting shared software or getting something **built around your business that you own**.

Everything else is noise. A vendor who can't map your price to those four levers is quoting a retainer, not a system.
