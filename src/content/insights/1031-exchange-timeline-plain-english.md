An owner in Whitman sells a flex building he has held for fourteen years. The sale price is good. Then his CPA shows him what is owed on the gain, and the number is larger than his first down payment was.

A [1031 exchange](/glossary/1031-exchange/) is the tool for that moment. It lets an owner of investment or business real estate sell, buy other real estate, and defer the tax on the gain. It is not complicated. It is strict. Two deadlines and one rule about the money decide whether it works.

ICS's founder is a licensed Massachusetts real estate salesperson, not an attorney or a CPA. What follows is the general federal structure. Talk to your CPA and your attorney before you list, because the tax decisions come first.

## What an exchange is, and what qualifies

Section 1031 of the tax code says no gain is recognized when real property held for business or investment is exchanged for other real property held for business or investment. Three points do most of the work.

- **Real estate for real estate.** Since 2018 the rule covers real property only. Equipment and vehicles do not qualify.
- **Like-kind is broad.** A warehouse can be exchanged for a strip center, land, or an apartment building. The properties do not have to match in type.
- **Held for business or investment.** Your home does not qualify. Property bought to flip does not qualify. A building your company operates from generally does.

The same taxpayer who sells has to buy. If an LLC sells, that LLC buys.

And it is a deferral, not forgiveness. The gain carries into the new property through a lower tax basis. Many owners exchange repeatedly over decades. What happens at the end is an estate planning question for your CPA and attorney.

## The intermediary and the money

In a normal sale the proceeds go to the seller. In an exchange they cannot. If you receive the money, or even have the right to receive it, the exchange is over.

So a qualified intermediary stands in the middle. The QI signs an exchange agreement with you before the sale, receives the proceeds at closing, holds them, and sends them directly to the closing on the replacement property.

Three practical rules:

1. **Hire the QI before the sale closes.** After closing is too late. A week ahead is comfortable.
2. **The QI must be independent.** Anyone who has been your agent in the last two years is generally disqualified, including your attorney, accountant, and broker.
3. **Vet the QI.** Intermediaries are lightly regulated. Ask how funds are held, whether accounts are segregated, and what bond and insurance coverage they carry.

Fees for a straightforward exchange are typically modest next to the tax at stake. The risk is not the fee. It is choosing a QI by price alone.

## Day 0, day 45, day 180

The clock starts the day the sale closes. That is day 0.

**By midnight of day 45** you must identify replacement property in writing, signed, delivered to the QI. The description has to be specific: a street address or legal description. Most owners use the three-property rule, which lets you name up to three properties of any value. There is also a rule allowing more than three if their combined value is no more than 200 percent of what you sold.

**By day 180** you must close on one or more of the identified properties. You cannot buy something that is not on the list.

Both periods are calendar days. They run concurrently, so after day 45 you have 135 days left, not 180.

A worked example. Sale closes Tuesday, January 12, 2027.

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Day 0, sale closes | January 12, 2027 |
| Day 45, identification due | Friday, February 26, 2027 |
| Day 180, purchase must close | Sunday, July 11, 2027 |

Day 180 falls on a Sunday. It does not roll to Monday. In practice the closing has to happen by Friday, July 9.

## The tax-return trap for late-year sales

The 180 days has a second limit that catches people every winter. The exchange period ends on the earlier of day 180 or the due date of your tax return for the year of the sale, including extensions.

Take a sale that closes November 20, 2026. Day 45 is January 4, 2027. Day 180 is May 19, 2027. But an individual's 2026 return is due April 15, 2027. Without an extension, the exchange period ends April 15, more than a month early.

The fix is simple: file an extension. It has to actually be filed. Tell your CPA about the exchange the week you sell, not in March.

## What the tax might be, and what boot is

Back to the Whitman owner. He bought for $600,000, took $180,000 of depreciation, and sells for $1,400,000. Ignoring selling costs, his adjusted basis is $420,000 and his gain is $980,000.

A rough, illustrative stack at top rates:

- Depreciation recapture: $180,000 at up to 25 percent, about $45,000
- Remaining gain: $800,000 at 20 percent federal, about $160,000
- Net investment income tax, if it applies: 3.8 percent, about $37,000
- Massachusetts, at 5 percent on long-term gain: about $49,000

That approaches $290,000. Your own figure depends on your income, your entity, and current rates, and Massachusetts has a surtax on very high incomes. Massachusetts generally follows the federal treatment of exchanges. Your CPA computes the real number.

To defer all of it, the general rule is to buy property of equal or greater value and reinvest all the net proceeds. Whatever you take out is called boot, and boot is taxable.

Example: he sells for $1,400,000 and pays off a $300,000 mortgage, leaving $1,100,000 with the QI. If he buys a $1,250,000 building, he has traded down by $150,000, and roughly that amount is taxable. If he buys at $1,500,000 using all the proceeds plus a new loan, nothing is.

## Where exchanges fail in practice

The rules rarely kill an exchange. The market does.

- **Nothing to buy.** Forty-five days is short on the South Shore, where good small buildings trade quietly. Owners who start looking after closing run out of time.
- **One property identified.** If it fails inspection on day 60, there is no backup. Name three.
- **A purchase that would not stand on its own.** Paying too much for a weak building to save tax is a bad trade. Underwrite the replacement as if there were no exchange. The [cap rate calculator](/cap-rate/) is a start.
- **Financing delays.** The lender's timeline has to fit inside day 180.

A reverse exchange, where you buy first and sell second, exists for owners who find the replacement early. It costs more and needs an experienced QI and attorney.

## What to do this week

1. Ask your CPA for a written estimate of the tax on a sale at your expected price. That number tells you whether an exchange is worth the constraints.
2. Interview two qualified intermediaries and ask how they hold funds.
3. Start looking for replacement property now, before the current building is under agreement.
4. Ask your attorney to include exchange cooperation language in the purchase and sale agreement.
5. Put the two deadlines on the calendar the day you close, with a reminder at day 30.
6. Write your replacement criteria into a [Buyer Brief](/buyer-brief/), and use the seller net sheet in the [deal tools](/deal-tools/) to see what you will actually have to reinvest.
