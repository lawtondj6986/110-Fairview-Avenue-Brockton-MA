Search is splitting in two. People still Google, but a growing share of "who should I hire?" questions now go to AI assistants — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and the AI summaries at the top of Google itself. When someone asks *"who's a good commercial paving contractor near Brockton?"*, an AI produces two or three names, not ten blue links.

If your business isn't structured to be one of those names, you're invisible in the fastest-growing search channel — even with a decent website.

## What AI assistants actually look for

AI systems recommend businesses they can **verify and understand**. In practice that means:

1. **A consistent digital identity.** Same name, address, phone, and service list everywhere — website, Google Business Profile, directories. Conflicting data makes AI skip you.
2. **Structured data.** Schema markup that tells machines, in their own language, what you do, where you operate, and what you charge. Most local sites have none.
3. **Genuine review velocity.** Not just a high rating — recent, specific reviews that mention services and towns. AI reads them for evidence.
4. **Content that answers real questions.** Pages that directly address "how much does X cost on the South Shore" get cited; brochure pages don't.
