A Brockton HVAC owner sends a furnace tune-up special on the first cold morning in October. His phone rings all day. He turns half the calls away because his two techs are already booked solid by his competitor's customers, who got their tune-up text on September 8.

That is the whole lesson of a marketing calendar. Demand on the South Shore is not random. It follows the thermostat, the school year, the tide chart, and a handful of Massachusetts dates everybody knows and almost nobody plans around. The business that sends the right message four to six weeks early owns the month. The one that reacts gets the leftovers.

Here is the year, with three campaigns worked out in numbers below the table.

## How to read the calendar

Each month has one job. Not four posts a week, not a new idea every Monday: one offer, timed to the demand, sent through three channels every small business already has.

1. **A Google Business Profile post** with one photo and the offer, which also feeds the AI summaries that now name businesses.
2. **One text to customers who consented** to marketing texts, sent on a weekday between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m., ending with STOP.
3. **One page or update on the website,** so "furnace tune-up Brockton" lands on a page with this month's offer and availability.

The lead time rule is four to six weeks before peak demand. The exception is the storm or heat wave you cannot schedule; have that message written in advance and send it the morning the forecast turns. The glossary's [content calendar](/glossary/content-calendar/) entry covers the planning mechanics; this article covers what goes in the boxes.

Towns differ. A Marshfield business lives on beach season; a Taunton business does not. Weight the months your own books show.

## Heating season: the September furnace campaign

Massachusetts landlords must provide heat starting in mid-September, and homeowners start thinking about the furnace the first week nights drop into the forties. Peak HVAC call volume runs from early October into November. The campaign therefore goes out the first week of September.

Take a Brockton HVAC company with 800 past customers, of whom 520 have consented to marketing texts. On September 8 it sends one text: "It's Dan at Fairview Heating. Tune-up season is here. $189 furnace tune-up through September 30, book here. Reply STOP to end." The same offer goes up as a profile post and on the site's tune-up page.

In our installs a consented list responds to a seasonal service text at roughly 5 to 7 percent. At 6 percent, that is 31 tune-ups booked in two weeks, $5,900 of September revenue pulled onto open calendar days. More important, about one tune-up in eight turns up a system that needs replacement. Four replacement conversations at a typical $9,000 to $14,000 each, started in September when the techs have time, not in January.

## Spring: Patriots' Day through prom

April and May are the busiest stretch of the year for salons and the start of the year for outdoor trades, and they share a lead-time problem. Prom at South Shore high schools clusters in May. Weddings start in June. A salon that posts "prom bookings open" in May is a month late.

Take a Hingham salon with 850 consented clients. On March 15 it sends: "Prom and wedding trial bookings are open. Trials in April, styling in May. Book your trial now, spots go fast." A profile post with last year's best updo goes up the same day, and the formal styling page gets this year's prices.

At a 4 percent response, 34 clients book a trial in April at about $65 each. Roughly 80 percent convert to the event booking at $120 to $150, often with a mother or friend alongside. Call it 27 event bookings at $135, about $3,600 of May revenue, booked in March when chairs were empty. The [salon and wellness playbook](/industries/salons-wellness/playbook/) covers the gift card and bridal party sequences that stack on it.

For landscapers and painters, Patriots' Day weekend is the first weekend homeowners are outside looking at the yard. The cleanup text goes out the week before.

## Summer: beach season and the Fourth

From Memorial Day to Labor Day, the coastal towns run on visitors and the inland towns run on heat. The two campaigns are different and both are time-sensitive.

A restaurant near Nantasket or on Plymouth's waterfront lives on a ten-week window. The profile needs summer hours updated before Memorial Day, the menu loaded with photos, and a post every two weeks with what is fresh. Beach traffic searches "seafood near me" from a parking lot, and the businesses in the Maps pack with current hours and a menu get the table. The [restaurant playbook](/industries/restaurants-food/playbook/) has the summer checklist.

For HVAC, July is emergency season, and the campaign is not an offer. It is a readiness text in late June: "Heat wave coming next week. Priority service for existing customers, reply YES to get on the list." A heat wave with eighty calls a day cannot be marketed into, only managed, and the business that contacts its base first keeps the calendar for its own customers.

Marine businesses open haul-out and winterization bookings in August, because the yard fills by October.

## Back to school through Thanksgiving

The August sales tax holiday weekend pulls appliance and equipment purchases forward across Massachusetts. For contractors, that is the week to quote heating replacements and generators. For salons, back-to-school is a two-week burst of cuts in late August, and a teacher offer in early September fills the slow days after.

October opens holiday party bookings for restaurants and salons; the business that posts "holiday parties, book by November 1" in early October fills December. November belongs to Plymouth: Thanksgiving weekend brings crowds to the waterfront, and every restaurant and shop within ten miles should have holiday hours, pre-order menus, and a profile post up by November 1. Small Business Saturday, two days later, is the gift card launch for every trade.

Then December, and the calendar starts over. The HVAC owner who ran the September campaign has a full January.

## What to do this week

1. Pull twelve months of revenue by month and mark your three strongest; the campaigns belong four to six weeks before them.
2. Pick next month's offer from the table and write the text, the profile post, and the web page sentence in one sitting.
3. Count your consented texting list; if it is under 200, make capturing consent this month's offer instead.
4. Put the twelve send dates on the shop calendar now, with each campaign written the week before.
5. Set a reminder the day before each send to check the forecast and the profile hours.
