If your marketing vendor sends a monthly report, open the last one and try to answer three questions from it in sixty seconds. Was the month good? Why? What are they doing about it? If you cannot, the report is doing its job for the vendor, not for you.

## Lead with the verdict

The first line of a report should be a sentence a busy owner can read standing up: the month was good, or it was not, and the one reason why. Everything below it is evidence. In the intelligence trade this is called the bottom line up front, and it is the single most useful habit a report can have, because it forces the person writing it to have a point of view.

## Measure the promise you paid for

Every vendor promises something. A useful report measures that specific promise every month, in the same way, against a number that was written down before the work started. For ICS clients the promise is a first response to every lead in under sixty seconds, so the report shows the month's median and, more importantly, the distribution: what share of leads were answered under thirty seconds, under sixty, under five minutes, and how many waited longer. A median can hide a bad week. The distribution cannot.

If your vendor's promise cannot be measured, it is not a promise.

## Show where leads came from and where they died

Two charts earn their place on every report. The first is leads by source, ranked: Google Business Profile, website form, missed-call text-back, referral, paid search, social. It tells you which channel to feed. The second is the pipeline: leads, answered, quoted, booked, completed. The drop between each step is where next month's work is. A business that quotes sixty jobs and books thirty has a follow-up problem, not a lead problem, and the report should say so.

## Put the money in the same place every month

Revenue booked and attributed, from a CRM the business owns, with "attributed" meaning the lead was tracked from first contact to a booked job. Nothing estimated. Beside it: average booked job, any paid media, the retainer, and cost per booked job all in. Owners can forgive a soft month; they cannot forgive not knowing.

## Flags, then the two lists

A short section of flags, each marked good or watch, with an icon and a word so nobody has to decode colors. Then two lists that are really a contract: what we did this month, and what we will do next month. The second list is what you hold the vendor to on the first of the following month.

## What it should not have

Impressions, reach, and follower counts leading the page. Twelve charts where four would do. A "sentiment score." Anything that cannot change a decision you would make.
